XBP Global (NASDAQ:XBP) reported a disappointing start to the year as it posted a sizable net loss driven by a sharp decline in sales. The technology-driven workflow automation provider is focusing on restructuring and AI integration, reporting results for the first quarter (released May 14, 2026) that capture its early progress and ongoing challenges. Overall, the period showed notable gains in contract bookings and gross margin, but persistent revenue decline outweighed those positives.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell to $197.1 million, dropping 14.2% year over year, mainly due to older contract declines.
Gross margin increased by 0.7 percentage points to 22.9%, reflecting improved automation and cost controls.
Contract wins surged, with total contract value up 68.8% to $108.1 million, pointing to strong sales momentum.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects its automation push to deliver between $55 million and $60 million in annualized cost savings, most of which should be realized in the first half of 2026. The board has launched a review of “strategic alternatives,” signaling the potential for major changes such as a sale or merger. Investors should closely watch the pace at which new contracts convert into actual revenue as this will determine if improving bookings can offset the current revenue decline.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter saw XBP Global book $108.1 million in total contract value, nearly 69% higher than a year ago, giving the company a sizable new pipeline despite ongoing revenue pressures. Gross margin improved to 22.9% company-wide, especially in its Applied Workflow Automation segment, where margin rose by 2.6 percentage points compared to the prior year. However, earnings were sharply lower, and the net loss widened due to falling sales and ongoing restructuring costs as the company shifts to a more AI-centric model.

Segment performance varied: The Applied Workflow Automation segment, which delivers automated solutions for finance and operations, remained the main source of sales, but its revenue shrank while margin improved. The Technology segment, which includes software and managed IT, reported a steep fall in both revenue (down 26.4%) and margin (down 9.3 percentage points to 52.4%).

Management also detailed plans to cut global headcount by 20% by the end of 2026 as part of its transformation, aiming to boost productivity and profitability through automation and workflow standardization. In parallel, the board's initiation of a strategic alternatives review shows readiness for a significant shift in company direction, should operating improvements fail to restore profitability.
