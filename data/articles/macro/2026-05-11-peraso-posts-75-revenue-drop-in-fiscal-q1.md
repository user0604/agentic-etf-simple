Peraso (NASDAQ:PRSO) came up short this quarter, as the delayed shipment of a key order and irregular sales from core customers weighed on results. The fabless semiconductor company develops millimeter wave (mmWave) wireless chipsets and modules used in fixed wireless access and tactical communications. It reported its first quarter of fiscal 2026 results on May 11, 2026. Overall, the period was marked by sharp revenue contraction and larger losses, though there was some technical progress in expanding into new markets.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped sharply year over year, falling 75% as a crucial customer order was delayed beyond the quarter.
Gross margin slipped to 61.5% in Q1 2026, primarily due to decreased sales of legacy memory ICs compared to the prior year.
Cash and cash equivalents declined slightly to $2.67 million, reflecting ongoing operating losses and continued investment in R&D and sales efforts.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide new financial guidance for upcoming quarters, highlighting ongoing uncertainty around order timing from major customers and emerging partners. It remains focused on winning new design contracts and converting customer interest into firm orders, particularly in tactical communications. The key issue for investors is if and when new sales in mmWave technology can fill the gap left by declining legacy product revenue and irregular fixed wireless orders.

Quarterly Highlights

The delayed shipment of a significant customer order resulted in revenue well below the company’s internal forecast for the quarter. In the prior quarter, management expected about $1.2 million in revenue but only $0.96 million was recognized, underlining ongoing demand uncertainty in fixed wireless access.

Nonrecurring engineering (NRE) revenue, mostly from early-stage tactical communications projects, increased year over year but did not compensate for the drop in product sales. Shipments to InTACT, an Israeli defense contractor, began with modules for a drone identification system, demonstrating Peraso's efforts to enter new markets.

Despite cost controls, the company's operating expenses held relatively steady while revenue declined, causing net losses to widen significantly year over year. Cash burn continued, and management raised the possibility of needing additional capital if the current pattern persists.

What Peraso Does

Peraso makes semiconductor chips and modules that support high-speed wireless connections over millimeter wave frequencies for fixed wireless access and specialized communications markets.
