Earnings Preview: V.F.

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 9:05 AM

Comments

V.F. (NYSE:VFC), a global apparel, footwear, and accessories company best known for brands like The North Face, Vans, and Timberland, is expected to report earnings for its fourth fiscal quarter on Wednesday, May 20, 2026, before the market opens.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, V.F. delivered results that exceeded analyst expectations. Revenue rose 1% year over year to $2.88 billion, with growth led by The North Face and Timberland brands, while Vans continued to decline. Gross margin improved to 56.6%, up 30 basis points from the prior year, driven by favorable product mix and lower product costs, despite the negative impact of new tariffs. Adjusted earnings per share came in at $0.58 (excluding the gain from the Dickies sale), which met analyst consensus. The company also completed the sale of its Dickies brand in November 2025, strengthening its balance sheet and reducing net debt. Operating margin improved to 10.1% from 8.0% a year earlier, reflecting ongoing cost savings from the Reinvent transformation program, which is now substantially complete.

Positives
Revenue growth exceeded expectations. The company returned to top-line growth, with total revenue up 1% and key brands like The North Face and Timberland each posting 8% global growth. This was ahead of guidance and marked a turnaround from prior quarters.
Gross margin expansion despite tariffs. Gross margin improved to 56.6%, showing the company’s ability to offset tariff costs with sourcing savings and a favorable sales mix.
Debt reduction and improved liquidity. Net debt fell to 70.1% of total capital, and the company ended the quarter with $1.5 billion in cash and no borrowings on its main credit facility, providing financial flexibility.
Reinvent cost savings delivered as planned. The Reinvent transformation program’s restructuring actions were substantially completed, with lower selling, general, and administrative expenses helping to boost operating margin.
Negatives
Vans brand continued to decline. Vans revenue fell 8% globally, with declines in all regions. The Active segment, which includes Vans, posted a loss for the quarter, reflecting ongoing challenges in revitalizing this brand.
Asia-Pacific region remained weak. Revenues in Asia-Pacific declined 6% year over year, and both The North Face and Timberland saw sales fall in the region, highlighting persistent macroeconomic and demand headwinds.
Impairment charge for Napapijri brand. The company recorded a $30.7 million goodwill impairment related to the Napapijri brand, reflecting lower future growth expectations following recent leadership changes.
Tariff impacts likely to persist. Tariffs negatively affected gross margin in Q3 and are expected to continue into Q4, with mitigation efforts such as price increases only partially offsetting the impact.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report
Tariff mitigation and pricing actions: Investors should monitor how much pricing increases and sourcing changes offset the ongoing tariff costs, as management expects gross margin to be flat to slightly up versus last year.
Vans brand trajectory and digital performance: The company expects Vans revenue to decline by mid-single digits in Q4, but digital sales trends have improved. Watch for signs that new products and marketing efforts are translating into a broader turnaround.
The North Face and Timberland growth sustainability: Management guided for The North Face to maintain its Q3 growth pace, while Timberland growth is expected to slow. Performance in the Americas and continued demand for premium products will be key indicators.
Cash flow and debt reduction progress: V.F. aims to end the year with leverage at or below 3.5 times. Free cash flow and further debt paydown will be important for the company’s financial health and future investment capacity.
Guidance practices and long-term targets: Management has indicated it is considering reintroducing full-year guidance, possibly as soon as the Q4 report. Investors should watch for any updates on this and on progress toward medium-term margin and leverage goals.
