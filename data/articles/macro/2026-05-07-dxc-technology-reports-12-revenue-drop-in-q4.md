DXC Technology Reports 1.2% Revenue Drop in Q4

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:55 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
DXC Technology faced continued top-line pressure, with bookings and revenue both declining from a year earlier.
Non-GAAP earnings per share decreased by 8.3% in the quarter.
Management’s guidance for fiscal 2027 points to further revenue decline and lower free cash flow.

DXC Technology (NYSE:DXC) struggled with demand and a drop in new business signings during the latest quarter. The global IT services and consulting provider continues its transition into new AI and cloud platforms, but its ability to bolster sales has not materialized yet. The company released its results for the three months ended March 31, 2026 (reported May 7, 2026), revealing further weakness on the revenue front and a subdued outlook for the year ahead. In summary, margin improvements could not offset falling revenue and a weaker pipeline, resulting in an uneven quarter.

Notable developments
Revenue came in at $3.13 billion, falling 1.2% from the previous year, reflecting weaker sales and contracting demand in core business areas.
Adjusted EBIT margin (earnings before interest and taxes, a measure of operating profit as a percentage of sales) was 7.6%, despite the top-line decline.
Bookings for new business, a crucial forward-looking metric, dropped by 13.5% year over year to $3.3 billion, signaling continued challenges in winning new contracts.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects the revenue slide to continue into fiscal 2027, forecasting a decrease of 3%–5% on an organic basis and a step down in free cash flow to around $600 million. It sees some margin resilience but warns that top-line pressures may linger. Investors should monitor how quickly new AI and cloud offerings translate into meaningful revenue and offset weakness in legacy contracts.

Quarterly highlights

Bookings, which represent the dollar value of new contracts signed, declined sharply to $3.3 billion, down from $3.8 billion a year earlier. This signals a tough environment for securing new work across IT outsourcing and consulting markets.

While adjusted EBIT margin showed a modest improvement, the company achieved this largely through cost controls and internal efficiencies, not by growing its client base. Free cash flow—cash generated after accounting for capital spending—was stable at $110 million for the period.

The Insurance Software & Services segment saw improved sales, up 7.3% year over year in the fourth quarter, but for the full fiscal year, segment profit declined 20.4% year over year and the profit margin decreased to 10.1%. This indicates rising costs or investment in the unit, even as demand grew.

What DXC Technology does

DXC Technology provides IT services, consulting, and software solutions to enterprises worldwide, focusing on digital transformation, cloud, and cybersecurity.
