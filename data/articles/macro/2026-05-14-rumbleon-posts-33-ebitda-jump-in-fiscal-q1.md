Key Takeaways
RumbleOn (B Shares) reported operating improvement but continued to see negative cash flow.
Adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP) rose 32.9% year over year.
Management remains focused on operational efficiency and liquidity as inventory swells ahead of peak season.

Powersports retailer RumbleOn (B Shares) (NASDAQ:RDNW) continued to improve operations in the latest quarter, but rising inventory and cash outflows kept pressure on its finances. The company operates the largest network of powersports dealerships in the United States, selling motorcycles, all-terrain vehicles, and other recreational vehicles. Its first-quarter 2026 results, released on May 14, 2026, covered performance through March and highlighted both progress and ongoing challenges. Overall, the quarter delivered higher retail and same store sales, but required substantial capital to fund seasonal inventory growth just as the market heads into its busiest months.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 6.4%, with same store sales up 13.1%, driven by more unit sales and higher sales of new retail vehicles.
Adjusted EBITDA rose by 32.9%, while net losses narrowed to $0.11 per share from $0.26 per share a year ago.
Adjusted free cash flow declined further into negative territory at ($28.2 million), mainly because inventory rose ahead of peak sales season.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its commitment to driving operational efficiency and long-term value, noting that higher working capital needs are linked to the spring and summer selling season. There was no forward guidance, but improving profitability and cash flow remain top priorities. Investors should watch whether sales levels in the peak season translate into stronger free cash flow and less reliance on debt.

Quarterly Highlights

The company posted robust same store revenue growth at 13.1%, boosted by a 16.3% increase in new retail vehicle sales. This metric signals healthy organic expansion at the dealership level and was a standout area of improvement compared to the same quarter the previous year.

While gross profit expanded and operating income doubled, free cash flow became more negative due to a $45.1 million buildup in inventory. This inventory growth is typical ahead of seasonal demand but adds pressure to the company's available liquidity and borrowing needs.

On the cost side, selling, general and administrative expenses rose just 1.6% year over year, leading to improved expense leverage as a share of gross profit. The margin on each unit sold dipped slightly, likely driven by sales channel and product mix.
