Cisco Systems (NASDAQ:CSCO) reported a business win for the quarter, as robust networking upgrades and accelerating demand for artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure boosted its results. The leading provider of networking hardware and software solutions saw gains driven by hyperscale customers and broad-based technology refreshes. For the quarter ending April 25, 2026 (released May 13, 2026), it posted record revenue and made upward revisions to its full-year guidance. Overall, the period featured double-digit growth but also introduced caution due to lower cash flow and margin pressure.

Notable Developments
Networking revenue increased 25% to $8.8 billion, led by significant AI infrastructure and large enterprise upgrades.
Non-GAAP gross margin declined 2.6 percentage points, mainly due to higher memory costs and a less favorable product mix.
Cash flow from operations dropped 7% year over year, even as net income rose and the dividend per share was raised to $0.42.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year revenue forecast to a range of $62.8 billion to $63.0 billion and lifted its outlook for AI-related orders by $4 billion. The company expects continued growth in networking but signals caution around near-term gross margin and services weakness. The most important factor for investors to follow next is whether the security segment’s new product adoption will translate into higher recurring revenue as Splunk’s cloud transition matures.

Quarterly Highlights

Networking products, including switches and routers for data centers and enterprise campuses, remained the standout growth driver, as product revenue rose 25% year over year. Demand came especially from hyperscale customers upgrading AI-related infrastructure, resulting in total product orders up 35%.

While security revenue stayed flat at $2.0 billion for the quarter, management pointed to strong customer additions from its refreshed security products. The security business, though not yet delivering growth in reported revenue, saw traction in its new cloud-based offerings and expects improvement once the Splunk cloud transition stabilizes.

Cash flow and margins both saw declines: gross margin dipped 2.6 percentage points year over year, while operating cash flow decreased by 7%. Higher memory costs and inventory build-up contributed to this trend, even as the company returned $2.9 billion to shareholders through dividends and share buybacks.
