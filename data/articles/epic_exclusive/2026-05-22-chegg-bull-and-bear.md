Chegg Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 11:06 AM

Comments

Chegg (NYSE:CHGG) is a global education technology company that has transitioned from traditional academic services such as textbook rentals and homework help toward a strategy focused on workforce upskilling and AI-powered learning solutions. This transformation centers on building an AI-first operating model, accelerating product iteration, and expanding enterprise partnerships to drive meaningful, profitable growth as its legacy business declines.

Potential Bull Cases
1. AI-Driven Product Innovation Enables Scalable, Personalized Learning and Sustained Differentiation

Chegg’s rapid adoption of an AI-first model is fueling faster curriculum updates, better personalization, and operating leverage. By leveraging proprietary data and a deep network of subject matter experts, Chegg is now producing some content on a two-week cycle for certain partners instead of the previous annual or quarterly cadence. This pace matches the demands of fast-evolving skills like AI literacy and positions Chegg to offer up-to-date, relevant learning at scale. The company’s AI-driven platform, including tools like Pulse for language learning, reduces the friction of content creation and ensures consistent quality—challenges that are otherwise hard to solve manually. These innovations not only improve learner outcomes but also drive margin expansion, supporting a defensible competitive position as AI reshapes the education and upskilling markets.

2. Successful Pivot to Skilling Segment Delivers Double-Digit Revenue Growth and Margin Expansion

The Skilling segment remains the main growth engine, delivering 9% year-over-year revenue growth in Q1 2026 and targeting double-digit increases for the full year. Chegg’s strategic shift from Academic Services, which is managed for cash flow amid secular decline, to a focus on professional and workforce development is already translating into financial results. The Skilling segment has seen accelerated investment, with 90% of capital expenditures now allocated here, and a forthcoming accredited AI master’s program through partners like Woolf. As Academic Services’ revenue wind-down is offset by Skilling’s ramp, the company has proven operational discipline with non-GAAP operating expenses down 55% year over year, and adjusted EBITDA margins reaching 24% in Q1 2026. This supports both profitability and reinvestment capacity to sustain further growth.

3. Expanded Enterprise Partnerships Unlock Broader Distribution and Reduce Channel Concentration Risk

Diversification beyond Chegg’s legacy Guild partnership has significantly reduced customer concentration risk and expanded growth potential. In the first half of 2026, the company renegotiated restrictive distribution terms with Guild and announced new partnerships with major enterprise clients such as Cornerstone and Woolf. Several additional new partners have also been signed, with launches lined up through 2026 and into 2027. These channels are already contributing and are expected to accelerate Skilling revenue as new programs roll out, broadening Chegg’s reach into technology, consulting, and manufacturing sectors worldwide. This shift limits downside from overreliance on single channels and enhances the company’s ability to benefit from macro trends in enterprise upskilling and continuous learning.

4. Improved Financial Health and Capital Allocation Enable Strategic Flexibility and Downside Protection

Chegg’s disciplined expense management and balance sheet strength provide a foundation for continued investment and risk mitigation. As of March 31, 2026, Chegg held $67.9 million in cash and investments and reported a net cash position of $34.1 million, with clear progress toward full repayment of convertible debt by September 2026. Free cash flow improved, even accounting for $12.9 million of severance in Q1 and an additional $2.1 million expected in Q2. With capital expenditures down 88% year over year, and most new investment directed to high-growth Skilling initiatives, the company maintains a share repurchase program but is prioritizing high-return organic growth. Such capital discipline ensures Chegg can weather temporary setbacks while investing in transformative opportunities.

5. Demonstrated Learner and Employer Outcomes Support Brand Credibility and Potential Pricing Power

Chegg’s outcome-driven approach underpins a compelling value proposition for both learners and enterprise clients. According to the 2026 Skills for Business Impact report, 75% of graduates from Skilling programs reported increased confidence in AI, 43% actively applied new skills on the job, and the majority cited measurable improvements in work efficiency and quality. Additionally, 92% of graduates remained with their employers 6 to 12 months postcompletion, strengthening retention for partner organizations. These employer-validated outcomes distinguish Chegg from generic education platforms and free AI tools, allowing the company to command premium pricing and deepen enterprise adoption—crucial levers as upskilling becomes a strategic priority across industries.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Accelerated Decline in Academic Services Could Outpace Skilling Growth and Squeeze Cash Generation

The risk that Skilling’s ramp may not fully offset shrinking Academic Services revenue puts near-term financial health at risk. The legacy Academic Services platform, encompassing textbook rentals, homework support, and Chegg Study, is facing ongoing traffic headwinds and remains in undeniable secular decline. While managed for cash flow, any sudden acceleration in this decline, whether from competitor advances or behavioral shifts, could constrain cash available for continued Skilling investment and AI initiatives. If Skilling segment growth stalls or is slower than forecast, the company could face a period where overall revenue declines compound, narrowing the runway to execute its transformation and potentially pressuring both operating margins and reinvestment capacity.

2. Execution Risks in Scaling Skilling and Delivering on New Partnerships Could Derail Growth Targets

Chegg’s future depends on rapid and successful scaling of multiple new products, partnerships, and enterprise channels—each carrying meaningful execution risk. The company’s renewed focus on partner-driven distribution, with several major deals still in early launch phases, introduces uncertainty around the timing and magnitude of incremental revenue. Any delays in rolling out new programs, slower-than-expected client adoption, or shortfalls in converting the signed pipeline could materially impact double-digit Skilling growth targets for 2026 and beyond. Additionally, the effectiveness of new AI-driven products in differentiating Chegg from entrenched competitors and free AI alternatives will be tested as the market matures. Missed milestones or underwhelming delivery would undermine both top-line acceleration and investor confidence in the pivot.

3. Increasing Competition From AI and Edtech Leaders Threatens Market Share and Margin Sustainability

Chegg faces sustained, intensifying competition from both traditional education providers and emerging AI-driven platforms, which could erode pricing and market position. Public perception, fueled by headlines claiming the company has been “This $14 Billion Business Is Officially the First Company to Be Completely Wiped Out By AI. Once the darling of Wall Street, Chegg is a cautionary tale of what happens when AI makes your entire business model obsolete overnight,” reflects the reality that generative artificial intelligence can replicate some core offerings inexpensively, if not for free. As large technology firms and fast-moving education start-ups introduce competing AI-powered tools, Chegg must continually demonstrate instructional quality, outcome superiority, and product relevance. Failure to maintain a clear edge could lead to commoditization of core services, downward pricing pressure, and customer defections, particularly if learners or enterprise partners find similar solutions elsewhere with lower switching costs. Over time, intensifying competition may compress margins and slow reinvestment in innovation, challenging the company’s ability to lead in a dynamic market.
