1. GitHub Stumbles in AI Coding Race

★ MSFT is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Microsoft's (NASDAQ: MSFT) GitHub unit had a head start in the booming AI coding market, but repeated outages and executive turnover have eaten away at its lead. Since March, GitHub has suffered more than a dozen incidents lasting over an hour, frustrating users and pushing some customers to consider alternatives.

Infrastructure strain: GitHub's drawn-out migration to Microsoft's Azure has limited its computing capacity as vibe coding demand surges. Some enterprise customers are now eyeing rivals like GitLab (NASDAQ: GTLB), Amazon's (NASDAQ: AMZN) tools, and Atlassian (NASDAQ: TEAM).
Copilot losing ground: GitHub's Copilot coding tool has slipped behind newer tools like Cursor and Anthropic's Claude Code. A March Jellyfish survey of 636 software professionals showed Copilot trailing Claude Code and Alphabet's (NASDAQ: GOOG) Gemini Code Assist in usage, and GitHub recently paused new individual Copilot signups amid capacity strain.
2. Tech CEOs Pin Layoffs on AI
★ META is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

A wave of tech layoff memos this year increasingly cite artificial intelligence as the driver of workforce cuts. Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) sent notices to roughly 8,000 employees this week, with CEO Mark Zuckerberg calling AI "the most consequential technology of our lifetimes."

Industrywide pattern: Cisco (NASDAQ: CSCO) is cutting about 4,000 roles, Cloudflare (NYSE: NET) announced 1,100 cuts, and Coinbase (NASDAQ: COIN) plans to eliminate 700 positions. Each CEO framed the cuts as repositioning for the AI era rather than cost reduction. Tech has accounted for more than 85,000 of this year's 300,000-plus announced job cuts, according to Challenger, Gray & Christmas.
"AI washing" concerns: Economists note actual AI-driven employment changes remain small, with a Census Bureau analysis finding more than 95% of businesses using AI reported no change in staff size. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman has flagged the trend of bosses blaming AI for cuts that may stem from pandemic-era overhiring or other pressures.
3. Mastercard Seeks Help on Brazil Losses
★ MA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

Mastercard (NYSE: MA) is asking Brazil's biggest payment processors to help cover losses from the failure of Banco Master and its fintech Will Financeira. The collapse left Mastercard on the hook for roughly 5 billion reais ($997 million) in cardholder payments to merchant acquirers.

Reimbursement push: Mastercard has already paid back processors for about 50% of that amount. The company now wants to use any money it recovers from card customers to reimburse itself first before passing additional funds to acquirers.
Pushback from processors: A draft contract went this week to acquirers including Redecard, Cielo, StoneCo (NASDAQ: STNE), and PagSeguro (NYSE: PAGS). Cielo said merchant acquirers are not responsible for these payments since they cannot choose which card issuers participate in the payment network.
