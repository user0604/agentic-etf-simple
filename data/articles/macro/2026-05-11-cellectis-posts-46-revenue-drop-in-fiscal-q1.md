Key Takeaways
Cellectis posted sharply lower revenue as collaboration income from AstraZeneca declined.
Revenue fell 45.8% year over year.
Management expects current funding to support operations into late 2027 but offered no new near-term guidance.

Cellectis (NASDAQ:CLLS) saw revenue decline steeply as its main partnership income slowed, while heavier spending continued. The biotechnology company specializes in allogeneic CAR T-cell therapies, using gene editing to target cancer and genetic diseases. In its earnings reported on May 11, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, revenue and cash balances both fell from the previous year, offset by a slight improvement in net loss due to one-off financial gains. Overall, the period reflected operational progress but amplifying financial and legal challenges.

Notable developments
Revenue dropped to $5.8 million, mainly due to lower collaboration income as AstraZeneca project milestones were recognized.
The operating loss widened to $25.2 million as research and development (R&D) expenses increased by 24% and personnel expenses by 35%, respectively.
Cellectis reported new legal disputes, including a key license arbitration initiated by a Thermo Fisher subsidiary, adding potential risk to its operations.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management stated that the company’s liquidity, including cash and term deposits, should support operations into the fourth quarter of 2027. However, no new guidance was issued for future financial results or clinical milestones. Investors should watch for updates on proprietary clinical programs and the outcomes of current legal proceedings.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue nearly halved compared to the same quarter last year, driven by a drop in AstraZeneca collaboration income as those project milestones reached later stages. Cash and financial asset balances also declined, with $34.8 million in cash and $150.6 million in term deposits available at period end.

Operating expenses rose as R&D spending increased to support ongoing enrollment in the BALLI-01 and NATHALI-01 trials, alongside higher personnel costs from stock-based compensation. Net loss stayed largely unchanged, owing to a $7.4 million gain from non-cash financial instrument revaluations that is not expected to recur.

Legal developments emerged late in the period as Life Technologies (a Thermo Fisher subsidiary) moved to terminate key licensing agreements and pursue damages, while patent litigation with Factor Bioscience continues, raising new uncertainty over important intellectual property rights.

What Cellectis does

Cellectis develops gene-edited, allogeneic CAR T-cell therapies designed to treat cancer and genetic illnesses using off-the-shelf immune cells.
