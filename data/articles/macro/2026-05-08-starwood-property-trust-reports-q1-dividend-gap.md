Starwood Property Trust (NYSE:STWD) reported another quarter in which its income did not fully cover its longstanding dividend. The company is a large real estate investment trust with a diversified portfolio spanning commercial and residential lending, infrastructure finance, property ownership, and loan servicing. On May 8, 2026, it announced results for the first quarter, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter showed active investment and capital markets activity, but the main issue of underearning the dividend remains unresolved.

Notable developments

Distributable earnings per share did not match the quarterly dividend, continuing a pattern from late 2025. The company invested $2.5 billion during the quarter and $1.5 billion after quarter-end, bringing year-to-date investment to $4.0 billion. Rental income from the property segment increased, reflecting the ongoing integration of the Fundamental Income acquisition.

Quarterly highlights

The period’s earnings figure again did not cover the dividend, as distributable earnings per share lagged the recurring payout. The company’s investment activity remained strong, with $4.0 billion deployed year to date across lending and property segments. The property segment’s rental income grew, supported by the Fundamental Income net lease platform, which added more recurring revenue streams.

Segment results showed commercial and residential lending as the largest contributor to distributable earnings, while the property and servicing businesses provided additional diversity. The company’s total assets rose to $62.1 billion as of March 31, reflecting continued portfolio growth.

Management did not report new short-term financial targets in the release. The main focus remains on scaling earnings so that the distributable earnings metric can fully cover the dividend in future quarters.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its focus on growing earnings to match the dividend but did not provide updated guidance. The most important factor for investors is whether future quarters will show improvement in dividend coverage, as this remains the company’s key financial challenge.
