Notable developments
Net loss narrowed sharply, falling to $(10.7) million from $(18.2) million, as research and development spending dropped by over 50%.
The company completed a $175 million equity offering in April, aimed at funding late-stage trials and extending its operational runway through 2028.
Sagimet reaffirmed plans to initiate a Phase 3 denifanstat acne trial in the second half of 2026, pending regulatory clearance, but shared no new clinical data this quarter.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management points to a pivotal move toward dermatology, specifically the acne market, with proceeds from the recent equity raise expected to fund operations through key 2028 milestones. The most important near-term event for investors is the start of the Phase 3 denifanstat acne trial, which depends on Investigational New Drug (IND) clearance from U.S. regulators. Investors should monitor progress toward this trial initiation and any updates on regulatory submissions.

Quarterly highlights

The quarter was marked by disciplined cost control, with research and development expense cut to $7.0 million as the company prioritized planning for its upcoming acne trials. This reduction contributed to a significantly improved net loss, even as general and administrative costs edged slightly higher.

With the successful secondary equity offering completed in April, Sagimet now has access to more than $100 million in cash, and pro-forma post-offering figures indicate a substantial financial buffer. Management says this will support development activities through the critical period leading to Phase 3 readouts in acne.

Sagimet’s R&D priorities have shifted almost entirely toward dermatology programs, especially denifanstat, while activity in its metabolic (MASH) program has paused pending new funding or partnerships. No further progress was reported on its strategic partnership with Ascletis (the Greater China licensee), and there were no new material updates on licensing or precision medicine initiatives.

What Sagimet Biosciences does

Sagimet Biosciences develops small-molecule drugs targeting fatty acid synthesis pathways for the treatment of acne, metabolic, and other diseases.
