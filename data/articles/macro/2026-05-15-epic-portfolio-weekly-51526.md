Dear Fools,

AI optimism paired with inflation fears is making for a very weird stock market. The S&P 500 has been booming since the start of April, but there's a lot going on under the surface. Even within the Epic Portfolio, which we designed to be well diversified and well balanced, there are some big winners and some big losers less than a month into our journey. We put 10% of our initial capital into cash, and I'd argue that's our most important position right now. With some stocks booming, some stocks sinking, and only some of those moves making any sense, we're happy to have some dry powder with which to take advantage of opportunities as they arise.

Portfolio Snapshot

AI and space are the two themes that have created our biggest winners so far. Sterling Infrastructure (NASDAQ: STRL) and Comfort Systems (NYSE: FIX), both deep in the trenches of the AI data center bonanza, have racked up solid gains for the portfolio thus far. Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB), which I'll discuss in the next section, has also been soaring.

There's quite a bit of red on the scorecard as well. TransMedics Group (NASDAQ: TMDX) and Shopify (NASDAQ: SHOP) are at the bottom of the list, with the latter beaten down by AI uncertainty. What becomes of Shopify's e-commerce empire when AI agents are doing the buying? The market is taking a pessimistic view.

The Epic Portfolio is trailing the S&P 500, but we're still in the very early innings. Over the long run, we believe our Epic principles will produce a resilient portfolio that will stand the test of time.

Stocks in the News

Rocket Lab stock is...well...it's rocketing. The launch specialist delivered $200 million in revenue in the first quarter, and the backlog jumped to $2.2 billion. Along with reporting those results on May 7, Rocket Lab made a flurry of announcements.

The company signed its largest-ever launch deal with a confidential customer involving five Neutron launches and three Electron launches between 2026 and 2029.
It was also selected by the U.S. Space Force to demonstrate capabilities for the Space-Based Interceptor Program.
It was awarded a $30 million contract for HASTE hypersonic test launches by defense company Anduril Industries.
Lastly, Rocket Lab announced the acquisition of Motiv Space Systems, a space robotics company.

Rocket Lab stock rose 34% last Friday, turning the Team Hidden Gems pick into a big winner for the Epic Portfolio.

Moving in the opposite direction was Cloudflare (NYSE: NET). The edge computing giant beat expectations when it reported results on May 7, with revenue surging 34%, but gross margin dipped, and the outlook was a bit weak.

The bigger news was a wide-reaching restructuring plan. Cloudflare is laying off 1,100 employees, approximately 20% of its workforce, as it positions itself for the AI era. The company's own usage of AI has shot up 600% over the past three months, and it sees this slimming down as a way to move faster.

The market did not like any of this, sending Cloudflare stock down 24% last Friday. One thing is for certain: AI agents running loose on the Internet is a fundamental shift, and we think Cloudflare is in a great position to benefit.

Stock in Focus: Nintendo

When Team Rule Breakers put forward Nintendo (OTC: NTDOY) for the Epic Portfolio, I was thrilled. For four decades, the Japanese video game giant has been a major player in the industry. Nintendo's business model is like a mashup of Apple's (NASDAQ: AAPL) and Disney's (NYSE: DIS). Like Apple, Nintendo keeps tight control over its ecosystem, and its first-party games almost never leave its own hardware. And like Disney, Nintendo owns a trove of IP that is iconic, timeless, and irreplaceable. Everyone knows who Mario is.

Absent the AI boom, Nintendo would likely have been sitting pretty with its Switch 2 game console. In the fiscal year ending in March, Nintendo sold close to 20 million Switch 2 units, along with more than 48 million software titles for the new console. The company also sold nearly 4 million original Switch consoles, along with 137 million software titles for the aging system. Overall revenue nearly doubled, and net profit shot up by more than 50%.

The current fiscal year is going to look quite a bit different. Component prices, particularly memory chips, have exploded thanks to unquenchable demand from the AI industry. Facing soaring input costs, Nintendo announced sweeping price increases last week. Japan will see higher prices in May, with the U.S. and Europe impacted in September. In the U.S., the price of a Switch 2 console will rise from $449.99 to $499.99.

Combined with weak consumer sentiment, these price increases will put a chill on sales. Nintendo expects Switch 2 hardware unit sales to plunge by nearly 17% this fiscal year, an unusually large second-year drop for a new game console. Nintendo still expects Switch 2 software units to grow, but original Switch software units are set to tumble. Taken altogether, Nintendo foresees revenue dropping by more than 11% this year.

Nintendo has been one of the worst performers so far in the Epic Portfolio, and it's facing down challenges that are largely outside its control and unlikely to end anytime soon. They will eventually end, though, and when that happens, Nintendo will still be a great company with incredible IP. In the long run, we think Nintendo will be just fine.

What We're Watching Next Week

Two companies in the Epic Portfolio are set to report earnings on Thursday, May 21. First up is Deere & Company (NYSE: DE), a pick from Team Hidden Gems, before the market opens. Rule Breakers pick Take-Two Interactive (NASDAQ: TTWO), the other video game stock in the portfolio besides Nintendo, will unveil its results after the market closes. Neither stock has moved much since our initial recommendations.
