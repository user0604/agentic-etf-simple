Under Armour Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 7:09 AM

Comments

Under Armour, Inc. (NYSE:UA) is a global athletic apparel brand focused on performance-driven sportswear, footwear, and accessories. The company is undergoing a multi-year transformation to shift away from volume-driven growth, streamline its product portfolio, and prioritize higher-margin, premium offerings. By investing in operational discipline, brand elevation, and innovation, Under Armour aims to restore profitability and achieve more predictable, sustainable growth across its international and direct-to-consumer businesses.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Strategic Product Simplification Is Poised to Unlock Sustainable Margin Expansion

Sharply reducing stock keeping units (SKUs) provides a catalyst for healthier margins and operational discipline – Over the past two years, Under Armour has cut its SKU count by 25%, with further reductions expected, as revealed in the May 2026 earnings call. This meaningful SKU rationalization, led by a newly appointed Chief Merchandising Officer, is intended to simplify workflows, focus consumer demand on higher-value products, and reduce supply chain complexity. By concentrating on fewer, more premium items, the company can command improved pricing, deepen partnerships with retailers, and enhance full-price sell-through. These moves are forecasted to drive gross margin expansion of 220 to 270 basis points in fiscal 2027, bolstered by both one-off tariff refunds and structural improvements—setting the stage for a leaner, more resilient business model in a competitive market.

2. Disciplined Operational Execution and Inventory Control Support Free Cash Flow and Working Capital Gains

Rigor in inventory management and cost discipline enables improved cash generation – Under Armour ended fiscal 2026 with inventory down 3% year over year at $915 million, reflecting both targeted clearance and higher-quality merchandise more closely matched to demand. Tighter inventory allows for lower markdowns and better use of working capital, which underpinned management’s outlook for positive free cash flow in fiscal 2027. Ongoing restructuring, completed by year-end 2026, is expected to deliver further efficiency. These operational improvements position the company to maintain financial flexibility, support marketing investments, and weather macroeconomic volatility while pursuing long-term growth initiatives.

3. European and Asia-Pacific Strength Offer Key Diversification as North America Stabilizes

International momentum provides ballast and growth opportunities beyond the challenged North American market – While North America continues to contract, the company’s Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) segment grew 9% in fiscal 2026 and is described as a “stable anchor.” Asia-Pacific revenue rebounded 13% in Q4 2026, particularly as China’s reset gained traction. These regional gains help counterbalance ongoing domestic weakness and provide a platform for further international expansion. As the reset in North America moderates—with revenue declines improving from -12% two years ago to expected low single-digit drops in fiscal 2027—international markets will serve as critical pillars for both growth and risk mitigation through geographic diversification.

4. Focused Innovation Pipeline and Targeted Marketing Underpin Brand Premiumization

New hero products and more measurable, story-driven marketing campaigns could support higher price points and consumer engagement – Under Armour is investing in innovation around core categories including running (Velocity Lead 3, Velocity Pro), base-layer apparel (like the BANT t-shirt), and women’s performance gear. These launches are paired with a $30 million incremental marketing investment in fiscal 2027, primarily aimed at amplifying successful product launches and maximizing return on marketing spend. By closely linking innovation and storytelling—drawing on recent successes like collegiate partnerships and Boston Marathon wins—the company is seeking to foster premium brand perceptions and move away from promotional dependency. This strategy, if successful, could drive higher average selling prices and better brand loyalty in key markets.

5. Wholesale Channel Engagement and DTC Growth Set the Stage for Revenue Quality Improvements

Strengthened relationships with wholesale partners alongside measured direct-to-consumer (DTC) gains offer revenue mix upside – Amid industry-wide channel pressures, Under Armour’s wholesale segment (approximately 60% of sales) exhibited improving order books and growing partner confidence during fiscal 2026. At the same time, owned store sales in the DTC channel grew 8% in Q4, showcasing potential for expansion in consumer-direct engagement. Management’s shift to deeper, more strategic collaborations with key retailers correlates with enhanced sell-through rates and supply discipline. As e-commerce initiatives and DTC traffic gather momentum, the company stands to benefit from a higher-quality mix and a more balanced, resilient revenue base over the coming years.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged North America Weakness Threatens Speed and Scale of Turnaround

Continued revenue and profit contraction in the core North American market poses systemic risks – Despite signs of stabilization, North America—a region comprising roughly two-thirds of total sales—remains stuck in decline, with revenue down 8% in fiscal 2026 following double-digit drops in prior years. Fiscal 2027 guidance calls for further low single-digit declines, and Q1 is expected to mark a trough.

2. Execution Risk Around Premiumization and Product Reset May Lead to Lost Share if Consumer Uptake Falters

Shifting to higher price points and a pared-down assortment could backfire if new products do not resonate or brand strength does not keep pace – The move to reduce SKUs and reposition as a more premium brand requires flawless execution, particularly as Under Armour launches new hero products and seeks to reduce promotional activity. If key introductions such as the Velocity running franchises or BANT shirts fail to gain traction, or if increased marketing investments do not yield measurable improvements in traffic and conversion, the company risks alienating legacy consumers and ceding shelf space to rivals. This misstep could impose lasting harm on both revenue and brand equity, especially as the marketplace grows more crowded and competitive innovation cycles accelerate.

3. Persistent Macro and Tariff Headwinds Erode Margin Gains and Operational Flexibility

External pressures from tariffs, geopolitical instability, and supply chain risk remain acute threats to profitability – Gross margin contracted by 220 basis points to 45.7% in fiscal 2026, driven largely by U.S. tariff impacts and heavy promotional activity. Although fiscal 2027 guidance benefits from a one-time tariff refund, the ongoing imposition of 10% tariffs and disruption from Middle East conflicts could continue to compress margins and constrain strategic investments. Further currency volatility and raw material inflation also threaten cost base stability. If macro pressures persist or escalate, the company’s ability to deliver consistent profitability improvements could be compromised, despite internal initiatives to boost efficiency and simplify operations.
