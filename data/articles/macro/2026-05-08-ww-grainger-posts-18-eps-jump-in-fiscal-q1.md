Key Takeaways
W.W. Grainger delivered strong growth with double-digit increases in both sales and profits.

W.W. Grainger (NYSE:GWW) posted a robust win in the latest quarter, with both of its main business models achieving standout growth. The leading industrial supply distributor serves more than 4.6 million customers, offering products and services essential for maintenance, repair, and operations (MRO). On May 7, 2026, it released results for its fiscal first quarter, highlighting momentum across the business after a slower prior year. Overall, the quarter marked a period of broad-based gains and raised expectations for the full year.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 10.1% year over year, with double-digit growth in both High-Touch Solutions North America and Endless Assortment segments.
Operating margin expanded by 1.1 percentage points, boosted by higher gross margin and efficiency gains from the exit of the U.K. market.
Full-year outlook for sales and profit was raised, reflecting improved demand and operational momentum. Quarterly dividend was increased 10%.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 expectations, now targeting $19.2–$19.6 billion in sales and $44.25–$46.25 per share in earnings, citing continued demand strength and strategic execution. It also forecasts operating cash flow between $2.2 billion and $2.4 billion. Investors should watch whether double-digit organic growth rates can be sustained in later quarters, as both margin expansion and tariff recovery remain key variables.

Quarterly Highlights

The High-Touch Solutions North America business, which focuses on knowledgeable sales support for customers with complex buying needs, delivered revenue growth above 10%. This reversed the slower growth seen in the segment last year, as volume recovered and price increases offset higher tariff costs.

The Endless Assortment segment, which includes digital-first platforms Zoro and MonotaRO, recorded year-over-year sales growth approaching 20%. Investments in technology, private label expansion, and delivery enhancements led to stronger repeat customer activity and improved margins within the segment.

Company-wide profitability improved, with gross margin rising 0.3 percentage points and operating expenses growing slower than revenue. A one-time benefit from the company’s exit from the U.K. market also contributed to the margin gains this quarter. The board raised the quarterly dividend by 10%.

What W.W. Grainger Does

W.W. Grainger is a distributor of industrial supplies, offering maintenance, repair, and operating products and related services to businesses and institutions primarily in North America and selected global markets.
