Key Takeaways
Ellington Credit reported a sharp loss in equity value as CLO equity investments declined.

Ellington Credit (NYSE:EARN) posted a significant loss in equity value as its large holdings in Collateralized Loan Obligation (CLO) equity tranches came under pressure. The specialty finance company, which focuses on CLO debt and equity, continued to generate recurring income but reported a material drop in net asset value for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 (earnings released May 20, 2026). Overall, the period showed further strain on portfolio value despite management actions to reposition risk and strengthen funding.

Business overview

Ellington Credit is a specialty finance company that invests primarily in CLO debt and equity, aiming to generate income and capital appreciation through expertise in structured credit markets. Its portfolio is concentrated in CLOs, with a focus on U.S. assets and a mix of debt and equity tranches. The company uses leverage and active trading to manage risk and pursue returns in the structured credit market.

Quarterly performance

The company’s net asset value per share dropped to $4.09 as of March 31, 2026, driven by mark-to-market losses on CLO equity holdings. Recurring cash distributions of $17.4 million were supported by ongoing portfolio income, but earnings fell short of the $0.24 per share distributed in the quarter. Management issued $54.0 million in new long-term unsecured notes late in the quarter, using proceeds to reduce reliance on short-term debt and provide capital for future investments.

Unrealized losses in U.S. CLO equity tranches accounted for the largest drag, posting a $(19.5) million segment loss, and the fair value of the total CLO portfolio declined to $307.9 million. Credit hedges covered more than 100% of net asset value at quarter end, but still did not fully protect against market-driven asset declines.

Recurring portfolio income remained consistent, with $17.4 million in cash distributions generated, helping fund monthly dividends of $0.08 per share. However, these distributions exceeded earnings on both a GAAP and adjusted basis, reflecting pressure on income from lower CLO yields and higher interest costs. The company enhanced its capital structure by issuing $54.0 million of long-term unsecured debt, reducing short-term borrowing and boosting liquidity to pursue new CLO investments in a volatile market environment.

Looking ahead

Management believes that the recent reset in the CLO market has opened up new investment opportunities and expects higher yields as a result. It signals optimism that new CLO investments, made with recently raised funds, will increase future net investment income. The key area to watch is whether ongoing market volatility and heavy exposure to CLO equity will continue to hit net asset value and income in upcoming quarters.
