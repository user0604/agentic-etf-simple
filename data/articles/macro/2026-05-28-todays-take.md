THE QUESTION

What was a moment you realized a stock you were skeptical of was actually a great business?

1. Shopify Proved Itself Amazon-Proof

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Several years back (maybe 10?), I remember deliberating Shopify (NASDAQ: SHOP) with several members of our team. At the time, it seemed like an interesting enough business, but given its size and the fact that it was pursuing the broad-based e-commerce market, there were concerns that Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) would just have its way — and that would be that.

Some time later, though, Amazon basically exited that line of business, and that was an attention-getter. All of a sudden, Shopify started growing like a weed (revenue went from $673 million in 2017 to $4.6 billion in 2021), and it became apparent that founder Tobi Lütke had something special. Thankfully, I had the wherewithal to buy shares in 2019, and I’ve been hanging on ever since.

2. Streaming Was More Lucrative Than I Expected

By Yasser El-Shimy
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I confess, I was initially skeptical about investing in Netflix (NASDAQ: NFLX). The streaming wars seemed destined to erode margins, with content costs spiraling whether producing originals or licensing. I questioned how they could sustain profitability amid such intense competition. However, Netflix systematically proved me wrong.

Its strategic investment in original content cultivated a loyal global subscriber base, demonstrating a powerful competitive advantage. Despite early negative free cash flow, it achieved positive free cash flow by 2020, showcasing financial discipline. With consistent subscriber growth and expanding operating margins, Netflix transformed from a risky bet into a compelling long-term investment, illustrating the power of vision and execution.

3. Buy Now, Pay Later Can Pay Off

By Matt Frankel, CFP®
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

I’ll admit I got the buy now, pay later (BNPL) business wrong, especially with Sezzle (NASDAQ: SEZL). A few years ago, when Affirm (NASDAQ: AFRM) went public, I thought its loans were extremely risky, and we’d see massive defaults as soon as we had any economic weakness. Well, we did in 2022-23, but that didn’t happen.

Without going too deep, the economics of BNPL loans (short-term, small balances, etc.) are very conducive to low defaults, and the numbers back that up. In Sezzle’s case, I initially thought the focus on membership products was a losing strategy, but management has been truly innovative. From offering credit-scoring options with BNPL loans to a $29-per-month mobile phone plan managed through Sezzle, members are clearly finding value.
