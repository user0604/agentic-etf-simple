Euroseas Posts 25% EPS Jump in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Euroseas delivered another highly profitable quarter with strong forward charter coverage and rising time charter rates.
Adjusted earnings per share climbed 25% year over year, while revenue held steady despite a smaller fleet.
Management raised the dividend and expanded the newbuild orderbook, citing high contract coverage for the next two years.

Container shipping company Euroseas (NASDAQ:ESEA) continued its streak of profitability, leveraging contracted charter coverage and higher shipment rates despite a slight decrease in revenue from a smaller vessel fleet. The operator specializes in feeder and intermediate containerships, serving regional and short-sea shipping markets. Reporting results for the first quarter (released on May 21, 2026), Euroseas highlighted its strategic focus on renewal and shareholder returns. Overall, the quarter showed strong operational efficiency and expanding orderbook, balanced by emerging cost pressures.

Notable Developments
Adjusted earnings per share increased to $4.70, up 25% year over year, reflecting improved operational profitability after normalizing for vessel sale gains last year.
Contracted revenue backlog grew to $650 million over the next five years, with forward charter coverage above 90% for the rest of 2026 and solid commitments for 2027 and 2028.
The quarterly dividend was raised 6.7% to $0.80 per share, and the company expanded its vessel orderbook to 10 newbuildings for delivery through 2029.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects steady profitability driven by high forward coverage and multi-year charter rates, but flagged rising operating costs from euro exposure and geopolitical uncertainty. Investors should closely watch vessel operating expenses and the successful delivery and employment of newbuilds, as well as any shifts in global container shipping rates.

Quarterly Highlights

The period saw stable revenue, with a smaller average fleet of 21 vessels compared to 23.68 last year. Despite the lower vessel count, time charter equivalent rates edged up to $30,354 per day--an increase of 10.1% from the prior year quarter.

Operational efficiency remained high, with 100% fleet utilization and daily vessel operating expenses up 4.7% to $6,989, mainly due to currency headwinds. No vessels were drydocked during the quarter, keeping maintenance costs exceptionally low.

Euroseas expanded its strategic focus by increasing the orderbook to 10 new vessels and reinforcing charter coverage, while also extending its share repurchase program. The company’s annualized dividend yield now stands near 5% on recent prices.

What Euroseas Does

Euroseas owns and operates a fleet of feeder and intermediate containerships, providing containerized shipping services and generating revenue from chartering its vessels on fixed contracts.
