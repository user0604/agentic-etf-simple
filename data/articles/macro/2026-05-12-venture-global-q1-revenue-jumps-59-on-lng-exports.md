Venture Global (NYSE:VG) achieved a major business win in the latest quarter, exporting a record number of liquefied natural gas (LNG) cargos and raising its full-year outlook. The company, a leading U.S. LNG producer and exporter, uses a modular "design-one, build-many" approach to construct facilities faster and at lower cost. It released first-quarter 2026 results on May 12, 2026, showing strong volume growth and higher profitability, while also noting margin pressures from increased costs and interest expenses. Overall, the quarter demonstrated robust expansion with upgraded earnings guidance, tempered by contracting margins as costs rose.

Notable developments
Revenue increased 59.1% to $4.60 billion, driven by LNG cargo exports more than doubling to 130 cargos compared to the prior year period.
The company raised its full-year 2026 EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) guidance from $5.2–$5.8 billion to $8.2–$8.5 billion, reflecting improved outlook for contracted cargos and liquefaction fees.
Venture Global contracted additional cargos, bringing the total sold for 2026 to 84% at a weighted average liquefaction fee of $4.51 per million British thermal units (MMBtu), providing greater revenue visibility.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management sharply increased its profit guidance for 2026, citing continued rapid growth in LNG sales volumes and improved pricing on new contracts. It reaffirmed construction milestones for key projects, with the Plaquemines Phase I facility on track to reach commercial operations by late 2026 and Phase II in mid-2027. Investors should watch how successfully the remaining uncontracted cargo volumes are sold in future quarters, as exposure to spot prices and market volatility remains a key area of risk.

Quarterly highlights

The period saw LNG cargo exports jump to 130 from 63, marking a new quarterly record as commissioning at the Plaquemines project accelerated. This rapid volume growth fueled the large revenue increase.

On the commercial side, the company contracted additional cargos, now having sold 84% of 2026 output at a weighted average liquefaction fee of $4.51 per MMBtu. These additional five-year agreements with buyers such as Vitol, TotalEnergies, and Trafigura signal greater medium-term revenue visibility. To support its expansion, Venture Global closed $8.6 billion in new project financing and completed significant infrastructure milestones at its CP2 project, reinforcing its vertically integrated supply chain strategy.

The company’s financial position grew, with total assets reaching $56.3 billion and equity up to $10.7 billion. However, management cautioned that the remaining 16% of uncontracted cargos for 2026 could be affected by global LNG price swings, and that rising interest expense (up 61% to $444 million) may continue to pressure future results.
