STERIS Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 29, 2026, 11:05 AM

Comments

STERIS plc (NYSE:STE) is a global leader in infection prevention, sterilization, and surgical support solutions for healthcare, pharmaceutical, and life sciences markets. The company’s growth strategy emphasizes recurring revenue from services and consumables, supplemented by targeted acquisitions, investment in automation and digital technology, and operational excellence. STERIS aims to combine market leadership with disciplined capital allocation to drive sustainable long-term expansion in both mature and evolving care settings.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership and Recurring Revenue Underpin Resilient Growth

STERIS’s competitive position and recurring revenues drive robust, high-quality growth even in volatile cycles – The company delivered 9% total as-reported revenue growth and 7% constant currency organic revenue growth in fiscal 2026, outpacing many medtech peers facing slower capital cycles and lingering procedure disruptions. Notably, recurring revenues from services and consumables continue to anchor stability, with the healthcare segment’s service and consumables up 12% and 7%, respectively, and Applied Sterilization Technologies (AST) services rising 11% year over year. These streams not only protect the top line during macro uncertainty but position STERIS as an essential provider to hospitals and pharma firms, sustaining pricing power and growing U.S. procedure volumes, which management expects to continue at mid-single digits in fiscal 2027.

2. Pricing Power and Cost Management Counter Inflation and Tariff Pressures

Steris’s ability to realize pricing offsets margin headwinds and preserves profit growth in difficult environments – Even with $46 million in incremental tariff costs in fiscal 2026, the company expanded its EBIT margin by 10 basis points to a record 23.3% and grew adjusted earnings per share by 10% to $10.17. Management achieved 230 basis points of pricing in the fourth quarter alone, leveraging strong differentiation and customer reliance on critical infection prevention offerings. These gains, combined with modest raw material and fuel exposure (oil-influenced inputs form only about 20% of cost of goods sold), enable STERIS to forecast additional margin expansion and maintain free cash flow guidance of $850 million for fiscal 2027, demonstrating robust cost discipline and flexibility even as sector inflation remains a concern.

3. Disciplined Capital Allocation Enhances Shareholder Value through Buybacks and Dividends

Strong free cash flow generation supports growing capital return and the ability to pursue strategic reinvestment – STERIS finished fiscal 2026 with $982.9 million in free cash flow and a modest 1.2x gross debt/EBITDA, well below its 2–2.5x target, providing headroom for capital deployment. The company increased its quarterly dividend for the 20th consecutive year and authorized a $1 billion share repurchase program, aiming for $200–300 million of annual buybacks. This commitment to dividends and buybacks not only reflects confidence in cash flows but also supports valuation amid broader medtech volatility. Coupled with targeted reinvestment, tuck-in M&A, and continued automation, STERIS’s capital allocation framework underscores long-term shareholder alignment.

4. Investments in Automation and Digital Technology Fuel Margin Expansion

Technology-driven productivity initiatives set the stage for sustained margin improvement and customer loyalty – The launch of a multiyear program to enhance service workflows with artificial intelligence (AI) and the development of a nearly fully automated sterility assurance facility represent meaningful steps toward transforming operating efficiency. By digitalizing both manufacturing and on-site service with tools like SPM (ERP for sterile processing), STERIS can capture higher productivity, quality, and compliance outcomes for clients, further differentiating its solutions. Management expects these initiatives to deliver not just operational cost savings but also improved customer retention and upselling opportunities, supporting EBIT margin expansion of 50 basis points in fiscal 2027 and ongoing competitive advantage.

5. Strategic Acquisitions and Capacity Investments Expand Global Reach and Portfolio Depth

Tuck-in deals and targeted capital spending boost segment growth and strengthen STERIS’s healthcare ecosystem – In late fiscal 2026 and early 2027, STERIS completed the vertical integration of a MEDglas supplier and acquired a family of gastrointestinal (GI) products, forecast to contribute $45 million in incremental revenue and approximately 100 basis points of additional healthcare segment growth in the coming year. These acquisitions enhance global reach, control over the value chain, and product breadth, while new investments—such as a $60 million center of manufacturing excellence—amplify capacity and automation in sterility assurance. Management's disciplined approach to M&A and strategic capital allocation ensures growth is both accretive and aligned with long-term portfolio strategy, reducing reliance on a single end market and supporting durable gains.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Exposure to Procedure Volume and Customer Inventory Cyclicality Poses Revenue Risks

Dependence on hospital procedure volumes and customer inventory cycles could derail segment growth when demand falters – Despite STERIS’s recurring revenue strengths, significant portions of its Applied Sterilization Technologies and Healthcare businesses remain tied to the pace of elective procedures and customer restocking patterns. Management acknowledged that medtech inventory drawdowns and weather disruptions led to temporary AST softness in late fiscal 2026, and recovery in international procedure volumes continues to lag. Should macroeconomic weakness, hospital labor disruptions, or further patient deferrals emerge, revenue could underperform guidance, with knock-on effects for operating leverage and segment profitability—especially if U.S. procedure growth slows from its mid-single digit trend.

2. Persistent Tariff and Tax Headwinds Could Stifle Margin Expansion and Cash Flow

Tariffs and rising tax rates threaten to erode profit margins and diminish free cash flow – STERIS absorbed $46 million in incremental tariff cost during fiscal 2026, with a $60–65 million annual run-rate expected to persist, acting as a structural margin headwind that is only partially offset by realized pricing. Additionally, withholding tax on cross-border dividends and buybacks is set to increase the company’s effective tax rate from 24.4% to 25% in fiscal 2027. These regulatory-driven costs constrain net income and restrain management’s ability to fully return gains to shareholders or reinvest at prior rates, posing an ongoing risk if either the trade policy or global tax environment worsens or remains stubbornly high.

3. Slower Post-M&A Growth May Limit Upside Amid Sectoral Rotation and Market Lows

Cooling acquisition activity and sector-wide rotations increase vulnerability to underperformance – With major integrations (such as Cantel Medical) now largely complete and no new transformational deals expected soon, STERIS’s incremental growth increasingly depends on organic execution and modest tuck-in M&A. This transition comes as the stock touched 12-month lows in early May 2026, potentially reflecting investor wariness toward medtech stocks amid macro uncertainty and sector rotation. If organic growth falls below the projected 7–8% range, or promised margin improvements lag, downside risk could be magnified by limited acquisition-driven catalysts and lingering valuation pressures relative to more defensive peers or higher-growth healthcare names.
