Aya Earns a Year's Profit in One Quarter

Aya's financial results reflect the extraordinary operating leverage embedded in a low-cost silver producer when spot prices are in a bull market.

Yasser El-Shimy, PhD, MBA

May 15, 2026, 11:27 AM

Comments

Aya Gold & Silver (NASDAQ: AYA) reported Q1 2026 results this morning, and the numbers are as good as the silver price implied they would be. Revenue reached $117 million -- up 247% year-over-year and 56% quarter-on-quarter. The two drivers are straightforward: more ounces sold (+34% YoY, +11% QoQ) and a dramatically higher average net realized silver price of $82.22/oz -- up 158% from the $31.87/oz achieved in Q1 2025, and up 41% from Q4 2025's already-elevated $58.39/oz. Net income came in at $49 million (basic EPS $0.34, fully diluted $0.33) -- more than the company's entire full-year 2025 net income of $46 million, earned in a single quarter.

But the most important number may be operating cash flow of $70 million, generated in just three months. Against a cash cost of only $18.40/oz at Zgounder, the margin picture is exceptional. The company closed Q1 with a cash position of $172 million -- up from $136 million at year-end 2025 -- after $4 million in capex and $14 million in exploration spending, and net of $98 million in debt. Aya's financial results in fact reflect the extraordinary operating leverage embedded in a low-cost silver producer when spot prices are in a bull market.

The key driver on both sides of the ledger is silver pricing. Silver has been on a historic run, breaching $80/oz in late 2025 and trading around that level through early 2026. With cash costs at Zgounder guided to approximately $21.50/oz, the implied cash margin per ounce in Q1 was extraordinary. At the same time, Zgounder's production has continued its ramp: the mill averaged approximately 3,800 tons per day in recent quarters -- ~40% above nameplate capacity -- with silver recoveries approaching 91–92%. Boumadine also contributed production from its pyrite stockpile reclaim operation in the quarter, though weather disruptions affected activity at the site. Guidance for 2026 total production of 6.2–6.8 Moz AgEq (Zgounder: 5.2–5.8 Moz Ag; Boumadine reclaim: ~1.0 Moz AgEq) was reaffirmed.

At current spot prices, the Boumadine post-tax NPV expands from $1.5 billion (base case) to $4.8 billion, the IRR rises to 108%, and the LOM post-tax free cash flow reaches $6.1 billion on initial capex of just $446 million. The payback period compresses to under a year. Management has been emphatic: no external financing is needed. Aya intends to self-fund Boumadine development entirely from Zgounder's operating cash flows. At $70M in a single quarter, that self-funding thesis is already playing out.

There is a reasonable argument that the market has not yet fully digested what Aya has become. The company's investor presentation shows it trading at 0.73x P/NAV -- the second-lowest among major silver producers, against a peer average of 1.40x, despite being the only TSX-listed pure-play silver producer with 100% silver revenue exposure. Ten sell-side analysts cover the stock, and the consensus target sits at C$33.70 -- a 15% premium to the May 13 close of C$29.32.

At current prices, US institutional holders already account for 60% of the institutional register, a figure that should increase now that Nasdaq trading commenced on May 4. The benchmarking slide makes the Boumadine opportunity particularly clear: at year 1–5 average production of 401,000 oz AuEq, Boumadine would rank among the top development-stage gold-equivalent projects globally -- yet Aya's pro forma market cap with Boumadine included sits at roughly $2.85 billion, a fraction of comparably productive peers.

At a market cap of ~US $3 billion and an enterprise value close to that (the company carries minimal net debt), investors are getting a world-class, low-cost Moroccan silver producer with a tier-one development asset in Boumadine and a management team with an exceptional track record of execution.
