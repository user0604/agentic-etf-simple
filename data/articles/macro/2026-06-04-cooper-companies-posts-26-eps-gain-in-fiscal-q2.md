Cooper Companies (NASDAQ:COO) reported solid underlying business performance, but a major product recall litigation settlement dominated its recent quarter. The global medical device company specializes in contact lenses and women’s health products through its CooperVision and CooperSurgical divisions. The results, released June 4, 2026, cover the second quarter of fiscal 2026 and highlight continued innovation and market expansion, offset by a one-time legal expense.

Overall, non-GAAP profitability and revenue growth were strong, yet the quarter’s headline was a large legal charge that led to a loss under Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP).

Notable Developments
Underlying revenue rose 8%, led by both CooperVision and CooperSurgical, with organic growth at 5% over last year.
A one-time litigation charge of $271.6 million linked to CooperSurgical’s 2023 product recall moved statutory earnings into a loss position for the period.
Asia Pacific sales fell 6% organically, highlighting ongoing weakness in that region compared to growth in the Americas and Europe/Middle East/Africa (EMEA).
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained its full-year revenue and earnings growth targets, projecting organic revenue gains of 3.5% to 4.5% and non-GAAP earnings per share guidance between $4.58 and $4.66. The most important item for investors to monitor next is whether the Asia Pacific region can stabilize and begin growing in coming quarters, as management expects.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue from CooperVision (contact lenses) and CooperSurgical (women’s health and fertility) both grew, underpinned by strong demand for premium and innovative offerings. New product launches and enhanced commercial capabilities supported gains, particularly for toric and multifocal contact lenses and fertility products.

The company recorded a large legal settlement in connection with a 2023 CooperSurgical product recall, totaling $271.6 million in pre-tax charges. This item was the main driver of the GAAP net loss but does not affect the company’s underlying cash flow or operating momentum.

Regional performance continued to diverge: the Americas and EMEA posted robust growth, while sales in Asia Pacific declined due to softness in legacy lens products, especially in Japan. Management said further rollout of new lenses and private-label offerings could aid regional recovery later in the year.
