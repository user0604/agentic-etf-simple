Kyndryl Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 20, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments (1)

Kyndryl Holdings, Inc. (NYSE:KD) is a global IT infrastructure services provider, spun off from IBM in 2021, focused on managing and modernizing mission-critical systems for large enterprises, governments, and highly regulated industries. The company is navigating a strategic transformation from legacy IBM-related contracts to higher-margin, consultative, and cloud-focused engagements, leveraging deep partnerships with leading cloud vendors and investing in AI-powered solutions to drive growth and operational resilience through 2028 and beyond.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Shift to High-Margin Cloud and Consulting Signings Sets Up Sustained Profit Growth

Kyndryl’s decisive pivot away from legacy IBM revenue toward higher-value engagements is rapidly transforming its earnings profile. By the end of fiscal 2026, nearly 80% of projected fiscal 2027 revenue is set to come from post-spin, higher-margin contracts, compared to a much smaller proportion just three years prior. The company’s cloud alliance revenue nearly doubled year over year to $2 billion in fiscal 2026, and Kyndryl Consult delivered double-digit revenue growth for the third consecutive year. These developments are critical because legacy IBM-related contracts, historically low margin, are being replaced by more profitable, multi-year deals, setting the stage for pretax margins to reach high single digits by 2028. As these signings roll onto the income statement, Kyndryl expects to achieve over $1.2 billion in adjusted pretax income and more than $1 billion in annual free cash flow by 2028, indicating significant value creation ahead.

2. Robust Backlog and Large Deal Momentum Enhance Revenue Visibility and Client Stickiness

The company’s pipeline of large, multi-year contracts underpins long-term revenue stability and deepens client relationships. In fiscal 2026, Kyndryl signed $13.5 billion in new contracts, including 38 deals over $50 million, and over 30% of those represented new scope or new logo clients. Over the last three years, more than 125 large deals were signed, supporting its strategy to become a trusted modernization partner. Kyndryl Consult’s signings consistently outpace segment revenue, suggesting ongoing momentum in high-value services. This large and improving backlog not only drives revenue visibility but also supports recurring income and reduces volatility, especially as more post-spin, higher-margin deals are converted into reported sales. Such multiyear relationships and expansion into new client domains make competitive displacement less likely while bolstering the case for ongoing top-line stability and future growth.

3. Cost Optimization and Restructuring Set the Stage for Margin Expansion and Strong Cash Generation

Workforce rebalancing and operational efficiencies are expected to deliver a step-change in profitability and free cash flow. In May 2026, management initiated a new restructuring plan entailing $200 million in pre-tax charges (primarily in fiscal 2027) to generate $400–500 million in annualized cost savings by 2028. This aggressive cost discipline, in conjunction with the strategic shift toward new, higher-margin contracts, is designed to push adjusted pretax margins steadily higher from the current 4% range toward 7-8% by 2028. Over the last two years, more than $800 million in free cash flow was generated off $1 billion in adjusted pretax income, reflecting strong earnings-to-cash conversion. Lower net leverage—down to 0.5x EBITDA at year-end fiscal 2026—places Kyndryl in a position to weather headwinds and consider further share buybacks, enhancing per-share value for long-term investors.

4. Strategic Alliances and Technology Leadership Enable Competitive Differentiation

Deep partnerships with hyperscale cloud providers and proprietary AI solutions fuel Kyndryl’s market positioning in digital transformation. The company’s expanding alliances with Google Cloud, Microsoft Azure, AWS, and others are not only driving headline client wins—hyperscaler alliance revenue surged 59% year over year in fiscal 2026—but also earning industry accolades, such as five Google Cloud Partner of the Year awards in 2026. These alliances position Kyndryl as a top enabler of hybrid and multi-cloud modernization for complex, regulated enterprises. The launch of Kyndryl Agentic AI and the Bridge platform provides customers tangible business outcomes—such as IT incident resolution up to 90% faster—reinforcing the company’s credibility as an innovation leader. This technological differentiation increases switching costs, supports higher margins, and expands opportunities for new business in a highly competitive sector.

5. Secular Demand for Digital Modernization and AI-Driven Resilience Drives Long-Term Growth

Widespread enterprise focus on digital transformation, cybersecurity, and AI adoption creates enduring growth tailwinds for Kyndryl. Across virtually all customer conversations, demand for agentic AI, data sovereignty solutions, and infrastructure modernization has intensified, particularly among large enterprises and governments. Recent wins, like the Arizona MVD contract renewal, showcase the company’s capacity to deliver on modernization projects critical for resilience and regulatory compliance. Industry recognition—such as leadership acknowledgments in mainframe modernization—validates Kyndryl’s expertise and positions it to capture a growing share of spend as organizations upgrade legacy systems and scale AI initiatives. These tailwinds, supported by a robust innovation pipeline and strengthened partner ecosystem, build a compelling case for Kyndryl’s continued relevance and the achievement of its multi-year financial targets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. IBM-Related Revenue Runoff and Direct Procurement Trends Threaten Top-Line Growth

Continued attrition of legacy IBM contracts poses a durable headwind to total reported revenue and backlog conversion. Kyndryl’s annualized revenue from IBM-related business dropped below $2 billion in fiscal 2026, compared to nearly $4 billion at spin-off. Increasingly, clients are procuring IBM hardware and software directly—rather than through Kyndryl—removing substantial revenue from the company's top line. While management asserts this change is profit-neutral, the persistent drag (3+ percentage points annual revenue impact since the spin) has led to flat overall revenue in fiscal 2026 and a flat-to-down outlook for fiscal 2027. If this shrinkage outpaces the ramp-up in new business, or if further purchasing shifts accelerate, Kyndryl could underperform even modest growth expectations through the multi-year transition.

2. Elongated Sales Cycles and Regulatory Complexity Delay Revenue Recognition and Growth

Protracted sales cycles, especially in Europe, impede deal conversion and cloud the timing of revenue inflection. The company’s fiscal 2026 results highlighted deal lengthening due to client uncertainty around AI strategy, as well as increasing data sovereignty and regulatory hurdles in critical regions such as the UK and Europe. These delays pushed anticipated Q4 closings into later periods, contributing to Kyndryl missing analyst earnings expectations by $0.25 per share in the final quarter of 2026. Persistent elongated cycles create risk that high-value backlog does not translate into near-term revenue, which could pressure book-to-bill health and postpone the realization of targeted revenue growth, especially if macro or regulatory disruptions intensify.

3. Execution Risks Around Cost Restructuring and Controls Could Undermine Margin Ambitions

Kyndryl’s sweeping workforce rebalancing and ongoing internal control remediation carry real execution risk that could derail margin improvement plans. Despite projecting $400–500 million in annual operating expense savings by 2028, the effectiveness of these actions depends on timely, globally coordinated implementation and realization of all intended efficiencies. Variabilities in local labor law, potential disruption among global delivery teams, and historical material weaknesses in internal controls (under remediation through 2027) introduce uncertainty about seamless execution. Should delays, cost overruns, or operational missteps materialize, the benefits to adjusted margins or free cash flow could fall short, challenging the plausibility of achieving Kyndryl’s ambitious profitability and cash targets within the promised timeframe.
