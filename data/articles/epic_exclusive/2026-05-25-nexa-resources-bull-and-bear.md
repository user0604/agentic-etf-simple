Nexa Resources Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 11:05 AM

Comments

Nexa Resources S.A. (NYSE:NEXA) is a leading South American polymetallic mining and smelting company, recognized especially for its zinc, copper, silver, and gold output. The company’s growth strategy centers on ramping up new production at its Aripuanã mine in Brazil, optimizing its integrated smelting operations, and extending mine life at key assets like Cerro Pasco in Peru. Nexa targets operational efficiency, disciplined capital allocation, and enhanced exposure to market prices for its diversified metals portfolio, while actively managing risks tied to the cyclical nature of commodity markets.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Strategic Aripuanã Ramp-Up Unlocks Volume and Margin Growth

Rapid operational gains at Aripuanã materially boost Group production and future earnings power – In the first quarter of 2026, Nexa’s Aripuanã mine set a new record with 13,000 tonnes of zinc output, representing a significant operational achievement since reaching commercial production. Management expects full production capacity in the second half of 2026 with the commissioning of a fourth tailings filter, which structurally de-risks volume in Brazil’s rainy season.

2. Exposure to Higher Market Prices for Silver and By-Products Fuels Cash Flow Upside

Cerro Lindo silver streaming step-down sharply amplifies Nexa’s sensitivity to strong silver prices – Starting in the second quarter of 2026, the portion of silver sales from Cerro Lindo subject to streaming agreements drops from 65% to just 25%, with 75% now sold at market prices. This transition is highly timely, as silver averaged a remarkable 164% higher in Q1 2026 versus the prior year, and recently eclipsed $120/oz before settling back. Robust industrial demand tied to solar, electric vehicles, and technology infrastructure continues to underpin a structural silver supply deficit. Nexa’s increased un-hedged silver exposure, coupled with strong sulfuric acid and copper by-product pricing, provides a meaningful recurring uplift to free cash flow and further strengthens returns during favorable commodity cycles.

3. Operational Improvements and Regional Integration Extend Mine Life and Quality

Asset integration and exploration expand resource base and cement Nexa’s leadership in South America – The company has successfully extended the life of mine at Cerro Pasco in Peru to more than 15 years following a recent integration and reserve update. This move not only provides visibility into longer run production but also lifts the average net smelter return (NSR) for the operation. Parallel to this, ongoing exploration at Aripuanã’s Massaranduba target has delivered new high-grade intercepts, while total drilling planned for 2026 was revised upwards by 12% compared to the original target. Such strategic investment positions Nexa as a premier operator in one of the continent’s richest polymetallic districts, supporting both organic growth and resilience against regional operational risks.

4. Prudent Balance Sheet Management Significantly Reduces Financial Risk

Deleveraging and ample liquidity underpin Nexa’s ability to weather commodity cycles – At the end of Q1 2026, the company’s net debt to last twelve months (LTM) adjusted EBITDA ratio had fallen to 1.59x, a notable improvement from 2.09x a year prior. Management has reaffirmed its commitment to remain below 1.7x leverage throughout 2026, and aims for a 1x target as price-driven cash generation accelerates deleveraging, particularly with the increased silver exposure. With $716 million in total liquidity and no material debt maturities until 2027, Nexa’s refinancing risk is minimal. This financial flexibility allows for disciplined capital expenditures, support for operational priorities, and potential for value-accretive mergers and acquisitions if suitable copper or polymetallic assets become available.

5. Robust Cost Control and Efficiency Initiatives Offset Inflationary Pressures

Productivity gains, by-product credits, and process improvements limit the impact of South American cost inflation – Although mining and smelting cost inflation remains a reality due to factors such as labor, energy, and local currency appreciation, Nexa’s cash cost net of by-products in mining came in at a notably low negative $0.76/lb for Q1 2026, well below year-ago levels and guidance. Smelter conversion costs were also on target, with active management of bottlenecks, recovery rates, and by-product monetization. Operating cash flow before working capital changes remained strong at $308 million. These efforts provide a buffer against input cost volatility and help preserve EBITDA margins even when core zinc prices are mixed, supporting longer-term earnings quality and capital return potential.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Heavy Reliance on Volatile Commodity Prices Creates Substantial Earnings Uncertainty

Nexa’s profits remain acutely sensitive to unpredictable swings in metals markets – The company’s earnings and cash flow are largely dictated by spot prices for zinc, silver, copper, and other metals, as evidenced by the 126% year-over-year jump in adjusted EBITDA for Q1 2026, mainly due to price strength. Should zinc or silver prices correct from recent highs, which can occur rapidly given global economic or policy shifts, Nexa’s revenues and margins would be directly and immediately affected. Historic cycles demonstrate that super-normal pricing episodes tend to mean revert, making sustained outperformance difficult to guarantee and leaving the company exposed should the market environment worsen in coming quarters.

2. Operational Interruptions and Cost Inflation in Peru and Brazil Threaten Execution

Incident-driven disruptions and local cost escalation can erode margins and delay production targets – While management resolved Q1 2026 operational disruptions in Peru, such as weather-related slowdowns, blockades, and equipment issues, the company’s operating environment remains inherently risky. For example, in May 2026, operations at the Cajamarquilla smelter in Peru experienced an interruption, with details on duration and financial impact pending. Additionally, ongoing inflation in energy, labor, and logistics in the region can raise operating costs. Frequent or prolonged incidents at key mines or smelters have the potential to undermine volume growth, elevate unit costs, and challenge delivery on annual guidance, ultimately weighing on profitability and investor confidence.

3. Prolonged Margin Squeeze in Smelting Segment Due to Low Treatment Charges

Persistent downward pressure on global zinc treatment charges squeezes smelting profitability – Despite strong operational recovery and higher output at smelting facilities in Q1 2026, segment adjusted EBITDA margin was just 8%, reflecting structurally weak global zinc treatment charges (TCs), especially in China and international spot markets. Low TCs compress the spread between concentrate purchase costs and realized metal prices, limiting earnings power from the smelting business. Although higher by-product credits (notably sulfuric acid and silver) offer a partial offset, slow improvement in TCs or further deterioration could result in chronically thin margins in this segment. This would constrain Nexa’s ability to realize the full benefits of increased sales volumes and may inhibit capital return or reinvestment capacity over time.
