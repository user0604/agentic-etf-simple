Key Takeaways
Sales and earnings grew, but organic demand softened and margins compressed.
Adjusted earnings per share rose 9%.
Management raised its full-year earnings guidance while warning of ongoing uncertainty.

Mettler-Toledo International (NYSE:MTD) delivered modest gains as revenue increased, but underlying demand was weak and profit margins slipped. The leading provider of precision instruments reported results for the first quarter, released May 7, 2026, that reflected slower organic growth and some pressure on cash flow. The period’s headline improvement was driven by acquisitions and favorable currency changes, yet cash generation and margins were lower than a year earlier. Overall, the quarter delivered steady progress but showed signs of strain in core markets.

Notable Developments
Adjusted earnings per share increased to $8.91, surpassing the company’s previous outlook for the quarter.
Revenue in local currency rose only 3%, with underlying organic sales growth just 1% as acquisitions and currency changes boosted the headline result.
Free cash flow dropped sharply, down 33% from a year earlier, reflecting higher working capital needs and timing of cash collections.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now expects local currency sales to increase approximately 4% for fiscal 2026, unchanged from prior projections. Adjusted earnings per share for the year are forecast between $46.30 and $46.95, up modestly from earlier guidance. Investors should monitor whether organic sales growth improves and if margin and cash flow pressures persist through the year.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s top-line gains came mainly from acquisitions and favorable currency effects, with reported revenue up 7%. But organic sales, which strip out acquisitions and currency, grew just 1%. Regional sales trends revealed that underlying demand in the Americas was flat and Europe only inched up.

Profitability was mixed. Adjusted operating profit improved 4.0%, and non-GAAP earnings climbed nearly 9%, but both gross margin and operating margin declined. The drop in gross margin to 58.7% was primarily due to higher input costs, mix changes in the types of products sold, and currency impacts.

Cash generation was notably weaker. Adjusted free cash flow fell by $60 million year over year, a decrease of 33%. The company pointed to higher working capital and normal seasonality, but this is a key area for continued scrutiny.

What Mettler-Toledo International Does

The company designs and manufactures precision instruments and related services used in laboratories, industrial production, and food retailing worldwide.
