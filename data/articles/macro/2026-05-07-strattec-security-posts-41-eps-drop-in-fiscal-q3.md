Key Takeaways
Strattec Security reported lower sales as customer volume softened and costs rose.
Earnings per share dropped 40.9% year over year.

Strattec Security (NASDAQ:STRT) saw sales slip and profits narrow as weaker volumes from major automotive customers and foreign exchange pressures weighed on results. The leading designer and maker of automotive lock and access systems released its third quarter fiscal 2026 results on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter reflected both progress in margin improvement and continued challenges from industry changes and cost pressures.

Notable Developments
Sales declined by $6.5 million as lower North American automotive production volumes and $3.4 million in customer electric vehicle (EV) program cancellations impacted results.
Gross margin improved to 16.5%, supported by $1.7 million in restructuring savings and cost recoveries from cancelled programs, even with a significant foreign currency headwind.
Cash from operations fell compared to last year but remained positive, aided by $5.0 million in tax recoveries and $1.5 million from pre-production cost recovery.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled it will continue pushing on cost savings and building relationships with carmakers for new platform wins expected in future model years. No formal guidance was given, but controlling selling and administrative costs and diversifying revenue sources remain key goals. Investors should watch whether Strattec can secure new customer wins and restore growth, especially as dependency on large OEMs creates risk.

Quarterly Highlights

Sales during the quarter dropped as production volumes at top automotive customers declined and electric vehicle (EV) program cancellations resulted in $3.4 million of lost sales, with only a partial offset from improved pricing and tariff recoveries. Despite this, Strattec’s gross margin edged up, benefiting from restructuring actions that provided $1.7 million in cost savings and recoveries from cancelled customer programs. Cash from operations was $11.4 million, less than the prior year, but remained solid thanks to collection of outstanding taxes and pre-production recoveries, supporting the firm’s $107 million cash position.

Higher costs for selling, administrative, and engineering (SAE) expenses reached 12.8% of sales, mainly due to $1.4 million in transformation and executive transition costs, as well as increased salaries and benefits. These expenses exceeded prior management targets for efficiency, adding pressure to operating leverage at a time of softer revenue. Foreign exchange was another headwind, contributing $2.5 million in additional costs, which weighed on profitability and underscored ongoing volatility in international operations.

While the period brought no new large customer or platform wins, Strattec emphasized ongoing investments in product innovation and efforts to expand its share in future vehicle lines, particularly for models launching after 2029. The loss of EV program sales highlights the need for further customer and product diversification as the industry evolves, but the company was able to partially defend its profitability through pricing, tariff recoveries, and cost control measures.
