Vestis (NYSE:VSTS) delivered a clear demonstration of its ongoing transformation work as profitability and cash generation improved sharply, even as sales edged lower. The leading North American provider of uniform and workplace supply rental services raised its full-year guidance for both profit and free cash flow on May 12, 2026, reporting results for its fiscal second quarter. The quarter highlighted margin gains and operational improvements, but top-line revenue slipped modestly, underscoring the company’s need to translate these financial wins into long-term growth.

Notable developments
Profitability improved, as adjusted EBITDA margin climbed 4.1 percentage points year over year to 11.3%, supported by operating efficiencies and better cost control.
Cash flow swung sharply positive, with adjusted free cash flow reaching $56.6 million compared to a $(6.9) million outflow in the prior year period, aided by cost savings and network optimization.
Revenue declined 0.9% from the year-ago quarter, reflecting ongoing volume pressure and challenging new business conditions even as pricing initiatives helped stabilize sales per pound.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 profit and free cash flow outlook, now targeting $295–325 million in adjusted EBITDA and $120–150 million in adjusted free cash flow. However, revenue expectations remain unchanged at flat to down 2% year over year. Investors should watch the company’s ability to maintain operational gains and move revenue back to sustained growth.

Quarterly highlights

Profit improvement came from a combination of lower costs per pound, better plant productivity, and ongoing transformation initiatives. The company realized $15 million in cost savings year to date and updated its full-year savings target to $50 million from $40 million.

Asset and network optimization continued, with two non-core property sales producing $6.5 million that was used to pay down debt. Net leverage improved to 4.47 times trailing twelve-month EBITDA, but the company’s absolute debt remains high at $1.25 billion.

Service quality metrics showed tangible progress: plant productivity increased 11% year over year, on-time deliveries rose 2.7 percentage points, and customer complaints fell 4%. Despite these gains, the volume of goods processed declined and revenue growth remained subdued.

What Vestis does

Vestis rents and services uniforms, mats, towels, and workplace supplies for customers across the United States and Canada, earning most of its revenue from recurring rental business.
