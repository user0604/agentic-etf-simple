Key Takeaways
Corbus Pharmaceuticals advanced its clinical pipeline, keeping major trial timelines on track.
Net loss widened year over year as research and development expenses increased.
Management reiterated that current cash reserves are expected to fund operations into 2028, with key clinical data due in the next quarter.

Corbus Pharmaceuticals (NASDAQ:CRBP) made tangible progress in developing its cancer and obesity drug candidates during the latest quarter. The biotechnology company focuses on advancing late-stage clinical assets, with special emphasis on oncology and obesity treatments. The results for the first quarter, released on May 12, 2026, showed sustained execution on research milestones but highlighted cost escalation associated with those efforts. Overall, the period underscored operational momentum as well as dependence on upcoming clinical trial readouts, with no major deviations from earlier management guidance.

Notable Developments
The company reached broad alignment with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) on the registration path for its cancer asset CRB-701, with plans to initiate a key registrational trial in head and neck and cervical cancers this summer.
Enrollment was completed for CANYON-1, a 16-week obesity trial for CRB-913, with topline results expected next quarter.
Operating expenses rose to $24.3 million, most of it directed toward research and development, as Corbus invests in advancing its clinical programs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated that its cash and investment balance of $138.2 million should fund operations into 2028, assuming no major changes to development plans. Guidance was unchanged from prior statements. Investors should watch for key clinical data releases for CRB-701 in oncology and CRB-913 in obesity, expected over the next two quarters, as these events are likely to shape future prospects.

Quarterly Highlights

Corbus hit major operational milestones, including reaching FDA alignment for the registration strategy of CRB-701 (a Nectin-4 antibody-drug conjugate for cancer) and completing enrollment in a Phase 1b clinical trial for CRB-913 (an oral CB1 receptor inverse agonist for obesity). Both programs remain on track for critical data presentations in the coming months.

Financially, the period saw a wider net loss of $23.0 million and higher research and development expenses of $19.8 million, reflecting the active phase of clinical trial spending. The loss per share improved year over year, but only because of additional shares issued for prior capital raises.

The company ended the quarter with $138.2 million in cash, cash equivalents, and investments, with no debt, supporting management’s expectation of runway through 2028 based on current spending plans.
