Earnings per share dropped to a loss of $(0.06) from $0.05 the previous year.

Fortitude Gold (OTC:FTCO) reported a difficult quarter as lower mining activity and new project start-up costs led to an operating loss. The precious metals producer operates the Isabella Pearl and newly launched County Line mines in Nevada. It saw less gold production and higher costs as it worked to restart and scale its asset base. The company released its first-quarter results on May 11, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter reflected ongoing operational and financial pressures as Fortitude attempted to grow its portfolio beyond a single asset.

Notable developments
Gold production and sales dropped sharply, with only 533 ounces sold from Isabella Pearl compared to 2,336 in the prior year’s quarter.
All-in sustaining cost per gold ounce at Isabella Pearl rose 61%, reaching $2,263 in the first quarter of 2026, up from $1,404 a year earlier.
The company raised $12 million in a private placement and ended the quarter with a cash balance of $10 million, supporting new development and exploration.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not issue formal production or cost guidance for fiscal 2026, instead highlighting that volatility is likely as new mines ramp up. The main focus is now on bringing County Line and other new projects to stable production. Investors should pay close attention to the pace of production growth and any improvement in cost efficiencies in upcoming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Production at the Isabella Pearl Mine fell significantly as the company transitioned mining activities and began processing lower-grade ore alongside initial output from County Line. Meanwhile, all-in sustaining costs, which estimate total per-ounce expenses including mine development and maintenance, surged as fixed costs were spread over fewer ounces.

Despite the operational setbacks, Fortitude Gold completed a $12 million private placement and launched a major joint venture for exploration at the East Camp Douglas property, which provides third-party funding for drilling and discovery work. These moves more than doubled its cash balance quarter over quarter.

The company’s dividend payments dropped to $0.8 million for the quarter, well below the $2.9 million paid in the prior year’s quarter, reflecting reduced earnings and a focus on conserving cash during this transition phase.
