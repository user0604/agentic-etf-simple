Bakkt (NYSE:BKKT) marked a foundational transition with significant cost controls, while revenue dropped steeply reflecting ongoing legacy business declines. The digital asset infrastructure platform reported results for its fiscal first quarter, released on May 11, 2026. Overall, the quarter set the stage for future growth, but topline performance weakened and losses grew.

Notable developments
Revenue fell sharply by 77.1%, primarily due to lower trading volumes and reduced contributions from legacy business lines.
Core operating expenses excluding direct crypto execution costs stayed nearly flat, helped by lower headcount, but higher professional service costs tied to acquisitions and expansion.
Bakkt closed the acquisition of Distributed Technologies Research after the quarter's end and announced a new payments corridor partnership targeting $1 billion in annualized volume, though neither yet contributed to revenues.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management described the period as the beginning of its "new chapter" focused on institutional-grade stablecoin payments and programmable money platforms. The company did not provide financial guidance for the upcoming quarters, instead highlighting foundational progress in regulatory licensing and technology. Investors should watch for revenue contributions from new partnerships and the effectiveness of commercial execution as Bakkt aims to shift from building infrastructure to generating business volume.

Quarterly highlights

The revenue figure declined sharply from the prior year as Bakkt continued to wind down legacy brokerage and trading service lines. Most revenue is now offset directly by crypto acquisition costs or third-party brokerage expenses, so gross profit remains limited until new product traction materializes.

Core operating costs (excluding direct costs for crypto and brokerage) held steady at $18.5 million, with lower compensation from leaner staffing partly offset by higher professional services. These professional service costs rose due to acquisition activity and international expansion efforts, including the Distributed Technologies Research deal, which brought new payment and compliance technology into the portfolio.

Bakkt announced a Memorandum of Understanding with Zoth for cross-border payment corridors across South Asia, the Middle East, and Sub-Saharan Africa, targeting $1 billion in annualized total payments volume. While the partnership expands Bakkt's regulatory and processing reach, the transaction had not yet shifted into definitive agreements or contributed reported revenue during the quarter.
