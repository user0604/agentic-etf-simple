Reservoir Media (NASDAQ:RSVR), a music rights management company focused on publishing and recorded music, is expected to report earnings for its fiscal fourth quarter on Thursday, May 28, 2026, before the market opens.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, Reservoir Media delivered revenue of $45.6 million, representing an 8% increase year over year, with organic growth at 5%. The company reported earnings per share (EPS) of $0.03, which beat analyst consensus of $0.02. Adjusted EBITDA rose 11% year over year to $19.2 million. Growth was driven by both the Music Publishing segment, which saw revenue climb 12%, and the Recorded Music segment, which increased by 8%. These gains were supported by recent catalog acquisitions and ongoing growth in digital streaming revenue. However, net income declined to $2.2 million from $5.3 million in the prior-year quarter, mainly because of higher interest expenses and a loss on the fair value of swaps.

Positives
Revenue and EPS beat estimates: Reservoir exceeded analyst expectations on both top and bottom lines, signaling operational momentum and effective execution.
Music Publishing segment strength: The 12% revenue growth in this segment reflects strong demand for the company’s catalog and successful acquisition integration.
Ongoing catalog acquisitions: Reservoir completed or announced several notable deals, including rights to works by Bertie Higgins, Gladys Knight, and TI, supporting future revenue streams.
Operating leverage improvement: Operating margins expanded as revenue growth outpaced increases in costs and expenses, indicating improved efficiency.
Negatives
Decline in net income: Net income fell 59% year over year, driven by higher interest expense, a loss on swaps, and lower other income, offsetting operating gains.
Rising debt levels: Net debt increased to $431.7 million from $366.7 million at the end of March 2025, primarily to fund acquisitions, which raises future interest obligations.
Synchronization revenue softness: In the Recorded Music segment, synchronization revenue decreased due to the timing of licenses, partially offsetting digital gains.
