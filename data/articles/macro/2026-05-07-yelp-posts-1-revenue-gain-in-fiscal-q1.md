Key Takeaways
Yelp delivered modest top-line growth, but profitability and cash flow declined compared to last year.
Revenue grew 1% year over year, while adjusted EBITDA margin decreased two percentage points.
Management reaffirmed the full-year outlook, focusing on expansion of AI-driven product lines.

Yelp (NYSE:YELP) advanced its new business initiatives while core advertising momentum remained weak. The well-known online local platform released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026, showing continued investment in artificial intelligence and a growing contribution from non-traditional revenue. Overall, the quarter revealed mixed performance, as AI-powered products and data licensing offset slower growth in the platform’s main advertising business and lower profits.

Notable Developments
Other revenue grew 75% year over year to a record $29 million, driven by scaling Yelp Host, Hatch, and data licensing. Management stated, “We are targeting an annual run rate of $250 million in other revenue by the end of 2028.”
Free cash flow dropped 48% compared to the prior year, partly due to the Hatch acquisition and higher overall spending.
Net income fell to $17.7 million, down 27% year over year, as investments in AI and product development increased expenses.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year guidance, projecting revenue between $1.455 billion and $1.475 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $310 million to $330 million, signaling steady expectations for 2026. The company continues to emphasize AI transformation and targets accelerated growth in new product lines. Investors should watch for updates on recovery in core advertising segments, the traction of AI-based features, and ongoing impacts of the Hatch acquisition on cash generation and debt levels.

Quarterly Highlights

The standout for the quarter was the acceleration in other revenue, which jumped to $29 million, reflecting the scaling up of business products such as the Hatch communication platform and data licensing deals. These newer business lines are increasingly central to the company’s strategy, even though they currently make up less than 10% of total revenue.

Profitability and liquidity both decreased. Adjusted EBITDA margin dropped to 22%, and free cash flow was halved compared to a year ago. This reflects the company’s investment in technology and acquisitions, including the cash outlay for Hatch and new AI-powered tools across the business.

Management continues to invest in over 35 new features, including a Yelp Assistant powered by AI, aimed at boosting engagement and opening new monetization opportunities. While these innovations show progress, the core advertising platform’s lack of growth and lower margins remain challenges that could affect longer-term financial flexibility.
