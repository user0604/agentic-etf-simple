Key Takeaways

Energy Services Of America (NASDAQ:ESOA) returned to profit after many years of losses, driven by higher demand for its core infrastructure services. The regional utility construction contractor completed projects in natural gas, petroleum, and water sectors for clients across multiple states. The company released its second quarter fiscal 2026 results on May 11, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31; these results show significant improvements compared to the prior year.

Overall, the period marked a clear turnaround in financial performance, with strong revenue growth, improved profits, and a record backlog position.

Notable Developments
Revenue grew 21.5% year over year, with management noting that Gas & Petroleum Distribution revenue more than doubled compared to last year.
Gross margin expanded to 11.0% from 0.1% as operational leverage improved with higher revenues and better sales mix.
Backlog hit $325.1 million, up 15.8% from the prior year and $23.4 million sequentially, supporting forward revenue visibility.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide formal financial guidance for the balance of fiscal 2026 but highlighted entering "seasonally stronger quarters" with a record backlog. Investors should watch for sustained growth in new contracts, margin preservation as costs rise, and further comments on future profitability or segment performance.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue grew broadly across segments, with Gas & Petroleum Distribution leading the way as this business more than doubled on a year-over-year basis. The company's other segments, including Gas & Water Distribution and Electrical, Mechanical, and General services, posted double-digit revenue growth, showing healthy business conditions across its portfolio.

Profitability rebounded sharply: the gross margin expanded by nearly 11 percentage points, and adjusted EBITDA turned positive at $4.7 million, reflecting strong operational leverage and improved project execution. Net income reached $216,000, marking the first profitable second quarter since 2009, reversing last year's multi-million dollar loss.

The company completed an equity raise, adding $21.2 million to its balance sheet and increasing the share count. Backlog increased both sequentially and year over year, giving management confidence heading into what it described as typically busier quarters for project work.
