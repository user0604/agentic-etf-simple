MicroVision Posts 50% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

May 13, 2026, 4:34 PM

Key Takeaways
MicroVision is making progress integrating recent acquisitions, but revenue growth has not yet accelerated to meet full-year targets.
Loss per share improved to ($0.08), while revenue increased 50% year over year.
Management reiterated efforts to streamline costs and emphasized the need for stronger revenue growth to achieve guidance.

MicroVision (NASDAQ:MVIS) continued to integrate its recent acquisitions, but revenue growth remains below the pace needed to reach its full-year goals. The advanced sensor company develops lidar systems for automotive, industrial, and defense applications. In its earnings release on May 13, 2026, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, MicroVision reported higher revenue and a narrower loss per share, but the topline result still lags the company’s annual target. Overall, the quarter showed operational progress but highlighted ongoing financial challenges.

Notable developments
Revenue rose to $0.9 million as sensor shipments increased, but remained far below the company’s full-year target of $10–15 million.
Total operating expenses jumped 69.5%, mainly driven by post-acquisition costs and higher research and development spending.
Cash on hand declined to $46.1 million as operating cash burn increased, highlighting the need for better revenue generation or additional financing.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management highlighted the completion of key acquisition integrations and announced moves to consolidate operations and reduce workforce costs. The company did not update previous revenue or margin guidance but restated efforts to streamline expenses. Investors should watch whether revenue from new lidar products accelerates in the next two quarters to support the previously stated full-year outlook.

Quarterly highlights

The increase in revenue came from higher volumes of short- and long-range lidar sensor shipments, especially into industrial and defense markets. Despite some repeat orders, the topline result suggests a slow start toward the annual sales target.

Expenses climbed sharply as MicroVision absorbed the costs of acquiring and integrating assets from Luminar Technologies and Scantinel Photonics. Research and development spending doubled, reaching $14.4 million, reflecting investment in expanding the lidar product portfolio—including 905nm, 940nm, and 1550nm time-of-flight and frequency-modulated continuous wave (FMCW) sensors. Operating expenses included restructuring and acquisition charges, with management noting a planned 15% global workforce reduction and operational consolidation.

Cash and cash equivalents dropped to $46.1 million at the period’s end, down from $74.8 million at December 31, 2025. The decrease was due to ongoing operating cash burn and the completion of acquisitions. With projected full-year spending expected to stay high, MicroVision may need to accelerate revenue or secure further liquidity to fund its business beyond several more quarters.

What MicroVision does

MicroVision develops and sells advanced lidar sensor systems for automotive safety, industrial automation, and security applications.
