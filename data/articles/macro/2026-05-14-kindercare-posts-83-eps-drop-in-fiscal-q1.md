Key Takeaways
KinderCare Learning Companies delivered mixed first-quarter results, with strengths in select business lines but ongoing challenges in core operations.
EPS fell sharply year over year as cost pressures and lower enrollment weighed on profitability.
Management raised its full-year adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS outlook, but core enrollment improvement remains a focus for the months ahead.

KinderCare Learning Companies (NYSE:KLC) posted a challenging start to fiscal 2026, showing notable gains in some segments but persistent weakness in its core business. The leading early childhood education provider saw improved results in its Champions before- and after-school programs and in employer partnerships, but continued to face lower enrollment in its main centers. The company released its first-quarter results on May 14, 2026, covering the period ended April 4, 2026. Overall, the quarter highlighted both pockets of momentum and ongoing operational difficulties with declining profitability and a significant impairment loss.

Notable Developments
Champions before- and after-school business grew revenue by 17.1% in the first quarter of 2026, driven by expansion and tuition increases.
Core early childhood education centers saw a 3.0% drop in enrollment, with revenue falling 0.8% despite a 2.2% tuition hike.
A non-cash impairment loss of $291.5 million, mostly from goodwill write-downs, pushed net income deep into negative territory.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year adjusted EBITDA and adjusted EPS guidance, now targeting $215–$235 million in adjusted EBITDA and $0.15–$0.25 for adjusted EPS. Core enrollment improvement remains a top priority, as management continues to invest in marketing and operational efficiency. Investors should watch for further progress on center occupancy, as ongoing declines are limiting overall profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Champions (the company’s before- and after-school care segment) stood out as the growth engine, adding new sites and lifting tuition, which drove segment revenue up 17.1%. The B2B employer partnership channel also performed well, providing some balance to softness in the main business.

In contrast, early childhood education centers—which remain the company’s core focus—faced enrollment declines, leading to a 0.8% revenue decrease in this segment even after raising tuition by 2.2%. This decline in occupancy continued a trend from prior quarters and put pressure on operating leverage as costs remained high.

The quarter was substantially affected by a $291.5 million non-cash impairment loss, including a $273.5 million goodwill write-down, resulting from a sustained drop in the company’s stock price and an increasing number of underperforming centers identified for closure or restructuring.

What KinderCare Learning Companies Does

KinderCare operates early childhood education centers and before- and after-school programs across the United States, serving families and working with employer partners to provide care and educational services for children.
