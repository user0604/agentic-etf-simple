Biogen Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 7:11 AM

Comments

Biogen Inc. (NASDAQ:BIIB) is a global biotechnology company leading therapies in neurology, rare diseases, and immunology. Once dominated by its multiple sclerosis (MS) portfolio, the company is now shifting its focus toward growth markets such as Alzheimer’s disease, rare genetic disorders, and nephrology. Through strategic launches, acquisitions, and expansion into emerging geographies—including China—Biogen’s management is executing a multi-year plan to reinvigorate growth, diversify its revenue base, and advance a differentiated late-stage pipeline.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Portfolio Transformation Accelerates Sustainable Growth Beyond Legacy MS

Growth products now outpace legacy franchises, signaling successful business pivot – For the first time, revenue from Biogen's key growth products—including LEQEMBI for Alzheimer’s disease and SKYCLARYS for Friedreich’s ataxia—surpassed those from the company’s longstanding MS franchise in Q1 2026, with growth products generating $851 million (up 12% year over year). This decisive shift demonstrates management’s effective resource reallocation and marks a pivotal transition toward a diversified and more sustainable revenue structure. As legacy MS revenues erode due to generic competition, these vital new products position Biogen to capture expanding therapeutic markets and mitigate the risk of overdependence on older franchises.

2. Alzheimer’s Franchise Establishes Market Leadership With Durable Patient Retention

LEQEMBI leads in key markets, offering high persistence and strong real-world adoption – LEQEMBI generated $168 million in revenue in the first quarter of 2026, accelerating 74% year over year while retaining the largest patient share in the U.S. Japan, and China. Notably, nearly 80% of patients remained on therapy at 18 months, and almost 70% at 2 years, underscoring persistent real-world demand and highlighting the product’s potential as a durable revenue engine. The anticipated regulatory approval of a subcutaneous formulation is poised to further improve uptake by increasing patient convenience, lowering logistical barriers, and enhancing the drug’s competitive profile, which could reinforce Biogen’s leadership in the growing Alzheimer’s market over the next several years.

3. Strategic Acquisitions Could Unlock New Revenue Streams and Pipeline Catalysts

High-profile deals are set to expand both near- and long-term growth potential – The pending $5.6 billion acquisition of Apellis is expected to close in Q2 2026, bringing complementary assets such as SYFOVRE (ophthalmology) and EMPAVELI (rare disease) into Biogen’s portfolio. Management projects the deal will be accretive to non-GAAP earnings per share by 2027, materially altering growth trajectories and enriching the pipeline with diverse late-stage assets. Recent moves—including the $145 million felzartamab rights purchase for China—also enable Biogen to build new growth franchises in nephrology and immunology, expanding the company’s global and therapeutic footprint. Rigorous capital allocation supported by healthy free cash flow (reported at $594 million in Q1 2026) ensures ongoing investment in future revenue engines.

4. International Expansion Drives New Growth, Especially in China

Geographic diversification taps high-growth markets and broadens revenue base – Biogen’s rapid geographic expansion, particularly in China, is a meaningful growth lever as it enters the world’s second-largest pharmaceutical market. Acquiring rights to felzartamab in Greater China aligns the company with unmet needs in nephrology, particularly immunoglobulin A nephropathy, which is prevalent in Asia. International launches also significantly boosted SKYCLARYS sales: for the first time, ex-U.S. revenue surpassed U.S. revenue in Q1 2026, with the product now available in 35 countries. This global strategy not only reduces U.S. reimbursement risk exposure but also opens access to large new patient populations, accelerating top-line growth in the coming years.

5. Deep and Disciplined Late-Stage Pipeline Offers Multiple Near-Term Catalysts

Robust late-stage pipeline provides steady flow of potential value-creating events – Biogen is progressing several late-stage assets, with pivotal data readouts expected within the next 18 months for candidates targeting lupus (LITIFILIMAB), antibody-mediated rejection (felzartamab), and rare epilepsy (ZOREVUNERSEN). The anti-tau Alzheimer’s agent BIIB080 is set for a key trial readout in mid-2026. Management’s disciplined approach—prioritizing programs based on rigorous go/no-go criteria—reduces attrition risk and ensures R&D resources are focused on the highest-potential assets. This sequence of upcoming milestones underpins a multi-year runway for clinical and regulatory catalysts that could drive renewed investor confidence and sustained share price momentum.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Legacy MS Decline Could Outpace New Product Ramp, Pressuring Results

Rapid erosion of mature franchises threatens to offset growth gains – Biogen continues to face pronounced headwinds as legacy MS products decline due to generic and biosimilar competition, with management acknowledging ongoing revenue erosion from these established brands. Despite strong launches, there is no guarantee that growth products can fully offset these losses in the near term, especially given the sometimes slow, lumpy ramp of specialty drug launches amid reimbursement and infrastructure challenges. If the pace of legacy decline exceeds adoption rates of new therapies, Biogen’s overall growth and operating leverage could be constrained, weighing on near-term financial performance and investor sentiment.

2. Regulatory Delays and Competitive Pressure Could Hinder Flagship Launches

Key approvals and market access are not assured, creating near-term risk – The U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) extended its review timeline for LEQEMBI’s subcutaneous injection formulation, illustrating how regulatory bottlenecks can delay broad patient access and revenue acceleration. Meanwhile, the competitive landscape is intensifying—particularly in Alzheimer’s disease, where rivals such as donanemab from Eli Lilly are expected to enter the market. Success depends not just on regulatory approvals, but also on swift payer reimbursement, widespread physician adoption, and overcoming safety concerns (such as amyloid-related imaging abnormalities). Any setbacks in these areas could constrain top-line growth forecasts and imperil expected benefits from market leadership claims.

3. Heavy Investment in Business Development and R&D Could Depress Earnings

Short-term profitability faces pressure from upfront deal and development costs – Biogen’s reduced 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance ($14.25–$15.25, down by $1.00 per share) directly reflects increased acquired in-process R&D charges and other deal-related costs. The integration of Apellis and global felzartamab rights, while potentially accretive long term, imply near-term dilution and operational risk, compounded by a $120–$130 million other income headwind in 2026. Sustained high investment in new specialty launches, pipeline programs, and international market entry creates the risk that cost inflation could outpace the realization of new revenues, especially if pivotal late-stage studies underdeliver or require further resource commitment before generating profitable returns.
