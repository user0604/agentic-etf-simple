Key Takeaways
The quarter marked a notable improvement for Polar Power, with significant progress on cost control and profitability.
EPS loss narrowed sharply and gross margin expanded compared to the prior year.
Management remains focused on operational efficiency and cost discipline while working to address ongoing risks and limited market diversification.

Polar Power (NASDAQ:POLA) showed a clear business turnaround as it sharply reduced its net losses and improved its operating margins. The manufacturer of direct current (DC) power systems primarily serves telecommunications infrastructure providers and backup energy markets. It released its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on June 2, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Despite progress, core revenue remained essentially flat, and the business is still concentrated in the telecom sector, underscoring persistent financial and operational risks.

Overall, the quarter reflected cost-cutting success and stronger profitability, but the improvement was helped by one-time items, not broad-based growth.

Notable Developments
Gross margin surged, but a non-recurring accounting item accounted for part of the gain; normalized margin improved to approximately 39.7%.
Operating expenses fell 22%, driven by successful renegotiation of headquarters rent and reduced general expenses.
Cash was bolstered by $2.4 million from new stock issued, offsetting operating cash outflows and lifting equity above the prior quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects ongoing progress through further cost-saving initiatives, including rent reductions and operational restructuring. It highlights the new engagement with Mammoth Crest Capital to address financial and strategic goals. The most crucial area for investors to monitor remains the company’s liquidity and execution on efforts to reduce reliance on the telecom sector.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s underlying profit results strengthened, largely due to margin improvements, with the gross margin expanding by 47.1 percentage points compared to the prior year; however, a $450,000 warranty reserve benefit lifted this result above what would be considered recurring performance. Excluding this one-time item, the margin was 39.7%, still a major improvement over last year’s period.

Operating expenses decreased substantially, as new lease terms nearly halved monthly rent, and cost discipline extended across general and sales functions. Despite these improvements, cash flow from operations stayed negative, requiring Polar Power to raise $2.4 million in equity during the quarter to maintain working capital and support day-to-day needs.

The business continues to depend heavily on telecommunications customers, who accounted for nearly three-fourths of new backlog orders. While backlog rose to $3.8 million by late May, there was no evidence of meaningful traction in military, electric vehicle charging, or other target markets outside telecom.
