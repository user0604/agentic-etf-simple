Earnings for Goldman Sachs Bdc (NYSE:GSBD) worsened as credit quality declined and core income streams weakened. The middle-market lender focuses on senior secured loans to U.S. businesses, using Goldman Sachs’ platform for investment management and sourcing. In its results for the first quarter (released May 7, 2026, covering the period ended March 31), the company reported lower income, an uptick in defaulted loans, and increased balance sheet leverage. Overall, the quarter highlighted clear pressures on both financial performance and portfolio risk controls.

Notable Developments
Non-accruals (loans not paying interest) rose to 3.2% of the portfolio at fair value, up from 1.9% last quarter, signaling higher credit risk.
Total investment income fell 18.7% year over year, due primarily to lower base interest rates and tightening credit spreads.
Despite shortfall in income, the company maintained its $0.32 per share quarterly dividend, which now exceeds core earnings by a wide margin.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management offered little specific guidance for the next quarter, with the only clear signal being the maintained dividend. The major focus for investors will be whether credit quality stabilizes or continues to deteriorate, as rising defaults and lower income could threaten future dividend payments and erode asset values further.

Quarterly Highlights

The sharp drop in net investment income per share reflects both weaker loan earnings and higher portfolio write-downs. Loans put on non-accrual status increased by more than a percentage point of the portfolio, nearly doubling since last quarter. This spike points to more underlying borrowers struggling to make payments.

The decline in net asset value per share was 3.7% sequentially, indicating asset write-downs and unrecovered losses are accumulating. Higher realized and unrealized losses ($38.4 million) directly affected this result, following similar but smaller write-downs in the previous period.

Despite weaker earnings, management kept its dividend unchanged at $0.32 per share. With net investment income only covering 69% of this payout, the company is relying on reserves or prior profits to support distributions. Persistent shortfall here could force cuts or weigh on future asset value.
