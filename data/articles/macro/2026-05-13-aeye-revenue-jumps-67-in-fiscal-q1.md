AEye Revenue Jumps 67% in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
AEye increased its roster of revenue-generating customers, but losses widened due to rising costs and negative gross margins.
Non-GAAP earnings per share improved to ($0.15) from ($0.31) in the prior-year quarter.
Management reaffirmed 2026 cash burn guidance and signaled a continued focus on converting customer engagements into lasting revenue.

Advanced lidar sensor company AEye (NASDAQ:LIDR) grew its commercial pipeline and expanded customer activity, but ongoing losses and stubbornly negative gross margins underscored ongoing challenges. Reporting first-quarter results for the period ended March 31 on May 13, 2026, AEye saw growth in revenue-generating customers alongside higher operating expenses. Overall, the quarter showed further commercial traction, though the company remains in a high-investment, loss-making phase.

Notable Developments
Revenue jumped approximately 60% year over year, reaching $101,000 as the number of customers with revenue rose from 16 to 21 since last quarter.
Operating expenses increased 32%, mainly from higher investment in research and development and sales activities, contributing to a net loss of $8.3 million.
Cash and marketable securities totaled $77.2 million at the period end, implying an operational runway well into 2028 based on projected spending.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 cash burn expectation of $30 million to $35 million, including $5 million allocated for working capital. It stressed ongoing progress in expanding the customer pipeline across key verticals but did not provide new revenue or margin guidance. The most important item for investors to monitor is the conversion of pipeline opportunities into meaningful, recurring commercial revenue.

Quarterly Highlights

Commercial activity accelerated, with 21 customers generating revenue in the quarter, a 31% sequential increase and broadening across all target sectors. AEye’s partnerships advanced, including expanded collaboration with NVIDIA on the Apollo lidar platform and deepened supply chain integration with Lite-On, enhancing credibility in automotive and defense markets. Despite these wins, persistent negative gross margins and rising operating costs highlight the need for greater revenue scale or improved unit economics.

The company’s pipeline showed substantial momentum, as active quotes and customer engagements each increased by nearly 40% quarter over quarter. However, the resulting revenue remained modest, indicating that most deals remain in early-stage or low-volume phases. Management positioned this pipeline growth as a sign of emerging product-market fit, but acknowledged that significant sales ramp is yet to materialize.

On the technology front, AEye continued to develop its portfolio, emphasizing the adaptability and precision of its Apollo and OPTIS lidar platforms. Engagements in the defense, trucking, and intelligent transportation system sectors generated one-time shipments, and international interest in APAC markets grew, but material revenue contributions from these initiatives are likely to take time.

What AEye Does

AEye designs and sells software-configurable lidar sensors for use in automotive, industrial, defense, and intelligent infrastructure applications.
