New Jersey Resources Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

New Jersey Resources Corporation (NYSE:NJR) is a diversified energy infrastructure company serving approximately 588,000 customers, primarily through its regulated New Jersey Natural Gas utility, as well as Storage & Transportation, Clean Energy Ventures, and Energy Services segments. The company’s growth strategy combines stable utility expansion, long-term contract-driven infrastructure investments, and aggressive solar project development, all supported by a disciplined capital allocation policy and a focus on sustainability and regulatory partnership.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Regulated Utility Expansion Drives Predictable, Sustainable Earnings Growth

The company’s core New Jersey Natural Gas utility delivers earnings stability and visible growth fueled by regulatory support and customer expansion. In fiscal 2026, this segment contributed about 60% of net financial earnings per share (NFEPS), underpinning consolidated results with steady, rate-base-driven growth. Capital spending on infrastructure—two-thirds of $400 million year-to-date capex—directly supports service reliability, customer additions (including Chester Township integration for further meter growth), and the SAVEGREEN energy efficiency program, which reached over 115,000 participants. With regulated cost recovery in place, these investments are expected to drive both rate base and approved returns higher through 2030, offering long-term dependable cash flow and supporting ongoing dividend increases.

2. Storage & Transportation Segment Poised for Step-Change Earnings Upside

Fee-based, long-term contracts and expansion projects position Storage & Transportation for outsized growth over the next two years. In fiscal 2026, operational results set records at key assets such as Adelphia Gateway and Leaf River, reflecting both peak winter utilization and successful re-contracting efforts with high-credit counterparties. The recently greenlit Leaf River expansion, now backed by long-term contracts and commencing capex, is expected to more than double the segment’s net financial earnings within two years. With Federal Energy Regulatory Commission approvals progressing and most revenues shielded from commodity volatility, this business is on track to become an even more material earnings driver, enhancing overall company earnings quality and margin stability.

3. Accelerating Clean Energy Ventures Buildout Capitalizes on Policy Tailwinds

Rapid growth in solar capacity leverages state and federal incentives, offering meaningful long-term upside as decarbonization policies strengthen. Clean Energy Ventures increased installed solar capacity by 25% year over year in fiscal 2025 and added another 33 megawatts early this fiscal year, surpassing the 500MW in-service milestone. The pipeline now exceeds 1.2 gigawatts—well beyond current capital deployment targets—and is situated in incentive-rich geographies like New Jersey and the PJM region. Supported by tax credit monetization and future-ready technologies (such as battery integration), CEV’s projects are structured to generate high single or low double-digit unlevered after-tax returns. This growth not only aligns with evolving environmental, social, and governance mandates but also locks in recurring earnings streams for the future.

4. Superior Capital Allocation Strengthens Balance Sheet and Protects Shareholders

Disciplined investment and prudent funding have maintained strong credit metrics while avoiding shareholder dilution. The company’s five-year capital expenditure plan of $4.8–$5.2 billion through 2030 prioritizes utility expansion while ensuring ample capital for solar and infrastructure growth. Year-to-date, roughly two-thirds of $400 million capex went to regulated opportunities, bolstering the rate base and future earnings. Robust cash flow—especially Energy Services’ exceptional recent performance—has enabled New Jersey Resources to keep funds-from-operations (FFO) to debt near the 20% target. Management has reiterated that no block equity issuance is envisioned, and the well-laddered debt profile further limits refinancing risk, protecting existing investors and supporting continued dividend growth aligned with long-term earnings increases.

5. Energy Services Performance Provides Incremental Upside and Flexibility

Recent outperformances from Energy Services have delivered significant earnings boosts and strengthened internal funding capabilities. In the second quarter of fiscal 2026, the segment’s ability to capture price differentials during a historically cold winter—while managing risk—allowed the company to raise full-year NFEPS guidance for the second time (now $3.48 to $3.62 per share, ahead of prior consensus). While management notes this outperformance is partly weather-driven, the resultant cash generation has reduced near-term debt and equity needs, providing funding flexibility for capex projects without reliance on external capital markets. This business complements regulated stability with opportunistic fundamentals, giving the company tools to navigate both growth and volatility.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Reliance on Non-Recurring Energy Services Gains Creates Earnings Volatility Risk

Exposure to market-driven swings in Energy Services threatens consistency of results and clouds earnings predictability. The second quarter fiscal 2026 beat—$2.20 per share non-GAAP earnings, well ahead of consensus—was heavily influenced by exceptionally favorable winter conditions and volatility in the natural gas market. Management has acknowledged these results may not be repeatable, raising concerns about over-reliance on episodic, non-recurring gains outside the company’s core utility business. Should market conditions revert or become less favorable, future quarters could disappoint investors expecting ongoing outperformance, potentially leading to negative surprises and a rerating of valuation to reflect lower-quality, less predictable earnings.

2. Regulatory and Policy Shifts Could Disrupt Growth Across Key Segments

Dependence on favorable regulatory outcomes and continued policy support exposes the company to downside if approvals or incentives change unexpectedly. The success of the regulated utility, Storage & Transportation expansion plans, and Clean Energy Ventures solar buildout are all contingent on policy environments that support cost recovery, infrastructure development, and renewable project subsidies. Delays or unfavorable decisions in state rate cases, Federal Energy Regulatory Commission approvals, or modifications to renewable energy incentives could impede capex deployment or reduce allowed returns. Any meaningful regulatory headwinds would slow anticipated earnings, force strategic pivots, and diminish the long-term growth narrative that underpins investor confidence.

3. Execution Risks in Major Capex Projects May Pressure Returns and Timelines

Large infrastructure investments face risks from construction delays, budget overruns, and supply chain challenges that threaten target returns. The company’s $4.8–$5.2 billion capital plan spans regulated utility upgrades, Storage & Transportation expansions, and a rapidly growing Clean Energy Ventures pipeline. This sizable commitment places pressure on management to deliver projects on time and on budget, particularly at Leaf River (storage) and as CEV pushes for a 50% increase in solar capacity through fiscal 2027. Any material setbacks—delays in permitting, rising materials or labor costs, or setbacks integrating new technologies—could inflate costs or defer revenue recognition. Such issues may erode segment returns, compel unplanned financing, and undermine the expected cash generation from these growth investments.
