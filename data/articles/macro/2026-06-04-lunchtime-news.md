1. Pinterest Inks $4 Billion AWS Deal

Pinterest (NYSE: PINS) signed a $4 billion deal with Amazon's (NASDAQ: AMZN) AWS for cloud services through 2031, marking the social media company's largest-ever agreement. Pinterest shares jumped nearly 5% while Amazon rose 1.5%.

AI is the headline: AWS will provide Pinterest with custom chip processors including Graviton and Trainium to power its AI initiatives. Pinterest plans to use AWS Trainium for the large language and vision-language models behind features like personalized visual search and AI-assisted discovery.
Competition pressure: Pinterest has been investing heavily in AI tools, including upgrades to its Performance+ ad suite, as it tries to fend off TikTok and Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META). The company forecast Q2 revenue above Wall Street estimates earlier this month and has worked with AWS since 2010.
2. SpaceX Rolls Out the Retail Welcome Mat

SpaceX launched its IPO website for retail investors at spacexipo.com on Thursday morning, posting the prospectus, an investor Q&A, and the roadshow presentation. The company also confirmed it will price the offering on June 11 with trading set to begin June 12, kicking off its institutional roadshow today.

First major IPO test: Wedbush analyst Dan Ives called the listing the "first major test for public markets after years of muted IPO activity," with Anthropic and OpenAI expected to follow. SpaceX's pricing would value the company at roughly $1.785 trillion, making it one of the largest debuts in history.
Where the proceeds go: SpaceX plans to use the cash to expand AI compute infrastructure, beef up launch capacity, and grow its satellite constellations. At least part will go toward repaying a $20 billion bridge loan within six months, with the recently announced Terafab joint venture with Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) tying SpaceX into chip production for full self-driving, Optimus robots, and orbital data centers.

Layoff announcements climbed to just over 97,000 in May, the highest May total since 2020, with AI cited as the leading driver for the third month in a row, according to Challenger, Gray & Christmas. Tech alone accounted for more than 38,000 cuts, its biggest monthly total since August 2024.

Amazon engineers push back: Three Amazon engineers criticized the company at a Seattle City Council hearing Wednesday for spending $200 billion on AI infrastructure this year while laying off 30,000 corporate employees since October. Officials unanimously approved a one-year moratorium on new large-scale AI data centers. Seattle joins a growing list of cities pushing back, with 14 states considering similar legislation and $156 billion in data center projects blocked or delayed in 2025.
Cooler than 2025 overall: While May was the worst month of 2026 so far, planned layoffs through May total roughly 400,000, far below the 700,000 announced in the first five months of last year. Tech is still cutting heavily but also led hiring plans in May, and official government data shows overall layoff rates and continuing jobless claims remain low.
