Key Takeaways
Manulife Financial delivered a business win with strong growth in Asia, helping to offset weakness elsewhere. Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) rose 11%. Management maintained its positive outlook, but flagged that asset management net outflows are a risk to watch.

Strong growth in Asia helped Manulife Financial (NYSE:MFC) post a resilient quarter, with new business in key Asian markets driving its performance. The global insurer and wealth manager reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 13, 2026, highlighting expansion in core earnings and new business despite net outflows in its Wealth and Asset Management segment. Overall, the quarter delivered solid progress for Manulife, although results were uneven across its global businesses.

Notable Developments
Core earnings per share rose 11% to $1.06, reflecting growth in Asia and continued progress in new business across all insurance segments.
Global Wealth and Asset Management saw net outflows of $4.4 billion, a significant reversal from net inflows of $0.5 billion last year, mostly due to retail mutual fund withdrawals in North America.
Management returned $1.2 billion to shareholders via dividends and share buybacks during the quarter; capitalization remained strong with a 136% LICAT ratio, a measure of insurance company solvency.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated confidence in its strategy and the company's position, especially highlighting growth potential in Asia and ongoing investments in technology. There was no update to financial guidance. Investors should closely monitor Global Wealth and Asset Management net flows, which moved from a small inflow last year to sizeable outflows this quarter.

Quarterly Highlights

Results in Asia stood out, with core earnings climbing 22% and new insurance business surging. This was a bright spot, as new business measures in Asia helped offset softness in other regions and segments.

The Wealth and Asset Management unit, which manages mutual funds and retirement accounts, reported higher assets under management but suffered net outflows driven by customer withdrawals in retail mutual funds. Despite new partnerships and acquisitions like Schroders Indonesia, these strategies have not yet translated to better net flow numbers.

In Canada, group insurance sales and profitability dropped due to higher disability claims and rising expenses. While individual insurance posted growth, overall Canadian results showed continued margin pressure in large group products.

What Manulife Financial Does

Manulife Financial is a global insurer and wealth manager that offers life insurance, retirement products, and investment management services across Asia, Canada, and the United States.
