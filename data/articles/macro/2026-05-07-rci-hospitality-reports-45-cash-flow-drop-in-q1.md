RCI Hospitality (NASDAQ:RICK)’s latest quarter highlighted both progress and setbacks as operational improvements in its core nightclubs were offset by margin pressure and steep declines in free cash flow. The leading operator of adult entertainment venues and themed restaurants saw stable revenue from Nightclubs, but the Bombshells segment continued to face significant challenges. The company released fiscal first-quarter results for the period ended December 31, 2025, on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed a stable nightclub business but underscored risks tied to falling cash generation and growing leverage.

Notable developments
Revenue in the Nightclubs segment was flat, with $62.3 million generated — a 0.9% increase due in part to five new or reopened clubs.
Bombshells segment sales dropped 12.6%, and non-GAAP operating losses returned as the restaurant-bar concept faced ongoing challenges.
Free cash flow fell 44.7% from the prior year, reflecting the impact of lower operating profits and higher one-time charges.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintained its capital allocation strategy, focusing on controlling costs, investing in turnaround initiatives for Bombshells, and continuing share repurchases. While it reiterated the long-term operational plans, the company did not update its prior performance targets. Watch for progress in restoring free cash flow growth and improvement in Bombshells segment performance as key issues for upcoming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Nightclubs revenue remained relatively steady, bolstered by $4.9 million from new or reopened clubs, offsetting softness in same-store sales and declines in alcoholic beverage revenue. Although overall Nightclubs operating margins narrowed to 31.3% (non-GAAP), the segment continues to generate most of the company’s profit.

The Bombshells restaurant-bar segment continued to weigh on results, posting a 12.6% revenue decrease compared to the prior year as several underperforming locations were closed or divested. Segment profitability declined, with non-GAAP operating margins dropping from a 6.4% profit a year ago to a 1.3% loss this quarter.

On the financial side, the company accelerated its share buyback program, reducing shares outstanding but increasing its total debt by 8.8%. Cash on hand declined to $28.6 million, with free cash flow falling by nearly 45%, pointing to concerns about liquidity if these trends persist.

What RCI Hospitality does

RCI Hospitality operates adult nightclubs and themed restaurant-bars across the United States, under brands such as Rick’s Cabaret and Bombshells.
