Key Takeaways
Gran Tierra Energy marked a business loss as headline earnings swung to a large net loss. Adjusted EBITDA declined from the prior year, highlighting margin pressures. Management raised its free cash flow outlook for fiscal 2026 despite ongoing hedging and country-risk headwinds.

Gran Tierra Energy (NYSEMKT:GTE) posted a challenging first quarter as operational progress was overshadowed by a steep net loss caused by non-cash hedging costs. The independent oil and gas producer focuses primarily on Colombia, with additional assets in Canada and Ecuador. Reporting results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (released May 7, 2026), the company saw improved gross profitability and lower spending but continued to wrestle with earnings volatility from derivative losses. Overall, the period reflected solid cost control and portfolio reshaping but left earnings under pressure.

Notable Developments
Production averaged 45,497 barrels per day, down 2% from the prior year, primarily due to the sale of Simonette assets in Canada and the timing of waterflood optimization responses in Colombia.
Capital expenditures fell sharply to $45 million from $95 million a year ago, reflecting disciplined cost control and asset portfolio changes.
The company completed the sale of its Simonette asset in Canada and announced entry into Azerbaijan, aiding in long-term diversification efforts.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management revised its 2026 guidance to project higher free cash flow, despite expecting substantial cash flow drag from hedging losses and continuing heavy exposure to Colombia. Investors should monitor the effect of quality and transportation price discounts in Colombia and Ecuador, as well as further progress on reducing net debt in the coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Production met management expectations, with quarterly average output in line with newly issued guidance. The sale of the Canadian Simonette asset reduced oil exposure there, while an acquisition added incremental volumes in Ecuador. The company also finalized a significant new partnership in Azerbaijan, giving it operatorship and acreage for long-term growth outside Latin America.

Financially, the quarter showed lower capital spending and a $42.8 million funds flow from operations but a slight negative free cash flow due in part to the timing of spending versus cash receipts. Net debt improved to $481 million, with the maturity of some debt extended to 2031 and opportunistic buybacks reducing outstanding obligations.

Profitability metrics such as gross profit and operating netback (a measure of cash profit per barrel before overhead and interest) improved compared to the prior quarter, but year over year, results were tempered by higher general and administrative costs and sharp increases in quality and transportation discounts, particularly in Colombian operations. These discounts—now approaching $15 per barrel in South America—directly lower realized oil prices.
