1. Globant Surges Despite AI Disruption Fears

Globant (NYSE: GLOB) is having a Friday. The IT outsourcing firm popped 14% after first-quarter revenue of $607.1 million topped estimates by $5.4 million -- not a blowout, but enough to make investors exhale after months of artificial intelligence (AI)-disruption hand-wringing. Motley Fool contributor Jason Hall noted in a Scoreboard video this week that Globant's AI focus should pay off long term, but acknowledged that "right now, it does certainly feel like a risk and a threat."

Down 41% and still standing: GLOB remains deep in the red for 2026, so today's celebration is more "didn't die" than "back in business."
The fine print: Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $1.50 hit estimates, but revenue still fell 0.7% year over year (YOY). Hall put it plainly: Since its 2014 initial public offering (IPO), Globant has underperformed the S&P 500 -- despite growing revenue roughly 20-fold.
2. Wix's Worst Week

Wix (NASDAQ: WIX) just had a week to forget. Shares cratered 31% after Q1 revenue of $541 million missed the $544 million consensus, and non-GAAP profit of $0.68 per share was nowhere near the $1.22 estimate. Multiple price target cuts followed the earnings miss, and the broader question now is whether Wix's "build your own website" model has a long-term seat at the table. WIX is recommended in Hidden Gems and in Stock Advisor (Team HG) -- most recently in 2020 for both services.

The AI wolf at the door: Anthropic's Claude Design -- an AI web builder that launched in April -- is exactly the kind of disruption Wix doesn't have a great answer for yet.
Foolish take: "As a SaaS business that has serious potential to be disrupted by AI coding models in its core website-building business, there's little room for error," Hidden Gems contributor Matt Frankel pointed out.
3. Is "Buyout Buzz" Ben & Jerry's Next Flavor?

Private equity (PE) firms scream for ice cream, too. Shares of Magnum Ice Cream (NYSE: MICC), Ben & Jerry's parent company and the world's largest stand-alone ice cream maker, scooped up a 9% gain Friday after Reuters reported a potential takeover -- only six months after the company split from Unilever (NYSE: UL).

Not all sunshine and sprinkles: Margin uncertainty and cost inflation are among the risks. Bidders are reportedly holding off for the moment. That's sensible, given that Q2 is roughly 35% of annual revenue; with the official start of summer still five weeks away, the most important data isn't in yet.
"Chocolate's getting old": Magnum's Moneyball Hidden Gems Primary database Superscore sits at just 46, with a one-year Product score of 37. The database flags a "perceived decline in product quality," straining customer satisfaction, and potentially weakening innovation -- citing a reported absence of research and development (R&D) investment in the most recent year.
