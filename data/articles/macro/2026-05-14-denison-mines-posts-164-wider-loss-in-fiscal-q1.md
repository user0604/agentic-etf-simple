Notable developments
The Wheeler River (Phoenix) project advanced to construction, with capital commitments now totaling $149.1 million for Denison's share.
Net loss increased to $114.9 million, impacted by a large $108.4 million non-cash fair value loss on convertible note derivatives.
Liquidity remains strong, with $418.5 million in cash and cash equivalents available for project spending and operational needs.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management remains focused on execution at Phoenix, cost discipline, and future uranium sales. They highlight the regulatory and market backdrop as favorable, with ongoing contracts to monetize existing uranium inventory. The most important factor to monitor is the cost and schedule of the Wheeler River construction, as delays or overruns could affect liquidity.

Quarterly highlights

This quarter marks the shift into construction at the Phoenix in-situ recovery uranium mine. Assets under construction increased by $15.9 million, and the company reported $149.1 million in commitments over the next two years related to this project.

The reported net loss of $114.9 million reflects increased accounting volatility due to fair value changes on the convertible note derivatives entered in the previous year. These losses do not impact near-term cash, but they do increase reported net loss and add complexity to financial statements.

Denison’s only revenue came from toll milling, with no uranium produced by its own mines in this period. The completion of uranium sales in April and May 2026 at prices above the reported carrying value shows potential for future cash inflows as production ramps up.

What Denison Mines does

Denison Mines explores for, develops, and holds interests in uranium mining projects in Canada, focusing on advancing the Wheeler River project in the Athabasca Basin.
