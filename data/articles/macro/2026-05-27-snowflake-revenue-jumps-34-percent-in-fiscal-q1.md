Key Takeaways
Snowflake posted significant gains in customer and product revenue growth, exceeding its internal forecast.

Cloud data platform provider Snowflake (NYSE:SNOW) delivered a growth-driven first quarter, marked by sharp gains in customer expansion and product adoption. The company, known for its data cloud solutions powering analytics and artificial intelligence, released its fiscal first-quarter results on May 27, 2026, covering the three months ended April 30. The period saw the company outpace its own product revenue guidance and raise its outlook for the remainder of the year.

Overall, the quarter brought notable momentum in large enterprise wins and the deployment of new AI-driven platform features.

Notable Developments
Product revenue growth outpaced company guidance, with the quarter’s $1.33 billion figure exceeding management’s midpoint forecast by roughly $67 million.
Non-GAAP operating margin rose 4 percentage points year over year, reflecting expanding efficiency amid higher investment in AI product features.
Management raised full-year product revenue guidance to $5.84 billion, up $180 million from its previous target due to accelerating demand in AI and large enterprise accounts.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Company leaders now project full-year product revenue to increase 31 percent, with continued double-digit gains in non-GAAP operating margin and substantial free cash flow. The key factor to monitor is the pace of AI-driven product uptake, as AI features drew rapid adoption but their contribution to future sales growth remains an open question.

Quarterly Highlights

Product revenue showed its strongest sequential dollar growth ever, reaching a new high and supporting management’s decision to lift its full-year outlook. The company highlighted that over 13,600 accounts adopted Snowflake’s AI-driven capabilities, with more than 7,100 using new platform features like Cortex Code for enterprise AI tasks.

Customer growth was robust, with 616 net new accounts added and 46 customers surpassing $1 million in trailing twelve-month product spend during the quarter. Both large enterprise wins and net revenue retention rate, now at 126 percent, pointed to ongoing customer expansion and upsell momentum.

Partnership expansion contributed to the company’s performance. Recent highlights included a multi-year $6 billion commitment with AWS, as well as general availability launches from SAP and OpenAI partnerships. These alliances are meant to extend Snowflake’s platform footprint and AI use cases both inside and outside current customer segments.

What Snowflake Does

Snowflake provides a cloud-based data platform that enables organizations to store, analyze, and share large volumes of data across multiple public cloud providers.
