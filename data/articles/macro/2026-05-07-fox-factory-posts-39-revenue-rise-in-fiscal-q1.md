Fox Factory Posts 3.9% Revenue Rise in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Revenue reached the high end of management's target, but profitability remains pressured by lower margins.
Net sales rose 3.9% year over year.
Management reaffirmed its full-year outlook despite persistent headwinds in certain segments.

Profit improvement plans helped Fox Factory (NASDAQ:FOXF) deliver on its own first-quarter sales target, while profit margins stayed under pressure from ongoing tariffs and weaker specialty segment demand. The specialty suspension manufacturer is known for high-performance products in the automotive, cycling, and powersports spaces. The company released its fiscal first quarter results on May 7, 2026, covering the period through early April.

Overall, the quarter showed stable revenue but highlighted persistent margin challenges and continued pressure in parts of the business.

Notable Developments
Powered Vehicles Group saw net sales rise 17.4%, contributing most to the company’s modest overall sales growth.
Specialty Sports Group revenue dropped 8.7%, as distributors and dealers reduced inventory and retail demand remained muted.
The company completed the divestiture of its Phoenix aftermarket operations, using proceeds to reduce debt and streamline its portfolio.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year guidance ranges for net sales and adjusted EBITDA, signaling confidence in ongoing cost-saving and restructuring efforts. The most important area for investors to monitor is progress on margin recovery, particularly in the face of ongoing tariffs and slow recovery in specialty sports. Signs of stabilization in the cycling-related segment could be key to a more sustained earnings rebound.

Quarterly Highlights

The Powered Vehicles Group (PVG), which makes shocks and suspension components for powersports and automotive markets, drove the company’s revenue growth, up 17.4% from the prior year. This growth came from strengthening demand in both the powersports and automotive aftermarket channels.

In contrast, the Specialty Sports Group (SSG) segment, which includes cycling and related gear, continued to struggle. Retail channel destocking and a weaker end-market reduced SSG revenue by 8.7% for the quarter. Management cited a “subdued” market environment and indicated that recovery in this segment is likely to remain slow in the near term.

The completion of the Phoenix aftermarket divestiture removed margin-dilutive operations from the portfolio. Proceeds were shifted to debt reduction, aligning with management’s goal of building a leaner, more focused company. Early cost-saving initiatives contributed, but improvement in company-wide gross margin remains a work in progress.

What Fox Factory Does

Fox Factory designs and manufactures premium suspension products and systems for bicycles, off-road vehicles, trucks, ATVs, and motorcycles, serving both original equipment manufacturers and aftermarket customers globally.
