Key Takeaways
Rocket Pharmaceuticals marked a milestone with its first U.S. Food and Drug Administration product approval but still reported no product revenue.
Net loss per share improved to $(0.42) from $(0.56) year over year, reflecting reduced operating expenses.
Management extended its projected cash runway into the second quarter of 2028, citing cost discipline and proceeds from a voucher sale.

Rocket Pharmaceuticals (NASDAQ:RCKT) achieved a major regulatory milestone with its first U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval, but the company has yet to generate product revenue. The gene therapy developer focuses on treatments for rare, inherited heart and immune diseases. On May 7, 2026, it reported results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, highlighting a reduced net loss and an extended cash runway due to cost cuts and a significant asset sale. Overall, the quarter featured operational discipline but ongoing dependence on late-stage clinical outcomes.

Notable developments
First product approval: KRESLADI received accelerated U.S. Food and Drug Administration approval for a rare immune disorder, but no sales were reported in the quarter.
Operating expenses fell sharply, with research and development costs down 12.2% and general and administrative expenses down 39.9% from the prior year.
Rocket sold a Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher after quarter-end, boosting its pro forma cash to about $322.6 million and extending its operational runway.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management indicated its current cash, including voucher proceeds, should fund operations into the second quarter of 2028. Upcoming milestones include new clinical data from its Danon disease gene therapy program and first patient dosing in its latest heart disease study. Investors should watch for progress toward meaningful product revenue and results from late-stage clinical trials.

Quarterly highlights

The company announced KRESLADI, its lead gene therapy, received accelerated approval for severe leukocyte adhesion deficiency-I (LAD-I), but the eligible patient group is extremely small, and sales have not begun to contribute materially. This approval also enabled Rocket to monetize a Rare Pediatric Disease Priority Review Voucher after quarter-end for $180 million, significantly enhancing its cash position and operational funding horizon.

Research and development as well as general and administrative costs dropped sharply year over year, reflecting ongoing effects of a 2025 company realignment aimed at extending the firm’s financial flexibility. Reductions included lower spending on manufacturing, staffing, and legal settlements.

Rocket resumed pivotal Phase 2 dosing in its RP-A501 gene therapy for Danon disease, a rare inherited heart condition, after a previous U.S. Food and Drug Administration clinical hold was lifted. The firm also initiated start-up activities for a Phase 1 trial targeting BAG3-related dilated cardiomyopathy, with dosing expected by mid-2026.
