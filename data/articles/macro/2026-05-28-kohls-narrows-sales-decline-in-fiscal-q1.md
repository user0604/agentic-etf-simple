Kohl's Narrows Sales Decline in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 28, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Net sales continued to decline, but Kohl's slowed the rate of sales losses compared to prior quarters.
Gross margin held steady at 39.9% while selling, general and administrative expenses decreased.
Management reaffirmed its full-year outlook, signaling a cautious stance for fiscal 2026.

Kohl's (NYSE:KSS) reported another period of falling sales, but narrowed its year-over-year sales decrease and kept profitability stable. The department store operator, with more than 1,100 locations in 49 states, described steady progress in its efforts to control costs and keep inventories lean. The earnings release, published May 28, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, suggested Kohl's is stabilizing but remains challenged by weak sales demand. Overall, the quarter delivered operational progress but continued top-line pressure.

Notable developments
Revenue dropped 1.7% year over year, but management noted this was the best comparable sales performance in over four years as the sales decline slowed to 1.1%.
Gross margin rate held at 39.9%, due to inventory management and lower markdowns, while SG&A expenses dipped by 1.6% despite revenue pressure.
Inventory ended the quarter at $2.9 billion, 8% lower than the prior year, with cash on hand increasing and no current borrowings under the revolving credit facility.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year forecast, expecting sales to range from down 2% to flat and adjusted operating margin between 2.8% and 3.4%. This cautious guidance points to persistent uncertainty around retail demand and consumer spending. Tracking sales growth and progress in proprietary brand initiatives will be key going forward.

Quarterly highlights

Sales continued to slide, but the company narrowed its year-over-year sales decrease from 2.8% in the previous quarter to 1.1% for comparable sales. This aligns with management’s stated goal of slow improvement, though overall sales remain lower than a year ago.

Profitability was stable thanks to cost and inventory control. Gross margin matched last year’s rate, with only a very slight improvement, and SG&A expenses declined modestly, suggesting better discipline over costs even as revenue decreased.

Kohl's reduced inventory levels sharply, cutting the risk of future markdowns and boosting its cash position. The company finished the quarter with no borrowings on its revolver and paid cash and equivalents of $429 million, up dramatically from $153 million the prior year.
