Coherent Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 11:07 AM

Comments

Coherent Corp. (NYSE:COHR) is a global leader in photonics and advanced optical materials, supplying key components for data centers, communications networks, and precision industrial applications. The company’s growth strategy centers on high-speed optical modules for artificial intelligence (AI) data centers, vertical integration in next-generation materials like indium phosphide, and expanding global manufacturing capacity to serve surging demand from cloud and hyperscale customers.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Secular AI Data Center Demand Drives Multi-Year Revenue Visibility

Rapid AI buildouts underpin Coherent’s long-term order pipeline – As of Q3 fiscal year 2026, Coherent’s datacenter and communications segment delivered more than 75% of total company revenue, with this division surging over 40% year over year as hyperscalers rapidly expand AI infrastructure. Management reports record bookings and a backlog stretching into 2028, with multi-year supply agreements (LTAs) and orders extending as far as 2030. This rare degree of forward visibility, reinforced by structural AI-driven demand, makes sudden revenue slumps less likely and allows for deliberate capital expansion. The shift to higher bandwidth modules (1.6 terabit and beyond) ensures Coherent is at the heart of an industry growth wave that could sustain outsized top-line gains over the next several years.

2. Vertical Integration and Technology Leadership Improve Margins and Resilience

Indium phosphide scale delivers both cost and supply chain advantages – Coherent’s accelerated ramp-up of 6-inch indium phosphide wafer production—now spanning facilities in Texas, Sweden, and upcoming in Zurich—sets the company apart in the optical components industry. Yields on these lines already exceed legacy 3-inch processes, with 6-inch products contributing to higher gross margins that reached 39.6% (non-GAAP) in Q3 FY2026, up 105 basis points year over year. This vertical integration delivers not only cost efficiency and yield improvement, but also greatly enhances supply chain reliability at a time when industry-wide materials constraints persist. The internal supply of key optical materials positions Coherent to outpace competitors in both responsiveness and profitability as the AI cycle accelerates.

3. Strategic Partnerships with Industry Giants Boost Credibility and Scale

Nvidia’s investment cements Coherent as a ‘must-have’ supplier – The company’s March 2026 announcement of a $2 billion equity investment from Nvidia, tied directly to a multi-year supply agreement for co-packaged optics and advanced photonic components, does more than secure capital. It validates Coherent’s technology roadmap and publicly signals strategic alignment with a dominant force in AI computing. Several other leading cloud and telecom players are reportedly under long-term agreements as well. These partnerships reduce uncertainty, protect against customer attrition, and support ongoing capital expenditures, all while raising the company’s profile with future buyers needing top-tier optical solutions.

4. Strong Financial Performance Provides Flexibility for Aggressive Growth

Balance sheet strength and operational leverage support expansion – During Q3 FY2026, Coherent grew revenue to $1.81 billion (21% GAAP year-over-year growth; 27% pro forma), while non-GAAP operating margin climbed to 20.3%, up from 18.6% a year ago. The company ended the quarter with $3 billion in cash, reduced its net leverage ratio from 2.1x to 0.5x in a year, and proactively increased capital expenditures to $290 million to fund urgently needed manufacturing ramp-ups. Rising operating margins and expanding capacity will further contribute to bottom-line potential as high-value product lines scale, while the strengthened balance sheet provides a cushion to weather industry volatility and capitalize on emerging growth opportunities.

5. Accelerating Product Mix Shift to High-Margin, Next-Gen Optics

Rapid migration to 1.6T and co-packaged optics expands both revenue and margin – Coherent’s revenue mix is increasingly tilted toward new high-bandwidth products, such as 800 gigabit and 1.6 terabit transceivers, co-packaged optics, and advanced optical circuit switches. In Q3 FY2026, communications revenue soared 60% year over year, and initial shipments of advanced modules—including those for Nvidia—are already driving sequential revenue and margin improvement. The company now expects 800G and 1.6T products to accelerate further into 2027, with new revenue streams from thermal management and energy-efficient modules set to ramp from the second half of 2027. This migration helps offset cyclical weakness in industrial markets and positions Coherent for higher-margin, sustainable growth as network bandwidth needs escalate.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Overreliance on Hyperscale Customers Creates Revenue Concentration Risk

Customer concentration may fuel volatility in the event of strategy shifts or order delays – While long-term supply agreements underpin Coherent’s outlook, a significant share of revenue is tied to a small group of hyperscale and AI data center operators, including a notable dependence on Nvidia. If key customers accelerate their own internal sourcing, delay orders, or diversify suppliers, Coherent could experience pronounced swings in revenue. The company acknowledges concentration risk in filings and commentary, which means the resilience of current order flows may be tested in the event of broader industry slowdowns or strategic pivots by its largest buyers.

2. Execution Risks Remain with Ambitious Capacity Expansion and Technology Transitions

Scaling 6-inch indium phosphide and commercializing next-gen products entails substantial operational risk – Success in ramping 6-inch indium phosphide production has so far exceeded expectations, but the transition to larger wafer sizes and integration of novel technologies such as co-packaged optics or advanced switches are not without challenges. Any faltering in device yields, unanticipated production bottlenecks, or delays in qualifying new facilities (like Zurich) could disrupt supplies to customers depending on timely adoption cycles. These execution risks, if realized, would materially impact both near-term revenue and the company’s competitive credibility at a pivotal moment in global AI infrastructure expansion.

3. Industrial Market Cyclicality Could Resurface as a Drag on Overall Financial Results

Weakness in legacy industrial and precision manufacturing segments may partially offset datacenter gains – Despite the outperformance in datacenter sales, Coherent’s smaller industrial division continues to face headwinds from macroeconomic softness and cyclicality. Q3 FY2026 saw further revenue declines in this segment, acting as a drag on consolidated financials. Should the anticipated cyclical recovery in industrial or semiconductor capital equipment markets stall or reverse, overall margin and earnings growth could moderate, reducing upside for investors and raising questions about diversification in the company’s long-term revenue stream.
