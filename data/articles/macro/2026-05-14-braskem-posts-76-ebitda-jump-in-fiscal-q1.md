Braskem (NYSE:BAK) lifted margins this quarter, but it continues to grapple with cash outflow and rising debt. The leading petrochemicals producer in Latin America benefited from government incentives and stronger spreads in its core Brazil/South America and U.S. & Europe segments. The company announced its first quarter 2026 results on May 14, 2026, covering the period's continued financial strain despite operational advances. Overall, the quarter balanced margin progress in Brazil against steeper losses in Mexico and pressure on its balance sheet.

Notable Developments
Recurring EBITDA for Brazil/South America grew 21% year over year to $241 million, while consolidated recurring EBITDA was $192 million. This lift followed a government tax incentive and better pricing in plastics and resins.
Cash and cash equivalents fell sharply by 49% from a year earlier, ending the period at $1.2 billion. The company faced operating cash consumption of about R$3.2 billion and recurring cash outflows near R$4.6 billion.
Adjusted net debt increased to $8.5 billion, up 27% year over year, with leverage at a high 16.81 times trailing EBITDA. The Mexico operations posted a sizable negative EBITDA and remain under financial stress.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide explicit new guidance for the next quarter or year. It points to continued uncertainty around liquidity, government support in Brazil, and potential restructuring of its distressed Mexico business. Investors should closely watch cash flow stabilization, any resolution in Mexico, and whether government incentives remain in place.

Quarterly Highlights

While recurring EBITDA expanded, cash burn continued at a high rate. Margin improvement in Brazil was fueled by government policy and better industrial spreads, but this did not translate to positive free cash flow.

Mexico operations sharply underperformed, showing negative $15 million recurring EBITDA as feedstock shortages and a 30 percentage point drop in utilization rate hit results. Financial distress at the Mexico joint venture (Braskem Idesa) raises the risk of judicial restructuring.

Net income increased mainly due to currency exchange effects, not improved profitability. Cash and liquidity remain pressured, with almost half the prior year's reserve gone, and debt metrics continuing to worsen.

What Braskem Does

Braskem is a Latin American chemical producer specializing in thermoplastic resins and chemicals, including polyethylene, polypropylene, and polyvinyl chloride for packaging, automotive, and construction applications.
