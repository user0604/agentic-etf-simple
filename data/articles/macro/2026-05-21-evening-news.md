1. Zoom Pops on AI Surge and a Billion-Dollar Buyback

Zoom Video Communications (NASDAQ: ZM) just proved it's way more than a pandemic-era relic. The collaboration giant breezed past first-quarter expectations with a 5.5% revenue bump to $1.24 billion, sending the stock up 7% in after-hours trading. Analyst Yasser El-Shimy called the results "a clean beat-and-raise that meaningfully advances the AI-monetization narrative." Zoom -- a recommendation for Stock Advisor (Team HG and Team RB) -- is converting corporate artificial intelligence (AI) migration into pure cash, expanding its adjusted operating margin to a massive 41.1%. To celebrate the windfall, the board tacked an extra $1.0 billion onto its share repurchase program, giving management plenty of firepower to fight off rivals Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) and Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG).

The AI Companion is a hit: Paid monetization is scaling fast. Zoom's automated AI Companion licenses skyrocketed 184% year over year, while its broader productivity app suite cleared 1.5 million users.
Big spenders offset retail churn: High-value corporate clients bringing in over $100,000 annually climbed 8.2% to 4,534. That enterprise strength helped cushion a slight tick upward in monthly online retail subscriber churn to 3%.
2. Workday's AI Bots Double Down

Workday (NASDAQ: WDAY) is making corporate automation look easy. The human capital and financial software pioneer posted a spectacular 13.5% revenue jump to $2.542 billion for Q1, sending shares soaring 13% after hours. The Stock Advisor (Team HG) and Rule Breakers recommendation widened its adjusted operating margin to 31.8%, as businesses rush to adopt its automated intelligence tools. With a massive $27.29 billion subscription backlog securing its defensive moat against Microsoft and Oracle (NYSE: ORCL), management confidently raised its full-year margin guidance while holding on to a $9.95 billion subscription revenue target.

Attack of the AI Agents: Enterprise clients using Workday's homegrown digital assistants more than doubled quarter-over-quarter. Proving its real-world utility, the company's recruiting software successfully processed 14 million automated hiring transactions.
Aggressive capital returns: Workday generated a robust $616 million in quarterly free cash flow and immediately put it to work for shareholders, aggressively swallowing up 12 million shares of common stock for $1.6 billion.
3. Deckers Steps Up With a Massive $5 Billion Buyback

Deckers Brands (NYSE: DECK) reported record full-year fiscal 2026 results, with total revenue climbing 10% to $5.47 billion and diluted earnings per share growing 11% to $7.02. Shares were up 4.5% after hours. Brand growth was fueled by a 15.9% surge from HOKA and an 8.2% advance from UGG. Looking ahead, management issued a confident fiscal 2027 outlook, with projected earnings reaching up to $7.45 per share, alongside a long-term framework targeting high-single-digit annualized sales expansion through 2030. Solidifying shareholder returns, the footwear leader increased its common stock repurchase authorization by an additional $3.5 billion, boosting its current total capital return allocation to approximately $5 billion.

Explosive international footprint expansion: Full-year international operational sales rocketed 26.8% to $2.28 billion, successfully insulating the retail platform from completely flat domestic market growth of just 0.2%.
Pruning portfolio underperformers: Wholesale channel and domestic metric contractions were driven by strategic brand restructurings, including the complete phase-out of Koolaburra stand-alone facilities and the successful corporate divestiture of Sanuk.
