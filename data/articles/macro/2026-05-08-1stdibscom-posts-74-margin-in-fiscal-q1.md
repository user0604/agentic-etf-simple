Key Takeaways
It was a disciplined quarter for 1stdibs.Com with continued profitability improvements but ongoing declines in core marketplace activity.
Gross margin improved to 74.4% while net revenue was nearly flat compared to the prior year period.
Management maintained its full-year outlook and expects marketplace growth to resume by the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026.

Online luxury marketplace 1stdibs.Com (NASDAQ:DIBS) delivered a mixed quarter, with continued improvements in profitability but persistent declines in buyer engagement and transaction activity. The platform, known for connecting buyers and sellers of antiques, jewelry, fine art, and high-end design, released its first-quarter 2026 results on May 8, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. While it achieved its goal of positive adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) and generated free cash flow, purchasing activity on the marketplace shrank for another quarter. Overall, the period highlighted progress in cost control and margins, against a backdrop of falling orders and active buyers.

Notable Developments
Gross margin rose to 74.4%, reflecting improved efficiency and cost control while supporting positive adjusted EBITDA for the second consecutive quarter.
Marketplace activity declined, with number of orders down 12% and active buyers falling 10% from the prior-year period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its outlook for fiscal 2026, reiterating plans to return to marketplace growth by the fourth quarter of this year. For the current quarter, it projected flat to slightly lower gross merchandise value (GMV) and net revenue, with the possibility of margin pressure as resources shift toward technology and product development. The top item for investors to monitor remains the stabilization of buyer activity and order volume, which are key to supporting any long-term growth narrative.

Quarterly Highlights

The company's profitability continued to improve, with both gross margin and non-GAAP adjusted EBITDA showing year-over-year gains.

Core marketplace activity continued to shrink. The platform recorded 31,000 orders and 58,000 active buyers for the trailing twelve months, reflecting double-digit declines versus the prior year. This signals continued demand challenges, even as the company invests more in technology and product initiatives.

While technology development spending increased 10%, management points to this as "our highest-ROI investment" aimed at future growth. Results from these investments are not yet visible in current engagement metrics, leaving progress against user and seller network expansion a key area for scrutiny in the second half of the year.
