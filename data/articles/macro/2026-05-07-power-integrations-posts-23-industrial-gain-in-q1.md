Power Integrations Posts 23% Industrial Gain in Q1

Key Takeaways
Industrial segment delivered strong revenue growth despite margin pressures.
Non-GAAP EPS fell 19.4% year over year.
Management projects sequential revenue and margin improvement for next quarter.

Power Integrations (NASDAQ:POWI) notched a business win with standout growth in its industrial business but continued to face cost headwinds and challenges in legacy segments. The leading designer of high-voltage integrated circuits (ICs) for energy-efficient power conversion reported its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter was directionally positive as industrial strength offset declines or stagnation elsewhere, though margins narrowed.

Notable developments
Industrial segment revenue rose 23% year over year, making up 41% of total revenue, up from 34% a year ago.
Margins contracted, with non-GAAP gross margin dropping 2.4 percentage points and non-GAAP net income decreasing year over year.
The company paid a quarterly dividend of $0.215 per share, unchanged from the previous quarter.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management projects second-quarter revenue of $115 million to $120 million, signaling anticipated sequential growth. It also expects slightly higher gross and operating margins, reflecting hopes for improved sales mix and cost control. Investors should focus on whether industrial segment momentum holds and if margin recovery materializes as forecast.

Quarterly highlights

The industrial end-market was the highlight of the period, growing revenue 23% year over year and increasing its share of total business. This growth was driven by applications in renewable energy, battery storage, and grid infrastructure, emphasizing successful expansion efforts into high-growth areas.

Profitability faced pressure, with both non-GAAP gross and operating margins lower than a year ago. The margin contraction was due to pricing pressures, increased costs, and $6.2 million in restructuring charges linked to workforce reductions.

Outside of industrial, consumer, computer, and communications segments either shrank or stagnated compared to the prior year, demonstrating continued segment imbalances. Free cash flow remained positive, and the company’s cash position stayed stable, ending with $63.4 million in cash and $193.8 million in short-term investments.

What Power Integrations does

Power Integrations develops and markets high-voltage integrated circuits and related components for power conversion in electronic devices and energy infrastructure.
