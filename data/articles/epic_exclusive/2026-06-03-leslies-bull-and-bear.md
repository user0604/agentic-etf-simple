Leslie's Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

11:08 AM

Comments

Leslie's, Inc. (NASDAQ:LESL) is the leading direct-to-consumer pool and spa care retailer in the U.S, offering chemicals, equipment, and services for both residential and commercial customers. The company is pursuing an ambitious turnaround strategy, centered on driving customer traffic and retention through price investments, omnichannel convenience, network optimization, and strict cost discipline, all while navigating high financial leverage and a rapidly evolving retail landscape.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Sustained Customer Count Growth Signals Turnaround Momentum

Leslie's posted 8% year-over-year customer count growth in Q2 2026, demonstrating the early impact of its customer-centric transformation strategy. The company’s focus on reactivating lapsed customers and attracting new shoppers through targeted marketing, improved service, and strategic price drops has resulted in more than 25% growth in reactivated customers. This influx, driven by initiatives leveraging proprietary customer data and omnichannel outreach, indicates a growing loyal base. If this trend persists, it positions Leslie's to regain lost market share, drive higher transaction volumes, and offset headwinds from previous sales declines or store closures. Growth in both new and returning customers provides a strong foundation for subsequent gains in comparable sales and long-term customer lifetime value, crucial for sustained revenue and profitability improvements in the competitive pool supply sector.

2. Price Drop Strategy Catalyzes Store Productivity and Competitive Positioning

The nationwide Price Drop initiative, launched in March 2026, has rapidly driven double-digit transaction growth and a 350 basis point improvement in store conversion rates. By resetting its value proposition and lowering core chemical prices, the company has both attracted price-sensitive shoppers and improved core category volumes. With strong customer feedback and chemical category outperformance in Q2 2026, there is evidence that price investments can lead to higher foot traffic and greater in-store engagement—such as increased use of Leslie’s proprietary water testing service, which also drives consultative add-on sales. If price optimization spreads to additional non-MAP (Minimum Advertised Price) protected categories, Leslie’s could further entrench its pricing leadership without eroding margins in protected segments. Over time, this could translate into sustainable market share gains and differentiated brand positioning against both big-box and online competitors.

3. Expanding Omnichannel Offerings Fuel Digital and Store Sales Growth

Strategic expansion of omnichannel services—especially buy online, pick up in store (BOPIS) and same-day Uber delivery—has driven strong growth in BOPIS adoption and has served as a key traffic driver to stores. Completing the nationwide rollout of digital and delivery offerings in Q2 2026 has fortified Leslie's ability to seamlessly transfer customers from closed stores to digital channels or other physical locations, exceeding initial expectations. This convenience-focused model keeps the brand relevant for both legacy in-store shoppers and new digital-first customers, enabling sales retention during footprint optimization. As the pool and spa market gradually digitizes, the company’s scalable multichannel platform could be a key differentiator—supporting higher sales per customer, reducing customer attrition risk, and opening new avenues for personalized marketing and upselling through integrated customer data systems.

4. Asset and Inventory Optimization Drive Long-Term Margin Expansion

Network rationalization and inventory discipline are delivering tangible cost savings and enhancing profitability even as the company closes underperforming stores. In fiscal 2026, Leslie’s closed 80 locations and streamlined its distribution center network, targeting $4–10 million in annualized net EBITDA benefit despite accompanying revenue impact. A 22% reduction in inventory year-over-year—with in-stock rates on key products at all-time highs—demonstrates effective working capital management and SKU rationalization, expected to yield $4–5 million in additional annual EBITDA improvement. These efforts contribute to a leaner, more efficient operating model that can withstand seasonal volatility and reduce future markdown risk, setting the stage for lasting improvements in gross margins as the company cycles through one-time transition costs.

5. Expense Discipline and Improved Adjusted EBITDA Support Financial Resilience

Leslie’s improved adjusted EBITDA by $9.2 million year over year in Q2 2026, reflecting over 220 basis points of SG&A (selling, general and administrative) leverage and the successful rollout of expense reduction programs. Careful control over labor and store operating costs, combined with lower occupancy and distribution expenses, enabled the company to strengthen profitability even as transformation investments ramped up. Management is tracking toward $7–12 million in annualized cost savings in 2026, and full-year guidance remains unchanged. With positive free cash flow forecasted, and no further store closures planned for this fiscal year, improved operating efficiency could help buffer against potential external shocks and provide the flexibility required to invest opportunistically in growth or further price initiatives.

Potential Bear Cases
1. High Financial Leverage and Liquidity Constraints Limit Strategic Flexibility

Leslie’s entered Q2 2026 with $753 million in long-term debt and $99 million outstanding on its revolving credit line, leaving less than $100 million in liquidity headroom. While the company’s transformation is showing some early traction, elevated leverage magnifies execution risk and limits the ability to absorb setbacks from seasonal variability, macroeconomic shocks, or competitive pricing pressures. If restructuring fails to drive projected cash flow, Leslie's could face higher borrowing costs or restrictive lender terms, particularly given past amendments to its credit facilities. This capital structure vulnerability may curtail investment in essential growth initiatives or leave the company exposed during off-peak pool seasons, undermining the sustainability of the turnaround effort.

2. Margin Gains May Be Unsustainable Due to Ongoing Pricing and Inventory Actions

Much of the recent margin expansion is expected to be offset in future quarters by continued price reductions and inventory optimization strategies impacting gross margin by 200 to 300 basis points in the back half of 2026. Management has explicitly warned that Q2 2026 margin improvement—driven by lower distribution and occupancy costs and inventory reserve reductions—contains several one-time factors. As Price Drop investments and inventory reductions flow through the income statement in Q3 and Q4, there could be renewed pressure on gross margins. If vendor negotiations fail to yield sufficient offsets, or if pricing actions outpace traffic gains, the company may struggle to sustain profitability improvements, limiting the scope for long-term operating leverage.

3. Execution Risks Remain High Amid Store Closures and Customer Transfer Initiatives

Ongoing store closures and network consolidation carry significant risk if customer transfers to remaining locations or digital channels underperform over time. While management reports above-expectation transfer rates in the wake of 80 store closures, these KPIs remain susceptible to erosion as the novelty of omnichannel outreach fades or as regional competitive dynamics intensify. Unexpected churn in transferred customers, especially during low-demand seasonality or under economic stress, could reverse recent gains in traffic and comp sales. Efficient recapture of lost sales from closed stores will determine whether the current turnaround is a springboard for reinvestment or a one-time correction that leaves the company less resilient to future shocks.
