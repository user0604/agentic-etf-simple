Key Takeaways
Clarus reported modest operational improvements but lowered its full-year outlook.
Adjusted earnings swung to a small profit while revenue rose 2.5% over the prior year.
Management trimmed guidance for 2026 revenue and adjusted EBITDA, citing expected Adventure segment declines and higher legal costs.

Clarus (NASDAQ:CLAR) posted modest gains in profitability and gross margin, but revised its full-year expectations downward as operating cash flow worsened. The outdoor and adventure equipment company reported improved results in some core metrics, yet increasing pressure in its Adventure segment and rising legal costs prompted a sharp reduction in guidance. The earnings release for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, was issued on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter reflected cautious progress amid headwinds, with the company now exploring strategic alternatives.

Notable developments
Revenue increased by 2.5% year over year, landing at $61.9 million, near the high end of the company’s Q1 outlook.
Gross margin expanded by 2.4 percentage points, driven by a shift to higher-margin products in both main segments.
Management sharply reduced 2026 sales and profit expectations after Adventure segment pressures intensified, and it launched a review of strategic alternatives.
Quarterly highlights

The Outdoor segment saw a slight revenue increase over the prior year, aided by "more premium, full-price business model" initiatives and a fourth consecutive quarter of apparel growth. Improved product mix and gains in global wholesale and distributor channels supported the result, though the sale of the PIEPS product line reduced year-over-year comparisons.

The Adventure segment grew revenue by 5.9% year over year, mostly from strong wholesale sales in Australia. However, management highlighted that demand in Australia is now weakening, leading to a cautious outlook and higher concentration risk for this segment.

Liquidity remained stable, with $29.8 million in cash and no debt at period end, but free cash flow eroded as cash outflow rose to $(5.7) million. Inventory levels stayed high, with only a small decrease versus year-end, which may become a risk if demand stalls.

Management outlook and what to watch

Clarus management lowered its 2026 revenue target to between $245 million and $255 million, and cut its adjusted EBITDA guidance to between approximately $3 million and $5 million. Leadership flagged legal and regulatory expenses and a challenging Adventure segment as key headwinds. Investors should watch upcoming quarters for updates on the strategic alternatives review and signs of improvement in international and domestic demand.

What Clarus does

Clarus produces and sells outdoor and adventure equipment and accessories under brands including Black Diamond, Rhino-Rack, and MAXTRAX, targeting global markets.
