Sportradar Group AG Returns to Buy

We believe the claims of malfeasance in two short reports are overblown.

David Meier

May 22, 2026, 12:05 PM

Comments
What We're Doing

We are taking Sportradar Group AG (NASDAQ: SRAD) off hold and restoring its buy rating in the Epic Portfolio.

What We Think About the Situation Today

As we communicated previously, two separate short-seller reports claimed that Sportradar generates revenue from illegal and/or unregistered betting websites. Although we do take short reports seriously, we work to determine the validity of their claims and think about the impact of those claims being true.

The short-sellers claimed that, despite branding itself as being against illegal sports betting, Sportradar actively did business with bad actors to generate revenue for the company. After looking at the "examples" from the reports, we question why the company would put its reputation, as well as the entire business, at risk by actively doing business with criminals. It doesn't make sense and isn't consistent with the company's decades of operating experience. And founder and CEO Karsten Coerl, in the company's most recent earnings report, once again denied the allegations, saying:

To be clear, Sportradar and I reject the unfounded and misinformed allegations contained in the reports. As the global leader in sports technology, trusted by leagues, operators and regulators around the world, we place integrity, transparency, professionalism at the heart of everything we do.

There is always a chance that some of Sportradar's data falls into the hands of these bad actors. And the company works to shut down those pathways as quickly as possible. But it does not make sense to our team that the company, after 25 years of successful operations, would change course and actively work with bad actors to generate additional revenue. As you will see below, there's plenty of growth expected ahead in 2026.

What We're Seeing and Looking For

Sportradar pulled forward its earnings report to April 28, 2026. Investors appreciated management addressing the short reports' allegations directly. Unfortunately, performance for the first quarter was a little underwhelming. Revenue growth came in under expectations, but that was countered by the strong cash-flow generation from those sales. Even with the revenue-growth shortfall, management reiterated its sales-growth guidance of 23% to 25% for the full year.

In addition to strong growth expected in 2026, Sportradar is actively fulfilling its $1 billion share repurchase program. During the quarter, the company "repurchased $90 million of shares under the share repurchase plan and announced a $250 million enhanced open market share repurchase program." What's more, CEO Carsten Koerl has purchased more than 750,000 shares on the open market recently.

It's always great to see executives purchasing shares on the open market. It's a sign they believe in the future of the business. And given there's still a substantial amount of purchasing capacity in the remainder of the repurchase plan, there should be buying pressure for the rest of the year. Both of those are good signs, in our opinion.

With strong growth ahead, even stronger cash-flow generation, and confidence regarding the lack of wrongdoing from management, we are taking Sportradar off hold and returning it to buy.
