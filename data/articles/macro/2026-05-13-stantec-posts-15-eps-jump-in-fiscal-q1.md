Key Takeaways
Stantec delivered a solid quarter with strong net revenue growth and record backlog.

Stantec (NYSE:STN) delivered robust revenue growth and secured a healthy pipeline of new contract wins. The global design and engineering consultancy drives its business through projects in infrastructure, water, environmental services, and buildings. For the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (reported on May 13, 2026), it showed continued strength in project wins but experienced a temporary dip in cash flow due to acquisition integration.

Overall, the quarter demonstrated resilience through a strong project backlog and improved profitability, even as some core regions experienced slower organic growth.

Notable Developments
Net revenue grew 9.1% year over year, supported by both acquisitions and 3.6% organic growth, with Water segment revenue up 14.3% organically.
Backlog reached a record $9.0 billion, rising 13.2%, as acquisitions added to Buildings and international business momentum was strong.
Free cash flow dropped to $(65.7) million from positive $50.9 million a year earlier, primarily due to the integration of a major acquisition and investment in working capital.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed annual targets for 2026, including net revenue growth of 8.5% to 11.5% and an adjusted EBITDA margin between 17.6% and 18.2%. Leadership expects organic growth to accelerate as the year progresses, citing a rebound in the United States and Canada as integration activity subsides.

Quarterly Highlights

The Water segment stood out, with organic revenue growth of 14.3%, fueled by international demand and long-term agreements. In contrast, the Buildings segment saw revenue rise due to recent acquisitions, though organic revenue declined as older projects completed and the company adjusted to changes in project mix.

Backlog expansion was a highlight: total backlog increased to $9.0 billion, mainly from acquisition contributions in Buildings and a 21.8% organic increase in the Global segment. This extensive backlog covers approximately 13 months of anticipated work, illustrating strong visibility on future performance.

Cash flow was a negative outlier this period as free cash flow slipped into the red, reflecting the impact of integrating a large acquisition and seasonal patterns. Management described this as temporary, expecting improvement as systems stabilize.
