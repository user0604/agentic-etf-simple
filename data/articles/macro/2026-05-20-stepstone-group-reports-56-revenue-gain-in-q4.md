Key Takeaways
StepStone Group delivered strong fee and asset growth, but profitability showed signs of slowing.
Fee-related earnings rose over the prior year, while adjusted earnings per share fell 16% in the fourth quarter.
Management sees stable core earnings, though expects incentive fees to moderate in the coming year.

StepStone Group (NASDAQ:STEP) posted continued expansion in its core business lines, as recurring fees and client assets climbed in the latest quarter. The global private markets investment firm saw another year of steady growth and broad client engagement. Reporting for its fiscal fourth quarter ended March 31, 2026 (release dated May 20, 2026), StepStone delivered solid core results, but sharp drops in certain profit figures highlighted the unpredictable nature of performance fees and the challenge of sustaining margins.

Overall, the period showed that fundamental growth in assets and fee income remains intact, even as profitability measures moderate against a backdrop of fluctuating incentive payments.

Notable developments
Fee-related earnings (FRE) reached $105.3 million, up 12% from a year ago, with nearly $6 billion in new fee-earning capital added during the quarter.
Performance fees showed high volatility, dropping sharply from the prior quarter, as realized performance fee-related earnings fell 57% year over year to $17.9 million.
The board approved a regular quarterly dividend of $0.28 per share, an increase of 17%, and a supplemental dividend of $0.55 per share reflecting the strong performance fee income in fiscal 2026.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not offer specific financial guidance for fiscal 2027, but stated that Fee-related earnings were $89 million, up 20% from a year ago, while core FRE was up 35% driven by strong growth in fee revenues. FRE margin was 37% for the quarter, both on a reported and core basis, up roughly a percentage point from last quarter. Shifting to expenses, adjusted cash-based compensation was $107 million, representing a cash compensation ratio of 44%, slightly lower as compared to the last two quarters.

General and administrative expenses were $40 million, up $6 million from last quarter. The increase was primarily driven by our StepStone 360 conference held in October. G&A will remain seasonally high in our fiscal fourth quarter driven by a venture capital conference in February. Gross realized performance fees were $253 million for the quarter, comprising $47 million of realized carried interest and $207 million of incentive fees.

Total NAVs for Spring of $5.5 billion more than tripled over the course of the year, while performance of 39% was more than double the prior year. Spring's investment performance was particularly strong in the back half of the year, which further benefited this year's incentive fees, as those fees were calculated on a higher average asset base.

Quarterly highlights

Recurring fee-driven income continued its expansion, with total assets under management climbing 23% year over year to $233.3 billion and fee-earning assets reaching $144.0 billion. The increase in undeployed fee-earning capital, now $40.1 billion, suggests future growth in recurring management fees.

Profitability measures were mixed. Adjusted net income per share declined to $0.57, and the fee-related earnings margin slipped compared to the prior year. Management flagged the volatility in performance fees, which led to a pronounced drop in realized incentives this period after a record showing in prior quarters.

Supplemental dividends increased significantly in step with the strong fiscal 2026 performance fees but serve as a reminder that these incentive-based payouts can vary widely from year to year. The company’s base dividend also rose, reflecting confidence in ongoing fee income streams.
