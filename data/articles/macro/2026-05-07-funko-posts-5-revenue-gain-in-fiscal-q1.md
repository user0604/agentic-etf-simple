Key Takeaways
Funko delivered a strong quarter as international growth and margin expansion offset domestic softness.
Revenue rose 5.3% year over year to $200.9 million.
Management reaffirmed its full-year revenue and profit outlook, while highlighting ongoing risks around loungefly and licensing dependency.

Funko (NASDAQ:FNKO) posted a solid business win as international sales and record-high gross margin offset a decline in U.S. sales. The pop culture collectibles company is known for its stylized figures and merchandise based on licensed entertainment properties. On May 7, 2026, it reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, showing operational improvements but continued challenges in select product lines. Overall, the period brought notable progress internationally, while risk factors persisted in U.S. and secondary segments.

Notable developments
Core Collectible sales increased 16.8%, while Loungefly sales fell 23.1% and Other sales dropped 54.7% compared to the prior year.
European sales surged 25.6% year over year, but they account for approximately 34% of company revenue.
Gross margin reached a record 44.2%, attributed to improved licensing terms with major studios and lower minimum royalty payments.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its full-year 2026 targets: flat to 3% net sales growth, gross margins of 41–43%, and adjusted EBITDA between $70 million and $80 million. Despite strong first-quarter results, the company did not raise its annual sales guidance, reflecting caution linked to U.S. softness and category declines. Investors should watch the pace of Core Collectible growth and stabilization in Loungefly, along with how sustainable licensing cost improvements prove over coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Core Collectibles, which include Funko’s signature stylized figures based on major intellectual property, were the main growth driver as net sales in this category increased to $168.8 million. In contrast, the Loungefly segment, which includes fashion accessories and bags, saw sales decline to $27.2 million, marking a significant drag on overall revenue growth.

International momentum, especially in Europe, helped balance sluggish domestic performance. European net sales rose by more than a quarter year over year, while U.S. sales declined by 3.7%, and Europe now represents about 34% of total company revenue for the quarter.

Record gross margin was helped by renewed licensing deals with major studios such as Disney and Netflix. These agreements featured lower minimum royalty guarantees, reducing costs and improving profitability as the company continued its focus on cost discipline and operational improvements.

What Funko does

Funko creates and sells licensed pop culture collectibles, including figures, toys, and accessories based on franchises from movies, TV, sports, and more.
