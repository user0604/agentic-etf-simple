Plains GP Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 3:10 PM

Comments

Plains GP Holdings, L.P. (NASDAQ:PAGP) is a leading crude oil midstream company that owns and operates pipeline, storage, and terminal infrastructure across key North American energy basins, particularly the Permian. The company is actively reshaping its operations through the sale of its natural gas liquids (NGL) business and strategic investments like the Cactus III pipeline. Its growth strategy centers on streamlining toward fee-based, stable cash flows, deleveraging, and disciplined capital allocation aimed at sustainable distribution growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Strategic Focus on Pure-Play Crude Midstream Enhances Earnings Stability

Sharpening its business on crude oil transportation and storage elevates Plains GP Holdings, L.P.’s earnings predictability and downside protection. The completed NGL divestiture, finalized in May 2026, removes exposure to more volatile, commodity-sensitive segments and clarifies the company’s core business model. Management expects this renewed focus to drive higher fee-based revenues, streamline operations, and unlock efficiencies, resulting in a more resilient financial profile anchored in minimum volume commitments and long-term contracts. This pivot enhances the company’s appeal as a lower-risk, income-generating investment, especially significant for institutional and yield-seeking investors.

2. Successful Asset Sale Fuels Deleveraging and Financial Flexibility

Plains GP Holdings, L.P. expects approximately $3.3 billion in proceeds from the Canadian NGL asset sale. Management has already earmarked these funds for debt repayment—including the paydown of term loans, commercial paper, and maturing notes—which is projected to reduce pro forma leverage from 4.1x to approximately 3.5x net debt to EBITDA following the NGL sale. This de-risking supports improved credit metrics, lowers interest costs, and positions the company to resume preferred equity redemptions and opportunistic buybacks. In a midstream sector that increasingly rewards balance sheet discipline, such moves set the stage for enhanced valuation and long-term payout capacity.

3. Raised EBITDA Guidance Reflects Robust Operational Execution and Market Demand

Plains GP Holdings, L.P.’s upward revision of its full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA outlook signals outperformance and positive business momentum. For the first quarter of 2026, Adjusted EBITDA attributable to the partnership was $730 million, prompting a $130 million increase in full-year guidance to a midpoint of $2.88 billion. The crude oil segment contributed $582 million in Q1, underscoring the benefits of the Cactus III acquisition and strong asset utilization. This upward revision, attributed to optimized volumes, tariff escalations, and minimal performance drag from weather or maintenance disruptions, demonstrates management’s ability to capitalize on favorable industry trends and efficiently integrate new assets, driving durable year-over-year profit growth.

4. Capital Discipline and Cost Reduction Drive Higher Returns on Investment

Continued focus on disciplined capital spending and efficiency improvements increases Plains GP Holdings, L.P.’s long-term value creation. In 2026, the company maintained guidance for $350 million in growth capital while increasing maintenance capital only modestly to $185 million due to delayed NGL sale timing. Plains reported successful progress toward $100 million in run-rate cost savings by 2027, with $50 million slated for achievement by year-end 2026. This disciplined approach ensures that every new project must meet clear return thresholds and provides a platform for sustainable EBITDA and free cash flow growth. The alignment of organic investment with market demand, especially for assets like Cactus III, should boost return on invested capital and shareholder confidence.

5. Strong Infrastructure Position and Geopolitical Tailwinds Support Long-Term Demand

Plains GP Holdings, L.P. stands to benefit from heightened global demand for North American energy infrastructure amid increased geopolitical volatility. The recent closure of the Strait of Hormuz, tightening of global shipping channels, and persistent U.S. upstream activity have elevated the strategic value of Plains’ pipeline and storage networks, particularly in the Permian Basin. Management expects long-haul utilization rates to remain robust, with market tightness and storage needs driving volume and tariff upside. As international supply chains realign and global restocking of petroleum reserves gathers pace, Plains is well-positioned to capture incremental volumes and fee income, securing future growth in an evolving energy landscape.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Heavy Hedging Limits Near-Term Upside from Higher Crude Prices

Plains GP Holdings, L.P.’s extensive hedging strategy restricts its ability to fully benefit from recent increases in crude oil prices. The company entered 2026 with most of its profit-linked adjustment mechanisms hedged at an average $62 per barrel, while spot prices averaged closer to $85 from June through December 2026. This means that even though market prices have surged due to geopolitical tensions, only modest incremental upside will flow through unless elevated pricing persists well into later quarters. This hedge profile can limit the potential for near-term earnings beats relative to some less-hedged peers, which may hinder share price momentum if commodity markets remain strong.

2. Regulatory and Legal Uncertainty Overhangs Potential Canadian Expansion

Active legal challenges from Canadian competition authorities cast uncertainty over Plains GP Holdings, L.P.’s ability to close and realize value from strategic Canadian transactions. Although management asserts that regulatory lawsuits will not prevent closing current deals, such as the Keyera transaction, the ongoing scrutiny introduces risk of delays, onerous conditions, or unexpected costs. In an environment where midstream projects already face elevated permitting and regulatory hurdles, prolonged legal processes may divert management’s focus, delay planned synergies, and cause investor concern over Plains’ ability to grow through cross-border or Canadian infrastructure initiatives.

3. Exposure to U.S. Production Cycles and Volume Fluctuations Remains a Key Risk

Concentration in U.S. crude oil midstream operations, particularly the Permian Basin, exposes Plains GP Holdings, L.P. to upstream volume volatility and cyclical risk. While the company has increased fee-based revenue and contracted minimum volume commitments, its revenues are still ultimately dependent on the health of U.S. production and drilling activity. Winter weather effects, system maintenance, or a downturn in rig counts—as seen in isolated production shut-ins in Q1 2026—could lead to meaningful, unrecoverable quarterly revenue impacts. If upstream activity slows or experiences extended disruption, Plains’ earnings could face pressure, underscoring a risk that may become more pronounced with the sharper focus on crude infrastructure post-NGL sale.
