Key Takeaways
PLBY Group posted improved profitability as direct-to-consumer growth offset licensing declines.

PLBY Group (NASDAQ:PLBY) reported higher profitability as growth in direct sales and cost controls helped offset a decline in licensing revenue. The lifestyle and brand management company, known for the Playboy brand and direct-to-consumer businesses like Honey Birdette, released its first quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed steady progress, with some ongoing pressure in certain segments and continued execution of its capital-light strategy.

Notable developments
Direct-to-consumer revenue rose 15%, supported by strong Honey Birdette sales in the United States.
Licensing revenue declined 5%, with management attributing most of the drop to the expiration of a few agreements.
The company paid down $15 million in senior debt using proceeds from a China joint venture, with more payments scheduled through 2028.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management described the quarter as a demonstration of improved efficiency and profitability, supported by ongoing debt reduction and a more recurring revenue base. While most licensing revenues are secured with contractual guarantees, investors should monitor renewal activity and digital content contributions in future periods.

Quarterly highlights

Direct-to-consumer sales, led by the Honey Birdette luxury lingerie line, continued to expand. In the period, this segment delivered a 15% year-over-year revenue increase, with strong full-price sales and a 57% gross margin, especially in the U.S. market.

Licensing revenue, which is highly predictable and provides high margins, fell by 5%. Management explained the decline was mainly due to a few contract expirations, but noted that some expired agreements may be replaced in coming quarters. About 90% of last year’s licensing revenue was backed by guarantees, and $333 million in unrecognized future revenue remains on the books.

On the financial side, adjusted EBITDA improved sharply, reaching $5.0 million from $2.4 million a year ago. The company reduced operating expenses by 9% year over year and paid down $15 million of senior debt, strengthening its balance sheet. Further proceeds from the China joint venture, totaling nearly $37 million through January 2028, are expected to support ongoing debt reduction plans.
