Sanmina Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments

Sanmina Corporation (NASDAQ:SANM) is a leading global provider of integrated manufacturing solutions and advanced engineering services for electronics and data infrastructure. Its recent strategic focus is defined by the acquisition and integration of ZT Systems, propelling expansion into high-growth cloud, artificial intelligence (AI), and hyperscaler data center markets. Sanmina is positioning itself as a mission-critical supplier for next-generation computing, while leveraging a global manufacturing footprint and robust capital allocation to drive long-term value.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Successful ZT Systems Integration Fast-Tracks Sanmina into the AI and Cloud Infrastructure Boom

ZT Systems is now the principal growth engine driving Sanmina’s transformation. In Q2 of fiscal 2026, revenue from Sanmina’s Integrated Manufacturing Solutions segment hit $3.58 billion, a 123.5% year-over-year increase, with ZT Systems contributing $1.88 billion. This acquisition has not only diversified Sanmina’s customer base, but it has also made the company a key hardware supplier for major hyperscalers deploying advanced AMD-based compute platforms. With phase one of the integration largely complete and production ready for next-generation accelerated compute, Sanmina is strategically positioned to capture a significant share of hyperscaler and AI cloud infrastructure spending, a segment expected to anchor industry growth through at least 2028.

2. Record Financial Performance Demonstrates Accelerated, Sustainable Profitability

Q2 2026 showcased a step change in both revenue and earnings growth, signaling robust operating leverage. Sanmina’s Q2 2026 topline soared 102% year over year to $4.0 billion, while non-GAAP diluted earnings per share more than doubled to $3.16, a 125% increase. Operating margin expanded 80 basis points to a non-GAAP operating margin of 6.4% and Non-GAAP free cash flow reached $342 million—remarkable figures for an electronics manufacturing services provider. Management raised its fiscal 2026 full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to $10.75–$11.35 and expects $16 billion-plus revenue in fiscal 2027, reflecting substantial confidence in the visibility and durability of this growth cycle. With non-GAAP pretax return on invested capital surging to 34.7%, Sanmina is demonstrating not only scale, but genuine economic value creation well above its cost of capital.

3. Expanding Engineering and Vertical Integration Unlocks Higher-Margin, Value-Added Services

Management expects that Sanmina’s transition from traditional contract manufacturing to value-add original design manufacturing (ODM) will raise its earnings potential. By leveraging ZT Systems’ portfolio and deepening its engineering capabilities, Sanmina is now investing in R&D for proprietary subassemblies, advanced liquid cooling, custom optical modules, and full system design. Management expects these higher-margin services to expand as the business grows with major hyperscaler and OEM customers. As this vertical integration ramps in fiscal 2027 and 2028, Sanmina stands to not only boost revenue, but also improve gross margin mix and reinforce the ‘stickiness’ of its AI/cloud customer relationships.

4. Disciplined Capital Allocation Enables Growth with Minimal Balance Sheet Risk

Sanmina maintains a strong financial position and alignment between investment, growth, and shareholder returns. In Q2 2026, the company generated $399 million in operating cash flow and ended the quarter with $1.58 billion in cash, no outstanding borrowings, and total available liquidity of about $3.7 billion. Leverage is low at 0.56 times EBITDA, leaving substantial headroom for future growth investments. Sanmina continues to prioritize internal investment, ROI-driven M&A, and significant share repurchases—including a new $600 million buyback authorization—demonstrating confidence in ongoing free cash flow and value creation without overextending its balance sheet.

5. Robust Multi-Year Pipeline in AI, Cloud, and Diversified End-Markets Sets Foundation for Long-Term Growth

While hyperscaler AI infrastructure drives current momentum, Sanmina remains diversified with strong demand across other strategic sectors. Communications networks, cloud, and AI infrastructure revenue reached $2.77 billion in Q2 2026 (up ~280% year over year), fueled by ZT, while legacy core revenues continued to grow at a healthy 6–22% pace. The book-to-bill ratio exceeded 1.1, and management referenced a strong multi-year pipeline in advanced compute and robust health in end-markets like industrial, medical, defense, and automotive. This broad base, together with proactive risk management and capacity expansion for the next generation of accelerated compute, increases Sanmina’s resilience and upside potential as cloud and AI adoption continue globally.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Customer Concentration and Hyperscaler Demand Volatility Expose Revenue to Sharp Swings

Heavy reliance on a limited number of hyperscaler customers poses significant concentration risk and order unpredictability for Sanmina. Much of the recent surge in revenue was driven by one major customer’s demand pull-in, and management indicated that Q3 2026 revenue will decline sequentially as a result. While pipelines and new customer engagements are expanding, the near-term trajectory of sales remains at the mercy of a few large customers’ capex cycles and deployment schedules. Any slowdown or pause in AI or cloud infrastructure investment by hyperscalers could lead to sudden revenue reversals, exposing Sanmina to periods of underutilization and margin pressure.

2. Supply Chain Disruptions and Inventory Build Heighten Execution and Margin Risks

Global shortages of memory, custom ASICs, and key electronic components continue to challenge Sanmina’s ability to meet demand and manage costs. Management acknowledged that both the Integrated Manufacturing Solutions and Components segments experienced delays and higher costs due to ongoing shortages, and that these headwinds may persist into at least 2027. The company has ramped inventory investment to $2.1 billion (up 75% year over year), driven by the ZT Systems acquisition. These supply chain risks, if not well controlled, could result in margin compression, working capital drag, and financial volatility.

3. Margin Compression Likely as Higher R&D and Lower-Margin Consignment Models Ramp Up

The shift toward more complex ODM solutions and consignment-based contracts could pressure profit margins until scale is achieved. While ZT Systems’ current margin profile is accretive, future earnings depend on a delicate mix of direct sales, consignment revenue recognition, and the profitable absorption of rising research and development investments. Management has cautioned that more R&D spending will be needed as next-generation platforms roll out, and that some new customer agreements may be lower margin, especially in high-volume cloud or AI programs. If growth in higher-value services lags or if pricing becomes more competitive, Sanmina’s profitability could fall short of recent highs, tempering the company’s ability to generate elevated returns for shareholders over time.
