Key Takeaways
The company delivered weaker results as revenue declined sharply amid continued margin pressures.
Adjusted EBITDA loss widened as operating expense reductions did not offset lower gross profit.
Management is prioritizing a consulting pipeline, but cash flow and revenue recovery remain central issues going forward.

HeartCore Enterprises (NASDAQ:HTCR) delivered a challenging quarter as steep revenue declines and thinner margins weighed on results. The business, a niche provider of IPO consulting and financial services for Asia-based firms seeking U.S. listings, reported its first quarter earnings on May 15, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed progress in building a strategic client pipeline but fell short financially due to ongoing pressure on sales and profitability.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell 40% year over year, driven by a steep drop in legacy software sales and lower gross profit from IPO consulting as a result of higher outsourcing costs.
Adjusted EBITDA loss widened 23%, reflecting that cost reductions in selling expenses were not enough to offset revenue and margin declines.
Management stated 16 active Go IPO clients, with six preparing for public registration, but this did not yet translate into material revenue growth during the period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management underlined its focus on expanding its consulting pipeline, targeting high-quality IPO candidates and preparing for regulatory qualification in Japan. However, with no quantitative guidance and continued investment requirements, near-term liquidity and the rate of client conversion remain key for investors to monitor.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue declined to $1.2 million for the first quarter ended March 31, 2026, as lower software and services volume, intense competition in the U.S. software market, and increased outsourcing fees in IPO consulting contributed to the decrease.

Operating expenses decreased from $1.7 million to $1.6 million in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the same period last year, with selling expenses dropping substantially, but these cuts did not offset the loss of gross profit. The adjusted EBITDA loss therefore deepened compared to the same period last year.

The period closed with $0.77 million in cash, little changed from a year before but down 61% from year-end, as HeartCore authorized a $2.0 million share repurchase program despite ongoing working capital pressures and uncertainty about proceeds receivable from discontinued operations.

What HeartCore Enterprises Does

HeartCore Enterprises provides IPO consulting services, helping Japanese and Asian companies navigate the process of listing on U.S. stock exchanges.
