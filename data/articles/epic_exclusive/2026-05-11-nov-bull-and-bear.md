NOV Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

NOV Inc. (NYSE:NOV) is a global leader in designing and manufacturing equipment and technology for oil and gas drilling, production, and processing, with an expansive footprint in offshore, subsea, and digital automation solutions. The company’s growth strategy is focused on leveraging strong technology positioning in high-barrier offshore markets, expanding capacity where industry demand is robust (notably in Brazil for subsea flexible pipes), and driving operational efficiency to deliver resilient margins and capital returns, even amid volatile commodity cycles and geopolitical disruptions.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Offshore Upcycle and Aging Infrastructure Drive Sustained Multi-Year Demand

A global offshore investment surge creates a powerful tailwind for NOV Inc. – The company is uniquely positioned to benefit from an emerging multi-year upcycle driven by global supply shortfalls and urgent reinvestment after decades of underinvestment and new conflict-driven disruptions. Management highlights that supply loss of around 10 million barrels per day due to Middle East conflict has shifted the market from surplus to deficit, forcing strategic reserve drawdowns and setting the stage for a significant, extended period of upstream reinvestment. This has direct implications: NOV’s substantial offshore product portfolio—especially flexible pipe with backlog now stretching into 2028—anchors its revenue visibility. An aging installed base further accelerates replacement cycles, amplifying order flow and project awards for years ahead.

2. Technology Leadership in Subsea Flexibles and Digital Solutions Enables Premium Pricing and Market Share Gains

Industry-leading subsea flexible pipe and digital products drive outsized growth relative to peers – NOV Inc. maintains a competitively advantaged portfolio, reporting record quarterly performance in its subsea flexible pipe business with over 100% book-to-bill for four consecutive years, deepening backlog and extending lead times. Proprietary technologies—such as new CO2-resistant subsea flexible pipe—differentiate NOV Inc.'s product offering. These products are essential for both new field developments and the crucial replacement cycle in regions like Brazil. In digital, market share gains—such as an 8% U.S. revenue rise in ReedHycalog drill bits even as rig count fell 7% year over year in Q1 2026—demonstrate the ability to outgrow end markets and capture wallet share through value-added innovation.

3. Strategic Capacity Expansion in Brazil Positions NOV to Capture an Outsized Share of Record Project Awards

Timely capital investment enables capture of growth as offshore Brazil becomes a global supply focal point – NOV’S approval of a $200 million expansion in its Brazil subsea flexible pipe facility directly addresses extended industry lead times and projected capacity gaps. With backlog already reaching into 2028 and customer projects ramping, this step ensures NOV can fulfill high-margin orders others may be unable to meet. The expansion underpins leadership in the world’s fastest-growing offshore market and provides exposure not just to greenfield projects but to vital infrastructure replacement as installed assets age. As additional FPSOs (floating production, storage, and offloading units) reach final investment decision, NOV is pre-positioned to convert pipeline opportunities into future revenue and earnings.

4. Operational Restructuring and Cost-Out Programs Set Stage for Margin Expansion

Efficiency initiatives drive structural improvement in earnings power as external cost headwinds ease – The company has aggressively cut costs, including an 8% reduction in global headcount and closure of over 40 facilities since 2025, while streamlining shared services and IT infrastructure. Although short-term savings have been eclipsed by elevated tariffs and logistics inflation, management expects the full benefit to emerge beginning in 2026 as cost reductions ramp and temporary headwinds fade. When disruptions subside and demand returns, improved operating leverage and additional cost take-outs should support a margin rebound, enabling more significant free cash flow conversion even at modest revenue growth rates.

5. Robust Balance Sheet and Capital Return Commitment Offer Downside Support and Shareholder Yield

Strong liquidity and disciplined capital allocation reduce financial risk while rewarding investors – NOV Inc. extended its $1.5 billion revolver to 2030, maintaining ample liquidity to weather volatility. Consistent with prior years, the company returned over $900 million to shareholders across dividends and buybacks in the past eight quarters, including a 20% dividend hike in Q1 2026 and plans for a supplemental payout. This disciplined return of at least 50% of excess free cash flow signals confidence and provides a tangible yield, helping to cushion valuation even during temporary periods of operating stress. The pursuit of a significant tariff refund could deliver additional upside if realized.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Continued Geopolitical and Supply Chain Turbulence Erode Bookings and Backlog

Persistent disruption in key energy regions threatens project delivery and converts deferrals to cancellations – The prolonged conflict in the Middle East caused Q1 2026 revenue to fall 2.4% year over year and bookings to slip to $520 million (a book-to-bill of 0.8x), the lowest needed to replenish backlog. With project logistics constrained and key straits closed, revenue and EBITDA impacts ($54 million and $32 million, respectively) point to the fragility of the order book. If instability endures, deferred orders may not resume, resulting in further backlog erosion and underutilization in critical segments such as process equipment and offshore flexibles, ultimately putting the company’s multi-year earnings visibility at risk.

2. Margin Compression Outpaces Internal Cost Savings Amid Inflation and Tariff Pressures

Failing to offset external cost surges results in weaker profitability and threatens free cash flow conversion – NOV’s adjusted operating margin slipped to 4% in Q1 2026, with adjusted EBITDA margin just 9%, due to a $30 million tariff burden, inflated freight, and adverse sales mix. The company’s restructuring initiatives have not yet been able to overcome these pressures, and ongoing cost pass-through challenges, particularly if capital equipment pricing remains competitive, could further erode profitability. Prolonged cost inflation, especially in raw materials and logistics, may not abate quickly, leaving the company exposed to additional margin downside and constrained operating cash flow.

3. Weakening Order Momentum and Project Deferrals Undermine Growth Narrative

Sub-1.0 book-to-bill signals a risk of declining future revenues and reduced visibility – The Q1 2026 book-to-bill ratio of 0.8x raises a caution flag, indicating that new orders are not keeping pace with deliveries. Despite management’s optimism for a full-year rebound, repeated underperformance or further macro shocks could convert deferred orders into cancellations, especially if offshore operators pause spending decisions. Share price support depends critically on the narrative of sustained backlog and order intake. Any protracted period of order softness or major project cancellations would call into question the company’s near- and longer-term growth trajectory, directly impacting investor confidence.
