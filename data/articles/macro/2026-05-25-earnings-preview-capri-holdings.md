Earnings Preview: Capri Holdings

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Capri Holdings (NYSE:CPRI), a global designer and retailer of luxury accessories and footwear, is expected to report its fourth-quarter and full-year fiscal 2026 results on Wednesday, May 27, 2026, before the market opens.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, Capri Holdings reported revenue of $1.025 billion, a decrease of 4% year over year, and diluted earnings per share of $0.81. Both revenue and earnings exceeded analyst expectations for the quarter. The company’s results reflect continued execution of strategic initiatives at its two main brands, Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo, following the completed sale of Versace in December 2025. Michael Kors saw revenue decline 5.6% to $858 million, mainly due to reduced promotional activity and ongoing repositioning, while Jimmy Choo posted a 5% revenue increase to $167 million, driven by strong retail and wholesale performance. Underlying gross margin improved by 70 basis points, but reported gross margin fell to 60.8% due to higher U.S. import tariffs. Operating expenses were lower year over year, and net debt was sharply reduced to $80 million after applying Versace sale proceeds.

Positives
Revenue and earnings exceeded consensus estimates: The company’s top and bottom lines both came in above what analysts expected, signaling better-than-feared demand and cost control.
Jimmy Choo delivered revenue growth and margin improvement: The brand saw a 5% revenue increase and a positive swing in operating margin, reflecting successful product launches and marketing campaigns.
Gross margin improved on an underlying basis: Excluding the impact of tariffs, underlying gross margin expanded by 70 basis points, showing benefits from higher full-price sell-throughs and reduced discounting.
Debt reduction following Versace sale: Net debt dropped to $80 million from $1.6 billion, giving Capri more financial flexibility for future investments and share repurchases.
Negatives
Michael Kors revenue declined: Sales fell 5.6% year over year, mainly due to intentional reductions in promotions and ongoing repositioning, which continued to pressure results in the near term.
Gross margin compressed due to tariffs: Reported gross margin decreased by 230 basis points as new U.S. tariffs on imports from key manufacturing countries raised costs.
Store closures and restructuring costs weighed on results: The company continued to close underperforming stores and recorded $12 million in restructuring expenses, reflecting ongoing network optimization.
Asia and Americas remained weak for both brands: Revenue in the Americas fell 7% and in Asia 4%, with Michael Kors particularly soft in these regions despite some sequential improvement.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report
Progress on Michael Kors brand repositioning and new product sell-through: Investors should monitor updates on the Jet Set brand strategy, full-price sales trends, and the ongoing shift in pricing and promotional activity.
Impact of U.S. import tariffs and cost-mitigation efforts: Watch for commentary on how Capri is offsetting higher tariff costs through sourcing changes, price increases, or other margin initiatives.
Jimmy Choo’s retail and wholesale momentum: Look for continued growth in accessories and casual footwear as well as updates on geographic performance, particularly in the Americas and Asia.
Store renovation program and capital allocation: The company is in the early stages of a three-year plan to renovate about half its store fleet. Updates on renovation progress and any early impact on traffic and sales will be important, as will details on the timing of the new $1 billion share-repurchase program (set to begin in fiscal 2027).
Full-year guidance and outlook for fiscal 2027: Investors should expect management to update on the path toward returning to revenue and earnings growth next year, including any changes to expectations for gross margin expansion, expense discipline, and regional trends.
