Monro Posts 7% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q4

Key Takeaways
Monro’s fiscal fourth quarter showed mixed results, as operational improvements partially offset weaker sales and customer demand.
Earnings per share on an adjusted basis declined year over year while revenue also fell.
Management declined to provide an outlook for fiscal 2027, citing uncertainty.

Auto service chain Monro (NASDAQ:MNRO) made progress in controlling costs and improving margins, but the benefits were dampened by lower sales and persistent customer demand weakness. The company, one of the largest providers of automotive maintenance, tire, and repair services in the U.S, reported its results for the quarter ended March 28, 2026, on May 27, 2026. Overall, Monro delivered some structural cost wins, yet faced top-line pressure and an uncertain path ahead.

Notable Developments
Revenue for the quarter declined 7.2%, primarily due to the closure of 145 underperforming stores and a 2.4% decrease in comparable store sales.
Gross margin improved by 0.9 percentage points to 33.9%, reflecting gains from lower technician labor costs relative to sales.
Inventory at the end of the quarter was $155.3 million, down 14% from the prior year, highlighting continued inventory optimization.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide financial guidance for fiscal 2027, citing lingering unpredictability in demand and category performance. It signaled continued focus on cost control and operational efficiency. Investors should monitor core same-store sales trends and the performance of key service categories, as improvement here will be critical for future revenue gains.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue fell, with the majority of the decline tied to the large reduction in store count and weaker same-store sales, especially in service categories such as brakes and tires. Management had previously aimed for modest year-over-year growth in comparable store sales but ended with a 2.4% decline in the quarter.

Despite sales pressure, Monro expanded its gross margin to 33.9%, driven mainly by lowering technician labor costs as a proportion of sales. Operating expenses also fell sharply due to fewer stores and lower impairment costs, though increased spending on marketing and consulting partly offset these savings.

The company reduced its inventory to $155.3 million by quarter end, a $26.2 million drop from the prior year. Store closures and improved real estate management helped with this effort, and the lower inventory level reflects ongoing supply chain improvements.

What Monro Does

Monro operates a nationwide network of automotive service stores providing tire sales, oil changes, brake repair, alignments, and other maintenance services.
