Acadia Realty Trust (NYSE:AKR) posted a strong quarter, with improved leasing activity and higher core profits. The company is a real estate investment trust focused on prime street and open-air retail properties in major U.S. urban and suburban markets. In its earnings release for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, published on May 13, 2026, management reported better rental spreads and higher occupancy, while noting a slight increase in leverage and payout ratios. Notable developments
Management raised its 2026 guidance, now targeting $1.22–$1.26 per share in funds from operations as adjusted and $0.37–$0.39 in earnings per share for the year. This signals increased confidence in profit growth for the rest of 2026.
The company’s REIT portfolio grew income 5.9% year over year, with leasing spreads on new street retail leases coming in at 49.9% on a GAAP basis and 30.6% in cash terms.
Acquisitions and recapitalizations totaled over $1 billion in the quarter, as the investment management platform added new properties in key cities. The company sold $497 million in properties while deploying new capital in high-demand retail corridors.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management slightly raised its full-year guidance, expecting higher funds from operations and earnings per share, with much of the anticipated growth coming from improved rental income and ongoing development projects. The key factor to watch in coming quarters will be how the company manages its balance sheet, as leverage and payout ratios have ticked higher with increased external investing activity.

Quarterly highlights

The REIT’s street and urban portfolio saw a jump in leasing activity, with the percent leased climbing to 93.1%, up from 90.8% a year ago. New leases rolled at much higher market rates, driving higher overall portfolio income and setting the stage for future growth. Despite gains in revenue and operating income, overall leased occupancy for the entire REIT portfolio edged slightly lower to 95.3%, down 0.2 percentage points.

The investment management business contributed more this quarter, with fee income rising and net operating income from this segment growing 38% year over year. The company’s acquisitions outpaced property sales, reflecting higher levels of new investment and active capital cycling into prime retail corridors.

Increased leverage, with net debt to adjusted EBITDA at 5.5 times, and a higher FFO payout ratio of 77% (up from 62% last year), will be important to monitor as both could affect financial flexibility should internal growth moderate.
