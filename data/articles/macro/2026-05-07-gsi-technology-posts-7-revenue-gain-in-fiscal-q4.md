Key Takeaways
GSI Technology’s government contract wins and a robust cash position were offset by wider losses and higher costs.
Revenue rose year over year in the quarter.
Management expects stable revenue and margins next period but cautions that design wins are still pending for its new AI products.

Government contract momentum and a strengthened balance sheet could not fully counterbalance higher operating losses for the quarter. GSI Technology (NASDAQ:GSIT), an edge artificial intelligence and high-speed memory provider, reported its fiscal fourth-quarter earnings on May 7, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31, 2026. Overall, the quarter combined steady sales growth and sizable cash reserves with operating cost pressures and lingering commercialization uncertainty for its new technologies.

Notable developments
Operating expenses rose significantly to $8.5 million, mainly due to higher research and development costs for next-generation chips.
Revenue increased 7.4% to $6.3 million, driven by ongoing demand for high-speed SRAM products, while military and defense shipments made up nearly half of sales.
Cash on hand jumped to $67.2 million after a capital raise, supporting continued investment in edge AI technologies.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects first-quarter fiscal 2027 revenue between $5.9 million and $6.7 million, with gross margin in the 54% to 56% range. Leadership emphasized progress in government-funded projects and early commercial interest but noted that design wins for new AI processors are pending. Investors should watch for initial revenue from these AI solutions, which management identifies as crucial for future growth.

Quarterly highlights

Operating expenses climbed by over 50% from the prior year, reflecting increased research and development spending on the Plato and Gemini-II application processing units—advanced processors designed for artificial intelligence and edge computing uses. These projects received partial funding from U.S. government Small Business Innovation Research grants, which provide support for early-stage research and development but do not guarantee future product orders.

The SRAM (static random-access memory) business continued to deliver stable revenue and supported gross margin. Sales to military and defense customers rose to 45.7% of total shipments, even as contributions from large commercial customers such as Cadence Design Systems and Nokia declined sharply during the period.

The cash position strengthened to $67.2 million at quarter end, largely from a $46.9 million capital raise in October 2025. This cash reserve gives management flexibility to invest in product development and withstand ongoing operating losses, which widened compared to the prior year and reflect the high upfront costs of bringing new AI technologies to market.

What GSI Technology does

GSI Technology develops high-speed memory chips and specialized processors for applications in artificial intelligence, telecommunications, and defense.
