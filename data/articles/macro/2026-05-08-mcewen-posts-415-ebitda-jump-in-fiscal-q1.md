Gold miner McEwen (NYSE:MUX) kicked off fiscal 2026 with a sharp return to profitability, thanks to surging gold prices and expanded production from its core mines. The company is a precious metals producer with operations in the United States, Canada, Argentina, and Mexico, focused on gold, silver, and copper projects. Notable Developments
Revenue more than doubled year over year, boosted by higher average realized gold prices and increased production volumes.
The San José mine in Argentina paid an $8.8 million dividend and achieved lower unit production costs, supporting McEwen's self-funding strategy.
Costs at the Gold Bar and Fox Complex remained at or above the upper end of guidance, with ongoing development driving temporary cost pressure.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year production target of 114,000 to 126,000 gold-equivalent ounces, with cost guidance unchanged. The success of several development-stage projects and the potential for higher or lower metal prices will be key as the year progresses. Investors should keep a close eye on operating costs at key mines and further updates on project timelines, especially at Stock, Grey Fox, and Los Azules.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure more than doubled compared to the prior year, supported by a 71% jump in the average price received per ounce of gold sold and a 26% increase in total production. This was driven by strong contributions from the San José mine, which ramped up output by 33% and delivered a material dividend that boosted McEwen's liquidity.

Operationally, the company encountered higher cash costs at both Gold Bar and the Fox Complex, with management indicating these increases relate mainly to lower grades and strategic development work expected to support future production. These costs exceeded full-year AISC guidance at both assets and reached the top end of full-year cash cost guidance, highlighting a need for discipline in execution and ongoing efficiency improvements.

The company advanced major growth projects on schedule, including development at the Stock underground mine and resource expansion at Grey Fox and Tartan. The Los Azules copper project reached milestones in feasibility and permitting, positioning it for a final investment decision later in the year and potential production by 2030.
