Babcock Wilcox: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 28, 2026, 10:36 AM

Comments

Babcock & Wilcox Enterprises (NYSE:BW) is an engineering and technology company specializing in power generation, renewable energy, and environmental technologies. The company’s growth strategy centers on leveraging its traditional expertise in industrial boilers and emissions solutions to capture surging demand from AI data centers, utilities, and customers pursuing decarbonization. With a diversified commercial portfolio and a strong push into infrastructure for next-generation data workloads, Babcock & Wilcox aims to transform itself from a steady legacy supplier into a major beneficiary of global electrification and energy transition trends.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Explosive Pipeline Growth Driven by AI Data Center Demand Creates Multi-Year Revenue Visibility

Babcock & Wilcox has reported a historic surge in bookings and backlog, signaling a new revenue trajectory. In the first quarter of 2026, the company announced bookings of $2.5 billion — a 1,900% increase year over year — while its backlog soared to $2.7 billion, up 483% from the prior year. The project pipeline ballooned by 17% during the quarter, reaching over $14 billion, with more than $2 billion directly tied to hyperscaler and utility-led AI data center projects. This unprecedented pipeline expansion is a response to secular growth from high-power AI data centers, giving the company a clear runway for long-term revenue conversion and supporting investor confidence in the business’s growth durability.

2. Substantial Operating Leverage Evidenced by Outperformance in Adjusted EBITDA and Rapid Debt Paydown

Recent financial performance demonstrates Babcock & Wilcox’s ability to convert growth into profitability and improve its balance sheet. Adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA), a non-generally accepted accounting principles metric, surged to $16.1 million in Q1 2026 — a 296% increase over the $4 million figure from Q1 2025. Positive adjusted net income from continuing operations was delivered despite large non-cash charges, with the business reporting net debt of just $42.4 million, less than 1x trailing twelve-month adjusted EBITDA as of March 2026. This trajectory, supported by reduced secured and unsecured debt by 87% in one quarter, gives the company ample capacity to pursue new opportunities, fund projects without dilution, and withstand market cycles.

3. AI and Grid Electrification Trends Position the Company as a Solutions Leader for “Next Gen” Infrastructure

The transformation of digital infrastructure requirements places Babcock & Wilcox at the nexus of urgent utility and data center investments. The company’s expertise in rapidly deployable steam and power systems directly addresses the gigawatt-scale challenges faced by data center operators and utilities supporting the AI revolution. By offering flexible, future-proof solutions — such as super-heated steam boilers with optional combined-cycle integration — the business appeals to customers seeking robust, scalable, and efficient power delivery for AI and cloud facilities. This positions Babcock & Wilcox as a preferred partner for both short-term “quick build” projects and longer-term grid modernization programs, aligning it with the world’s most prominent technology and infrastructure spenders.

4. Recurring Parts and Services Revenue Provides Stability Through Industry Cycles

Babcock & Wilcox’s legacy parts and services business is demonstrating renewed strength, mitigating reliance on unpredictable project work. The company posted its strongest first quarter in parts and services in recent history, driven by higher utilization and greater capital investment from utilities extending the life of coal plants amid elevated natural gas prices. This segment is expected to maintain momentum throughout 2026, with typical seasonality providing additional upside in the back half of the year as maintenance and outage-related work intensifies. Recurring aftermarket revenue not only softens the impact of project timing variability, but also deepens long-term customer relationships and sticks with the business during periods of industry or macro uncertainty.

5. Leadership in Energy Transition Technologies Enhances Long-Term Competitive Position

Investment in decarbonization platforms, including innovations like BrightLoop for hydrogen and carbon capture, creates future growth vectors beyond conventional power. As the regulatory environment tightens and industrial customers seek greener solutions, Babcock & Wilcox’s environmental segment is positioned to capture new spending for emissions controls, fuel conversions, and zero-carbon process upgrades. Projects such as the Massillon, Ohio BrightLoop demonstration point to leadership in commercializing low-cost hydrogen and advanced carbon management. This dual capability — serving both near-term reliability needs and long-term sustainability imperatives — broadens the company’s competitive moat and makes it an essential partner in a shifting global energy landscape.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Class Action Lawsuits and Allegations of Pipeline Overstatement Threaten Credibility and Future Contracting

Emerging legal challenges raise material concerns regarding management accountability and the quality of future earnings. In May 2026, multiple shareholder class actions were filed alleging that the company’s executives overstated the size, likelihood, and timing of large contracts, including a much-publicized $2.4 billion project, and failed to disclose associated risks. If these claims are substantiated, consequences could include reputational damage, regulatory penalties, leadership turnover, and impaired contractor or customer relationships. The mere perception of weak internal controls and inflated pipeline figures could erode trust, impact valuation, and undermine the business’s ability to compete for additional high-profile projects.

2. Execution Risk on Mega-Projects Could Erode Margins and Delay Revenue Realization

Transitioning to complex, multi-year data center and grid projects exposes Babcock & Wilcox to heightened delivery and cost risks. As the company ramps up gigawatt-class projects for AI infrastructure and utilities, its ability to convert bookings and pipeline into timely, profitable revenue becomes a key uncertainty. Management’s own disclosures highlight the importance of securing steam turbine and pressure part capacity as order volumes grow. Any material missteps in supply chain, project timing, or cost management, especially with many projects scheduled for ramp-up in 2027–2028, could result in margin compression, overruns, or push-outs, derailing the expected financial payoff from the pipeline surge and inviting further investor skepticism.
