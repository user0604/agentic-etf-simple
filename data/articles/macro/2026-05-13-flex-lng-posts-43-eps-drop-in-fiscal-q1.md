Key Takeaways
Flex LNG reported a soft quarter as vessel availability and spot market exposure reduced earnings.
Adjusted earnings per share declined year over year.
Management raised its full-year 2026 financial guidance, citing stronger charter coverage.

Flex LNG (NYSE:FLNG) came through a seasonally weak period as scheduled vessel drydockings and soft spot market rates weighed on its results. The liquefied natural gas (LNG) shipper owns and operates a fleet of modern LNG carriers, transporting cargoes worldwide under a mix of long-term and short-term contracts. It published first quarter results on May 13, 2026, reporting on performance for the three months ended March 31.

While challenges were clear this quarter, the company responded quickly to changing market conditions with new and extended charter agreements and has now raised its guidance for the rest of fiscal 2026. Overall, the quarter showed lower shipping revenues but much stronger visibility for the year ahead.

Notable Developments
Vessel operating revenues fell to $80.5 million, impacted by two ships in scheduled drydock and a soft spot market during early quarter.
Contract backlog increased, with 91.2% of available vessel days covered for the remainder of 2026 after new multi-year charters were secured.
The quarterly dividend of $0.75 per share was maintained, marking the 19th consecutive payout since early 2021.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now expects full-year 2026 revenue of $345–370 million and higher average charter rates, following strong contract signings after LNG shipping rates surged late in the quarter. The key factor for investors to monitor is the company’s progress in maintaining high vessel employment as existing charters roll off later in the year and into 2027.

Quarterly Highlights

The period saw vessel revenues drop as two ships underwent scheduled drydock maintenance and others faced weak spot shipping rates early on. While these factors pressured results, offhire days were anticipated and are regular for maintaining a modern fleet.

Flex LNG secured several multi-year charters in the quarter, including a new time charter for the Flex Aurora and two contract extensions, increasing revenue visibility. At quarter end, firm contract backlog stood at 54 vessel-years, rising to 81 years if charterers exercise extension options.

Despite the softer financial performance, the company maintained its $0.75 per share dividend by using accumulated prior earnings. Operating cash generation in the period was lower at $9.7 million but supported by a cash balance of $389.1 million.
