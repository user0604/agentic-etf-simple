Versant Posts 7% EBITDA Drop in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Versant posted a mixed quarter as progress in digital platforms was offset by declines in traditional TV segments.
Adjusted EBITDA fell year over year.
Management reaffirmed focus on digital expansion and continued capital returns, broadly aligning with previous full-year guidance.

Versant (NASDAQ:VSNT) reported a mixed start to its first quarter as an independent public company, with digital platform growth offset by declines in traditional TV and advertising. The leading multiplatform media company, known for brands like MS NOW, CNBC, and Golf Channel, released results on May 14, 2026, for the three months ended March 31. The period showed momentum in digital initiatives but ongoing headwinds in legacy segments.

Notable developments
Platforms revenue increased 9.5% to $192 million, driven by growth at Fandango and GolfNow.
Adjusted EBITDA declined 7.0% as lower TV and advertising revenue outweighed digital gains.
Versant returned $100 million to shareholders through buybacks and declared a $0.375 per share quarterly dividend.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its focus on accelerating digital and platform growth, with continued investment in technology and content. No update was provided to full-year guidance, but results remain broadly in line with previous targets for revenue and adjusted EBITDA. Investors should watch how quickly Versant can shift its revenue mix away from declining linear and advertising segments.

Quarterly highlights

The Platforms segment, which includes digital services like GolfNow and Fandango, outperformed other areas with strong consumer engagement and new product launches, such as the integration of INDY Cinema into Fandango. This segment's growth helped offset some of the declines in legacy businesses.

Traditional TV distribution and advertising both declined during the quarter, reflecting ongoing industry trends such as cord-cutting and lower ratings. Content licensing and other revenue surged, mainly due to the timing of a large agreement for titles including Keeping Up with the Kardashians.

Profitability was pressured by higher costs related to Versant's transition to independence, but free cash flow remained robust, supporting continued share buybacks and dividend payments.

What Versant does

Versant operates a portfolio of television networks, digital platforms, and content licensing businesses that reach over 50 million viewers weekly across news, sports, and entertainment.
