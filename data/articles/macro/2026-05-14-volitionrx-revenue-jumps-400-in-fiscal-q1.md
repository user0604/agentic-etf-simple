VolitionRx (NYSEMKT:VNRX) delivered a strong business win this quarter, with revenue surging from a low base. The healthcare diagnostics company, which develops blood-based cancer and disease detection tests using its Nucleosomics platform, reported first-quarter fiscal 2026 results on May 14, 2026. Overall, the period showed breakthrough gains in product development and revenue, but recurring sales remain slow and financial risk persists.

Notable developments
Revenue rose fivefold compared to the same quarter last year.
The pending Nu.Q Vet feline cancer test publication could trigger a $5 million contractual milestone payment.
The company raised $5.4 million through equity sales and $1.9 million from convertible debt to fund operations.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not issue financial guidance for the year, repeating prior comments about the unpredictable nature of revenue. The focus remains on reaching clinical and commercial milestones, such as licensing agreements and regulatory progress for the Nu.Q product family. The key factor to watch is whether milestone-driven events, like the Nu.Q Vet feline assay publication or France lung cancer reimbursement, convert into recurring revenue and reduce reliance on external funding.

Quarterly highlights

The $1.0 million revenue figure for the quarter marks substantial growth, primarily tied to milestone contracts rather than broad-based clinical adoption. Management reported active commercial discussions with over a dozen diagnostics and liquid biopsy companies, but no new formal licensing deals closed during the period.

Nu.Q Vet advanced with a feline cancer test manuscript submission, which, once published, will unlock a $5 million payment outlined in its contracts. Nu.Q Lung Cancer’s reimbursement process is underway in France through partnership with the Hospices Civils de Lyon, and approval would be the final step before routine clinical use in that market.

Net cash used in operations reached $5.3 million for the quarter. The company relied on $5.4 million from at-the-market equity offerings, $1.9 million in convertible notes, and $1.0 million in non-dilutive government funding from the Walloon Region, reflecting ongoing concern about liquidity and dilution while awaiting revenue from product adoption and licensing.

What VolitionRx does

VolitionRx develops and commercializes blood-based diagnostic tests for early detection of cancer and other diseases, applying its Nucleosomics technology in both human and veterinary health markets.
