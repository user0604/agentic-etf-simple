Accendra Health Posts 39% EBITDA Drop in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
The quarter reflected continuing revenue and profit pressures as the company managed through a major payer contract loss.
Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) declined 39.2% year over year.
Full-year guidance for net revenue and adjusted EBITDA was reaffirmed, with improvement expected in the second half.

Home care provider Accendra Health (NYSE:ACH) reported further financial pressure in its most recent quarter, reflecting the ongoing impact of a large commercial payer contract loss and still-slow expense reductions. The company focuses on delivering products and services for home-based healthcare, including medical supplies for diabetes, sleep therapy, and wound care. The first quarter 2026 results, released on May 11, 2026 (for the period ended March 31), were broadly in line with what management had signaled, but underscored ongoing challenges with profitability and cash flow. Overall, the quarter showed that the transformation to a home-based care specialist is well underway, but improving margins and cash generation remains a work in progress.

Notable Developments
Net revenue declined 6.8% year over year, mainly due to the anticipated loss of a large commercial payer contract.
Adjusted EBITDA dropped by nearly 40%, outpacing the decline in revenue and highlighting margin pressures even as cost reductions trailed revenue declines.
The company progressed with a $1.5 billion refinancing plan to reset its capital structure, ending the quarter with $336.9 million in cash on hand.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance for net revenue of $2.55–$2.65 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $335–$355 million, emphasizing that about 60% of adjusted EBITDA is forecast for the second half. It pointed to ongoing efforts to boost cost reductions and replace lost revenue through new contracts. The most important factor to monitor in upcoming quarters will be evidence of improved profitability and the delivery of stronger free cash flow as promised in guidance.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s financial performance reflected a sharp decline in revenue following a previously disclosed commercial payer loss. This drop in revenue, which management had anticipated, led to weaker profitability and a negative free cash flow result for the quarter.

Operating margins fell as cost savings were not able to fully offset the rate of revenue reduction. Selling, general, and administrative expenses decreased by only about 2.7%, compared to the 6.8% decrease in revenue, signaling that expense takeout measures are still catching up.

On a positive note, the company advanced a major refinancing plan, obtaining commitments to address $1.5 billion of debt and extend its revolving credit facility. Period-end liquidity improved, with cash and equivalents rising to $336.9 million from $59.4 million a year earlier.

What Accendra Health Does

The company provides home-based healthcare products and services, supplying medical equipment and supplies for conditions such as diabetes, sleep disorders, and wound care.
