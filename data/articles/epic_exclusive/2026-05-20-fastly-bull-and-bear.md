Fastly Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 20, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments

Fastly, Inc. (NASDAQ:FSLY) operates a developer-centric edge cloud platform used by enterprises to deliver web and application content, APIs, and security solutions at speed and scale. The company’s growth strategy centers on shifting its revenue mix toward higher-margin security and compute offerings, capturing surging demand from artificial intelligence and agent-based workloads, and expanding globally, especially across Asia-Pacific and Japan, while maintaining an efficient, programmable infrastructure to differentiate against legacy competitors.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Rapid Growth in Security and Compute Segments Accelerates Margin Expansion

Security and compute products are driving both higher growth and profitability, reshaping the company's long-term earnings power. In the first quarter of 2026, Security revenues grew 47% year over year to $38.8 million, now making up 22% of total revenue, while Compute/Edge posted 67% growth, fueled by AI and agentic customer adoption. These rapidly expanding segments contributed to a record 65.1% non-GAAP gross margin, up 1190 basis points year over year, and accounted for over half of incremental revenue. As their share of the revenue mix expands, these high-margin products provide structural tailwinds for operating efficiency and sustainable profit growth, supporting management’s guidance for full-year gross margins of 64% and operating margins of 9% in 2026.

2. Multi-Year Contract Commitments and Net Retention Improvements Boost Revenue Visibility

Multi-year deals and enterprise customer stickiness provide foundation for ongoing revenue growth and resilience. The company finished Q1 2026 with 634 customers generating over $100,000 in annualized revenue and recorded a net retention rate (NRR) of 113%, up from 100% a year ago. Remaining Performance Obligations (RPO) grew 63% year over year to $369 million, of which 75% is scheduled for recognition within twelve months, highlighting strong forward demand. This commitment to multi-year contracts reduces volatility risk and positions the business to weather macro fluctuations, supporting an outlook of 15% revenue growth in 2026 at the midpoint of guidance.

3. Developer-Centric, Programmable Platform Enables Fast Innovation and Wins in AI Workloads

The company’s programmable, developer-first edge network is gaining recognition and adoption in fast-evolving markets, particularly in artificial intelligence. Its architecture supports rapid product innovation, co-development with enterprise partners, and integration with modern AI use cases at the edge, such as distributed inference. Recent product launches like API discovery and ContentGuard address new customer pain points, and Fastly was named one of only two leaders in the 2026 Forrester Wave for Edge Development Platforms, receiving a perfect innovation score. This platform advantage enables adaptation to changing technology trends and facilitates growth in new verticals and emerging workloads that legacy competitors struggle to serve efficiently.

4. Capital Structure and Liquidity Support Aggressive Investment Amid Supply Chain Shocks

Fastly maintains ample liquidity and manageable debt alongside proactive infrastructure investment to capture future demand surges. The company ended Q1 2026 with $330 million in cash and investments following a $39 million repayment of maturing debt. Management’s approach to front-loading capital expenditures—guiding to 10–12% of revenue for 2026, compared to 5% in 2025—allowed the company to secure key hardware ahead of expected memory price inflations, helping to mitigate risk and maintain platform efficiency. With convertible debt maturities not arriving until 2028 and 2030, and free cash flow guidance of $40–50 million for 2026, the current financial position enables Fastly to sustain innovation and expansion without near-term refinancing needs or liquidity pressure.

5. Go-to-Market Transformation and International Expansion Broaden Customer Base

Accelerated enterprise wins, rising mid-market adoption, and global outreach are expanding the addressable market. The company’s go-to-market strategy includes focused international growth, evidenced by new offices and partnerships in Asia Pacific and Japan and by adding Joan Jenkins as Chief Marketing Officer to improve brand awareness and AI-focused messaging. Q1 2026 saw sizeable new logo wins (multiple seven-figure contracts) and a broader upsell/cross-sell motion across security and compute products, driving customer value beyond legacy content delivery. The shift from a solely developer audience to a broader enterprise buying center, along with growing stickiness and diversification, underpins a robust growth outlook beyond the maturing core CDN market.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Core Network Services Segment Faces Ongoing Price Pressure and Softer Demand

Structural commoditization in the legacy content delivery network business threatens overall growth and profitability. Despite record results in higher-margin segments, Network Services—still 73% of Q1 2026 revenue—grew just 11% year over year as it faces mid-single-digit price erosion and declining market expansion. Industry peers have begun raising prices, but Fastly has opted to honor existing long-term customer contracts to maintain trust, limiting margin relief. If pricing pressure deepens or usage growth slows, the company’s ability to offset these headwinds with Security and Compute may be challenged, especially while those segments are still ramping in overall scale.

2. Free Cash Flow Volatility and CapEx Escalation Create Execution and Profitability Risks

Heavy ramp in capital expenditures exposes Fastly to swings in hardware pricing and potential future liquidity strains. In Q1 2026, free cash flow fell to $4.1 million from $8.2 million the year prior, mainly due to an $18.4 million increase in infrastructure spend to meet anticipated demand and protect against supply chain constraints. Full-year CapEx is guided to 10–12% of revenue, with further increases possible if growth outpaces projections. If memory or hardware prices spike unexpectedly or if demand softens, these fixed investments may compress cash flow and force reductions in innovation or operating flexibility, despite the current healthy liquidity position.

3. Rapid Expansion in AI and Edge Compute Segments Faces Scaling and Adoption Risks

The company’s bet on AI, edge compute, and security for future growth will require broad market adoption and flawless execution. While Compute/Edge revenues surged 67% year over year to $8 million in Q1 2026, this still represents about 5% of total revenue, and the AI/agentic workload market remains nascent. Realizing enterprise-scale opportunity will depend on sustained customer adoption, ongoing competitive differentiation, and timely execution across new product launches and design partnerships. Any misstep—such as delayed feature integration, lack of enterprise traction, or competitive leapfrogging—could limit the ability to offset maturing legacy segments, making sustained high growth rates and margin improvement more difficult to achieve in the coming years.
