Key Takeaways
IHS Holding marked a transition quarter as it advanced portfolio reshaping alongside the announcement of an MTN Group acquisition.
Revenue rose year over year, with a 6.0% increase to $415.4 million in the quarter.
Management outlook remains focused on asset disposals, cost control, and closing the MTN merger.

IHS Holding (NYSE:IHS) experienced a pivotal period marked by major asset disposals and a pending ownership change. The leading independent telecommunications tower operator in Africa and select international markets issued its quarterly report on May 12, 2026, covering financial results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Despite headline improvements, core growth remained subdued, with cost pressures and one-time items shaping the latest quarter.

Overall, the quarter reflected stable operations, but also signaled ongoing challenges and a company in the midst of strategic transition.

Notable Developments
Revenue rose 6.0%, but the gain was driven mainly by foreign exchange translation, while organic revenue declined 1.7%.
Operating income fell 32.9% from the prior-year quarter, mainly due to higher administrative costs, including one-off merger expenses.
The company completed key asset disposals in Latin America and is advancing toward a buyout by MTN Group at $8.50 per share.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management is prioritizing completion of Latin America asset sales and the proposed MTN Group merger. No specific financial guidance was given for future quarters, reflecting the transitional focus. Investors should watch for updates on the merger process, especially regulatory approvals and the timing of asset sale proceeds.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue increased year over year, reaching $415.4 million, although nearly all growth stemmed from currency effects rather than underlying business expansion. Organic revenue slipped, with management citing the impact of currency resets and lower power-related indexation, particularly in Nigeria.

The company’s profit from ongoing operations benefited from a swing in net finance income of $80.7 million, thanks to unrealized gains from the appreciating Nigerian Naira. However, operating income fell sharply as administrative expenses jumped $68.0 million, driven by merger-related charges and one-time staff costs.

IHS completed its Brazil fiber disposal in May and remains on track to exit its Latin America tower assets, as part of a move to refocus on Africa. Its total tower count declined 4% year over year, primarily due to these exits and rationalization efforts, while cash balances improved to $966.5 million by the end of the quarter.

What IHS Holding Does

IHS Holding develops and operates shared mobile telecommunications tower infrastructure across Africa and selected international markets, providing colocation and related services to mobile network operators.
