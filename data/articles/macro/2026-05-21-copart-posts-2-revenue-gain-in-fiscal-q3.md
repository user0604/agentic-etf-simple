Key Takeaways
Copart achieved modest growth in revenue and earnings per share, but net income declined due to higher expenses.
Revenue and gross profit both increased year over year in the third quarter.
Management did not provide a near-term financial outlook, and U.S. insurance unit volumes remain under pressure.

Copart (NASDAQ:CPRT) delivered a mixed third quarter, with incoming growth in revenue and profitability partly offset by a slight decline in net income. The leading online vehicle auction platform helps insurance companies, dealerships, fleet operators, and others buy and sell salvage and used vehicles worldwide. Its results for the three months ended April 30, 2026, were released on May 21, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed stable but slow progress, as international strength supported headline results while U.S. operations remained sluggish.

Notable developments
International operations stood out, with segment revenue up 14.1% and operating income up 25% compared to the prior year.
Net income decreased by 1.0%, impacted by higher operating expenses and a drop in non-operating income.
Copart repurchased $1.63 billion in common stock year-to-date, reducing diluted shares outstanding by 3.6% year over year.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not issue new financial guidance for the near term, sticking to a long-term approach focused on international growth and technology investment. Attention remains on trends in U.S. insurance assignment volumes, which continue to lag. Investors should watch for signs of a rebound in core U.S. insurance unit activity and the ongoing impact of cost management efforts.

Quarterly highlights

The international segment was the main engine for growth. Segment revenue rose to $234.2 million from $205.3 million, while segment operating income reached $73.8 million, demonstrating the payoff from Copart’s expanding global footprint.

Profitability remained strong, with gross profit rising faster than revenue, but net income slipped due to increases in general and administrative costs and lower other income. The revenue figure, at $1.24 billion, represented a return to growth after contractions in prior quarters.

Copart’s ongoing share buyback totaled $1.63 billion so far this fiscal year, significantly reducing the number of diluted shares and helping boost earnings per share, even as absolute net income edged down.

What Copart does

Copart operates a global online marketplace that connects vehicle sellers — including insurance companies, dealerships, and fleet operators — with buyers for vehicles that are damaged, salvaged, or used.
