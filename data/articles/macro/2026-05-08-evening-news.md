1. Intel Inside? Apple May Say Yes Again

Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) and Intel (NASDAQ: INTC) -- both Stock Advisor recs -- have reached a preliminary agreement for Intel to manufacture chips for Apple devices, The Wall Street Journal reports. The deal is the product of more than a year of negotiations and significant pressure from the Trump administration, which bought the U.S. government a 10% stake in Intel. Since Intel joined the Stock Advisor (Team HG) scorecard in February, shares are up 127%, annihilating the market's -- albeit impressive -- 9% return over the same period.

The supply crunch behind it: Apple has struggled to secure enough chips from Taiwan Semiconductor (NYSE: TSM) as Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) and other artificial intelligence (AI) chipmakers compete for capacity.
Intel's new momentum: The chipmaker has also struck manufacturing partnerships with Nvidia and Elon Musk's Terafab project in recent months, signaling a broader foundry revival under CEO Lip-Bu Tan.
2. Rocket Lab's Record Deal Ignites 35% Surge

Shares of Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB) blasted 34% higher on Friday, the stock's best single day ever, after first-quarter revenue beat estimates and the company unveiled its largest launch contract in history with a mystery customer, covering Neutron and Electron rockets through 2029. Plus, backlog more than doubled to $2.2 billion. And Q2 guidance of $225 million to $240 million crushed Wall Street's $207.5 million estimate. Rocket Lab is a Stock Advisor Foundational Stock; shares are up 78%, 61 percentage points better than the S&P 500, since joining the scorecard in August.

To the moon (literally): The record contract follows a $190 million hypersonic test-flight deal signed just weeks ago. Rocket Lab is also scooping up space robotics firm Motiv Space Systems.
Rising tide lifts all rockets: The space-sector party spread fast: Peers Firefly Aerospace (NASDAQ: FLY) and Intuitive Machines (NASDAQ: LUNR) each popped 20%+ on the good vibes alone.
3. Micron Recovers as Memory Demand Holds

That was quite a 24-hour stretch for Micron (NASDAQ: MU)! Shares tumbled 3% Thursday after Bernstein raised the alarm on the computer memory spot market, then rocketed 9% Friday morning, with Bernstein once again pulling the strings, to end the day up 15.5%. Leave it to Wall Street...

But here's the twist. April DRAM prices surged 57% versus Q1 averages, and NAND jumped 65% to 70%. The worry is that DRAM and NAND prices have gotten so hot that some buyers are being priced out. Meanwhile, the buyers who can afford memory are still buying aggressively.
That price target, though: Bernstein has a $510 target while Micron trades well above it, suggesting a coming revision. Foolish investors would do well to tune out the noise. After all, Micron is among the highest-scoring companies in both the Hidden Gems Primary and Rule Breakers Primary Moneyball databases.
