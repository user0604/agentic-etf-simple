Key Takeaways
Iren booked a large net loss as it accelerated its transition from Bitcoin mining to AI cloud services.
Adjusted EBITDA came in at $59.5 million for the quarter.
Management reaffirmed its target of $3.7 billion in annual recurring revenue by the end of calendar 2026.

Iren (NASDAQ:IREN) booked a significant net loss in its latest quarter as it pressed forward with a shift from Bitcoin mining toward high-performance AI infrastructure. The global data center operator focuses on providing renewable-powered computing capacity for customers in the artificial intelligence, cloud, and enterprise sectors. The quarter ended March 31, 2026, and results were released on May 8, 2026. Overall, performance reflected continued progress in the transition to AI services, though profits remained under pressure due to ongoing legacy costs.

Notable Developments
AI Cloud services revenue jumped to $33.6 million as GPU-powered capacity expanded during the period.
The company recorded a net loss of $247.8 million, including $140.4 million in non-cash charges linked to winding down Bitcoin mining operations.
Iren signed a $3.4 billion, five-year contract with Nvidia for AI Cloud, pushing contracted annual recurring revenue to $3.1 billion.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its goal to reach $3.7 billion in annual recurring revenue by the end of calendar 2026, with $3.1 billion under contract so far. Leaders stressed that growth depends on building and activating new GPU infrastructure as quickly as possible. The most important factor to monitor is the timely execution of large-scale GPU deployments supporting the AI cloud transition.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s AI Cloud business grew sharply, with revenue nearly doubling from the previous quarter as new high-power GPU services came online and a major contract with NVIDIA was secured. This offset, in part, the planned decline in Bitcoin mining revenue as older hardware was decommissioned.

Adjusted EBITDA, a measure of ongoing earnings before key non-cash charges, fell compared to prior quarters, reflecting both the natural drop in Bitcoin mining income and continued costs linked to the operational pivot. The steep net loss was mainly due to $140.4 million in non-cash asset impairment, as the company retired outdated mining equipment ahead of its capacity upgrades.

Available cash remained strong at $2.6 billion, supporting ongoing capital investment in new sites and data center conversions. Management confirmed that all currently operational GPU infrastructure is fully contracted, with additional capacity coming online in the second half of the year to meet growing demand from hyperscaler and enterprise clients.

What Iren Does

Iren operates renewable-powered data centers that deliver computing infrastructure for artificial intelligence, cloud, and enterprise customers, and is in the process of phasing out its Bitcoin mining business in favor of AI and high-performance cloud services.
