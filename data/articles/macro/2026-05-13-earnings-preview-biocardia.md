BioCardia (NASDAQ:BCDA), a clinical-stage company focused on developing cell therapies for heart disease, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings on Friday, May 15, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

BioCardia released its fourth-quarter and full-year 2025 results on Mar. 24, 2026. The company continued to advance its lead CardiAMP autologous cell therapy program for patients with ischemic heart failure with reduced ejection fraction. The company also progressed its CardiAMP Heart Failure II confirmatory trial and moved forward with regulatory submissions in both the United States and Japan. Financially, BioCardia reported no revenue for the quarter, reflecting its pre-commercial stage, and posted a modestly higher net loss compared to the prior year. Research and development spending increased due to trial closeouts and new enrollment, while general and administrative costs declined. The company ended the year with $2.5 million in cash and cash equivalents. These results met analyst consensus for both earnings per share and sales.

Positives
Completion of pivotal CardiAMP Heart Failure trial: The company finalized data collection and analysis for its Phase III trial, providing a foundation for regulatory discussions in the United States and Japan.
Statistically significant results in key patient subgroup: Patients with elevated heart stress biomarkers showed meaningful improvements in heart structure and reduced adverse events, supporting the therapy’s potential value.
Controlled operating expenses: Selling, general, and administrative expenses declined 10% year over year, reflecting cost discipline despite ongoing development activity.
Cash burn remained stable: Net cash used in operations decreased slightly from the prior year, helping to preserve available resources.
Negatives
No revenue generated: The company reported zero revenue for 2025, highlighting its continued reliance on external funding and the lack of commercial-stage products.
Net loss increased: The annual net loss rose modestly to $8.2 million, mainly due to higher research and development costs as clinical activities expanded.
Uncertainty around future funding: With $2.5 million in cash at year-end, management disclosed that current funds are not sufficient to support operations beyond May 2026 without additional capital.
Primary endpoint not met in main trial: The pivotal CardiAMP Heart Failure trial did not meet its primary endpoint in the overall population, which may impact regulatory review and market perception.
