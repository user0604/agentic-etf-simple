Key Takeaways
Bank Of Nova Scotia delivered strong gains, driven by Canadian Banking and stable asset quality.
Adjusted diluted earnings per share rose 32.9% compared to a year ago.

Bank Of Nova Scotia (NYSE:BNS) posted impressive growth this quarter as its core Canadian operations outperformed and asset quality trends remained steady. The large Canadian and international bank offers retail, wealth management, and investment services across the Americas and globally. Its latest results were released on May 27, 2026, covering the second quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the quarter featured higher profitability and advances in most major business segments.

Notable developments
The Canadian Banking segment saw net income climb 53% year over year, led by higher revenue and improved credit loss provisioning.
Overall provision for credit losses declined by 13%, pointing to ongoing improvement in asset quality compared to the previous year.
Adjusted return on equity, an important profitability measure that compares net income to shareholders’ equity, improved nearly 3 percentage points to 13.2%.
Quarterly highlights

The standout performance came from the Canadian Banking segment, where net income increased from $613 million to $935 million. This reflected both rising customer activity and lower credit provisions, with segment return on equity reaching 17.8%.

International Banking provided steady earnings, with reported net income up 4%. However, on a constant currency basis, growth was limited to just 1%, highlighting subdued expansion when excluding foreign exchange impacts.

Management raised the quarterly dividend 4% to $1.14 per share, reflecting improved profitability and capital strength. Capital ratios such as the Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio stayed at 13.3%, comfortably exceeding regulatory minimums.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management repeated its confidence in achieving a return on equity over 14% by fiscal 2027, driven by continued growth in core Canadian and wealth management businesses. Its capital ratios remain solid, and the company targets further expense discipline. Investors should watch for credit trends, especially in international and corporate portfolios, and any sustained improvements in international banking growth.
