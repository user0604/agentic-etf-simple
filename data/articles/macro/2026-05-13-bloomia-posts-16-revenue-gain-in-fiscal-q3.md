Bloomia Posts 16% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q3

Key Takeaways
Bloomia delivered higher revenue but saw profitability fall due to rising costs and lower stem volumes.
Revenue rose year over year.
Management is optimistic about improved profitability in the next fiscal year as cost headwinds begin to ease.

Bloomia (NASDAQ:TULP) struggled with profit margins even as it grew sales for the period. The leading U.S. specialty tulip grower and supplier, reporting results on May 13, 2026 for its fiscal third quarter, saw higher revenue primarily from price increases, but shrinking volumes and higher costs pressured earnings. Overall, the period showed progress in sales but persistent challenges in profits and cash flows.

Notable Developments
Revenue grew 16% compared to the same quarter last year, driven mostly by higher prices, though stem volumes declined by about 3%.
Gross profit margin fell to 19.8% from 31.3% as higher bulb costs and tariffs outpaced the price increases.
Bloomia completed a $12.1 million rights offering to reduce debt, which resulted in debt forgiveness of approximately $10 million and boosted working capital.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management anticipates stronger profitability in fiscal 2027, expecting that reduced raw material costs and more favorable currency rates will help margins recover. They are especially focused on meeting higher demand in the historically busy fourth quarter thanks to increased stem availability. Investors should monitor whether volume sales stabilize and if margin pressures begin to ease as projected.

Quarterly Highlights

While Bloomia's revenue increased from the prior year due to higher prices for its tulip stems, the actual volume of stems sold was lower. The company cited weaker Valentine’s Day sales but benefited from a shift in Easter-related demand that moved sales into this quarter.

Profitability declined as gross profit and operating income both dropped. This was a result of both higher input costs, including bulb expenses and tariffs, and softness in volumes, despite the benefit of a small grant offsetting some cost pressure.

The company took active steps to strengthen its financial position by completing a rights offering, using the proceeds primarily to pay down debt and secure approximately $10 million in debt forgiveness, which improved liquidity and working capital but left debt at $47.5 million.

What Bloomia Does

Bloomia grows, ships, and supplies tulip stems—primarily to large U.S. grocery retailers—using greenhouse automation and hydroponic farming.
