Notable Developments
Total revenue more than doubled in the quarter, primarily due to technology transfer revenue from a Merck agreement and growth in R&D revenue.
Product gross margin increased by 16 percentage points, supported by sales of more profitable enzymatic products and a reduced focus on legacy items.
Operating costs declined, with research and development expenses down $1.5 million and selling, general, and administrative costs down $2.6 million year over year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated full-year revenue guidance of $72 to $76 million and confirmed that cash on hand is expected to fund operations through the end of 2027. Leadership highlighted a focus on progressing its ECO Synthesis platform into further commercial supply agreements. Investors should watch for updates on new licensing deals and continued clinical progress, as these are critical for transitioning toward recurring, high-margin revenue.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue jump in the quarter was largely driven by recognition of $6.3 million in technology transfer fees, mainly from the Merck partnership, which helped lift overall sales and reduce the company’s net loss from the prior-year period. Underlying product sales and research revenues also rose, evidencing momentum in strategic partnerships with pharmaceutical clients.

Gross margin surged as Codexis sold more high-margin enzymatic products and cut back less profitable, legacy offerings. Management noted that this margin improvement reflects the company’s ongoing strategy to emphasize next-generation enzyme products and value-added solutions for RNA therapeutics manufacturing.

Cost reduction initiatives continued from the prior year, with significant decreases in both research and development and selling, general and administrative costs. This operating discipline, combined with rising revenues, was key to the narrowed net loss and improved cash efficiency in the period.

What Codexis Does

Codexis develops and sells engineered enzymes that improve drug manufacturing processes and enable the production of RNA therapeutics such as small interfering RNA (siRNA).
