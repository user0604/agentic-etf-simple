Consolidated Water Posts 11% Revenue Drop in Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 8:53 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Consolidated Water saw a setback as weakness in manufacturing and weather-hit retail weighed on results.
Diluted EPS fell to $0.23 and revenue dropped 11.1% from the prior year.
Management expects manufacturing revenue for fiscal 2026 to be below last year's record and continues to cite project and licensing delays as watchpoints.

Consolidated Water (NASDAQ:CWCO) faced a tougher start to the year as manufacturing orders declined and wet weather reduced retail water sales in Grand Cayman. The water utility and desalination specialist operates across the Caribbean and U.S, with business lines in bulk supply, water services, manufacturing, and retail water distribution. Breaking from the prior year’s record earnings, its report for the first quarter ended March 31, 2026 (released on May 12, 2026) showed lower revenue and profit as key segments struggled while regulatory and project timing clouded the short-term view.

Overall, the quarter highlighted the challenges of lumpy order flows and external factors, although ongoing investment and a strong balance sheet provided some stability for the business.

Notable Developments
Manufacturing segment revenue fell sharply by 76% as order intake slowed following a large contract last year.
Retail segment performance was hampered by a 10% drop in water volume sold, linked to significantly higher rainfall in Grand Cayman.
Services revenue climbed 12% year over year, with operations and maintenance contracts leading growth in this segment.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now expects manufacturing revenue for fiscal 2026 to be lower than last year’s record level and continues to track permitting for its Hawaii desalination project. The renewal of the key Grand Cayman retail license is also in negotiation and presents some uncertainty. Investors should monitor any updates on regulatory approvals for both Hawaii and Grand Cayman as well as signs of manufacturing order recovery.

Quarterly Highlights

The manufacturing segment experienced a pronounced fall from a strong prior year quarter, with revenue dropping to $1.4 million. Management attributed this to fewer new orders and the absence of major contracts realized last year, and it no longer forecasts growth in this segment for the full year.

Retail revenue dropped as water sales in Grand Cayman declined by about 10%, driven by “significantly greater rainfall.” This offset previous strong results that had benefited from drought and a rise in air tourist arrivals in 2025.

The services segment was a relative bright spot. Operations and maintenance services grew as new contracts began, especially a three-year California municipal project. Construction revenue in this segment remained nearly flat as the start of work on the Hawaii project continues to be delayed by permitting issues.

What Consolidated Water Does

Consolidated Water provides potable water through desalination plants that use reverse osmosis technology, and delivers water services, manufacturing, and bulk supply solutions in the Caribbean and U.S.

The quarterly dividend was raised 27% year over year to $0.14 per share.
