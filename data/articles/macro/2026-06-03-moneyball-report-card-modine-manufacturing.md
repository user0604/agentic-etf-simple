Moneyball Report Card: Modine Manufacturing

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 3, 2026, 6:00 PM

Comments

What the Hidden Gems Primary database scores can tell us about Modine.

If you'd been told a few years ago that one of the cleaner ways to invest in the AI build-out would be a century-old radiator company from Wisconsin, you'd have been forgiven for laughing. Yet that's exactly the story the Moneyball database tells about Modine Manufacturing (NYSE: MOD), a legacy thermal-management business that reinvented itself as a data center cooling supplier just as the industry discovered it couldn't run AI chips without somewhere for the heat to go.

The transformation is the heart of the piece, and the five-year numbers make it real. Financial 5Y of 79 reflects a company that grew revenue from roughly $2 billion to over $3 billion at an 11.6% compound rate, expanded gross margins from 15% to 23%, and cut its debt-to-equity ratio from above 1.0 down to 0.4. Operating cash flow rebounded from almost nothing to nearly $250 million. This isn't financial engineering; it's a genuine portfolio overhaul, exiting commodity markets to concentrate on high-margin, mission-critical thermal solutions. The Growth score of 80 and Financial 1Y of 78 confirm the momentum carried straight through to the present.

The technology positioning is what makes the data center angle credible. A Tech score of 75 and an AI score of 73 capture an important nuance: Modine doesn't embed AI logic in its products, but it builds the physical infrastructure that makes AI computation possible. As chip density climbs, the heat problem becomes existential for hyperscalers, and Modine's liquid and hybrid cooling systems address exactly that. Landmark long-term agreements with top-tier technology partners and aggressive capacity expansion mean a substantial and growing share of sales now comes from data center cooling. It's a picks-and-shovels play on AI, with the durability that implies.

Here's where Modine breaks the pattern of this batch. Governance scores an 84, which is among the strongest marks in the database, and a sharp contrast to the oversight problems affecting HPE (NYSE: HPE) and Autodesk (NASDAQ: ADSK). The database credits transparent financial reporting, disciplined accounting, rigorous internal controls, and an independent board with no dual-class voting games. The Owner score of 81 reinforces it: a stable, heavily institutional shareholder base that's been expanding its holdings for years. Leadership earns an 88, with CEO Neil Brinker and CFO Michael Lucareli credited for steady execution and specific, data-backed guidance.

The one genuine caveat is executive compensation. Across multiple score categories, the database keeps returning to the same concern: a steep, recent escalation in executive pay that hasn't tracked cleanly with the growth in business scale. It's not a structural governance failure -- the bones of the framework are sound -- but it's the kind of optics shareholders should watch, and it's why even a strong governance profile carries an asterisk.

So the central question for Modine flips the usual one. With most names in this batch, investors weigh a great business against real governance risk. Here, the governance is a strength, the transformation is proven, and the AI infrastructure demand is structural. The real questions are valuation -- a 3.4x 200-week average multiple says the market has noticed the turnaround -- and execution discipline as the company scales through supply chain and margin pressure. The radiator company became an AI play. Now it has to prove it can grow into the price.
