Key Takeaways
Stoke Therapeutics reported a sharp loss as revenue normalized after a one-time licensing payment last year.
Revenue dropped significantly compared to the prior year due to the absence of a large collaboration payment.
Management reaffirmed clinical timelines and expects cash to fund operations into 2028.

Stoke Therapeutics (NASDAQ:STOK) reported a substantial loss as revenue returned to typical levels following a major one-time payment in the prior year. The clinical-stage biotechnology company develops genetic medicines for severe diseases, with a focus on epilepsy and rare eye disorders. The latest results, released on May 7, 2026 for the first quarter, showed expected financial volatility but steady progress in its lead clinical program. Overall, the period reflected anticipated swings in revenue and expenses as the company advanced toward key clinical milestones.

Notable developments
Enrollment for the pivotal Phase 3 study of zorevunersen, the lead drug candidate for Dravet syndrome, is expected to complete in June 2026.
Research and development spending increased to $39.7 million, supporting clinical progress and launch readiness, while cash holdings reached $411.0 million after capital raising.
The large drop in reported revenue this quarter was due to the absence of last year’s $150.8 million license payment, not a decline in ongoing operations.
Quarterly highlights

The company advanced its core clinical program for zorevunersen, supported by four-year open-label extension data showing continued benefits and tolerability. By early May, about 130 of the planned 150 patients had been enrolled in the Phase 3 EMPEROR study, with final enrollment expected in the next quarter.

Research and development costs rose as the company expanded clinical activity, and sales, general and administrative expenses increased due to commercial launch planning and additional personnel. Stoke Therapeutics raised $80.7 million through selective at-the-market equity sales, which, combined with its starting cash, brought total liquidity to $411.0 million at quarter end.

The sharp decline in revenue and the swing to a net loss were anticipated, as the prior year included a one-time licensing payment from a collaboration agreement. Core business spending increased to support late-stage clinical programs, and capital raising extended the company’s funding runway into the next phase of development.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed guidance for clinical timelines, stating that the pivotal Phase 3 trial should finish enrollment in June 2026, with data expected in mid-2027. Cash resources are projected to last through the planned U.S. drug launch in early 2028. The most important milestone for investors is the successful completion and data readout from the EMPEROR Phase 3 trial of zorevunersen.

What Stoke Therapeutics does

Stoke Therapeutics develops genetic medicines to treat severe diseases caused by a single missing gene, including rare central nervous system and eye disorders.
