RMR Group Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 11:07 AM

Comments

The RMR Group Inc. (NASDAQ:RMR) is an alternative asset manager specializing in real estate, primarily through the management of a diversified family of publicly traded Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) under evergreen contracts and, more recently, via a rapidly growing private capital platform. The company’s strategy centers on stable, recurring cash flows from these long-term management contracts while expanding fee income and carried interest through private fund initiatives, joint ventures, and selective balance sheet investments. RMR’s approach balances capital discipline with ambitions to transform into a broader real estate asset manager amid an evolving capital markets environment.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Durable Evergreen Contracts Anchor Predictable Cash Flows and Support Valuation Upside

Long-term management agreements provide RMR with unparalleled revenue stability and downside protection relative to peers. The company’s core business revolves around managing major public REITs—including Diversified Healthcare Trust, Service Properties Trust, Industrial Logistics Properties Trust, Office Properties Income Trust, and Seven Hills Realty Trust—under evergreen management contracts, typically spanning 20 years with recurring renewal provisions. These contracts generated recurring service revenues of $42 million in Q2 2026, and are forecast to lift to $44 million next quarter, according to management guidance. Such durability is rare in the asset management space, affording RMR a baseline of highly visible, lower-risk revenue streams. With shares currently trading at just five times EBITDA generated from these contracts—materially below direct industry peers—the market may not be fully appreciating the contractual revenue durability and built-in margin of safety this model provides, creating re-rating potential if recognized.

2. Accelerating Private Capital Growth Creates Significant Upside Optionality

RMR’s successful buildout of its private capital platform is transforming the company from a niche REIT manager into a diversified real estate asset manager. Since 2020, assets under management (AUM) in the private segment have grown from virtually zero to nearly $12 billion as of early 2026. The company recently closed a nearly $350 million off-market multifamily portfolio in Connecticut, is actively syndicating the Enhanced Growth Venture fund targeting $250 million, and maintains a $4.7 billion value-add residential platform with high occupancy and retention. This expansion gives RMR access to new institutional limited partners, higher-fee revenue streams, and lucrative carried interest that can compound over time. Despite near-term industry fundraising headwinds—with global real estate fundraising down 50% year over year due to Middle East conflict—North American value-add strategies remain in demand, positioning RMR to further scale as the macro environment rebounds. Successful fundraising and deployment will diversify the company’s earnings mix and moderate its reliance on managed public REITs, presenting powerful long-term earnings growth potential.

3. Principal Investments and Strategic Capital Deployment Drive Incremental Earnings Growth

Opportunistic use of RMR’s liquid balance sheet enhances both alignment with clients and direct return generation. The company maintains a robust liquidity position of $133 million as of Q2 2026, including significant undrawn revolving credit capacity. Management has strategically anchored new offerings—such as a $50 million investment in Service Properties Trust’s recent equity raise, delivering $420 thousand in incremental quarterly dividends—and seeded key joint ventures, including a $6 million co-general partner investment in a major multifamily acquisition. These deals not only align RMR’s interests with shareholders of its managed vehicles but also produce direct, recurring cash flows via dividends and carried interest, while preserving capital flexibility for future cycles. As syndication efforts progress, the ability to recycle capital and expand the range of fee-earning and investment income streams becomes a key lever for delivering higher distributable earnings and supporting a strong dividend.

4. Performance-Driven Incentive Fees and Managed REIT Turnarounds Provide Near-Term Earnings Momentum

Operational improvements and turnaround success at RMR’s managed REITs unlock performance fee upside and strengthen the company’s earnings power. In 2025, incentive fees accrued by the company totaled $23.6 million, with management expecting further performance-linked compensation in 2026. Recent quarters have seen a marked recovery in REIT operating metrics: DHC delivered normalized funds from operations (FFO) of $0.14 per share and 13.5% same-property net operating income growth year over year in Q1 2026; ILPT secured robust leasing spreads and a transformative $1.6 billion joint venture refinancing; Seven Hills Realty Trust set new records for loan commitments amid expanding net interest margins. Restructurings, such as Office Properties Income Trust’s bankruptcy emergence and new five-year management contract, further cement stable fee revenue. As turnaround measures take effect, the upside from performance and incentive fees can meaningfully outpace base management fees, driving outsized short-term and medium-term adjusted EBITDA improvement.

5. Discounted Valuation Versus Peers Offers Re-Rating Potential as Business Mix Evolves

RMR’s current trading multiple materially discounts the durability and optionality of its evolving business model. The company’s sum-of-the-parts analysis shows shares trade at only five times contract EBITDA, well below peer real estate managers that commonly command higher multiples for less visible cash flows. As private capital AUM grows, fee revenue becomes more diversified, and carried interest streams start to materialize, the market may begin to reprice RMR’s shares to reflect both its low-risk REIT contract base and the incremental, higher-growth segments. Enhanced disclosure, execution on fundraising, and further evidence of successful fee diversification could catalyze a positive market response, especially as new private fund economics and capital-light earnings streams gain scale within the business model.

Potential Bear Cases
1. High Concentration in Public REIT Clients Exposes Earnings to Asset Performance and Market Shocks

Dependence on a handful of managed public REITs leaves RMR’s recurring fee base vulnerable to idiosyncratic client risks and real estate cycles. The vast majority of the company’s service revenues—$42 million out of total recurring revenues in Q2 2026—derive from management fees charged to its controlled REITs. Any adverse developments in these vehicles, such as asset value declines, credit downgrades, or loss of fee-paying properties, can quickly erode RMR’s revenue. The recent bankruptcy of Office Properties Income Trust highlights this risk, even though management secured a restructured five-year contract. Should more severe performance issues or management turnover occur at other REITs, or if key contracts are renegotiated or terminated, future fee income and the company’s reputation for reliability could be threatened, amplifying the inherent cyclical risk across a highly concentrated client base.

2. Fundraising Slowdown and Macro Volatility Threaten Private Capital Expansion and Fee Diversification

External headwinds in the global real estate fundraising environment could stall RMR’s most promising growth engine at a critical inflection point. The company’s strategic push into private capital depends on successfully syndicating new funds and attracting institutional partners. However, sector fundraising fell 50% year over year in 2026, primarily due to the destabilizing effects of the Middle East conflict and broader macroeconomic uncertainty. With investor due diligence processes slowing and capital allocations to alternatives constrained, RMR’s ambitions to raise new AUM, and thus diversify earnings, face meaningful delays. This protracted softness increases execution risk, potentially leaving the business structurally over-reliant on public vehicle management fees and unable to achieve the full revenue and earnings potential of its private strategies, especially given the highly competitive institutional fundraising landscape.

3. Fee Margin Compression and Limited GAAP Earnings Growth Undermine Valuation and Dividend Coverage

Persistent margin pressure and lagging GAAP net income may weigh on the company’s perceived quality as a yield investment and asset manager. While distributable earnings and adjusted EBITDA met or exceeded guidance in Q2 2026 (with distributable EPS of $0.44 and adjusted EBITDA of $18.5 million), GAAP net margin remained low at 3.52%, and reported earnings per share missed consensus by $0.03. Much of the headline growth has come from incentive fees and episodic gains, while recurring management fees face pressure from asset sales and fee base shrinkage at certain REITs. As the mix of balance sheet investments increases (carrying depreciation and interest expense), RMR no longer provides guidance for adjusted net income, raising questions about future dividend coverage and core profitability under standard accounting. Should margins remain compressed or one-off fees prove lumpy, investor confidence in the company’s yield reliability and equity valuation may decline, especially compared to more diversified peers with stronger earnings momentum.
