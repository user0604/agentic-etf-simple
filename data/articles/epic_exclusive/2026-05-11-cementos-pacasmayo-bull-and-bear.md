Cementos Pacasmayo: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 7:05 AM

Comments

Cementos Pacasmayo S.A.A. (NYSE:CPAC) is a leading Peruvian cement producer with a dominant position in northern Peru, focusing on infrastructure and self-construction markets. The company's growth strategy emphasizes operational efficiency, sustainability leadership, and expansion through strategic partnerships, most notably with the recent acquisition of a controlling stake by global cement giant Holcim. Management’s roadmap centers on margin improvements, cost discipline, and capitalizing on ongoing infrastructure development in its region.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership in Northern Peru Enables Sustained Pricing Power and Volume Growth

Regional dominance and robust demand are providing a foundation for continued revenue expansion. In 2025, the company achieved a 7% year-over-year revenue increase and a 7.2% rise in sales volumes, buoyed by infrastructure spending and self-construction demand in northern Peru. This stronghold, reinforced by local economic activity in agroindustrial and fishing sectors, positions the company to capture a significant share of future government and private projects. As national elections hint at further infrastructure investment, this regional edge is expected to drive both volume growth and pricing resilience well into 2026 and beyond.

2. Holcim Acquisition Creates Strategic Synergies and Access to Global Best Practices

Integration with Holcim provides a transformative catalyst for operational excellence and new market opportunities. In December 2025, Holcim announced an agreement to acquire a 50.01% controlling interest in the company, valuing it at PEN 5.1 billion – an approximate 9x trailing twelve months EBITDA multiple as of July 2025. Management anticipates that Holcim’s operational expertise, procurement scale, and sustainability initiatives will deliver significant synergies. This partnership is expected to accelerate innovation, expand addressable markets, and enhance financial flexibility, boosting the company’s competitive positioning on a national and potentially international level.

3. Operational Efficiency Enables Margin Expansion Despite Cost Pressures

Relentless focus on process improvements and input cost management has translated into expanding profitability. For full-year 2025, gross profit rose 10.8% and all-time high EBITDA reached PEN 594.2 million, up 6.4% year over year (excluding one-off transaction costs). Notably, gross margin for the year improved by 1.9 percentage points, driven by lower raw material expenses, increased use of in-house clinker, and enhanced maintenance strategies. These structural improvements provide a buffer against inflation and supply chain volatility, supporting management’s guidance for sustainable or even slightly higher EBITDA margins for 2026.

4. Sustainability Credentials Bolstered by National Recognition and Demand Tailwinds

ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) leadership is enhancing reputation and aligning with major market trends. The company has achieved three consecutive years of greenhouse gas emission reductions, recognized by Peru’s Ministry of Environment with a 3-star recognition. Its continued presence in the top-10 of Peru’s most responsible companies underscores its commitment to environmental stewardship. Such credentials not only facilitate access to increasingly ESG-conscious investors and customers but also closely align with Holcim’s global mandate for sustainable concrete and cement products, positioning the company to benefit from future green infrastructure programs and regulatory incentives.

5. Improved Product Mix and Flexible Cost Structure Support Resilience Through Cycles

Adaptable sales strategies have diversified revenue streams and enhanced shock resistance. In Q1 2025, cement sales surged by 30.6% year over year, with an 8.7% increase for the full year, while annual gains in concrete and precast segments capitalized on changing customer needs and project profiles. The company responded nimbly to project delays — such as the Motupe River Bank pause — by redeploying focus to high-margin segments and public tenders. With advanced energy-saving projects scheduled for late 2026, the business is positioned to further optimize costs and adapt to market shifts, providing stability even in periods of macro volatility.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Rising Administrative and Selling Expenses Could Erode Profitability

Escalating operating costs create headwinds for sustained margin growth. In 2025, administrative expenses soared by 50% (year over year), largely due to higher personnel costs from collective labor negotiations, and selling expenses increased 40% due to expanded advertising and promotional activities in the first nine months. While management has taken steps to curb these increases, the company’s cost base remains exposed to wage inflation and the need for continued commercial investment, a dynamic that could limit future profitability improvement if not contained.

2. Project Delays and Political Uncertainty Threaten Near-Term Demand and Cash Flows

Reliance on large infrastructure projects exposes the business to cyclical and execution risk. The temporary halt of projects like the Motupe River Bank defense led to a 25.1% year-over-year volume drop in concrete-related products during Q4 2025, highlighting vulnerability to public sector delays. With upcoming elections in Peru and typical post-electoral budget reshuffling, construction spending may see further lulls, potentially weighing on volumes and working capital. Such disruptions, if prolonged or widespread, could challenge management’s optimism for volume and margin expansion in 2026.

3. Integration Risks from Holcim Acquisition May Result in One-Off Costs or Strategic Distraction

Ownership transition carries execution challenges that could offset potential benefits. The Holcim transaction triggered significant one-time expenses related to change-of-control provisions and could bring integration costs as systems, personnel, and processes are harmonized. Delays in realizing operational or strategic synergies, or cultural clashes, could distract management or result in unexpected charges. Until clear evidence of cost savings or accelerated growth emerges from the new partnership, investors must consider the risk that projected benefits take longer to materialize or fall short of expectations.
