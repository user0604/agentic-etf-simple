Notable Developments
Cash resources rose to $134.9 million, supporting operations through at least 2029, with no debt reported.
Research and development expenses increased to $6.9 million, driven by the MAIA-ovarian clinical trial expansion.
Partnerships with AstraZeneca and Gilead advanced, but no new milestone payments were booked during the period.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management repeated its expectation that cash and cash-related assets are adequate to fund activities into 2029. The company is not guiding for new revenue or expense targets for the rest of 2026, citing the planned interim analysis for COM701 in early 2027 as its main near-term milestone. Investors should monitor the pace of enrollment and any new data releases in partnered programs, as major revenue events depend heavily on external progress.

Quarterly highlights

The company ended the period with $134.9 million in cash, cash equivalents, and liquid investments.

Compugen’s investment in research climbed as it ramped up the MAIA-ovarian trial for its COM701 antibody, a cancer immunotherapy program. These higher expenses are supporting broader trial enrollment, with the next major result expected in early 2027; management noted that all sites are now actively enrolling patients.

The company’s collaborations with AstraZeneca and Gilead remain central to its business, supplying all recognized revenues in the form of milestone-related payments. AstraZeneca’s rilvegostomig program has advanced from 10 to 11 ongoing Phase 3 trials, while the Gilead-partnered GS-0321 candidate remains in early studies, but no new payments or product revenues were recognized this period.

What Compugen does

Compugen uses computational biology and artificial intelligence to develop new drug candidates for cancer immunotherapy, focusing on both internal programs and partnership-driven clinical assets.
