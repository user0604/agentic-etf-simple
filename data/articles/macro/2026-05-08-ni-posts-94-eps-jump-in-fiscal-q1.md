Key Takeaways
Underwriting profitability improved while top-line premiums declined due to the exit from Non-Standard Auto insurance.
Earnings per share nearly doubled, and the combined ratio improved by 14.7 percentage points year over year.
Management reiterated a focus on core markets but did not provide explicit forward guidance for the coming quarters.

Ni (NASDAQ:NODK) delivered stronger profits and improved underwriting metrics as it continued its retreat from unprofitable insurance lines. The property and casualty insurer serves the upper Midwest and leverages a close relationship with the North Dakota Farm Bureau for core Home and Farm insurance. In its first quarter results released on May 8, 2026, for the period ended March 31, 2026, the company reported sharply higher earnings on lower overall revenue as its refocused strategy took hold. Overall, the quarter featured much stronger profitability but ongoing pressure on premium volume and investment income.

Notable Developments
Gross premiums written dropped 15.1% as the company wound down nearly all Non-Standard Auto insurance exposure, with that category virtually eliminated compared to the prior year.
The combined ratio, which measures insurance profitability by comparing all claim and operating costs to premium revenue, improved by 14.7 percentage points to 79.7%, indicating a sharp boost in underwriting results.
While Home and Farm insurance premium volumes rose 7.3%, net investment income declined 6.4% as returns on fixed income assets and short-term investments softened.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its focus on disciplined underwriting and growing profitable, core business lines in the Midwest and Farm segments. No explicit earnings or premium targets were provided for future quarters. Investors should monitor whether growth in Home and Farm or other segments compensates for the reduction in Non-Standard Auto and helps stabilize the company's overall revenue base.

Quarterly Highlights

The sharp improvement in combined ratio and underwriting profitability stemmed from both reduced exposure to high-loss insurance lines and stronger reserve development in Home and Farm and Private Passenger Auto. A combined ratio below 100% means the company is earning more in premiums than it pays out in claims and expenses, a key profitability mark in insurance.

Revenue fell year over year due to lower gross premiums written and declines in net investment income, even as premiums in Home and Farm rose from rate increases and new business in core states like North Dakota and South Dakota. The “All Other” segment also grew rapidly, helped by more assumed premiums from reinsurance activities connected to Farm Bureau catastrophe programs.

Earnings per share nearly doubled from the prior year, primarily due to greater underwriting profitability and lower claim costs. The return on average equity climbed to 20.4%, reflecting improved leverage of shareholder capital without any noted weakening of reserves or regulatory capital positions.

What Ni Does

Ni is a property and casualty insurer offering personal lines and specialty coverage in the upper Midwest, with a focus on Home, Farm, and affiliated Farm Bureau insurance products.
