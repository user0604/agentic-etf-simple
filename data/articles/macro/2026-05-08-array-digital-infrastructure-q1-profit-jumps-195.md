Major spectrum asset sales gave Array Digital Infrastructure (NYSE:AD) a significant boost in the first quarter, with core profit and revenue climbing sharply. The company operates thousands of wireless towers across rural and suburban U.S. markets, supporting carrier networks for 5G and advanced wireless services. It released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 8, 2026, showing headline gains that were mainly due to asset dispositions rather than recurring operations. Overall, the quarter reflected strong reported growth but highlighted the temporary nature of some gains.

Notable Developments
Profitability soared as a major spectrum asset sale produced a $156.6 million pre-tax gain and a special dividend payout of $885.5 million in the quarter.
Tower site rental revenue jumped 92%, with growth fueled by new tenancy deals, especially with T-Mobile and Verizon, despite a decrease in total colocations due to DISH Wireless defaulting on obligations.
Management reaffirmed full-year guidance for revenue and profitability, maintaining previous outlooks while highlighting risks tied to concentrated tenant exposure and ongoing legal disputes.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management left its full-year targets unchanged, projecting revenue of $200–$215 million and adjusted EBITDA (Non-GAAP) between $200–$215 million. The most critical factor for investors is whether recurring tower operations income can keep growing as the boost from spectrum sales fades. The progress of pending spectrum transactions and resolution of the DISH Wireless payment dispute also merit close attention.

Quarterly Highlights

Spectrum monetization was the key driver, with a closed sale in January generating $1,018 million in proceeds and most of the quarter's profit. A special dividend resulted from these gains, providing a one-time payout to shareholders.

Tower operations saw strong underlying revenue growth year over year, supported by new carrier deals and sales force changes, although overall colocation numbers and tenancy rates declined due to excluding DISH from totals.

The company now reports 4,452 owned towers and a period-end cash balance of $253.6 million, up from $113.4 million at year-end, thanks to both operating inflows and spectrum sales.

What Array Digital Infrastructure Does

Array Digital Infrastructure operates wireless tower assets and related spectrum holdings, enabling national wireless carriers to expand their networks—especially across less populated areas.
