Key Takeaways
Mosaic reported a business loss as higher input costs and asset charges drove negative operating earnings. Revenue rose, but adjusted earnings per share dropped sharply from the prior year. Management withdrew phosphate production guidance and cut capital spending for the remainder of fiscal 2026.

Mosaic (NYSE:MOS) faced steep operational challenges in the first quarter, with sharp raw material inflation and major asset restructuring resulting in a significant business setback. The leading producer of concentrated phosphate and potash crop nutrients posted these first-quarter results on May 11, 2026, for the period ending March 31. The quarter was defined by margin pressure and a shift toward risk mitigation, as management scaled back investment plans and reviewed its production outlook.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased approximately 15% year over year, supported by higher phosphate and potash sales volumes, but operating losses reached $373 million due to record input costs and large one-time charges.
Phosphate segment profitability collapsed as raw material costs -- especially sulfur, which Mosaic reported hit new highs -- drove gross margins per tonne down from $111 to $2 year over year.
The company initiated $442 million in asset charges tied to the idling of its Araxa and Patrocinio sites in Brazil, and withdrew its full-year phosphate production guidance for 2026.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management has adopted a cautious stance, cutting capital expenditure guidance to $1.25 billion for 2026 and withdrawing earlier phosphate production targets due to ongoing raw material constraints. The company is focused on cash preservation and operational flexibility, with an explicit warning that near-term results will depend on sulfur price normalization and Brazil market recovery. The key factor for investors to monitor is how sustained high input costs and supply chain pressures affect Mosaic's ability to restore profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Mosaic's Potash segment was a relative bright spot, with net sales up to $667 million and operating earnings growing to $177 million, driven by steady demand and improved pricing. In contrast, the Phosphate segment swung from a $139 million profit to a $48 million loss despite increased revenue, as gross margins eroded almost entirely due to input inflation.

Mosaic Fertilizantes in Brazil deteriorated sharply, recording a $422 million operating loss following significant restructuring charges and an 11% year-over-year fall in product volume. Management has now begun a strategic review of these assets, reflecting the problems from persistent credit constraints and unequal regional demand.

Across the business, working capital actions helped offset some of the pressure on cash flow -- inventory reductions released $122 million of cash, although free cash flow remained negative, at $(253) million for the period. The regular quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.22 per share.
