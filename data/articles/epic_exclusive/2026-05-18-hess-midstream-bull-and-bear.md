Hess Midstream Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 7:09 AM

Comments

Hess Midstream LP (NYSE:HESM) is a leading energy infrastructure company that owns and operates natural gas and crude oil gathering, processing, and storage assets concentrated in North Dakota's Bakken shale region. Its strategy centers on long-term, fixed-fee and cost-of-service contracts—primarily with Chevron after the Hess acquisition—delivering steady cash flows, distribution growth, and margin resilience. With its asset base now essentially fully built out, management is focused on optimizing operations, enhancing efficiency, and returning substantial capital to unitholders through reliable distributions and buybacks.

Potential Bull Cases
1. High-Visibility, Contractual Revenues Provide Exceptional Downside Protection

Stable, predictable cash flows underpin robust income visibility for investors – The company derives approximately 85% of revenues from fixed-fee and cost-of-service contracts, mostly with Chevron, which are protected by minimum volume commitments through 2028 and reset annually through 2033. This structure shields Hess Midstream LP’s results from commodity price swings and spot market volatility, as evidenced by Q1 2026 performance—net income of $157.7 million and EBITDA margins near 83%—despite severe winter weather that temporarily reduced throughput. These ironclad contracts lock in mid-teen returns and annual cash flow sustainability, giving investors strong confidence in predictable payouts and resilience against cyclical downturns well into the next decade.

2. Leading Distribution Yield Supported by Strong Free Cash Flow Growth

Rising distributions at an 8.4% yield are well-covered by excess free cash flow – In Q1 2026, the company raised its quarterly distribution by 2%, translating to an annualized increase of roughly 8% for Class A unitholders and maintaining its peer-leading yield around 8.4%. Adjusted free cash flow guidance for 2026 was raised by 20% to $910–$960 million, mainly due to efficiency-driven reductions in capital spending and deferral of cash taxes until after 2028. Even after funding targeted 5% annual distribution growth, Hess Midstream LP expects about $280 million in excess free cash flow for additional buybacks and debt repayment. This approach not only sustains but also grows capital returns, making the company particularly attractive for income-focused investors seeking reliable yield amid a rising rate landscape.

3. Operational Efficiencies and Mature Asset Base Drive Margins and Capital Discipline

Sustained EBITDA margins well above target reflect cost leadership and asset optimization – The company achieved an adjusted EBITDA margin of 83% in Q1 2026, far exceeding its 75% target, driven by operational excellence and optimized use of its Bakken-based infrastructure. Completion of the final compressor station in early 2026 marked the culmination of the latest buildout phase, and ongoing efficiency gains—including Chevron’s adoption of longer well laterals—have sharply reduced required capital expenditure (down a third year over year to ~$100 million in 2026). With less spending needed to maintain or grow throughput, more cash is available for capital returns, and margin durability is further enhanced, outpacing most midstream peers.

4. Conservative Leverage Strategy and Deferred Tax Liability Strengthen Balance Sheet Flexibility

Planned deleveraging and no cash taxes until late decade enhance financial resilience – The company’s leverage ratio stands at about 3.0x EBITDA, with explicit plans to reduce it to 2.5x by 2028 using growing free cash flow after distributions. Recent IRS guidance now defers any meaningful corporate cash taxes until post-2028, freeing up additional liquidity for buybacks, debt reduction, or opportunistic returns. This disciplined approach ensures Hess Midstream LP is less vulnerable to credit market disruptions and can navigate regulatory changes or industry headwinds without risking payout cuts or balance sheet stress.

5. Optionality From Third-Party Volumes Offers Incremental Upside Without Added Risk

Growing third-party throughput provides revenue diversification and system leverage – While the majority of volumes and revenues come from Chevron, the company is strategically targeting a 10% share of third-party throughput in its 2026 guidance, with any additional volumes considered upside rather than base case. In Q1 2026, third-party operators used the system during operational challenges elsewhere, showcasing the value of Hess Midstream LP’s flexible, fully built-out asset footprint. This optionality enables incremental financial resilience and strengthens the economic moat of the system, laying the groundwork for potential diversification, higher future utilization rates, and additional margin capture without requiring material new capital outlays.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Excessive Customer and Geographic Concentration Magnifies Long-Term Dependency Risk

Relying almost exclusively on Chevron's Bakken activity creates vulnerability – Nearly all revenue and throughput for Hess Midstream LP depend on Chevron’s ongoing drilling plans in North Dakota, with minimal contribution from other producers and no meaningful geographic diversification. Current minimum volume commitments and contractual safeguards limit near-term risk, but starting in 2028–2033, any reduction in Bakken investment, renegotiation, or loss of contract exclusivity could impair volumes, undercut cash flows, and pressure distribution policy. Unlike more diversified peers, the company’s payout growth and income stability are inextricably linked to a single counterparty’s long-term strategic priorities and regional oil and gas outlook.

2. Limited Growth Prospects Beyond Contractual Tariff Increases and Cost Savings

Growth is increasingly capped as organic expansion opportunities dwindle – With its infrastructure buildout now largely complete and Chevron shifting to production plateau maintenance rather than aggressive expansion, future growth for Hess Midstream LP is primarily confined to contractual tariff resets, incremental operating optimizations, and modest third-party volumes. Major capital projects are not anticipated, and efficiency-driven CapEx reductions—while positive in the short run—do not signal expanding earnings power. Over time, this mature, steady-state business model may lag peers with more diverse assets, dynamic customer bases, or multibasin exposure, especially as contract roll-off periods approach in the late 2020s and early 2030s.

3. Regulatory, Tax, and Macroeconomic Uncertainties Could Erode FCF and Payout Capacity

External risks threaten the predictability of cash flows and planned returns – Although recent IRS guidance has deferred material corporate cash tax payments until after 2028, any future changes in taxation, regulation, or pipeline permitting could rapidly increase costs or restrict operating flexibility. Additionally, macroeconomic shocks—such as a prolonged energy price downturn, persistent inflation, or increased cost of capital—might affect upstream Bakken output, third-party demand, or the sustainability of premium distribution yields. As Hess Midstream LP’s financial model depends on contractual resets and cost control, unforeseen regulatory actions or industry headwinds could put downward pressure on projected free cash flow, balance sheet targets, and investor distributions, particularly beyond the next several years of current contract protection.
