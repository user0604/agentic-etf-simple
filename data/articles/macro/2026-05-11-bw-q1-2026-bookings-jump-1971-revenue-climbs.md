Notable developments
Bookings soared to $2.5 billion, driven by major contract wins with AI data center and industrial customers, a 1,971% increase year over year.
The company reduced net debt by 87% to $42.4 million, primarily through large cash inflows and debt repayment, supported by improved operational cash flow.
Revenue grew by 44%, led by expanding demand for core parts and services, including large-scale power projects for digital infrastructure clients.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA target range of $80 million to $100 million, expressing confidence in continued demand from digital and industrial customers. The company will continue to focus on maintaining its balance sheet strength and capturing opportunities tied to data center energy needs. Investors should closely monitor contract conversion and backlog execution, as large project timing and cost control will remain critical.

Quarterly highlights

Management attributed revenue growth to rising volumes of large projects, especially those linked to AI data center infrastructure, and continued robust performance from the company’s core aftermarket parts and services business. The standout bookings figure reflected both new wins and longer-term contracts, positioning the company with a much larger backlog and pipeline than in prior years.

Cash flow from operations turned sharply positive, enabling the company to pay down a significant portion of debt during the period. Net debt is now well below prior-year levels, supported in part by new equity issuance but also improved project advance payments and collections.

Reported net loss remained high due to non-cash expenses tied to warrant and stock appreciation rights, but on an adjusted basis, net income swung to a gain for the quarter, highlighting an underlying improvement in core profitability. Selling, general and administrative expenses rose, reflecting a larger revenue base and greater investment to support new projects.
