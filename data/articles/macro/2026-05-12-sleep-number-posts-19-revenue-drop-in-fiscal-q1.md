Key Takeaways

Sleep Number (NASDAQ:SNBR) posted a larger loss as its turnaround efforts could not offset falling sales and continued margin pressure. The leading smart bed manufacturer and direct-to-consumer sleep wellness brand reported significant declines in both revenue and profitability for its fiscal first quarter 2026 (released May 12, 2026). Overall, the quarter highlighted ongoing liquidity concerns and the urgent need for sustained demand improvement.

Notable developments
Net sales dropped to $319 million, reflecting lower demand and a smaller store base as the number of company-run locations fell from 637 to 577.
The gross margin shrank to 57.9%, pushed down by discounting legacy inventory and rolling out a new product lineup, while operating expenses were trimmed by 18% after special items.
Liquidity remains tight, with only $1.5 million in cash on hand at quarter-end and additional short-term loans required to support operations.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide financial guidance for the upcoming quarters as it continues an active review of strategic and financing options. The focus remains on stabilizing liquidity and pushing its refreshed product lineup while evaluating longer-term capital solutions. Investors should watch closely for any sequential sales improvement and announcements regarding a sustainable capital structure.

Quarterly highlights

Sales continued to decline across all channels, with both retail and online revenue seeing decreases of 15% or more compared to the prior year period. The company completed a major product reset, introducing its revised ComfortNext portfolio to all stores, but this did not stop the revenue slide.

Average revenue per mattress unit rose slightly, but overall sales fell, and average sales per store and per square foot both dropped sharply. Despite aggressive cost cuts, including significant reductions in selling and administrative costs, margins compressed further.

The gross margin decline was mainly due to discounting the prior product lineup and lack of sales leverage from the smaller store footprint. Non-recurring expenses, such as restructuring and legal costs totaling about $22 million, also contributed to deepening losses for the quarter. Liquidity constraints forced the company to secure an additional $25 million term loan, emphasizing the urgency of finding a more permanent capital solution. Borrowings under its credit facility increased to more than $605 million, and the quarter closed with negative free cash flow and a rising shareholder deficit. Management continues to work with advisors to identify longer-term strategies that will stabilize the balance sheet and support a return to growth.

What Sleep Number does

Sleep Number designs, manufactures, and sells smart beds and sleep technology products directly to consumers through its own retail stores and digital channels.
