White House Expects Oil Relief From Shipping Deal

12:07PM

White House National Economic Council Director Kevin Hassett stated that the Trump administration expects a sharp drop in oil prices if a geopolitical deal reopening key shipping routes is finalized.

Speaking on Fox News, Hassett noted that buyers are already hesitant to purchase oil at current spot prices in anticipation of a supply increase. He pointed to potential production boosts from Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates, alongside high U.S. output, as stabilizing factors.

West Texas Intermediate crude futures closed at $97 a barrel on Friday. While this is up 59% over the past 12 months, Hassett dismissed earlier predictions that prices would exceed $150.

He argued that broader inflation remains contained due to deregulation, lower grocery and drug costs, and AI-driven productivity gains. According to Hassett, falling energy prices could trigger negative inflation, creating "a lot of room" for the Federal Reserve—led by newly installed Chair Kevin Warsh—to cut interest rates.

Hassett also dismissed recent weak consumer sentiment data from the University of Michigan as partisan, highlighting stronger retail sales and Q2 GDP growth tracking above 4% as proof of consumer resilience.

Stocks Surge While Consumers Gloomy, WSJ

10:11AM

A stark disconnect has emerged between the U.S. stock market and the American public, The Wall Street Journal reports. On Friday, the University of Michigan reported that its consumer sentiment index plunged to its lowest level in over 70 years of tracking. Low sentiment at the start of the year worsened following the outbreak of the Iran war in late February, which caused gas prices to spike. According to survey director Joanne Hsu, high prices, a weakened labor market, and war are fueling the record gloom.

Conversely, Wall Street is thriving. On the same day, the S&P 500 secured its eighth consecutive weekly gain, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average hit another record close. Furthermore, stock valuations are exceptionally high. The S&P 500’s cyclically adjusted price/earnings ratio reached 40.8—a level surpassed only during the dot-com bubble peak around 2000.

Economists suggest three potential reasons for this historic divergence: stocks may be overvalued and due for a correction, markets might be correctly anticipating future economic growth, or artificial intelligence is simultaneously boosting corporate profits while fueling public anxiety over job security.

Retailers Deploy Dual Strategy for Split Market

3:10PM

★ HD is recommended in Dividend Investor

The growing divide between high- and low-income Americans is forcing major U.S. retailers to split their strategies. Recent quarterly results from Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT), Target (NYSE: TGT), Home Depot (NYSE: HD), and Lowe’s (NYSE: LOW) reveal a starkly bifurcated consumer environment. While affluent households, boosted by a strong stock market, continue to drive spending, lower-income families are increasingly stretched paycheck-to-paycheck by inflation and fuel prices hovering above $4 per gallon.

To capture both ends of the spectrum, retailers are simultaneously slashing prices and introducing premium offerings. Walmart is cutting prices on over 7,200 items while expanding its Walmart+ membership. Target is broadening its assortment to include more $1 items alongside pricey collectibles and premium wellness brands.

Meanwhile, housing market pressures are shifting behavior at Home Depot and Lowe’s. High mortgage rates and economic uncertainty are prompting even wealthier homeowners to defer large renovations, turning instead to smaller, affordable projects like painting and patio construction.

While overall retail sales and household credit spending remain resilient for now, analysts warn this balance is fragile. If oil prices remain high over the coming months, rising transportation costs could squeeze all consumer segments, forcing even aspirational middle-income shoppers to pull back.

What To Know About the Upcoming SpaceX IPO

9:41AM

As SpaceX prepares for - what is expected to be - the largest initial public offering in history, the tech giant will rely on old-school Wall Street methods to launch its stock, The Wall Street Journal reports. Some points of interest for investors include: 

The Old-School Shuffle: Despite SpaceX's cutting-edge tech, bankers will manually use phone calls to feverishly allocate some $80 billion worth of shares line by line, a process virtually unchanged since the 1980s.

Echoes of Meta’s (NASDAQ: META)Launch: The high-stakes role of stabilizing the stock falls to Morgan Stanley's (NYSE: MS) John Paci, the same trader who stepped in during Meta's - then Facebook - tumultuous 2012 debut when a tech glitch sent shares dropping.

Mixed Financials: Recent filings reveal a strong satellite internet business generating over $11 billion in revenue last year, but net losses grew to nearly $4.9 billion in 2025.

Ultimately, executives and bankers are aiming for an ideal scenario: at least a 20% pop in the share price on day one.

SpaceX Launches Upgraded 400-Foot Starship

7:09AM

SpaceX successfully launched its upgraded, 400-foot-tall Starship V3 rocket from its Starbase facility in southern Texas on Friday at 6:30 p.m. ET, following a postponement on Thursday.

The test flight yielded mixed results for the $15 billion development program. While the spacecraft successfully separated and reached space—despite losing one engine during ascent—the rocket’s booster suffered an engine maneuver failure and crashed into the Gulf of Mexico. The spacecraft later re-entered Earth's atmosphere, withstood intense heat, and exploded upon touching down in the Indian Ocean.

The vehicle is central to SpaceX’s investor pitch for an upcoming IPO that could value the company at $1.5 trillion or more. Designed to deploy Starlink and future AI satellites, Starship has yet to loft operational payloads. SpaceX aims to begin operational launches in the second half of the year.
