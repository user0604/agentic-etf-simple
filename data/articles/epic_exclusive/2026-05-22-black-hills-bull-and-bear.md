Black Hills Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 11:07 AM

Comments

Black Hills Corporation (NYSE:BKH) is a regulated electric and natural gas utility serving customers across eight states in the central United States. The company’s growth strategy is anchored in rate base investment, operational efficiency, and securing major load additions—most notably hyperscale data centers—while maintaining an extraordinary 56-year streak of annual dividend increases. Strategic initiatives include regulatory optimization, clean energy transitions, and a planned merger with NorthWestern Energy to further scale operations and expand its footprint.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Large-Scale Data Center Demand Catalyzes New Long-Term Revenue Streams

Data center growth is poised to structurally lift Black Hills Corporation’s revenue and earnings base through 2030 and beyond. As of the first quarter of 2026, the company reports a pipeline exceeding 3 gigawatts of prospective large-load projects, with 600 megawatts already included in its financial plan for service by 2030. Critically, Black Hills has executed an early-stage agreement to reserve equipment for a potential 1.8-gigawatt data center in Cheyenne, Wyoming, while companies like Microsoft have begun acquiring land for future facility builds in its service territory. These high-capacity, high power factor customers smooth seasonality, drive sustained rate base growth, and set the utility apart from peers with slower load adoption. With industry-leading contract structures that shield the balance sheet and mitigate stranded asset risk, Black Hills could see a meaningful shift to less weather-dependent, higher-margin regulated earnings if even a portion of this pipeline executes as planned.

2. Defensive, Consistently Growing Dividend Remains a Compelling Investor Draw

Black Hills’ unmatched record of 56 consecutive years of dividend increases cements its status as a Dividend King—offering income dependability valued by utility investors. The company declared a quarterly dividend of $0.703 per share in April 2026, targeting a sustainable payout ratio of 55–65%. This steady, growing dividend is central to the company’s capital return profile—an especially attractive trait amid volatile markets and sector consolidation. With nearly six decades of increases, management’s reaffirmed earnings guidance (adjusted EPS of $4.25–$4.45 for 2026, up 6% at midpoint from 2025) provides further confidence that dividend continuity and modest growth remain highly probable. In an environment where few regulated utilities maintain such exceptional records, Black Hills stands out for income-focused and risk-averse portfolios, particularly with its strong coverage and conservative payout planning.

3. Regulatory Momentum and Rate Base Expansion Support Predictable EPS Growth

Sustained progress on rate cases and cost recovery mechanisms underpin Black Hills’ ability to invest and deliver targeted earnings growth. The firm is currently advancing major rate reviews across several jurisdictions, including a $50.6 million annual revenue request in South Dakota Electric (based on a 10.5% allowed Return on Equity) and similar filings in Wyoming, Arkansas, and Kansas. Timely approvals and the use of riders/trackers—such as generation riders for new capacity—ensure predictable cost recovery as capital spending rises. The company manages an active cycle of three to four rate cases annually, helping align its regulatory outcomes with its $4.7 billion five-year capital plan. These regulatory strategies provide stable, forward visibility into earnings and cash flows, driving the 6% adjusted EPS compound annual growth rate targeted through 2026 and supporting dividend commitments.

4. Planned Merger with NorthWestern Energy Expands Scale and Strategic Optionality

The pending combination with NorthWestern Energy will create a more diversified, regionally powerful regulated utility with enhanced scale and growth potential. After receiving strong shareholder support and clearing the Hart-Scott-Rodino antitrust waiting period in April 2026, the merger is on track for regulatory approval and closing in the second half of the year. This transaction promises operational synergies, greater geographic/regulatory diversification, and a larger rate base—potentially enabling improved bargaining strength, more efficient capital allocation, and lower relative financing costs. With utility sector consolidation accelerating, Black Hills’ proactive approach may lead to higher strategic value, new customer opportunities, and more robust longer-term earnings potential for shareholders in the combined entity.

5. Clean Energy Initiatives and Wildfire Risk Mitigation Enhance Regulatory Alignment and Resilience

Black Hills’ ongoing capital commitments to renewables and grid resilience reinforce regulatory support and protect future returns. The company is constructing a 99-megawatt natural gas project and a 50-megawatt battery in Colorado, scheduled for completion before 2028. It also has a 200-megawatt solar power purchase agreement serving its Colorado utility, all aligning with the state’s mandate for an 80% emissions cut by 2030. Such projects position the utility for continued regulatory favor and rate base growth. Newly enacted wildfire liability protections in South Dakota (effective July 2026) and similar measures in other states substantially reduce potential outsized risks from climate-driven events, providing an important safety net for regulated returns. These actions not only lower operational risk but also enhance Black Hills’ strategic standing among peer utilities navigating increasingly complex sustainability and regulatory demands.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Ongoing Weather Sensitivity Exposes Earnings to Unpredictable Volatility

Heavy reliance on weather-sensitive natural gas sales leaves quarterly results at risk from abnormal temperature swings. In the first quarter of 2026, Black Hills’ earnings were reduced by $0.18 per share versus the prior year—primarily due to one of the warmest winters on record, which sharply curtailed heating demand. Although the company pilots weather normalization in some states and is adding large, less-weather-volatile loads, not all jurisdictions employ these protective mechanisms. This persistent vulnerability means that, until a greater portion of gross margin comes from data centers and industrial contracts, reported earnings could continue to fluctuate sharply based on uncontrollable weather patterns, creating short-term uncertainty that may challenge the company’s ability to meet guidance if adverse conditions persist or worsen.

2. Complexities and Execution Risks Related to Data Center and Large-Load Contracts Could Derail Growth Projections

Delays, contract renegotiation, or counterparty risk within major data center deals could hinder anticipated load growth and financial upside. While Black Hills has announced substantial pipeline progress with hyperscale customers, these agreements involve intricate, bespoke contract structures, long lead equipment procurement, and milestone payments from counterparties to protect against stranded asset risk. However, failure to convert preliminary agreements into definitive, revenue-generating contracts or any project cancellation could undermine a key pillar of the bullish thesis. Furthermore, successful project execution depends on regional permitting, grid interconnection, and rapidly evolving customer needs. Any misstep could slow or limit the anticipated smoothing of seasonal earnings volatility and risk falling short of management’s 2030 large-load addition targets, tapering both earnings momentum and regulatory goodwill.

3. Regulatory and Political Risks Around Large-Scale Projects Remain a Material Threat

Local opposition, political scrutiny, or unfavorable regulator decisions could impede rate approvals and slow new growth initiatives. Although Black Hills has maintained constructive relationships with public utility commissions and local governments, major load additions such as hyperscale data centers can be controversial, raising questions about resource allocation, project siting, and broader community impacts. Any shift in public sentiment or regulatory scrutiny in key territories (such as South Dakota, Wyoming, or Colorado) could delay or dilute approvals for necessary infrastructure and cost recovery. In a worst-case scenario, such dynamics may force the company to accept lower-than-expected allowed returns on equity or prevent realization of planned capital investments, jeopardizing the multi-year growth trajectory and placing negative pressure on both earnings guidance and the company’s dividend safety profile.
