Key Takeaways
Merger agreement with Amazon marks a potential inflection point for the business.
Revenue increased 17% from the prior-year period.
Management suspended forward guidance due to the pending acquisition.

Globalstar (NASDAQ:GSAT) advanced its strategic goals with a pending Amazon (NASDAQ:AMZN) merger, while posting notable revenue gains and ongoing progress in network and regulatory milestones. The period ended March 31, 2026, saw the company announce a planned acquisition and continue working to expand service capacity and upgrade its satellite constellation. Despite rising revenue, the quarter closed with persistent net losses and increased cash burn, indicating some operational challenges remain as Globalstar transitions to new ownership.

Overall, the quarter featured strong topline growth and transformational developments, but profit improvement at the bottom line remains elusive.

Notable Developments
The company entered a definitive merger agreement with Amazon, offering shareholders a mix of cash or Amazon stock (deal valued at $90 per share, subject to conditions and operational milestones).
Wholesale capacity services revenue grew 26% year over year, while commercial IoT revenue increased 13%, but legacy product lines like SPOT and Duplex saw subscriber and revenue declines.
Globalstar secured an FCC decision reaffirming exclusive rights to key spectrum, positioning its network assets as a long-term advantage and supporting the Amazon merger rationale.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management suspended all forward guidance due to the pending Amazon merger but reiterated expectations that key operational milestones and regulatory approvals will shape the final deal terms. The most important factor to watch is whether Globalstar achieves the required network expansion and operational goals tied to the merger, as a shortfall could reduce total consideration for shareholders by up to $110 million.

Quarterly Highlights

The potential Amazon acquisition, unveiled in April, would see Amazon integrate Globalstar’s satellites and spectrum with its own low Earth orbit network. Shareholders can opt for cash or Amazon stock, with the deal expected to close in 2027 pending regulatory approvals.

Wholesale capacity and commercial IoT segments delivered double-digit growth, partly offset by continued churn in legacy SPOT (satellite messenger devices) and Duplex (two-way satellite voice/data) consumer products, where subscriber counts fell 8% and 28%, respectively. Government and other services revenue nearly doubled year over year, as new contracts moved past their initial trial phases.

Capital expenditures remained high at $116.4 million, financing investments in next-generation satellite launches and updated ground infrastructure. This spending contributed to a $89 million net decrease in cash on hand, even as much of the capex is reimbursed through customer agreements.

What Globalstar Does

Globalstar provides mobile satellite services and related devices to connect users and the Internet of Things in remote areas where traditional wireless networks are unavailable.
