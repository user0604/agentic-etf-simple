Sfl (NYSE:SFL) delivered a business win in the first quarter by returning to profitability as operational discipline and selective asset sales improved its bottom line. The company is a diversified owner of shipping and offshore assets—including crude oil tankers, dry bulk carriers, container vessels, car carriers, chemical tankers, and drilling rigs—generating most of its revenue from long-term charter agreements. The quarter, reported on May 13, 2026 (fiscal 2026 Q1), saw revenue decline year over year, but cost controls and one-time gains helped offset softness in the core charter business. Overall, Sfl reported a mixed period: steady execution on its charter strategy and portfolio management, though revenue and earnings both fell year over year as the fleet shrank.

What Sfl does

Sfl operates a large and diversified portfolio of ships and offshore drilling rigs, generating revenue primarily through multi-year charter contracts with other shipping, oil, and industrial companies.

Notable developments

Time charter revenues, which make up most of Sfl’s business, dropped 14% from a year ago after vessel sales and reduced fleet size.

Voyage charter and pool revenues more than tripled to $14.7 million as the company capitalized on short-term market opportunities for some vessels.

Net income swung to $26.1 million from a loss of $31.9 million, helped by lower operating expenses and a gain on vessel sales.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management continues to emphasize stable cash flow from medium- and long-term charter contracts, noting the importance of replacing divested vessel earnings with new charter arrangements as the fleet continues to evolve. Investors should focus on the company’s progress in securing long-term contracts, managing upcoming debt maturities, and maintaining a balanced cash position in the coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The decline in time charter revenue reflected Sfl’s active portfolio management, including selling or retiring older vessels, which resulted in a smaller but generally higher quality fleet. While this reduced contracted revenue, it also trimmed operating costs and helped streamline operations.

Spot market activity increased as voyage charter and pool revenue jumped sharply. This indicates the company found new opportunities for some of its vessels in more volatile short-term markets. These gains, however, can be unpredictable compared to long-term charters.

Sfl continued to prioritize cost efficiency, with significant reductions in operating expenses due to asset sales and tighter fleet management. However, administrative costs increased and short-term debt grew, which may require additional management attention if elevated over several quarters.
