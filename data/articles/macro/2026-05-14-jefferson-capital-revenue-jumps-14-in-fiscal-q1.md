Notable Developments
Collections hit a record $309.9 million, climbing 18.8% compared to the prior year. Growth came mainly from expanded portfolio activity in the U.S. and international regions.
Total operating expenses jumped 46.8% to $95.6 million, driven by higher servicing and legal channel costs as well as increased stock-based compensation.
The company maintained its quarterly dividend at $0.24 per share and further reduced its leverage ratio to 1.79x, reflecting a stronger balance sheet.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management stated it remains confident in its position, highlighting “low leverage and ample capital resources” and a strong supply pipeline of portfolios. There was no formal financial guidance for future quarters, but leadership emphasized that elevated levels of consumer delinquency continue to support business activity. Going forward, investors should closely monitor expense trends and the pace of new portfolio acquisitions, given recent cost increases and a seasonal dip in deployments.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s revenue rose 13.9% year over year, while total collections set a new record at $309.9 million—up 18.8%. U.S. collections remained the largest contributor, but Latin America achieved the highest growth at 54% relative to last year.

Operating expenses jumped sharply, up 46.8%, with notable increases in legal costs and non-cash stock-based compensation. These rising expenses led to a decline in adjusted pre-tax income despite record collections, signaling that efficiency gains did not fully offset the step up in costs.

Portfolio purchases, or new deployments, came in at $149.7 million—a 14.6% decline from the prior year’s period. Management attributed part of this to typical seasonal patterns, while also emphasizing a forward pipeline of $353.2 million in committed purchases to help support future growth in remaining collections.

What Jefferson Capital Does

Jefferson Capital is a financial services company specializing in acquiring, managing, and recovering distressed consumer receivables from banks, credit issuers, and other lenders.
