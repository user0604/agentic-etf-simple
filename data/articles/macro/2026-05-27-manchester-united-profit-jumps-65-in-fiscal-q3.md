Manchester United Profit Jumps 65% in Fiscal Q3

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 7:12 AM

Key Takeaways
Manchester United posted higher profits and raised revenue guidance on the back of improved team performance and stronger commercial results.
Adjusted EBITDA (non-IFRS measure) was up 65.4% as revenue rose year over year.
Management lifted forecasts for full fiscal 2026 and cited further progress in cost controls and brand initiatives.

Manchester United (NYSE:MANU) returned to profitability and expanded its financial outlook, helped by increased broadcasting and merchandise sales. The global sports club posted a profit in the third quarter of fiscal 2026, advancing major brand, cost control, and on-field objectives. Results for the period ended March 31, 2026 (announced May 27, 2026) reflect both improved on-pitch performance and continued commercial momentum. Overall, the quarter balanced solid operational gains with lingering cost and foreign exchange headwinds.

Notable developments
Broadcasting revenue surged 57.1% in the quarter, driven by the men’s team higher Premier League finish and an increase in the value of international TV rights.
Commercial revenue climbed 10.3%, with merchandise sales up 36.3%, though sponsorship fees dropped due to the expiration of a key deal.
Exceptional items totaled £16.7 million, mainly related to coaching staff transition, while net finance costs increased over fivefold to £20.3 million due to currency losses.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its guidance for full-year revenue to between £655 million and £665 million and expects adjusted EBITDA of £200 million to £210 million. Executives highlighted ongoing cost controls and efficiency gains alongside continued commercial opportunities. The most important factor for investors to monitor is progress on replacing major sponsorships and sustaining higher team performance for future broadcast and merchandise earnings.

Quarterly highlights

Broadcasting revenue growth stood out, with the club’s third-place Premier League finish and higher-paying international TV rights boosting this stream’s share of total revenue to 34.3% from 25.7% in the prior year. This was directly tied to the club’s on-pitch results, as higher league placement unlocks more prize and rights income.

Merchandising and apparel sales benefited both from improved match outcomes and a one-time benefit related to in-house e-commerce operations. This segment’s 36.3% rise made it the fastest-growing commercial subcategory, partly offsetting the 9.4% sponsorship decline after the Tezos training kit deal ended.

Exceptional operating expenses played a role in holding back the bottom line, with £16.7 million linked to the departure of head coach Ruben Amorim and staff. Despite this, strong growth in core profit measures and steady operating cash inflows supported an upgraded outlook and ongoing stadium development projects.

What Manchester United Plc does

Manchester United Plc operates a professional football club, generates revenue from media rights, sponsorships, merchandising, and matchday activities, and manages the Old Trafford stadium.
