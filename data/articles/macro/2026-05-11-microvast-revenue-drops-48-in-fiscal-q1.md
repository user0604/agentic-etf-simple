Key Takeaways
Business loss as revenue nearly halved and core operating performance swung negative.
Revenue fell sharply with adjusted EBITDA turning from profit to loss.
Management anticipates a gradual recovery as new production ramps, but offers no numerical guidance for the year.

Battery maker Microvast (NASDAQ:MVST) saw its business contract sharply as delivery bottlenecks and regional headwinds in Asia hurt results. The company, a specialist in advanced battery technologies for commercial vehicles and energy storage, reported its first-quarter earnings on May 11, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter saw steep revenue declines, a swing to core losses, and only limited offset from margin discipline.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell 48.0%, driven by regulatory and demand challenges in India and Korea, plus order delays from key customers.
Gross margin slipped by 5.3 percentage points to 31.7%, as lower production volume limited the ability to cover fixed costs.
Cash on hand rose to $174.0 million, supported by short-term borrowing and working capital changes despite negative operating cash flow of $22.8 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects delivery schedules to recover and production to ramp up later in 2026 as new capacity comes online. However, it has not provided specific revenue or profit targets for the year. Investors should closely monitor progress at major expansion projects like Huzhou Phase 3.2 and whether customer platforms launch on the planned schedule.

Quarterly Highlights

The sharp drop in revenue this quarter was linked to a combination of evolving regulations, especially in India and Korea, and delays in customer project launches. These challenges outweighed the company’s prior expectation from the previous quarter that revenue would grow as Huzhou’s new battery line ramped production.

Despite the collapse in sales, the company maintained a gross margin of just over 31%, which management attributed to its differentiated technology and production discipline. This margin figure remains above the company’s full-year average from fiscal 2025, though it is 5 percentage points lower than a year ago and reflects underutilized factory capacity.

Cash levels increased compared to a year ago, but the jump relied on new borrowings and released working capital, not true operating profits. With core profitability turning negative, persistent negative cash flows will require careful attention in future quarters, particularly as investment continues in new capacity and technology innovation.
