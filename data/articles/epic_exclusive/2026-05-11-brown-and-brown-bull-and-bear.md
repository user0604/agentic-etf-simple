Brown and Brown Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 7:07 AM

Comments

Brown & Brown, Inc. (NYSE:BRO) is one of the largest insurance brokerage and risk management firms in the United States, scaling its reach through a combination of organic expansion and aggressive acquisitions. The company’s growth strategy is deeply embedded in its record pace of mergers and acquisitions (43 deals in 2025, including the transformative Accession acquisition), while targeting operational efficiencies and margin improvement as it seeks to surpass $8 billion in annual revenue.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Acquisition-Driven Expansion Fuels Rapid Revenue Growth

Brown & Brown’s aggressive M&A program is delivering outsized headline growth, positioning the firm for continued market share gains. Total revenues rose 35.4% year over year to $1.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, driven primarily by contributions from new acquisitions, especially the integration of Accession. Over the past year, Brown & Brown completed 43 deals that collectively brought in $1.8 billion in acquired revenue. This scale of inorganic growth demonstrates the company’s commitment to building a nationwide specialty platform. As the benefits of integration and cross-selling materialize in coming quarters, the broad addition of scale and specialty expertise could enhance both pricing power and customer retention in key markets.

2. High-Quality Cash Flow and Margin Resilience Support Long-Term Value Creation

Resilient margins and robust cash generation bolster Brown & Brown’s ability to invest and return capital to shareholders. In full-year 2025, the company’s operating cash flow reached $1.45 billion, up 23.5% year over year and maintaining a high conversion rate of 24.6% of sales. Adjusted EBITDAC (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, amortization, and contingent commissions) margin improved 70 basis points to 35.9% for the same period, and management raised its long-term margin target range to 32%–37%. This margin discipline—even amid large-scale integrations—enables ongoing investment in technology, talent, and further acquisitions, while supporting higher dividends (up 10% year over year in Q4 2025) and share buybacks. Consistent cash flow and expense management provide financial flexibility to weather industry cycles or invest opportunistically.

3. Synergy Potential from Accession Deal Can Unlock Significant Profit Upside

Accession’s integration and synergy realization are poised to drive meaningful profit gains in coming years. As the largest acquisition in company history, Accession added over 5,000 employees and hundreds of millions in revenue, but early integration weighed on Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 margins. Management projects $30–40 million in EBITDA synergies for 2026 alone, and expects additional integration benefits through 2028. This synergy pipeline, encompassing both cost and revenue efficiencies, could be a powerful lever for margin rebound as integration matures. Successful realization of these synergies would not only offset short-term dilution but also add permanent earnings power, justifying the deal’s up-front price and validating the bolt-on acquisition strategy at scale.

4. Sticky Contingent Commissions Provide a Profitable Earnings Buffer

High and recurring contingent commission income boosts resilience in soft organic growth periods. Contingent commissions—fees earned for placing business with insurers achieving strong underwriting results—comprised over $250 million of revenue in 2025, including a $37 million Q4 surge (over half of which came from Accession). The Q1 2026 performance also benefited from elevated contingents, driving “organic revenue with contingents” to 2.2% in an otherwise flat organic environment. While subject to volatility, these commissions have proven counter-cyclical, typically rising in periods of low claims. Their role as a recurring earnings stream offers a buffer for profitability, particularly in times of muted rate growth or slow macroeconomic conditions, supporting margin stability and cash generation for the company.

5. Disciplined Capital Allocation Balances Growth with Shareholder Returns

A balanced approach to dividends, share buybacks, and debt reduction underscores management’s focus on sustainable value creation. In Q4 2025, Brown & Brown increased its dividend by 10% (with a $0.165 per share payout in Q1 2026) and executed $100 million in share repurchases, even as it continued sizable M&A activity. The company also paid down $100 million of revolving credit, demonstrating prudent leverage management. This disciplined capital allocation signals to investors that management can pursue transformational growth without sacrificing the company’s ability to return capital and maintain a healthy balance sheet. Such flexibility is critical for adapting to changing market conditions and sustaining long-term shareholder value as the business scales.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Heavy Reliance on Acquisitions Masks Weak Underlying Organic Growth

Flat organic revenue growth highlights a risk that Brown & Brown’s core businesses are not expanding meaningfully absent M&A. For the first quarter of 2026, organic revenue growth (excluding contingent commissions) was 0%, with the full-year 2025 organic rate just 2.8%. Most headline top-line gains stemmed from large deals like Accession, raising concerns about the sustainability of current growth rates once the acquisition spree moderates. If integration drag persists or industry headwinds weigh on core client volumes, the company could struggle to deliver on normalized growth targets—particularly if market conditions soften and contingent commissions revert. Persistent reliance on M&A could also lead investors to discount headline growth, viewing it as less durable than true organic expansion.

2. Integration Costs and Execution Risks Could Depress Margins Longer Than Expected

Integration of Accession and other recent acquisitions presents significant execution risk that may continue to weigh on profitability. Margin pressure has become evident, with Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 results showing dilution from integration-related expenses, particularly those associated with Accession. Management acknowledges that full synergy realization will not occur until at least 2028, leaving multiple years of potential cost overhang and operational distraction. Delays in achieving planned efficiencies, unanticipated culture clashes, or customer attrition during integration could erode expected earnings gains. If integration complexities prove greater than expected, margin recovery could disappoint investors and threaten Brown & Brown’s upgraded long-term profitability targets.

3. Volatility in Contingent Commission Revenue Exposes Earnings to Unpredictable Swings

Heavy dependence on contingent commissions introduces a volatile and largely uncontrollable variable into Brown & Brown’s earnings model. The size of contingent commissions is closely tied to factors beyond company control, such as catastrophic loss events and carriers’ underwriting profitability. While contingents reached more than $250 million in 2025 and partially offset flat core growth in Q1 2026, management has cautioned that these revenues are inherently cyclical and may decline sharply following periods of favorable underwriting results. Downturns in contingent commissions—driven by unexpected weather events or softening carrier profitability—could quickly compress margins and make underlying earnings less predictable, undermining the company’s reputation for stable cash generation and long-term value creation.
