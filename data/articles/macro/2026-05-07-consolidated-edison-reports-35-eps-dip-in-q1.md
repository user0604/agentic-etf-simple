Key Takeaways
Consolidated Edison showed stable core business performance, but non-GAAP earnings dipped due to rising costs and share dilution.

Consolidated Edison (NYSE:ED) delivered steady underlying operations in the first quarter, but faced modest headwinds from higher costs and the issuance of new shares. The company, a major regulated utility serving the New York City area, reported results for the first quarter on May 7, 2026 (covering the three months ended March 31, 2026). Overall, the results show stable performance but also highlight cost pressures that slightly reduced earnings per share.

Notable developments
Headline net income was lifted by a $134 million after-tax gain on the sale of the company’s stake in Mountain Valley Pipeline (MVP), which does not recur in operating results.
Rising electric and gas rate bases at Con Edison’s main New York utility businesses added $29 million to the quarter’s earnings, following regulatory approval for higher customer rates.
Operating and maintenance costs at the largest business unit rose by $28 million, and new share issuance diluted earnings by $0.08 per share.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its target range for adjusted full-year earnings per share, highlighting regulatory stability and capital investment. The company is funding grid upgrades and clean energy projects, but rising costs and dilution remain key pressure points. Investors should monitor ongoing cost control efforts and possible new capital needs as infrastructure spending continues.

Quarterly highlights

The gain from the Mountain Valley Pipeline sale boosted reported net income, but adjusted profitability was flat after stripping out this one-time event. Such non-recurring gains can make year-over-year comparisons less clear for investors focusing on the company’s ongoing operations.

Regulatory rate approvals allowed the electric and gas businesses in New York and Orange & Rockland to grow their “rate base,” which is the asset pool used to set customer rates. This added about $29 million to earnings this quarter, in line with the company’s strategy of capital reinvestment.

Higher operating costs and more interest payments on debt, along with issuing 7 million new shares, partly offset these gains. The cost increases were mainly related to labor, storm response, and ongoing investment in reliability and clean energy projects.

What Consolidated Edison does

Consolidated Edison delivers electricity, gas, and steam to customers in the New York metropolitan area and invests in power infrastructure and clean energy projects.
