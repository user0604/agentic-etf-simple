Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now expects stronger cash flow and earnings in the second half of fiscal 2026 and into 2027, as newly reactivated rigs contribute full-quarter results and capital expenditures ease. The most important item for investors is the ramp of backlog conversion into revenue and the associated improvement in free cash flow, especially as mobilization payments are collected in the next quarter.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s contract backlog increased sharply, anchored by a three-year extension for the West Polaris unit with Petrobras and multi-rig contracts in the U.S. Gulf and Angola. This backlog now covers over 90% of the midpoint of revised 2026 guidance, providing a buffer for future revenue evolution.

Operational performance advanced, with the average contractual dayrate rising from $319,000 to $343,000 and economic utilization reaching 94.6%. Seadrill completed major fleet projects ahead of schedule, including the West Capella and West Jupiter reactivations, which began generating revenue late in the quarter and are expected to boost earnings in the second half of the year as mobilization payments are received.

Despite narrowing quarterly net losses and improved EBITDA, free cash flow remained negative at ($35 million). Working capital outflows and the timing of mobilization revenue collection weighed on cash generation, but these are expected to ebb in future quarters as the earnings ramp materializes.
