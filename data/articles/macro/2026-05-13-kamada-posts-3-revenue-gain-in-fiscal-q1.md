Kamada (NASDAQ:KMDA)’s start to 2026 was steady, though it faced a mix of small wins and challenges as overall growth lagged expectations for the full year. Notable Developments
Proprietary product revenue fell about 10% from a year ago, even though the distribution segment more than doubled to $9.0 million.
Gross margin, which gauges what portion of revenue is left after direct costs, declined to 42%, down five percentage points from last year, due to a shift in sales mix.
Kamada paid a $0.25 per share dividend in April 2026. As of March 31, 2026, Kamada reported cash and cash equivalents of $32.9 million and short-term investments of $40.2 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management kept its guidance for the year unchanged, targeting $200–205 million in revenue and $50–53 million in adjusted EBITDA for fiscal 2026. This means Kamada expects an acceleration in both sales and profitability in the coming quarters. Investors should focus on recovery in proprietary product sales and whether gross margins stabilize as distribution expansion continues.

Quarterly Highlights

Proprietary product revenue dropped to $36.2 million as other products offset an increase in KEDRAB sales. Meanwhile, the distribution segment grew strongly, up 125% from the prior year, thanks to expanding biosimilar launches and entry into new markets like the Middle East and North Africa.

Gross margin was squeezed due to a higher share of distribution sales, which typically carry lower profitability than proprietary drug products. Margin stability is a key concern for the year ahead.

On the operations front, Kamada received U.S. FDA approval for its San Antonio plasma collection center, bringing the number of approved sites to three. This should eventually support capacity and further growth.

What Kamada Does

Kamada develops, manufactures, and markets plasma-derived biopharmaceuticals, offering both proprietary therapies and products distributed for partners in global markets.
