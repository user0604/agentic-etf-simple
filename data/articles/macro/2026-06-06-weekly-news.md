Weekly News: Berkshire Goes Shopping; SpaceX Preps Liftoff

Ahead of the largest (and perhaps most anticipated) IPOs of all time, Berkshire Hathaway puts some of its capital to work, and the AI trade fails to impress.

The Premium Team

Jun 6, 2026, 6:00 AM

Comments
7.5

Catch up quickly on what happened in the market this week.

S&P 500 7,384 (-2.59%) • Nasdaq 25,709 (-4.68%)
Dow 50,867 (-0.32%) • Bitcoin $60,820 (-13.06%)

1. Berkshire's Buying Binge

Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE: BRKB) went on a shopping spree this week, putting some of its nearly $400 billion in cash to work. The company announced plans to buy homebuilder Taylor Morrison Home (NYSE: TMHC) for $6.8 billion, adding to its real estate empire, which already includes brokerage services, modular homes, and other offerings. The company also committed $10 billion to an $80 billion equity raise by Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG), designed to help the company finance its data center spending spree. Berkshire finished the week up 3%, while Taylor Morrison was up 22%, and Alphabet was down 3%.

"This looks like a well-run high-quality company." Jim Gillies thinks Berkshire CEO Greg Abel has found a hidden gem in Taylor Morrison, confessing he is irked he didn't find the company first. Gillies likes the deal but said it will be "hard-pressed to really make a difference" inside Berkshire Hathaway's sprawling balance sheet.
"Berkshire provides a pretty good seal of approval." Maybe artificial intelligence (AI) investing can move the needle? On Motley Fool Hidden Gems Investing, Travis Hoium, Tyler Crowe, and Lou Whiteman discussed whether Berkshire Hathaway is picking winners in the AI race.
2. IPO Race Heats Up

SpaceX set final coordinates for next week's initial public offering (IPO), planning to offer 555.6 million shares at $135 apiece, raising $75 billion. Meanwhile, the next big IPO after SpaceX is also being moved to the launchpad: Anthropic said it has filed its initial prospectus with regulators. The filing, though confidential for now, "gives us the option to go public after the SEC completes its review," Anthropic said, adding that the timing of an offering will depend on market conditions and other factors. Both IPOs are likely to easily surpass Saudi Aramco's $25.6 billion in proceeds, which is currently the largest global IPO in history.

OpenAI is on the clock: Both SpaceX and Anthropic have a jump on OpenAI in getting to market, but investors should expect a third option to surface soon. OpenAI is reportedly working with banks to prepare its own filing in the coming days or weeks, meaning the three largest IPOs in history could all launch this summer.
"Elon Musk is likely hoping the filing remains confidential." Lou Whiteman notes the awkward timing of the filing for SpaceX, which goes to market next week, touting a much less mature AI business. Between Anthropic and Alphabet, there are a lot of large companies competing for investor attention right now.
3. Broadcom: Massive Growth, but Not Massive Enough?

Broadcom (NASDAQ: AVGO) grew earnings by 54% and revenue by 48% year over year on the strength of 140% growth in sales of chips for AI. But revenue growth was slightly below expectations, and guidance was not as strong as investors had hoped. Investors were disappointed, sending the stock down 15% the day following earnings. For the week, the stock fell 14%.

"We reiterate our AI semiconductor revenue guidance to be in excess of $100 billion." On the post-earnings call, CEO Hock Tan reiterated his ambitious fiscal 2027 AI chip target, which would represent a double over this year's expected number.
Sky-high expectations: Emily Flippen believes the stock sell-off has more to do with "expectations than it does any true fundamental issues," but she's still carefully watching a number of potential data points, including software and AI chip growth.
4. Lululemon Remains Out of Fashion

It has been a rough few years for Lululemon Athletic (NASDAQ: LULU) holders, with the stock down more than 70% since late 2023. The company's latest earnings report provided little reason for optimism, as Lululemon managed modest revenue growth but reported a 35% drop in profit. Management also cut full-year guidance, saying they now expect flat to lower sales in the current quarter. Shares of Lululemon finished down 13% for the week.

No reason to rush: Jim Gillies says, "I will probably be adding to my holdings at some point," but given Lululemon's turmoil and the lack of clarity on leadership, he's content to wait it out for now.
"The threads of this growth story are starting to unravel." Sanmeet Deo says the sky is not completely falling on Lululemon, but the characteristics that made it a Rule Breaker are beginning to fray. He's keeping a close eye on this turnaround story.
5. Costco Delivers Value to Customers, Investors

Costco Wholesale (NASDAQ: COST) recorded another quarter of growth, reporting revenue up 11.6% and earnings per share up 15.2% from the same three months a year ago. Digitally enabled sales grew by 20.8%, and management sees opportunities to add warehouse locations and continue driving same-store sales growth from here. Investors seemed satisfied with the progress, sending Costco shares up 2% for the week.

"We are closely monitoring the longer term inflationary impact of higher oil prices as well as the future impacts of tariffs." Costco has a well-earned reputation as a go-to destination when consumers are pinched, but management made it clear they are not resting on past success. CEO Ron Vachris said on the call that they hope to be "the first to lower prices and last to raise them."
Strength across the board: Alicia Alfiere said Costco didn't just impress in terms of profits: Shopping frequency, average ticket, memberships, and membership upgrades were all up as well. "All of which hints at the strong valuation proposition that Costco has -- even when consumers are feeling strapped."
6. Your Turn: The Best of SpaceX

As mentioned, this week, investors will finally have their opportunity to invest in SpaceX. The company has a vast array of assets, from space and satellites to AI and even social media. Our analysts chimed in this week about what part of the business they find most intriguing, but we want to hear from you: If you could buy only one part of SpaceX, which would you choose?
