Sequans Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 21, 2026, 3:07 PM

Comments

Sequans Communications S.A. (NYSE:SQNS) designs and sells 4G and 5G semiconductors focused on Internet of Things (IoT) connectivity, spanning products for smart metering, asset tracking, e-health, defense, and industrial markets. The company’s growth strategy centers on transitioning its base of 4G design wins to next-generation 5G eRedCap, expanding high-margin RF transceiver offerings, and capitalizing on a sizable design-win pipeline while maintaining financial discipline and strengthening its balance sheet.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Large and Maturing Design-Win Pipeline Secures Multi-Year Revenue Visibility

Sequans enters the next phase with a robust backlog and an expanding pipeline converting into sales. As of Q1 2026, the company reported over $300 million in potential product revenue from its three-year design-win pipeline, with 44% already in the production phase. This figure is expected to exceed 50% by June 2026, translating to approximately $150 million in secured multi-year business. Such pipeline conversion heightens management’s confidence, drives sequential revenue growth throughout 2026, and helps stabilize future cash flows. In industries where demand visibility is often murky, this degree of order conversion significantly reduces uncertainty for both management and investors.

2. Strategic Transition Toward 5G eRedCap Positions Sequans as an Early IoT Leader

Early investment in 5G eRedCap could unlock higher margins and future-proof Sequans’ product offering. Management describes eRedCap—5G reduced capability—as the ultimate successor to all 4G IoT standards, projecting a more unified and lucrative replacement cycle. Sequans achieved its milestone of engineering test chips in Q1 2026 and targets customer sampling by the second half of 2027. With competitors in the U.S. unable to certify new Cat-1bis modules, Sequans is set to leverage its existing customer base and technical know-how, reinforcing leadership in the next major IoT deployment wave. Higher average selling prices (ASPs) and streamlined competition may sustain profitability further into the decade.

3. Nearly Debt-Free Balance Sheet and Prudent Capital Allocation Boost Financial Flexibility

Sweeping away convertible debt and managing digital assets leaves the company less exposed to capital market risk. By June 2026, Sequans expects to have eliminated $94.5 million in convertible debt by redeeming Bitcoin previously held as collateral. Following this, the company will be nearly debt-free—outside of minor government-related loans—and will maintain at least 600 unencumbered Bitcoin as discretionary capital. There are no planned equity raises for operating needs, with cost reductions and digital asset monetization supporting cash requirements instead. This financial structure increases resiliency during industry downturns and gives Sequans latitude to invest in growth or pursue opportunistic buybacks without diluting shareholders.

4. High-Margin RF Transceiver and Defense Segment Provides Diversified Upside

The RF business offers a high-margin niche that shields Sequans from commodity pressures in core IoT. The company’s RF transceiver business—strengthened through the ACP acquisition and the Iris product family—contributes revenue with gross margins approaching 99%, targeting drone and defense customers where ASPs and market growth rates far exceed typical IoT. Management forecasts $4–5 million in near-certain 2026 revenue from this segment, with global leads and new design wins under negotiation. As core IoT supply chains endure price shocks and margin compression, this high-margin diversification acts as a hedge and a profit booster for the overall business.

5. Proven Cost Discipline Accelerates Timeline to Breakeven

Ongoing expense reduction initiatives are on track, providing a credible runway to operating breakeven by late 2026. Sequans’ operating expenses fell from $12.3 million (Q4 2025) to $11.8 million (Q1 2026), driven by staff optimization, contractor cuts, and a refocus of R&D from 4G toward 5G. Management targets a sub-$10 million operating expense run rate by year-end, with plans to reach $9 million. This discipline—combined with a stabilized top line and potential licensing/service upside—positions Sequans to reach non-GAAP cash flow breakeven even before 5G eRedCap volumes scale, appealing to long-term investors seeking a clear path to self-sustainability.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Supply Chain Cost Inflation and Component Volatility Threaten Margins and Growth Rates

Sustained input price shocks could erode gross margins and delay profitability even if revenue grows. Management cites memory and component cost inflation as particularly acute, with certain memory prices rising two to three times versus historical norms. Although Sequans has secured sufficient supply for 2026, any upside scenarios force the company to procure at higher spot prices, risking gross margin contraction beyond the 37.7% reported in Q1 2026 (down from 41.4% in Q4 2025). If these inflationary trends persist or escalate, net losses may widen, and the timeline to operating breakeven could slip, especially if top-line upside does not materialize as quickly as hoped.

2. Timing Risks Around Cat-1bis Design-Win Conversion Could Cause Revenue Shortfalls

Delayed ramp-up of newer design wins introduces execution risk and potential revenue gaps. While more mature Cat-M projects are over 90% “locked in” for 2026 targets, only about 30% of projected Cat-1bis revenue was in production as of Q1 2026. Management acknowledges that any conversion delays—common in new customer programs—could shift revenue out by one to two months or more, particularly in the less mature segments. This timing risk could result in periodic disappointments versus expectations, impacting investor confidence and cash flow, especially since the pipeline conversion is a central pillar for the Sequans growth narrative.

3. Bitcoin Treasury Holdings Remain a Source of Balance Sheet and Earnings Volatility

Residual exposure to cryptocurrency introduces unpredictable swings in reported earnings and liquidity. Even after settling all convertible debt, Sequans will retain at least 600 Bitcoin as unencumbered assets, with management planning to monetize these “over the coming quarters.” Fluctuations in Bitcoin prices have already contributed to substantial non-cash losses—$29.3 million in Q1 2026 due to mark-to-market adjustments, and $11.7 million in realized losses—reaching well beyond core operational performance. These swings can mask underlying business health, complicate financial modeling for investors, and pose risks if digital asset prices weaken before full monetization, potentially undermining liquidity for near-term needs.
