Key Takeaways
CleanSpark reported a sharp downturn in core bitcoin mining revenue and profitability.
Revenue fell 24.9% year over year with significant deepening of net losses.
Management stressed progress in digital infrastructure strategy but did not provide specific new guidance for the coming quarters.

Cryptocurrency miner CleanSpark (NASDAQ:CLSK) experienced a tough quarter as its main mining business delivered much lower revenue and much deeper losses than a year ago. The leading U.S.-based digital infrastructure and Bitcoin mining company released results for its fiscal second quarter on May 11, 2026, highlighting both the ongoing pain of a Bitcoin price slump and the costs of its ongoing push into AI and high-performance computing. Overall, the quarter brought significant deterioration in mining results as well as a lack of new commercial assets contracted in AI or data center hosting.

Notable Developments
Bitcoin mining revenue fell by $45.3 million year over year as network difficulty rose and Bitcoin prices weakened.
Non-cash losses on Bitcoin asset values led to a net loss of $378.3 million, worsening from a $138.8 million loss last year.
The company doubled its contracted megawatt power capacity but reported no new signed AI or data center hosting contracts.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management continues to highlight progress in expanding its digital infrastructure and AI/high-performance computing initiatives, but did not announce new customer contracts or specific future targets. The most important thing for investors to monitor is whether these infrastructure assets can be commercialized in the coming quarters to offset ongoing volatility in bitcoin mining.

Quarterly Highlights

Mining operations faced a sharp decline in revenue as Bitcoin prices remained low and network difficulty increased following the most recent halving event.

Profitability declined as non-cash markdowns on Bitcoin holdings and increased operating expenses amplified net losses. Payroll, general and administrative costs, and professional fees all rose despite lower revenue, reflecting the upfront investment needed for infrastructure diversification.

The company reported further progress in securing land and power approvals, particularly in Texas and Georgia, and now has 585 megawatts of ERCOT-approved capacity. However, progress toward commercializing these assets--especially signing AI or data center tenants -- remains unproven, and management gave no new details about timelines or expected cash flows.

CleanSpark is a technology company specializing in Bitcoin mining and the development of digital infrastructure assets, including data centers and energy management solutions.
