Wheaton Precious Metals (NYSE:WPM) saw a standout quarter as robust metal prices and increased output powered record results. The leading precious metals streaming company holds agreements on a wide range of gold, silver, and other metal production from mining partners, aiming to provide strong cash flows with lower direct operating risk than traditional miners. The company released its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026, showing its business model’s strong leverage to higher gold and silver prices. Overall, the quarter delivered robust revenue and cash flow gains, supported by partner mine outperformance and new portfolio additions.

Notable Developments
Revenue almost doubled year over year, reflecting a spike in realized gold equivalent prices even though the number of gold equivalent ounces sold dipped 3.4%.
The quarterly dividend rose 18% to $0.195 per share, following Wheaton’s progressive dividend policy and higher operational cash flow.
Cash costs per gold equivalent ounce increased 74% to $681, while margins per ounce more than doubled, highlighting the impact of higher metal prices.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its annual production guidance, pointing to portfolio diversity and new growth projects as key strengths. The most important factor to monitor now is the integration of the large Antamina streaming acquisition and the business’s ability to sustain strong cash flows as metal prices fluctuate. Investors should also keep an eye on the expansion of “produced but not yet delivered” ounces, which can affect timing of reported revenues and earnings.

Quarterly Highlights

The steep jump in revenue and net earnings came mostly from a surge in metal prices, with average realized gold equivalent prices almost doubling year over year. Even as sales volumes slipped slightly, improved partner mine output and new streams supported a 22% increase in gold equivalent ounces produced.

Wheaton expanded its streaming agreements, closing a major deal on the Antamina mine and adding new royalty interests in Australia and Canada. The company's portfolio now covers 22 operating mines and 26 development projects, with 80% of production from low-cost sources.

Cash costs per ounce climbed sharply, mostly due to higher metal prices affecting stream payment formulas and shifts in the asset mix. However, the record cash operating margin per ounce underlines the company’s ability to capture value in a strong price environment. The quarterly dividend was raised 18% to $0.195 per share.

What Wheaton Precious Metals Does

The company provides funding to mining companies in exchange for the right to purchase streams of precious metals at fixed costs, then sells those metals into the market.
