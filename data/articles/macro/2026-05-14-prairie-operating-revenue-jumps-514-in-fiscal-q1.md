Key Takeaways
Prairie Operating posted strong revenue growth but continued to report a net loss due to non-cash items.
Revenue rose year over year, with adjusted EBITDA also seeing substantial improvement.
Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance and signaled ongoing focus on cost and capital structure.

Prairie Operating (NASDAQ:PROP) reported a significant jump in revenue, supported by increased oil production and operational execution in its key Denver-Julesburg Basin assets, but high non-cash charges kept the company in the red. The independent oil and gas operator drills and develops assets in the DJ Basin in Colorado. The results, released on May 14, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, capture accelerated production alongside lingering complexities around derivative losses and preferred equity dilution. Overall, the period showed strong operational momentum and improved financial performance, though persistent net losses and high administrative costs remain important caveats.

Notable developments
Production reached 2.1 million barrels of oil equivalent for the quarter, averaging 23,200 barrels per day with 81% of revenue driven by oil sales.
Adjusted EBITDA rose over six-fold year over year, supported by higher production and acquisitions, while net loss remained significant at $174.4 million due to non-cash derivative impacts.
Capital expenditures fell by 12.6%, as the company maintained cost discipline and efficiency gains in well drilling and completions.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance, targeting average daily production between 25,500 and 27,500 barrels of oil equivalent and adjusted EBITDA of $240 to $260 million. The company continues to stress capital efficiency, reducing preferred equity, and maintaining operational momentum. Investors should closely monitor progress on production growth and cost discipline in coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Total revenue grew over 500% quarter over quarter, reflecting the full integration of last year’s asset acquisitions and a focus on oil production, which contributed 81% of sales. Production in the DJ Basin, a prolific oil field in Colorado, benefited from operational efficiency measures, such as reduced drilling times and below-budget well costs.

Despite operating gains, non-cash charges related to derivative contracts and preferred equity still produced a net loss, totaling $174.4 million for shareholders. Much of this stemmed from unrealized losses on commodity hedging, which, while providing future price protection, affected headline results for the quarter.

The company refinanced part of its Series F Preferred Stock, which helped lower potential equity dilution. Capital expenditures for the period dropped 12.6% to $34.1 million, indicating tighter control of outflows while still executing on key drilling programs.
