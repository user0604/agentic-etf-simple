Key Takeaways
Sangamo Therapeutics reported a steep revenue decline and heightened funding risks as partnership discussions remained unresolved.
Earnings per share loss narrowed to $(0.08) from $(0.14) year over year.
Management warned that operations are only funded into the third quarter of 2026, making a near-term financing or partnership critical.

Sangamo Therapeutics (OTC:SGMO) faced a difficult quarter as revenue dropped sharply and the company's future now hinges on urgent capital-raising or partnership efforts. The biotechnology company, a developer of gene-editing therapies for diseases such as Fabry and chronic pain, released its first-quarter (Q1 2026) results on May 14, 2026. The current period saw scientific progress, but it was overshadowed by severe cash constraints and a new delisting from the Nasdaq. Overall, the quarter was marked by acute financial pressure despite continued pipeline advancement.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell to $1.4 million, down from $6.4 million a year ago, due to the absence of new milestone payments or licensing deals during the period.
Total operating expenses decreased 7.5 % to $33.4 million, with ongoing cost controls, but research and development investment remained steady near last year’s level.
The company’s cash position stood at $27.6 million, enough to fund planned operations only into the third quarter of 2026, creating a critical near-term funding need.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management stated that Sangamo’s future operations now rely on finding additional funding or closing a commercialization partnership. Without such a deal or fresh capital, the company warned it may need to cease operations or seek protection under bankruptcy law. The key event for investors to watch in the coming months is the outcome of ongoing partnership discussions for the Fabry disease program, as well as any financing developments or updates to the cash runway.

Quarterly Highlights

The sharp drop in revenue resulted mainly from the expiration of collaborative payments, especially those tied to a prior Pfizer sublicense, and no new upfront or milestone receipts this quarter. This shortfall runs counter to management’s strategic target from earlier quarters, which emphasized non-dilutive partnerships to strengthen finances.

Sangamo kept its central research programs moving forward, in particular with the continued rolling Biologics License Application for isaralgagene civaparvovec (ST-920) in Fabry disease and the activation of six clinical trial sites for its ST-503 program in chronic pain. However, leadership stressed that further clinical advancement is now contingent on securing fresh funding, increasing the risk of delays or future program cuts.

The period brought a serious setback as Sangamo transitioned its stock listing to the OTCQB Venture Market following a Nasdaq delisting due to its share price falling below minimum requirements. Management noted an appeal is planned for June, but this change in trading venue raises new challenges for investor access and company reputation.

What Sangamo Therapeutics Does

The company develops gene-based medicines that use zinc finger protein technology to treat genetic and neurological diseases.
