Key Takeaways
Build-A-Bear Workshop posted mixed results as core retail softened and profit was lifted by a one-time tariff refund.
EPS was $1.03 on an adjusted basis, down 12.0% from the prior year period.
The company now expects flat to modest revenue growth for fiscal 2026 and is monitoring demand trends closely.

Build-A-Bear Workshop (NYSE:BBW)’s business momentum softened as lower retail and e-commerce demand offset growth in commercial and international revenue. The leading make-your-own plush toy retailer, known for its in-person customization experience, announced results for its first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 28, 2026. Core profitability was boosted by a one-time tariff refund, but underlying results showed pressured sales and reduced full-year guidance.

Overall, the quarter reflected ongoing diversification, but revealed key challenges in the direct-to-consumer channel that will shape the months ahead.

Notable Developments
Net retail sales decreased 5.1% as core in-store and e-commerce demand softened, with online demand declining by over 26%.
Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes a one-time tariff refund, fell to $20.8 million, reflecting a 9.9% year-over-year decrease.
Inventory rose 7.7% to $77.8 million as the company invested in new stores and prepared for potential future sales growth.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management lowered full-year revenue guidance to $530 million to $550 million, down from a prior mid-single-digit growth goal. The company cited pressure in consumer traffic and ongoing uncertainty about demand. Investors should watch for trends in e-commerce and retail traffic recovery, as well as the real impact of new experience locations opening later in the year.

Quarterly Highlights

Retail sales and consolidated e-commerce demand both fell, with the digital channel dropping 26.1%. This was a steeper slide than the company had previously planned for, following prior year declines and ongoing pressure in the direct-to-consumer business.

The Commercial segment, including wholesale and partnership sales, was a bright spot. This area grew more than 34% and accounted for almost 9% of the quarter’s revenue, signaling the benefit of diversification efforts.

Inventory accumulation outpaced sales growth, ending the period up nearly 8% compared to last year. This rise, alongside a shrinking cash balance and higher capital spending, reflects continued investment in new stores and international expansion, especially with a focus on developing a presence in additional markets.
