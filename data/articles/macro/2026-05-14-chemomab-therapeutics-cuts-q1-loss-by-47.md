Chemomab Therapeutics (NASDAQ:CMMB) trimmed expenses and reduced its losses as it focused on advancing its lead drug candidate for liver disease. The clinical-stage biotechnology firm is developing nebokitug, a monoclonal antibody targeting inflammatory and fibrotic pathways in primary sclerosing cholangitis (PSC). In its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, released May 14, 2026, Chemomab reported a sharper financial focus and new findings but flagged that progress depends on external partnerships or new funding ahead. Overall, the period showed cost discipline alongside scientific progress, but ongoing financial constraints remain a key challenge.

Notable Developments
Net loss was sharply reduced as research and development spending fell, due mainly to completion of Phase 2 trial activities.
Cash on hand at period-end was $8.0 million, which management expects will fund operations through the first quarter of 2027.
New scientific data confirmed that nebokitug, the company's lead monoclonal antibody, impacts key fibrosis and inflammation pathways in PSC patients with or without IBD.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management highlighted a cash runway into early 2027 but made clear that forward progress will rely on securing a strategic partnership or new financing to support a pivotal Phase 3 trial. The main factor for investors to watch is whether Chemomab can reach a collaboration agreement or raise additional funds before current resources are exhausted.

Quarterly Highlights

Research and development costs declined significantly as Chemomab wrapped up activities linked to its Phase 2 SPRING trial for nebokitug, cutting its cash burn and net loss compared to the same quarter last year.

The company presented new clinical and biological data at major medical congresses, showing that nebokitug can reduce biological markers of inflammation and fibrosis in PSC, especially in patients who also have inflammatory bowel disease. These data underline the potential of nebokitug to address unmet needs in difficult liver and autoimmune illnesses.

While regulatory progress continues with nebokitug holding orphan and fast track designations, future advancement toward approval and commercialization depends heavily on forming external partnerships, as cash reserves alone will not support a large Phase 3 program.
