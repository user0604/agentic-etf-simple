Key Takeaways
Weibo saw revenue rise, but its net income dropped sharply as costs and investment losses weighed on results.
Non-GAAP earnings per share fell 24.4% to $0.34 while revenue increased by 6% year over year.
Management sees continued single-digit revenue growth, but margin pressure and weak value-added services remain concerns.

Weibo (NASDAQ:WB) delivered higher revenue but faced a sharp decline in net income as investment-related losses and rising costs pressured profitability. The leading Chinese social media platform reported its first quarter 2026 results on May 28, 2026, covering the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter showed modest top-line progress, but profitability was squeezed by higher expenses and significant non-operating losses.

Notable developments
Advertising and marketing revenue climbed 9% to $369.8 million, with non-Alibaba advertisers up 10% year over year.
Value-added services revenue dropped 11% to $51.6 million, mainly from weaker gaming-related sales.
Net income attributable to Weibo’s shareholders fell 67.6%, as non-operating losses and margin compression outweighed higher revenue.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its focus on product innovation, AI investment, and stronger industry partnerships. It still expects single-digit revenue growth, reflecting a mature user base and persistent cost pressures. Investors should watch closely whether the core advertising business can offset ongoing declines in value-added services and non-operating volatility.

Quarterly highlights

Advertising and marketing continued to anchor the business, with revenue from non-Alibaba clients rising to $326.5 million—offsetting only part of the decline in value-added services like online gaming. Growth was stronger in sectors such as internet services, auto, and local services, though much of the headline gain came from currency effects rather than underlying demand.

Cost discipline became a concern, as total expenses rose 8% while revenue grew just 6%. General and administrative costs dropped year over year, but higher ad production and marketing spending reduced operating margin from 28% to 26%.

Non-operating results had a major impact, with a $59.9 million loss stemming from declines in investment values and equity pick-up losses. This led to a bottom-line net income drop to $34.7 million, even though core operations were relatively steady. The company’s board declared a dividend of about $150 million, now shown as “Dividends payable” on the balance sheet.

What Weibo does

Weibo operates a social media platform in China, offering microblogging, video, and community engagement services to both users and advertisers.
