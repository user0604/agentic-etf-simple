Conmed: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 10:43 AM

Comments

Conmed Corp. (NYSE:CNMD) is a global medical technology company specializing in surgical devices for minimally invasive procedures, including orthopedics and general surgery. The company recently accelerated its shift toward high-growth, high-margin platforms by divesting its gastrointestinal (GI) business and doubling down on core brands like AirSeal, Buffalo Filter, and BioBrace. This streamlined focus, coupled with ongoing innovation and targeted acquisitions, is at the heart of Conmed’s long-term growth strategy as it aims to enhance profitability, drive international expansion, and deliver consistent shareholder value.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Portfolio Realignment Unlocks Higher Margins and Sustainable Growth

Conmed’s strategic exit from the GI product lines is paving the way for durable, margin-rich growth. By divesting all gastrointestinal assets in early 2026 and focusing investments on its flagship AirSeal, Buffalo Filter, and BioBrace franchises, the company has oriented itself around platforms with higher expected long-term growth and profitability profiles. Notably, adjusted gross margin improved by 100 basis points year over year to 57.4% in the first quarter of 2026, with management further guiding for 50–100 basis points of margin expansion each subsequent quarter. This shift not only simplifies operations and frees up capital and talent for continued innovation, but also reduces exposure to volatile, lower-margin product revenue, making the company’s earnings base more resilient and attractive relative to medtech peers.

2. AirSeal and Buffalo Filter Poised to Capitalize on Underpenetrated Surgical Markets and Regulatory Tailwinds

Key growth platforms are positioned to ride major industry trends in minimally invasive surgery and regulatory-driven demand. AirSeal, with an installed base exceeding 10,000 systems worldwide, saw over 50% more new capital placements in the first quarter of 2026 compared to the same period last year. Its U.S. penetration remains low — just 6%–7% of eligible laparoscopic procedures — indicating significant headroom for growth. At the same time, Buffalo Filter’s direct smoke evacuation business stands to benefit from smoke-free operating room legislation, now enforced in 20 states covering 51% of the U.S. population, with further bills pending. These platforms are expected to provide high single- to low double-digit growth rates over the long term as clinical standards evolve and hospital compliance increases, driving both revenues and improved sales mix.

3. BioBrace’s Clinical Differentiation and Early Lifecycle Position Create a Long Runway for Orthopedics Growth

The BioBrace platform offers a unique clinical value proposition with ample expansion potential. As the only U.S. Food and Drug Administration-cleared implant combining structural reinforcement and biologic healing for soft tissue repair, BioBrace continues to gain surgeon endorsement, underpinned by more than 30 published studies. Orthopedic segment sales grew 4.5% year over year in constant currency in Q1 2026, with BioBrace still in the early stages of its adoption curve. A robust pipeline, including a 268-patient randomized controlled trial completing enrollment in 2026, promises additional data-driven momentum. Endorsements from key clinical guidelines, such as the American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons, reinforce this growth, offering sustained contribution to both revenue and margin performance as orthopedic care continues to shift toward biologically advanced solutions.

4. International Expansion Provides a Counterbalance to U.S. Softness and Diversifies Revenue Streams

Rising international sales are reducing reliance on potentially volatile U.S. markets. In the first quarter of 2026, international revenue accounted for 45% of total sales, up dramatically from just 32% in fiscal year 2025. This surge was fueled by orthopedics strength abroad and reflects successful commercialization strategies outside North America. Management expects further sequential acceleration in international revenues as post-pandemic procedure volumes stabilize and core platforms gain traction. This increasing global mix not only cushions against softness in U.S. general surgery but also expands the company’s addressable market, reducing risk tied to regional fluctuations and providing a stable, diversified base for multi-year growth.

5. Strong Cash Generation and Disciplined Capital Allocation Underpin Shareholder Returns Despite Sector Headwinds

Conmed’s robust cash flow, balance sheet management, and targeted buybacks support investor confidence even in challenging environments. Operating cash flow is forecasted at $145 million–$155 million and free cash flow at roughly $125 million for full-year 2026, despite a temporary decline in Q1 due to purposeful inventory builds. Leverage is maintained at a manageable 3.1x, and the company repurchased 858,000 shares ($37.4 million) in the first quarter alone. The recent choice to refinance with bank debt rather than issuing new convertible notes — owing to historically low medtech sector valuations — signals prudent capital stewardship and a commitment to minimizing dilution. These factors, in combination with sustained investment in innovation, grant the company financial resilience as it advances its repositioning and growth initiatives.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Execution Risks Around Portfolio Transition and GI Divestiture Could Lead to Revenue Disruption

The ongoing divestiture of GI assets exposes Conmed to short-term operational and revenue risks. Transitional Service Agreements will require management attention through at least 2027, with a real risk of lost transitional revenue, delayed hand-offs, or customer attrition. The company acknowledged that Q1 2026 sales declined 1.3% year over year — primarily due to the GI exit — and total adjusted net income also fell 8.5%, with earnings per share dropping 6.3%. These disruptions may persist longer than anticipated, complicating the company’s ability to seamlessly shift resources and recapture lost volume, particularly as it works to rebuild and strengthen supplier and hospital relationships post-divestiture.

2. Macroeconomic and Inflationary Pressures Could Curtail Margin Gains and Demand Recovery

Elevated inflation, volatile input costs, and shifting healthcare capital budgets represent persistent challenges. Management indicated ongoing cost of goods sold (COGS) headwinds from rising oil, gold, and component prices, even as supply chains normalize. While efforts are underway to offset these increases through vendor negotiations and supply chain optimization, sustained cost pressures may erode planned margin improvements or require further price increases, risking demand elasticity. At the same time, hospital procedure volumes remain vulnerable to macro shocks or capital spending downturns, especially in the U.S. Any reversal of recent margin gains could undermine confidence in the company’s ability to deliver on its profitability and cash flow targets.

3. Higher Leverage and Interest Burden Post-Refinancing Could Limit Strategic Flexibility

The choice to refinance debt with bank loans instead of convertible notes raises interest expenses and balance sheet risk. Management expects adjusted interest expense to increase by at least $0.10 per share for 2026 due to higher borrowing costs tied to this shift. While current leverage sits at 3.1x and the company plans to maintain it near 3x, higher debt service obligations could crowd out investments in innovation or limit the ability to pursue strategic acquisitions. If profitability underdelivers against expectations or macro conditions deteriorate, Conmed could face tighter financial constraints, reduced flexibility in capital allocation, and diminished ability to compete aggressively with larger, better-capitalized medtech peers.
