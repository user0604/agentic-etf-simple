Universal Electronics reported another challenging quarter, with ongoing declines in both Connected Home and Home Entertainment product revenues.

Universal Electronics (NASDAQ:UEIC) struggled to reverse its recent sales slide, as both its key product categories saw lower demand. Notable developments
Total revenue dropped by 14.4%, with Home Entertainment sales falling 16.3% and Connected Home revenue down 10.7%.
Non-GAAP operating expenses were reduced by $5.3 million year over year, demonstrating continued cost-cutting efforts.
Inventory was cut by $9.8 million in the period, helping to strengthen cash discipline despite negative operating cash flow.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management continues to expect a decline in full-year revenue but maintains guidance for increased adjusted earnings per share in fiscal 2026. The company will stay focused on reducing expenses and tightly managing working capital. Investors should watch whether Universal Electronics can stabilize or grow new business areas, such as climate control products and software services, to offset legacy revenue declines.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue decreased in both major segments, with Home Entertainment and Connected Home each experiencing double-digit declines. This broad softness underscores that demand challenges remain across core product areas, not just in one part of the business.

Gross margin fell by 2.2 percentage points, highlighting the impact of sales declines and the difficulty of covering fixed costs with lower revenue. Cost-saving measures, such as moving manufacturing and reducing overhead, have not fully offset thinner margins from reduced volume.

Despite these headwinds, the company achieved a significant reduction in operating expenses and inventory. This operational tightening helped limit further financial deterioration and gives management more control moving into the remainder of the year.

What Universal Electronics does

Universal Electronics designs and manufactures wireless control products, climate control platforms, and smart home technology for consumer electronics, subscription broadcast, and HVAC industries.
