Key Takeaways
Axe Compute marked the start of its enterprise GPU services but continued to face losses as legacy business declined.
Revenue declined sharply compared to the prior year as new compute service revenue was only recognized in late March.
Management expects a jump in revenue starting in Q3 with its major contract deployment.

Axe Compute (NASDAQ:AGPU) took a noticeable step forward this quarter, launching its GPU compute services but still grappling with losses as its traditional business faded. The data infrastructure provider pivoted toward enterprise cloud GPU services, with its largest-ever contract signed in April. The company reported results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, on May 15, 2026. Overall, the quarter set the stage for future revenue but highlighted ongoing financial headwinds.

Notable Developments
Recognized its first GPU compute revenue of $7,000 in the quarter, with legacy drug discovery revenue falling to $28,000 from $110,000 a year earlier.
Signed a $260 million, 36-month GPU cluster contract in April, with $21 million per quarter expected starting Q3 2026.
Net loss widened to $7.7 million, in part due to $4.3 million in non-cash digital asset losses and elevated operating expenses linked to leadership changes.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide formal financial guidance but emphasized upcoming revenue growth from deploying its major GPU contract in Q3. It highlights confidence in liquidity, though heavily tied to digital asset valuations.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue for the period was substantially lower than the prior year, reflecting the late start for compute services and shrinking contributions from the legacy drug discovery segment. Compute services only began contributing in late March, and legacy revenue nearly faded out entirely.

The company secured its largest contract to date: a $260 million, three-year agreement to deploy 2,304 NVIDIA B300 GPUs, expected to generate $21 million per quarter once operational. This contract represents a turning point, shifting the revenue profile towards enterprise GPU compute services.

The company burned $3.7 million in operations, largely due to investments in infrastructure and a one-time CEO severance expense. Management reported $6.9 million in cash and $20.2 million in ATH digital assets but remained exposed to digital asset price swings, which led to significant reported losses this quarter.

What Axe Compute Does

Axe Compute operates an on-demand, enterprise-focused GPU compute platform for artificial intelligence and high-performance computing workloads.
