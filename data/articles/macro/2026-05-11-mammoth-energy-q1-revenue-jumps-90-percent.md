Mammoth Energy Q1 Revenue Jumps 90 Percent

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 8:14 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Mammoth Energy Services delivered a notable business improvement with a clear operational turnaround.
Revenue nearly doubled compared to the prior-year period, and profit metrics returned to positive territory.
Management raised its outlook for fiscal 2026, now targeting full-year positive Adjusted EBITDA.

Mammoth Energy Services (NASDAQ:TUSK) posted a strong turnaround in its first quarter, returning to profitability and delivering significant revenue growth. The diversified energy and infrastructure company, known for its aviation, rental, sand, and construction services, provided results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (announced May 11, 2026). Overall, the period showed broad improvements in most key business areas, though some segments still face challenges.

Notable Developments
Rental services revenue soared to $13.0 million, driven mostly by aviation equipment utilization and a one-time $6.5 million auxiliary power unit sale.
SG&A expenses dropped to $3.6 million, a 12.2% decrease, signaling improved cost management alongside higher revenues.
The sand proppant segment remained under pressure with revenue down 41.8%, as lower volumes and pricing weighed on results.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 outlook, now expecting positive Adjusted EBITDA for the year, citing momentum in the rental and aviation businesses. The company’s liquidity remains solid, though future cash flows will be closely tied to payment timing from the PREPA settlement. Investors should watch for follow-through in infrastructure services and signs of recurring profit, not just one-off gains in equipment sales.

Quarterly Highlights

The aviation and rental services segment led the turnaround, as expanded asset utilization and the major APU equipment sale created a surge in segment profit. Average aviation units rented rose sharply over the prior year, adding to predictable rental income.

Cost control contributed to margin improvements, with selling, general and administrative costs falling nearly $0.5 million year over year despite higher sales. Management pointed to meaningful cost out of the structure and a more scalable overhead base.

Weakness persisted in sand proppant and infrastructure services, where end-market demand remains uncertain and profitability lags other units. Management did not report major new contract wins in infrastructure, leaving open questions about growth under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act.

What Mammoth Energy Services Does

Mammoth Energy Services provides rental and aviation equipment, natural sand proppant for hydraulic fracturing, accommodations, and infrastructure construction services to energy and utility customers across North America.
