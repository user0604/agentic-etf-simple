Key Takeaways
Strong revenue growth driven by digital ads and core user engagement.
Revenue increased 12.4% year over year.
Management expects continued expansion, highlighting revenue guidance for the next quarter.

Live streaming platform Joyy (NASDAQ:JOYY) delivered expanding revenue led by digital advertising and user engagement in its first quarter, but profitability came under pressure from higher costs. The global online entertainment and e-commerce company operates platforms such as Bigo Live, Likee, and Shopline. The latest results, released May 26, 2026, cover the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and show the company surpassing its own top-line guidance, yet experiencing margin compression and slower profit growth.

Overall, the quarter was marked by robust revenue gains but muted profit expansion as rising expenses outweighed much of the top-line momentum.

Notable Developments
Total revenue increased 12.4% to $555.7 million, boosted by a 55.6% rise in BIGO Ads digital advertising revenue.
Profitability lagged revenue growth, as non-GAAP diluted earnings per share declined 5.9% and operating income dropped 44.1% year over year.
The company raised its quarterly dividend to $1.50 per share, signaling stronger shareholder returns for the fiscal year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management provided revenue guidance for the next quarter in the range of $562 million to $581 million, which would reflect continued year-over-year growth. They highlighted ongoing investments in AI-powered features and international expansion, noting a cautious approach in the face of macroeconomic uncertainties. Investors should closely monitor whether rising costs and margin pressure continue as the company scales its newer business lines.

Quarterly Highlights

Advertising technology remains the fastest-growing segment, with BIGO Ads revenue surging to $124.8 million, supported by rapid growth in third-party ad network partnerships. Social Entertainment, which includes live streaming and virtual gifting, posted a return to year-over-year growth at $400.4 million, fueled by both user engagement and higher spending from paying users.

While revenue from Shopline, the e-commerce software-as-a-service platform, rose 16.1% year over year, the business saw a sequential drop due to typical first-quarter seasonality in online commerce. Meanwhile, total operating expenses increased nearly 10% year over year, driven by higher sales and marketing as well as a steep rise in share-based compensation.

User engagement continued to improve as global average monthly active users climbed to 276.3 million, up 6.1% from last year. AI-generated interactive virtual gifts became a larger portion of total gifts, indicating growing monetization from new features across platforms like Bigo Live.
