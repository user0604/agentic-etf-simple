Notable Developments
Reported revenue soared to $179.1 million, almost entirely due to the one-off settlement with Moderna regarding its lipid nanoparticle (LNP) technology.
Net income turned sharply positive at $169.7 million after years of operating losses, again driven by this licensing event.
Research and development spending was cut by more than half as restructuring measures, including workforce reductions and ending in-house research initiatives, took full effect.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects to receive the cash from the Moderna settlement in July 2026, which will further strengthen the balance sheet. The company gave no new operational or financial guidance, emphasizing that future revenue depends on progressing clinical trials or further intellectual property settlements. Investors should focus on upcoming trial advancements for imdusiran and the outcome of ongoing patent litigation with Pfizer/BioNTech.

Quarterly Highlights

The first quarter was fundamentally changed by the recognition of $178.7 million in license revenue from a litigation settlement involving Moderna. This one-time transaction accounted for nearly all reported revenue for the period and led to a rare profit for the company.

Operational expenses reflected significant cost discipline, with research and development costs dropping 54% year over year as the company completed restructuring and narrowed its focus to late-stage clinical programs. General and administrative costs remained flat, as higher legal spending was offset by lower employee compensation costs.

The company achieved positive momentum for its lead product candidate, imdusiran, as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) granted it Fast Track status in April. This designation should speed regulatory review, but no new pivotal trial data or advanced clinical readouts were reported in the period.
