Key Takeaways
Fox’s revenue declined as the lack of a Super Bowl broadcast led to lower advertising sales. Adjusted earnings per share rose 20%, reflecting improved margins despite the revenue drop. Management expects digital streaming to grow but notes that upcoming live events will drive cost and revenue swings.

Fox (NASDAQ:FOX)’s results this quarter show how much major sporting events can affect its business from year to year. The leading U.S. media company focused on live news, sports, and entertainment reported its third quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on May 11, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31, 2026. Overall, the period featured stronger margins from cost control, but headline revenue fell due to the absence of the Super Bowl.

Notable developments
Total revenue dropped 8.7%, mainly because Fox did not broadcast the Super Bowl this quarter, which led to a $480 million year-over-year decline in advertising revenue.
Cost controls helped operating margins, with adjusted EBITDA up 11.4% and adjusted EPS rising 20% even as revenue fell.
Digital initiatives like Tubi and Fox One continued to expand, but the company did not share updated subscriber or growth figures for these platforms this quarter.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management remains positive about live programming and digital growth, highlighting Tubi and the Fox One streaming launch as key areas for future expansion. The company did not issue specific financial guidance, but it noted that major upcoming live sports events, such as the FIFA World Cup, will affect both revenue and costs in future periods. Investors should look for more detailed streaming metrics and watch how digital performance balances out declines in traditional TV.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue fell compared to the prior year, mainly because there was no Super Bowl broadcast in the period, but advertising at Cable Network Programming rose 5% due to higher news pricing and the World Baseball Classic. Tubi, Fox’s ad-supported streaming service, was again cited as a digital growth driver, though no new user or revenue data was provided, leaving the scale of digital transition unclear.

Adjusted EBITDA climbed 11% as Fox reduced expenses tied to major sporting events, including lower programming and production costs from not airing the Super Bowl. Segment results show Cable Network Programming’s EBITDA was nearly flat, as higher sports rights fees offset revenue gains.

Fox ended March 2026 with $3.6 billion in cash, down $1.75 billion since June, reflecting share buybacks and investments. Free cash flow was lower than last year, underscoring the importance of digital operations as a future profit source.
