Notable Developments
Revenue increased 6.7% due in part to the July 2025 acquisition of seven Tucson water systems and organic growth in connections.
Total operating expenses rose by 15.1%, with depreciation and medical costs driving the increase and contributing to a net loss for the quarter.
Monthly dividend was held steady at $0.02533 per share, maintaining the company’s annualized payout at $0.304 despite reduced earnings.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management points to an expected boost from a newly-settled rate increase in its largest system, effective November 2026, and further connection growth as supportive for future results. The company acknowledges that elevated expenses and regulatory lag remain near-term headwinds. Investors should watch for updates on the timing of new approved rates and trends in utility operating margins as rate relief comes into effect.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure climbed year over year on new customer connections and added service areas, with growth linked to both acquisition and higher consumption. Excluding the recent Tucson system acquisition, organic connection growth reached 1.9%, while a warm, dry quarter drove an almost 8% increase in water usage. These underlying trends reinforced the company's focus on expanding within Arizona’s robust growth corridors.

Higher costs, especially for depreciation (up 28%) and employee benefits, constrained operating income and pushed quarterly net results negative. The expense increase was driven by infrastructure investment in fiscal 2025 and rising personnel and utility costs, with management citing medical expenses as a notable factor. Cash flow from operations also weakened from the prior year due to lower net income and greater working capital needs.

On the regulatory front, management finalized an agreement for a $2.3 million annual revenue increase at the Santa Cruz system that will take effect later in the year, providing a path to margin recovery. Meanwhile, GW-Palo Verde committed to seek an increase in the amount of the temporary bill credit for its customers until the next rate case is resolved, following the withdrawal of the current Palo Verde rate case and a refiling set for 2027. The company continues to emphasize its Total Water Management approach and advanced metering but disclosed no major new technology initiatives in the period.

What Global Water Resources Does

Global Water Resources provides regulated water, wastewater, and recycled water utility services to residential and commercial customers in growing Arizona communities.
