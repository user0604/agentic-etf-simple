Performance Shipping (NASDAQ:PSHG)’s first quarter highlighted robust business momentum from a bigger and more modern fleet, offset by rising costs and lower per-share earnings. Notable Developments
Revenue jumped 59% to $33.8 million as the average number of vessels rose from 6.9 to 10.9, reflecting the delivery of three newbuilds and two Suezmax tankers.
Profitability metrics were pressured, with diluted earnings per share dropping to $0.26 due to higher vessel and administrative costs and a lack of last year’s gain on vessel sales.
The company secured a backlog of nearly $500 million, with around 90% of 2026 and 80% of 2027 days covered by contracts, supporting future cash flow visibility.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management indicated strong near-term visibility with high charter coverage through 2027 and highlighted liquidity of about $127 million at quarter end, set to climb further after two vessel sales. It noted robust global oil demand and high shipping rates but warned of possible volatility in trade routes due to geopolitical developments. Investors should watch cost trends, charter renewals, and debt as the global orderbook continues to expand.

Quarterly Highlights

The expansion of the fleet was a major driver in the period. With 11 vessels operating by quarter-end, the company capitalized on strong shipping markets as shown by higher average daily charter rates and nearly full vessel utilization at 99.7%.

Profitability softened compared to the prior year’s quarter, which included a nonrecurring asset sale gain. This year, vessel operating and administrative expenses climbed more quickly than charter rates, putting pressure on per-share results despite higher overall net income if excluding the prior year’s one-time gain.

The contract backlog now covers most of the company’s days through the next two years, which supports visibility in an uncertain shipping environment. With a growing capital commitments for new vessels and a larger debt balance, careful management of expenses and leverage will be crucial.

What Performance Shipping Does

Performance Shipping owns and operates a fleet of crude oil and product tankers, providing global seaborne transportation services to energy companies and traders.
