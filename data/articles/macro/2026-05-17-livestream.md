China Pledges $17B of U.S. Agriculture Buys

4:45PM

China has agreed to purchase at least $17 billion in U.S. agricultural products over the next three years, The White House has announced. The development marks a potential thaw after escalating tariffs caused U.S. agricultural exports to China to plummet nearly 66% in 2025 to $8.4 billion. China will also restore market access for select U.S. beef and poultry exports, and both nations plan to establish new trade and investment boards.

The deal offers a potential lift to industries burdened by years of trade volatility and shrinking Chinese demand. If purchases materialize, investors could see renewed momentum in U.S. agricultural producers, grain traders, meat exporters, and transportation companies. Specifically, businesses tied to soybeans, beef, poultry, fertilizer, and farm equipment may benefit from stabilized economic relations.

Inflation May Force Fed Rate Hike, Gundlach

2:20PM

DoubleLine Capital CEO Jeffrey Gundlach warned that stubborn inflation and surging commodity prices could force the Federal Reserve to implement another interest-rate hike. Speaking Sunday on Fox News, Gundlach stated that DoubleLine’s model projects the next CPI reading will exceed 4%.

He noted that negative real interest rates have fueled widespread market speculation, contributing to the S&P 500 doubling over the past four years. While many investors anticipated 2026 rate cuts, Gundlach argued that Treasury-market signals point toward a hike instead.

Consequently, he views commodities as an attractive alternative, noting the Bloomberg Commodity Index has surged roughly 28% year-to-date. Gundlach remains bullish on energy and industrial metals but neutral on gold. He also cautioned against expanding private credit into retail products, warning of liquidity risks for hard-to-sell underlying loans.

Swatch, AP Collaboration Sparks Chaos

2:04PM

Swatch (OTC SWAGAY) temporarily closed more than 30 stores worldwide on Saturday after chaotic crowds gathered for the launch of its "Royal Pop" pocket watch collection.

The $400-to-$420 bioceramic timepieces, a collaboration with luxury watchmaker Audemars Piguet, drew massive lines from New York to London, leading to police intervention and at least one arrest. Although Swatch emphasized that the watches are not limited editions, shoppers camped out overnight.

The frenzy underscores the growing power of "accessible luxury" collaborations, where premium brands partner with mass-market companies to generate social-media buzz. Fueled by this speculative demand, the watches quickly surfaced on eBay (NASDAQ: EBAY) at heavily inflated prices. Ultimately, the collaboration offered consumers a rare opportunity to buy into the Audemars Piguet aesthetic, a luxury brand where entry-level prices typically start at around $20,000.

Cruise Bookings Surge Despite Virus Scares, AP

12:28PM

Despite recent headlines surrounding hantavirus and norovirus outbreaks on cruise ships, consumer demand for cruise vacations remains resilient, Associated Press reports. According to travel analysts and industry data, bookings continue at a record pace.

This strong momentum is a positive indicator for major operators like Carnival Corporation (NYSE: CCL), Royal Caribbean (NYSE: RCL), and Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings (NYSE: NCLH). The Cruise Lines International Association projected that 38.3 million passengers will take ocean cruises this year, marking a 4% increase from last year’s record. Furthermore, travel marketplace CruiseCompete.com reported that cabin bookings surged nearly 32% year-over-year during the first half of May, while Viking (NYSE: VIK) noted that most of its 2026 sailings are already reserved.

Analysts state that the industry is less vulnerable to short-term news scares because trips are typically booked months in advance, making cancellations rare. Furthermore, cruise lines are successfully capturing younger demographics—particularly Gen Z and millennials—and budget-conscious travelers by offering shorter itineraries that emphasize value compared to traditional land-based hotel vacations. Driven by this sustained demand, operators continue to expand capacity and order new ships.

Middle East Conflict Fuels Coal Comeback

8:35AM

The ongoing war with Iran has triggered a global resurgence in coal usage after effectively closing the Strait of Hormuz and cutting global liquefied natural gas (LNG) supplies by 20%.

With Asian LNG imports hitting a six-year low, countries are turning to coal as a reliable energy hedge. Taiwan is restarting idled coal plants, while South Korea increased its coal-generated electricity by over 35% recently. India has also issued an emergency directive to maximize coal plant output ahead of summer.

The shift has driven Australian benchmark spot coal prices up 12%, briefly surpassing $140 a metric ton in mid-March. While European nations like Italy and Germany keep coal plants on standby, the surge threatens to delay global climate goals, as burning coal emits double the carbon dioxide of natural gas.

Manufacturers Stockpile as Supply Stress Soars

8:05AM

Global manufacturers are stockpiling raw materials at the fastest pace since the pandemic, racing to secure supplies as the war with Iran disrupts global supply chains.

This purchasing surge pushed the GEP Global Supply Chain Volatility Index to 1.64 in April—its highest level since October 2022. While the stockpiling boosted headline economic data, including the U.S. Manufacturing PMI to 54.5, experts warn it masks underlying weakness in demand and is driving up global transportation costs.

The crisis is fueling broader inflation, with U.S. consumer prices rising 3.8% in April and Eurozone inflation accelerating to 2.5% due to soaring energy costs. The European Central Bank has warned of stagflation risks, prompting Germany to slash its 2026 growth forecast to 0.5% as global oil inventories rapidly deplete.

U.S. electricity providers NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE) and Dominion Energy (NYSE: D) are in talks to combine in a mostly stock-based deal that could create a utility behemoth valued at over $400 billion, The Financial Times reported Saturday. 20 Deaths in Japan Reportedly Linked to Amgen's Tavneos

8:45AM

★ AMGN is recommended in Stock Advisor

Twenty people in Japan have died after receiving Amgen’s (NASDAQ: AMGN) rare disorder therapy, Tavneos, prompting its local partner, Kissei Pharmaceutical, to urge healthcare professionals to stop prescribing the drug to new patients.

According to a report by The Wall Street Journal, at least 22 patients also developed potentially fatal liver injuries. In response, Kissei has advised doctors to refrain from initiating new treatments and to carefully assess whether existing patients should continue therapy.

An Amgen spokesperson noted that a causal link between the drug and the mortalities has not been established in all 20 cases, which occurred out of more than 8,500 patients treated in Japan.

Tavneos, which treats ANCA-associated vasculitis, recently faced regulatory scrutiny in the U.S., where the FDA sought to pull the product from the market in April over safety concerns and potential data manipulation.
