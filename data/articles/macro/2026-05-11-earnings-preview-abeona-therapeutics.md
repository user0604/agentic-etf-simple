Earnings Preview: Abeona Therapeutics

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 5:03 PM

Comments

Abeona Therapeutics (NASDAQ:ABEO), a biotechnology company focused on gene and cell therapies for rare diseases, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Wednesday, May 13, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

The fourth quarter of 2025 marked the commercial launch of ZivaSkin (also known as ZEVASKYN), the first autologous cell-based gene therapy approved for treating recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (RDEB), a rare genetic skin disorder. The company treated its first commercial patient in December 2025, following a delayed launch due to additional sterility testing requirements. Total revenue for the year ended Dec. 31, 2025, was $5.8 million, including $2.4 million in product revenue from ZivaSkin and $3.4 million in license and milestone payments. Net income for 2025 was $71.2 million, driven primarily by a $152.4 million gain from selling a rare pediatric disease priority review voucher. Excluding this one-time gain, the company remained unprofitable on an operating basis. The results met analyst expectations for the quarter, with no significant surprises relative to consensus.

Positives
First commercial patient treated with ZivaSkin: The company achieved a major milestone by treating its first patient in December 2025, confirming the product's transition from development to commercial use.
Strong payer coverage established for ZivaSkin: All major commercial insurers and all state Medicaid programs have published coverage policies, supporting future revenue growth and reducing reimbursement risk.
Growing pool of eligible patients identified: The number of identified patients eligible for ZivaSkin has doubled since launch, now exceeding 100, which helps build a foundation for future sales.
Significant cash position: With $191.4 million in cash and investments at year-end, the company is well-funded to support its commercial rollout and ongoing operations.
Negatives
Slow initial launch ramp for ZivaSkin: Only two patients were treated since launch, and the pace of onboarding new treatment centers (Qualified Treatment Centers, or QTCs) has been gradual, limiting early product revenue.
High operating expenses: Selling, general, and administrative expenses rose to $65 million for the year, reflecting the costs of commercializing ZivaSkin and building out the company’s infrastructure.
Reliance on one-time gain for profitability: Net income for 2025 was driven by the sale of a priority review voucher, not by recurring product sales or operations, highlighting the need for sustainable revenue growth.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Pace of ZivaSkin patient treatments and biopsies: Investors should watch for updates on the number of patients treated and biopsied in Q1, as this is a key indicator of commercial momentum. Management previously indicated that treatment timelines are expected to improve as centers gain experience.
Expansion and activation of Qualified Treatment Centers: Progress toward the goal of having at least seven active QTCs by year-end will be important. The onboarding process for new centers is lengthy, but additional activations are expected in Q2 and beyond.
Gross margin trends as payer mix evolves: The initial product revenue came from a Medicaid patient, but as more commercially insured patients are treated, average net revenue per treatment and gross margins are expected to improve. Investors should look for commentary or data on shifts in payer mix and margin improvements.
Updates on manufacturing capacity and reliability: Management reported that the company is currently able to manufacture for six patients per month, with plans to scale to ten per month by the second half of 2026. Any changes or challenges in manufacturing capacity or sterility testing could impact the commercial ramp.
Status of preclinical pipeline and registry studies: While most resources are focused on ZivaSkin, Abeona is required to conduct a registry study for post-approval monitoring. Any updates on preclinical ophthalmology programs or progress toward first-in-human trials may also be discussed, though major spending in these areas is not expected in 2026.
