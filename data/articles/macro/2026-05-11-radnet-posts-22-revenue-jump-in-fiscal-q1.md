RadNet posted a strong quarter as patient imaging volumes surged and its facility network expanded. The leading U.S. outpatient diagnostic imaging provider achieved record revenue and continued momentum in digital health, despite some margin pressure from investment and one-time events. The results, released May 11, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, prompted management to raise full-year guidance for its core Imaging Center business. Overall, the quarter showed broad-based operational progress, with sustained growth in advanced imaging procedures and ongoing digital health integration.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 22.1 % year over year, hitting $575.6 million as overall imaging demand and acquisition contributions lifted results.
Digital Health revenue grew 51.5 %, and annual recurring revenue more than doubled to $96.9 million, but segment adjusted EBITDA slipped due to ongoing investments.
Cash on hand fell to $455.3 million, down from $767.2 million, reflecting acquisition spending and facility growth; leverage remained moderate.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased its full-year fiscal 2026 Imaging Center revenue, adjusted EBITDA, and cash-flow outlook, citing strong demand and new acquisitions, but left Digital Health guidance unchanged despite rapid sales growth. The most important thing to monitor is whether margin improvement and profitability can keep pace with revenue gains in both core and digital segments, especially as investments accelerate.

Quarterly Highlights

Imaging Center operational progress was marked by a 19.7 % jump in advanced imaging volumes across MRI, CT, and PET/CT procedures, fueled by both new acquisitions and robust same-center growth. Severe winter weather caused some disruption early in the quarter, but strong March volumes helped offset lost revenue and earnings. The facility network grew to 435 outpatient centers, as RadNet accelerated acquisitions in Indiana, Florida, and other growth markets.

In Digital Health, revenue rose 51.5 % year over year, and annual recurring revenue climbed to $96.9 million, boosted by new contracts and the recent acquisition of Gleamer, an AI provider. More than $16 million in new DeepHealth business was signed during the quarter, with management estimating that by year-end, over 70 % of studies could benefit from clinical AI solutions. However, the segment’s adjusted EBITDA fell as the company continued to invest in infrastructure and absorbed short-term losses from recent acquisitions.

Cash and equivalents dropped as RadNet funded acquisitions and facility expansion, but the company kept its net debt to Adjusted EBITDA ratio below 2.0 times, providing continued capital flexibility. Capital expenditures for the quarter totaled $69.9 million, largely directed toward expansion and technology investments.

What RadNet Does

RadNet operates the largest network of outpatient diagnostic imaging centers in the United States, offering MRI, CT, mammography, ultrasound, and digital health solutions to patients and healthcare providers.
