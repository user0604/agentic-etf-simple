Fortis Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 7:06 AM

Comments

Fortis Inc. (NYSE:FTS) is a North American electricity and gas utility holding company operating in Canada, the United States, and the Caribbean. With more than 99% of its assets in rate-regulated utilities, Fortis pursues stable, long-term growth through disciplined investment in its regulated rate base—enabling decades of consecutive dividend increases. The company’s ongoing strategy centers on large-scale capital programs, operational efficiency, and a commitment to grid modernization and sustainability.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Decades-Long Dividend Growth Track Record Supports Ongoing Shareholder Value Creation

Fortis’s status as a Dividend King with over 50 consecutive years of annual dividend increases emphasizes a culture of consistent value delivery for shareholders. In 2026, the company declared a quarterly dividend of CAD $0.64 per share and guided for 4–6% annual dividend growth through 2030. The company has maintained an adjusted dividend payout ratio of approximately 70%, underpinned by highly predictable, regulated utility cash flows. This ongoing commitment backed by sustainable free cash flow and a prudent capital allocation policy signals to investors that management prioritizes reliable total returns—a major differentiator in an uncertain macro environment.

2. Robust, Multi-Year Regulated Capital Plan Drives Predictable Earnings and Rate Base Growth

Fortis’s $28.8 billion five-year capital expenditure plan—its largest ever—fuels an expected 7% annual rate base expansion and supports continued earnings momentum through the end of the decade. The majority of planned investments are low-risk, grid-focused projects that are already rate-regulated, minimizing execution and regulatory risk while enabling organic growth. Over the three years leading up to 2025, annual average rate base and earnings per share (EPS) growth exceeded 6%. Regulators in supportive jurisdictions like Arizona and Ontario have approved cost recovery and allowed returns, underpinning steady earnings streams and the capacity to absorb capital program costs without undue pressure on the balance sheet or dividend payout.

3. Diversified Geographic Footprint and Customer Base Mitigates Localized Risk and Enhances Stability

The company’s operations span multiple utilities across various provinces, U.S. states, and the Caribbean, serving millions of customers and reducing exposure to single-market shocks. Recent divestments in Belize and Turks & Caicos slightly reduced Caribbean exposure but did not materially impact overall diversification or earnings quality. Growing data center demand in U.S. service territories like Arizona offers additional growth avenues, while regulatory frameworks in Canada and much of the U.S. continue to favor grid modernization and electrification—a trend that aligns with long-term secular growth drivers.

4. Prudent Financial Management Maintains Investment-Grade Balance Sheet Amid Sector Headwinds

Fortis has preserved strong investment-grade credit ratings and liquidity while executing its ambitious capex plan, allowing continued access to low-cost capital and resilience through macro volatility. As of Q1 2026, the company had nearly $4 billion available on its credit facilities. S&P reaffirmed an A- issuer rating and BBB+ on senior unsecured debt during 2025, and the company continues to fund growth largely from operating cash flow and utility-level financing—not dilutive equity issuance. Stable funding enables the company to withstand inflation and rate pressures while maintaining its dividend and supporting strategic investments in climate resiliency and grid infrastructure.

5. Strategic Positioning for Secular Growth Trends Supports Long-Term Relevance and Opportunity

The company is well-positioned to benefit from the North American energy transition, with capital deployment focused on grid resiliency, decarbonization, and meeting electrification demand. Fortis’s near-term pipeline includes major transmission projects, large-scale data center connections, and approval for $300 million in incremental LNG infrastructure in British Columbia. Early adoption of artificial intelligence and grid-enhancing technology helps the company manage operating costs, enhance service reliability, and stay ahead of sector peers on innovation, supporting both regulatory goodwill and future growth prospects.

Potential Bear Cases
1. High Dividend Payout and Leverage Leave Less Room for Downturns or Accelerating Capex

Fortis’s dividend payout ratio near 75% of net income and debt-to-capitalization at 57% remain at the upper end of sector sustainability thresholds, which could constrain financial flexibility if growth slows or macro headwinds intensify. While cash flows are currently predictable and free cash flow covers the dividend, the company’s multi-year capital intensity and lack of share repurchases indicate most excess capital is allocated to maintaining the dividend and funding regulated investments. Any regulatory changes, adverse rate case outcomes, or unexpected cost overruns—coupled with persistently high interest rates—could force a reassessment of the payout policy, limiting upside for total shareholder returns or growth reinvestment.

2. Dependence on Supportive Regulation Exposes Earnings to Political and Policy Shifts

Roughly 99% of the company’s asset base relies on regulatory decisions regarding allowed returns, capital approvals, and cost recovery mechanisms—making Fortis vulnerable to shifts in regulatory climate or policy priorities. Even as recent rate case outcomes in Arizona and Canada have been favorable, the utility industry faces ongoing review of formula rates, return-on-equity, and environmental mandates. Any significant reduction in allowed returns or delays in rate base expansion could erode the business’s fundamental earnings growth proposition. This risk is amplified for long-duration capital projects, where regulatory lag or politicization of energy costs might delay approved returns or introduce unanticipated costs not fully recoverable via rates.

3. Foreign Exchange Volatility Could Erode Returns for Non-Canadian Investors

The company’s dividend and earnings in Canadian dollars are exposed to CAD/USD exchange rate swings when measured by U.S. or global investors, potentially offsetting stable local-currency returns with currency headwinds. In 2025, currency movements benefitted reported earnings by $0.08 per share for U.S.-based shareholders, but this factor remains inherently volatile and cyclical. While some natural hedging occurs through cross-border operations, sustained weakness in the Canadian dollar versus the U.S. dollar could knock several percentage points off total return, creating a headwind for diversification-minded investors who require global currency exposure to be stable or appreciating.
