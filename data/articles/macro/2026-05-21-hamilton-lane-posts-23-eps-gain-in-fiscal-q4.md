Key Takeaways
Hamilton Lane delivered steady gains in its core fee and asset management business, but saw incentive fees fall sharply. Non-GAAP earnings per share climbed 23% year over year in the quarter. Management raised the dividend and emphasized continued strength in recurring fee revenues but gave no formal quantitative guidance.

Private markets investment specialist Hamilton Lane (NASDAQ:HLNE) continued to show underlying strength in its core business, even as volatile incentive fees pulled total revenue lower for the period. The firm, a global leader in managing private equity and alternative investment assets for institutions and high net worth clients, reported fiscal fourth quarter results on May 21, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. Overall, the quarter showed success in expanding recurring fee streams and client base, but headline profit drivers remained unpredictable due to the sharp drop in incentive fees.

Notable Developments
Recurring management and advisory fees rose 21% while total revenue fell as incentive fees declined 45% over the same quarter last year.
Assets under management reached $141.8 billion, up 3% from the prior year, with fee-earning AUM growing 13%.
The quarterly dividend increased to $0.60 per share, and share repurchase authorization was renewed at $100 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management highlighted continued growth in recurring fee revenue, stable fee rates, and strong client retention, while noting the unpredictable nature of incentive fees as a source of earnings swings. The company remains focused on scaling specialized funds and evergreen products while managing expense growth. Investors should watch for further developments in fee-earning asset inflows and the impact of expense discipline on future margins.

Quarterly Highlights

Management and advisory fees, the core recurring income for the business, grew to $155.2 million in the period, reflecting both client expansion and a shift toward specialized fund products. Specialized funds saw particularly strong inflows, with fee-earning assets under management in these vehicles rising 24% year over year, compared to a 4% increase in customized separate accounts.

Incentive fees, which represent earnings when investment funds exceed performance benchmarks, fell significantly in the quarter to $38.4 million, down 45% year over year. Management emphasized that these fees are "unpredictable" due to timing of asset realizations, reinforcing the variable nature of this income stream.

The board increased the quarterly dividend to $0.60 per share, reflecting an 11% full-year rise in dividend payouts. The company also boosted its share repurchase program to $100 million, signaling confidence in its balance sheet and future cash flows.
