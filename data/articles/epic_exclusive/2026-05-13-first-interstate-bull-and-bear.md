First Interstate Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 3:08 PM

Comments

First Interstate BancSystem, Inc. (NASDAQ:FIBK) is a regional banking company operating across 10 contiguous states, focused on delivering community banking services with a personal touch. In recent years, the company has pursued a growth strategy centered on optimizing its branch network, improving efficiency through organizational redesign, and concentrating capital on high-potential markets while maintaining attractive shareholder returns through dividends and share buybacks.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Strong Capital Position Enables Strategic Flexibility and Shareholder Returns

Elevated capital ratios provide a foundation for both growth and generous capital return programs. First Interstate ended the fourth quarter of 2025 with a Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) ratio of 14.38%, up 48 basis points quarter over quarter. This level stands well above regulatory requirements and many regional bank peers, giving management flexibility to repurchase shares, maintain a sizable dividend, and selectively reinvest in core markets. With $180 million of authorized share buyback capacity remaining as of early 2026 and a steady quarterly dividend of $0.47 per share (annualized yield of approximately 5.7%), the company signals its confidence in underlying earnings and balance sheet resiliency. A robust capital base is crucial for weathering economic volatility and opportunistically growing or consolidating assets when market conditions turn favorable.

2. Net Interest Margin Expansion Supports Sustainable Earnings

Positive net interest margin (NIM) trends differentiate the company from industry peers facing margin compression. In the fourth quarter of 2025, First Interstate reported a fully tax equivalent NIM of 3.38%, marking a year-over-year increase of 18 basis points and the third straight quarterly improvement. This upward trend continued into early 2026, bucking a sector-wide headwind from rising funding costs. Management credits disciplined repricing of loans and low-cost, granular deposit funding for cushion against rate volatility. Persistent NIM expansion supports steady core earnings, helping offset weaker fee income or volume-driven revenue. If current spread management continues, the company could exceed its target of a 3.5% NIM by year-end 2026, providing an earnings tailwind even without strong loan growth.

3. Efficiency Initiatives Drive Cost Savings and Enhance Profitability

Organizational redesign and branch network optimization are expected to reduce expenses and improve operational leverage. In late 2025 and early 2026, management undertook a significant restructuring—exiting five lower-growth states, consolidating additional branches, and streamlining management layers in favor of a flatter, state president-led model. While restructuring costs modestly inflated fourth-quarter 2025 expenses, underlying cost trends are expected to be flat or slightly lower through 2026, with a targeted expense run rate of $159–160 million per quarter. Such discipline allowed First Interstate to beat EPS expectations in the first quarter of 2026 despite consecutive quarterly revenue declines, and positions the company for margin expansion once loan or fee growth resumes. Consistent execution on efficiency can help protect or bolster returns even in a sluggish revenue environment.

4. Focused Geographic Strategy Concentrates Resources on Highest-Potential Markets

Branch sales and redeployment efforts sharpen the company’s competitive edge in attractive geographies. By completing branch divestitures in Arizona, Kansas, Nebraska, North Dakota, and Minnesota, First Interstate is now concentrated in 10 contiguous states, improving density and efficiency. Proceeds from these sales have been redeployed into share buybacks and targeted expansion in high-opportunity markets such as Colorado and Montana. This shift supports organic growth ambitions while minimizing the complexity and overhead of peripheral markets. With nearly $1 billion in lending capacity available given a loans-to-deposit ratio of just 68.8% at the end of 2025, the company is poised to accelerate loan production as conditions normalize and local market pipelines improve through 2026 and beyond.

5. Attractive Dividend Yield and Book Value Growth Appeal to Value Investors

The company offers a compelling combination of high dividend yield and rising tangible book value. First Interstate maintained its quarterly dividend of $0.47 per share for the first quarter of 2026, providing one of the highest yields among regional peers (annualized at approximately 5.7%). At the same time, tangible book value per share increased by 2.9% in the fourth quarter of 2025, even as capital was returned to shareholders and balance sheet risk was reduced. This dual track of income and intrinsic value growth strengthens the case for investors seeking total return, while the company’s prudent capital allocation and low payout ratio help to support the sustainability of the dividend over time.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Sustained Revenue Contraction and Loan Shrinkage Threaten Growth Prospects

Persistent declines in core revenue and loan balances raise concerns about the company’s ability to drive future expansion. First Interstate reported a 2.7% year-over-year drop in revenue for the first quarter of 2026, following a 4.1% decline in the previous quarter. Loan balances have continued to shrink due to both intentional run-off of non-core assets and sluggish new production in a competitive environment. Although management anticipates stabilization and a potential return to growth later in 2026, repeated sales and lower earning asset levels may constrain organic revenue prospects. Without a clear reversal in these trends, market share loss or operating deleveraging could weigh on long-term profitability and valuation.

2. Overreliance on One-Time Gains and Cost Cuts May Obscure Underlying Weakness

Nonrecurring branch sale gains and aggressive expense management have masked softer core earnings and may not be repeatable. Much of the earnings growth in late 2025 stemmed from a $62.7 million after-tax gain on branch divestitures, rather than growth in sustainable net interest or fee income. While efficiency improvements have protected bottom-line results, the fundamental drivers—ongoing NIM and fee growth, balance sheet expansion, or robust service revenues—remain pressured. If further revenue headwinds persist and the company exhausts expense levers, future quarters could see earnings and returns underperform versus current expectations, limiting upside for shareholders.

3. Macro and Industry Risks Could Erode Margin and Credit Quality Tailwinds

Shifts in the interest rate environment or unexpected credit deterioration could reverse recent financial improvements. Net interest margin expansion has been a key support for earnings, but the pace is heavily dependent on interest rate trends and successful redeployment of capital into higher-yielding loans. Any abrupt decline in rates, increased competition, or deposit runoff could compress NIM, especially as asset growth has recently stagnated. Additionally, while credit quality has stayed benign, with declining criticized and non-performing loans in late 2025, sector-wide risks related to commercial real estate or consumer portfolios could emerge with little warning. Rising charge-offs or reserve builds would directly undermine both profitability and capital returns, challenging the company’s outlook if economic or sector conditions deteriorate.
