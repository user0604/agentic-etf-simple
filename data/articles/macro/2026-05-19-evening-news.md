1. Cava Beats on Traffic, Raises Outlook

Cava Group (NYSE: CAVA) just dropped a feast of a first quarter: Revenue surged 32% to $438.3 million, blowing past estimates, while same-store sales jumped 9.7% (nearly double the 6% expected). Management promptly raised full-year guidance. "Amid today's broader macroeconomic environment and geopolitical uncertainty, our first quarter results reflect our position as a clear industry leader and our ability to meet the moment for the modern consumer," CEO Brett Schulman said. CAVA is recommended in Rule Breakers.

Customers actually showing up: The comp beat was traffic-led, not price-driven -- meaning real humans are choosing Cava over the competition, not just paying more for the same bowl.
Great guidance: Full-year same-store sales guidance lifted to 4.5% to 6.5% (up from 3% to 5%), and adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) guidance rose to $181 million to $191 million. Plus, Cava opened 20 net new restaurants in Q1, including first bites in Columbus, Ohio; Cincinnati; and St. Louis.
2. Google Wants to Whisper AI in Your Ear

Alphabet's (NASDAQ: GOOG) Google, recommended by both Team Rule Breakers and Team Hidden Gems, just gave Gemini a fashion upgrade. At its I/O developer conference, Google unveiled audio smart glasses with Samsung (OTC: SSNLF), Rule Breakers rec Warby Parker (NYSE: WRBY), and Gentle Monster -- hitting stores this fall, with Gemini whispering directions, messages, and answers straight into your ears.

Meta (NASDAQ: META) sold 7 million Ray-Ban AI glasses last year: That's proof that people -- Fools among them -- will absolutely wear a computer on their face. "I think this wearable AI is exciting and I am definitely hopping on the train. I think this will be the next new trend," TMFPrestonFreeman wrote recently.
The demo was peak sci-fi casual: A product manager DoorDashed (NASDAQ: DASH) coffee, checked her texts, and added a calendar event -- all hands-free, all through Gemini. Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) is reportedly working on its own smart glasses, too, so this party is just getting started.
3. SEC Moves to Unclog IPO Pipeline

The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) wants to throw open the initial public offering (IPO) gates. Two new proposals from Chair Paul Atkins would slash the red tape that keeps smaller companies out of public markets -- and potentially put more early-stage growth stories within reach of everyday investors. Not every investor is rushing the gate, though. Stock Advisor member PhazFool wrote that they avoid IPOs "like the plague," adding, "If it's doomed, I'd rather not be the founder's exit liquidity."

The velvet rope is coming down: Companies could file for shelf offerings the moment they go public, skipping the current 12-month waiting period, the biggest shake-up to public equity raising in about 20 years.
Less paperwork, more runway: The five-category filer maze shrinks to two, companies under $35 million in assets get extended reporting deadlines, and newly public firms get a five-year grace period before the heaviest compliance requirements kick in.
