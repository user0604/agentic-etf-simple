Houlihan Lokey: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 20, 2026, 10:14 AM

Comments

Houlihan Lokey (NYSE:HLI) is a global investment bank specializing in mergers and acquisitions, financial restructuring, and financial and valuation advisory. Employing a sector-specialist strategy, the company seeks growth through a combination of disciplined acquisitions, focused geographic expansion, recurring advisory revenues, and heavy investment in technology to drive productivity and client value across volatile market cycles.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Record Backlog and Pipeline Signal Imminent Revenue Acceleration

Houlihan Lokey enters fiscal 2027 with the largest backlog and most active pipeline in its history, positioning the company for strong near-term revenue growth. In its most recent earnings call, management reported record-high backlog across its advisory businesses, with pitch activity described as “the most active we have seen in years.” This surge reflects renewed momentum in private equity-sponsored deal flow, the resumption of long-delayed M&A processes, and strong strategic demand outside the U.S, especially in Europe and Asia. Robust pipeline visibility not only boosts confidence in base-case growth for fiscal 2027, but also offers resilience against episodic deal delays — a perennial challenge in investment banking. The company is well positioned to capitalize as cycle dynamics turn favorable.

2. Geographic and Product Diversification Mitigates Cyclical Volatility

Rapid growth in non-U.S. revenues and expansion into new products like capital solutions and valuation advisory reduce the company’s dependence on any single sector or country. During both Q4 and full-year fiscal 2026, the company’s corporate finance revenues outside the U.S. grew significantly faster than its domestic business, propelled by acquisitions in France (Mellum Capital) and organic expansion in Asia. At the same time, Capital Solutions now represents over 20% of corporate finance revenue, while Financial and Valuation Advisory (FDA) continues to expand its total addressable market through recurring portfolio valuation mandates. This strategic breadth allows Houlihan Lokey to benefit from localized upswings and avoid earnings cliffs commonly associated with single-market or single-product investment banks.

3. Consistent Compensation Discipline Safeguards Operating Margins

The company’s steadfast adherence to its compensation expense target of 61.5% was maintained in fiscal 2026. In fiscal 2026, adjusted compensation expenses decreased year over year, and the long-term target ratio was maintained even as the company invested in senior hiring and integration of new managing directors. This financial discipline contrasts with some peers in the investment banking sector, where compensation costs frequently rise during periods of talent competition. By effectively balancing investment in human capital and expense control, Houlihan Lokey sustains profitability and margin stability — key ingredients for shareholder value creation and ongoing dividend growth.

4. Technology Investment and Scale Enable Market Share Gains in Data-Driven Advisory

Ongoing investment in technology, particularly artificial intelligence and workflow automation, gives the company a unique competitive edge in high-volume, data-intensive advisory products. Houlihan Lokey’s FDA segment benefits directly from scalable tech and AI-enabled platform tools, which management describes as transferable across corporate finance and restructuring segments. This not only enhances productivity and consistency of service, but also places the company ahead of traditional mid-market boutiques that may lack resources for similar investment. Management anticipates continued industry consolidation as technology spend becomes a barrier to entry, positioning the company to capture share from less capable boutique competitors over the next three to five years.

5. Proven Capital Return Track Record Underpinned by Balance Sheet Strength

Sustained dividend growth — up 17% from the prior quarterly dividend to $0.70 per share, effective for the first quarter dividend paid in June — and ongoing share repurchases highlight robust cash generation and shareholder alignment. The company ended the quarter with $1.4 billion in cash and investments, supporting both opportunistic acquisitions and consistent capital returns. This conservative capital structure, paired with the board’s long-standing commitment to incremental dividend increases and maintaining share count stability through buybacks, demonstrates a disciplined approach that rewards long-term investors without sacrificing operational flexibility. These capital return policies also reflect management’s tangible confidence in the firm’s future earnings power and acquisition pipeline.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Geopolitical and Sectoral Uncertainty Threatens Transaction Volume and Fee Levels

Ongoing volatility — particularly from the Middle East conflict and sector-specific softness in technology — continues to delay deal closings and compress average fee sizes. In Q4 fiscal 2026, the company’s corporate finance segment saw lower average fees per transaction despite increased volume, and restructuring revenues fell 3% year over year due to fewer completed deals and smaller average deal size. Management noted that M&A timelines remain extended as clients wait for geopolitical clarity, and that fee pressure in the technology/software sector is expected to impact revenue from that vertical in fiscal 2027. If macro and sector shocks persist longer than anticipated, earnings may face continued pressure, and the near-record pipeline could stall rather than convert.

2. Acquisition and Integration Risk Could Disrupt Organic Growth Trajectory

The company’s long-term growth depends on successfully sourcing, acquiring, and integrating new managing directors and boutique acquisitions, which carries operational and cultural risk. While Houlihan Lokey has a strong record of selective M&A and senior hiring, the pool of attractive acquisition targets is shrinking as industry consolidation progresses. Management has warned that finding culturally compatible partners is now more critical — and challenging — than targeting geography or product gaps. Failure to integrate new teams or realize anticipated synergies could slow expansion, strain internal culture, or dilute margins, jeopardizing the firm’s strategy to outpace both larger banks and small boutiques in key markets.

3. Revenue Model Remains Highly Sensitive to Deal Timing and Market Cycles

Despite diversification efforts, a large share of the company’s revenues derives from episodic or ‘lumpy’ transactional fees, making financial results inherently volatile. The Q4 2026 earnings miss and variability in both M&A and restructuring deal flow underscore this structural challenge. Even with increased recurring advisory revenues and a growing backlog, a sudden downturn in global deal markets or a credit shock could sharply curtail high-margin opportunities. Such unpredictability complicates forecasting, can trigger negative investor sentiment, and may challenge the company’s ability to consistently grow dividends and protect operating margins, particularly if broader market conditions deteriorate more than expected.
