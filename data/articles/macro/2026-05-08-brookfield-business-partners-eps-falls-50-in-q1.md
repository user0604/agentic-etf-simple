Brookfield Business Partners EPS Falls 50% in Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 7:00 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Earnings declined as asset sales and an absence of one-time gains drove lower profit.
EPS fell 50% year over year.
Management signaled ongoing focus on core growth, disciplined investment, and stable liquidity.

Profit fell for Brookfield Business Partners (NYSE:BBU) as fewer one-time gains and recent asset sales weighed on its latest results. The diversified investment firm, which specializes in acquiring and operating majority stakes across the industrial, services, and infrastructure sectors, delivered results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 (released May 8, 2026) that reflected this transitional period. While its core business posted steady operational gains, headline earnings and revenue both declined from the prior year. Overall, the quarter highlighted steady progress in underlying operations but revealed some pressure from structural changes and normalization of its earnings mix.

Notable Developments
Adjusted EBITDA in the Industrials segment was $320 million; this represented a 7% increase over the prior year when excluding the impact of recent acquisitions, dispositions, and tax credits.
Asset recycling continued with the sale of a 27% stake in La Trobe Financial, yielding an internal rate of return over 35% and a three-times return on original investment.
The company ended the quarter with $4.52 billion in cash and maintained the quarterly dividend at $0.0625 per share.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management communicated confidence in the company’s investment pipeline, highlighting strong demand for essential business services and industrial companies. No new quantitative financial guidance was provided for fiscal 2026, so investors will need to watch for future acquisition activity and the impact of government policy on key portfolio companies, especially as tax credits remain a major performance driver.

Quarterly Highlights

The Industrials segment posted strong operational growth, helped by “advanced energy storage” and engineered components holdings. Management noted that Clarios, a major subsidiary, received $1 billion in tax credits this period, with similar amounts expected in future years. The company's ability to access and use these credits has been a crucial factor in Industrials’ profit improvement, though future eligibility depends on continued policy support.

Asset recycling—a hallmark of the company’s capital allocation strategy—was prominent as it sold part of its La Trobe Financial stake, generating a more than threefold return on investment in four years. This sale, along with the recent $500 million commitment to DeployCo (a partnership focused on scaling artificial intelligence across businesses), shows ongoing rotation of capital into growth priorities.

Headline revenue and total adjusted EBITDA each declined from the prior year, primarily because of asset disposals and large tax credits that benefited the previous period. However, when these one-time items are excluded, underlying like-for-like EBITDA actually increased around 5%, according to management. The steady cash position and consistent quarterly dividend reinforce management’s confidence in recurring business performance.

What Brookfield Business Partners Does

Brookfield Business Partners acquires, operates, and improves major companies in the industrial, business services, and infrastructure sectors, seeking to grow value through hands-on management and active capital deployment.
