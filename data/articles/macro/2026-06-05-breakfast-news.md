4. My “Young Buffett” Japan Experiment

JOSHUA SIMKA, MARKET NEWS EDITOR

In December, I decided to assemble a basket of Japanese "net-nets." The term -- from Buffett's teacher and mentor, Benjamin Graham -- refers to stocks so cheap that even if the business were to go bust tomorrow, you'd still come out ahead on the liquidation value alone. Just take a company's current assets (cash, inventory, receivables) and subtract all liabilities. That double subtraction is where the "net net" comes from: net current assets, net of total liabilities.

One disclaimer up front: This was a winter-break thought experiment, not a live portfolio; I didn't actually buy any of these stocks. But I screened for and researched them as if I were going to. That turned out to be half the fun. Not so fun? My sense of regret. Because as you'll see shortly, I wish I had bought.
