IAC Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 11:12 AM

Comments

IAC Inc. (NASDAQ:IAC) is a digital media holding company undergoing a major transformation. The firm is now sharply focused on two pillars: People Inc, its core digital publishing platform, and a substantial equity stake in MGM Resorts. IAC is divesting non-core businesses, scaling proprietary ad technology, deepening AI content licensing, and emphasizing recurring, high-margin digital revenue streams to drive sustainable long-term growth amid disruptive industry changes.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Rapid Growth in High-Margin Non-Session Revenue Diversifies and Future-Proofs the Business

Explosive non-session-based revenue growth is shifting IAC’s earnings profile toward resilience and scalability – In Q1 2026, non-session-based revenues at People Inc. (including licensing, social/off-platform, and custom ad programs) surged 24% year over year, now representing 41% of total digital revenue, up from 35% a year earlier. This shift reduces reliance on traditional web sessions, which are increasingly vulnerable to platform shifts, and supports margin expansion. With further AI licensing deals (e.g, with Meta, OpenAI, Microsoft, and Apple News) anticipated, and management targeting the rollout of additional product-driven (inversion) revenue streams, IAC is positioned to compound high-margin, multi-channel digital income streams. This strategic transition could buffer the company against advertising volatility and search-driven headwinds, creating a more predictable, durable earnings base as the sector evolves.

2. Proprietary AI-Powered Ad Tech Platform Unlocks Incremental Revenue and Margin Expansion

Scaling the Decipher adtech suite empowers both revenue growth and cost efficiency – IAC’s in-house advertising technology platform, Decipher, leverages artificial intelligence for advanced ad targeting and campaign optimization. After demonstrating success across People Inc.’s brands, Decipher is being integrated into the M&I media agency arm, opening new distribution channels, particularly in sectors like political advertising and independent agency markets. Management projects this technology will add an incremental 200–300 basis points to growth rates in the back half of 2026 and into 2027, while supporting robust incremental digital margins (reported at 45% in Q1 2026). As first-party data becomes an increasingly valuable asset in a privacy-restrictive digital environment, IAC’s technological moat could translate into higher share of ad budgets and superior profitability relative to peers.

3. Streamlined Portfolio and Organizational Restructuring Unlock Substantial Cost Savings and Focus

Major simplification accelerates focus, improves capital allocation, and materially reduces costs – Over the past year, IAC divested Care.com for $296 million in net proceeds, exited its underperforming search business (including Ask.com), and undertook a major corporate restructuring. More than half of IAC’s corporate staff, including senior leaders, will exit by early 2027, generating run-rate annual operating expense savings of $40 million plus $20–25 million in reduced stock-based compensation. These savings begin to take full effect in the second quarter of 2027. Combined with targeted reinvestment in core digital properties and strict M&A discipline, this sharpened structure should support higher adjusted EBITDA margins (already expanded to 20% in Q1 2026) and improved free cash flow, while enabling agile pursuit of growth opportunities within the streamlined core business.

4. Significant MGM Resorts Stake Provides Long-Term Hard Asset Value and Diversification

The 26% equity position in MGM Resorts offers both a hard asset hedge and potential for value recognition – IAC steadily increased its stake in MGM Resorts, now at 26% as of Q1 2026, viewing it as a strategic, diversified “hard asset” balance to its mainly digital portfolio. MGM’s management is executing well, having reduced share count by 45% over five years and advancing the major $12 billion Japan integrated resort development, which could transform earnings power after opening. This investment provides IAC with downside protection as well as the potential for value unlocking, especially if MGM’s operations and new projects, like the Japan resort, realize their growth plans and the market begins to close the persistent sum-of-the-parts holding company discount.

5. Capital Returns and Prudent Balance Sheet Management Directly Benefit Shareholders

Ongoing share buybacks and disciplined capital allocation signal shareholder-friendly management – IAC repurchased 2.9 million shares ($111 million) in Q1 2026 alone, reducing share count by 13% since early 2025, and is prioritizing continued buybacks and selective MGM accumulation. With nearly $50 million in free cash flow generated in Q1 and net leverage remaining stable (net debt at $1.1 billion), management is emphasizing return of capital and tax-efficient growth via its material net operating loss carryforwards. These actions, paired with a possible future dividend and minimal dilution risk, enhance per-share intrinsic value—especially during periods where the market undervalues IAC’s core assets relative to their underlying performance and cash generation trajectory.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Structural Declines in Search Traffic and Web Sessions Threaten Core Economics

Sustained erosion of Google-driven traffic risks undermining the digital publishing economics – IAC’s core web sessions originating from Google search are down 65% from peak levels as of Q1 2026. This trend, driven by the proliferation of AI-generated search answers and evolving user behaviors, represents a secular headwind. While off-platform audiences have grown (up 27% year over year), the monetization economics and long-term sustainability of these channels remain less certain, particularly as competitive pressures intensify and platforms can change algorithmic incentives at any time. If IAC cannot scale alternative high-margin income streams fast enough to offset these losses, future revenue and profitability may come under persistent pressure.

2. Near-Term Profitability Pressured by Restructuring Costs and Losses from Discontinued Operations

Ongoing operating losses and restructuring costs constrain financial flexibility – Despite digital margin improvements, IAC reported a GAAP net loss in Q1 2026 (negative 4.35% net margin and a return on equity of -2.43%), missing consensus earnings per share estimates by $0.60. The wind-down of legacy segments like Ask.com, while necessary for strategic clarity, imposed transition costs and stripped out even low-margin revenues. Additional one-time costs from severance, legal, and integration, alongside multi-year cost realization timelines, will likely keep near-term reported profitability under pressure. Prolonged unprofitability risks reducing management’s flexibility to reinvest, buy back stock, or withstand macroeconomic shocks without raising capital.

3. Platform Dependency and Ad Market Volatility Expose Revenue to External Shocks

Reliance on major platforms and advertising markets leaves IAC vulnerable to external risks – While IAC mitigated Google dependency by expanding off-platform distribution, it now faces exposure to content algorithms and referral economics on Apple, Meta, TikTok, and others. Sudden changes in platform policies or ad product prioritizations could reduce traffic and pricing. The current ad market is described by management as “stable but not booming,” with certain sectors remaining soft. A downturn in digital advertising, especially if coupled with AI-driven commoditization of content or less favorable licensing terms, could compress both top-line growth and margins. Additionally, the outcome and timeline of ongoing litigation against Google’s ad tech practices remain uncertain, adding potential risk to both expense outlook and possible windfall gains.
