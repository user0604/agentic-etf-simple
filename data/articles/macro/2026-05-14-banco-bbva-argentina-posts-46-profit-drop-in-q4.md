Key Takeaways
Profitability dropped sharply as credit costs jumped and interest margins shrank.
Diluted EPS fell 46.2% from fiscal 2024.
Management did not provide updated 2026 guidance, keeping the business outlook uncertain.

Profitability fell for Banco Bbva Argentina (NYSE:BBAR) this quarter, as higher credit provisions outweighed asset and deposit growth. The leading universal bank in Argentina reported its full-year and fourth-quarter results on May 14, 2026, covering fiscal 2025. While the bank made headway in expanding its loan and deposit base, earnings ultimately dropped as tighter margins and cost pressures persisted.

Overall, the period reflected a challenging balance of growth and risk as management navigated a complex macroeconomic environment in Argentina.

Notable Developments
The bank’s loan book grew 45.7% and total deposits increased 31.7%, both outpacing prior guidance for market share gains.
Despite rising fee and foreign exchange income, net operating income declined 33.4% year over year as net interest income fell.
Dividend payouts dropped to $117.6 million, following a policy to retain more capital given the profitability pressures.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue updated guidance for fiscal 2026, referencing only prior targets for loan and deposit growth, efficiency, and returns. The absence of a new forecast makes future profitability less predictable. Investors should focus closely on credit quality trends and any signs of improvement in net interest margins in upcoming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

The bank delivered strong expansion in total loans and deposits, driven mostly by its commercial and corporate banking operations. Private sector loans reached ARS 14.2 trillion, up from ARS 9.8 trillion the previous year.

Net interest income, a key measure of earnings from lending activities after funding costs, fell over 29%. This drop reflected lower inflation, reduced nominal interest rates, and a negative impact on the margin between what the bank earns on assets and pays on deposits.

Credit costs surged as provisions for bad loans more than doubled, reaching ARS 805 billion, which significantly reduced net profits. Management noted that retail credit quality remains an area of risk and that most credit losses are still coming from consumer lending portfolios.
