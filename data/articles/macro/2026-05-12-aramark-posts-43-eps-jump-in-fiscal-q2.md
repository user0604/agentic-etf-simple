Key Takeaways
Aramark’s revenue and profit climbed sharply as record new business wins and client retention supported results.
Earnings per share rose 43.1% to $0.49, with free cash flow more than doubling year over year.
Management raised full-year organic revenue growth guidance to the high end of its prior range, while pointing to strong ongoing momentum.

Food and facilities services provider Aramark (NYSE:ARMK) delivered a business win this quarter as strong contract wins and client retention drove notable revenue and profit gains. The company, anchored as a top-two player in North America and top-three internationally, reported quarterly results for the period ended April 3, 2026 (released May 12, 2026). Overall, operational momentum remained robust, but the period saw some help from favorable calendar timing that inflated certain year-over-year figures.

Notable Developments
New business wins exceeded $1 billion fiscal year to date, marking a record high and driving both U.S. and international segment growth.
Client retention held above 98%, supporting double-digit organic revenue increases and higher adjusted operating income in core segments.
Aramark Nexus™, a new business platform focused on AI data centers, signed a significant contract with an unnamed global technology client, although related revenue is not yet in guidance.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Aramark management increased its full-year organic revenue growth outlook to the high end of 7% to 9%, supported by ongoing record business wins and high retention. The guidance for adjusted operating income and earnings per share remains unchanged, with leadership expecting the recent entry into the AI data center market to provide future upside. Investors should watch for the scale and profitability of this emerging business line and monitor any material changes in corporate overhead costs or client concentration risk.

Quarterly Highlights

Both U.S. and international operations saw double-digit organic growth, underpinned by record new contracts and exceptional client retention. In the United States, higher attendance and per-person spending at sports venues, strong onboarding in healthcare accounts, and growth in business and industry contracts supported results.

Adjusted operating income climbed 25.8% to $258 million, with margin improvements helped by supply chain efficiency, technology implementations, and enhanced cost management. This profit jump included a benefit from calendar timing, contributing about one-fifth of the headline increase.

Free cash flow more than doubled compared to a year ago, reaching $305 million, as higher earnings combined with favorable working capital. The company paid down debt and repurchased shares during the quarter. The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.12 per share.

What Aramark Does

Aramark provides food and facilities services to clients in education, healthcare, business, sports, and other settings around the world.
