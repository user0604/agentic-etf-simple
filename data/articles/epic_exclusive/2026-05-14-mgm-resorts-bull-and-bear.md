MGM Resorts Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 11:09 AM

Comments

MGM Resorts International (NYSE:MGM) is a leading global hospitality and entertainment company with a deep presence on the Las Vegas Strip, significant operations in Macau through MGM China, and a growing digital footprint via BetMGM and LeoVegas. Its growth strategy focuses on leveraging large-scale events, enhancing group and convention offerings, expanding internationally—especially in Japan and potentially the UAE—and investing heavily in digital gaming, while implementing an asset-light model and returning capital to shareholders.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership in Las Vegas Enables Pricing Power and Resilience

Las Vegas convention and event business is fueling demand and margin stability for MGM Resorts International even amid economic volatility. For the first quarter of 2026, the company delivered its first year-over-year Las Vegas net revenue growth in over a year—despite a challenging comparison against record leisure spend in the prior year. Group and convention bookings are setting new records, evidenced by a 20% convention room night mix in Q2 2026 (up two percentage points year over year) and record first-quarter average daily rates and catering revenues. Major upcoming events, such as the 2029 Super Bowl, 2027 College Football Playoff, and prospective NBA expansion, further extend demand visibility and reinforce MGM’s pricing power. This ability to maintain premium occupancy and rates through group business underpins Las Vegas as a reliable revenue and EBITDA engine, differentiating the company from peers in down cycles.

2. Rapid Digital Growth and Path to Profitability Unlock New Revenue Streams

MGM’s digital business is evolving from an investment phase to a scalable profit engine poised to diversify earnings. In Q1 2026, digital operations posted 43% year over year net revenue growth with the LeoVegas business in Sweden and the U.K. exceeding 30% top-line growth. Notably, BetMGM achieved its first profitable quarter, demonstrating early operating leverage and setting the stage for future digital contributions to consolidated cash flow. Management expects digital losses to materially narrow in 2026 with a clear target for breakeven by 2027. As the digital business continues to integrate technology (such as BetMGM’s platform migration and international expansion) and consolidates market share, especially in iGaming’s top two positions, it significantly reduces MGM’s historical reliance on physical properties and U.S. gaming regulations, opening the door to higher-margin, recurring digital revenues.

3. Asset-Light Model and Aggressive Buybacks Provide Shareholder Downside Protection

MGM’s asset recycling and share repurchase strategy enhances capital flexibility and supports long-term value creation. Over the last five years, the company has reduced its outstanding share count by nearly 50%, including buybacks of 2.5 million shares for $90 million just in Q1 2026. The sale of Northfield Park at 6.6x trailing EBITDA—well above MGM’s implied enterprise multiple—demonstrates that private market valuations for MGM’s assets remain robust. By redeploying capital from these asset sales into buybacks at a perceived discount, MGM improves per-share earnings power and shields investors from further equity dilution. This financial discipline allows the company to continue substantial growth investments while maintaining a strong balance sheet, even in periods of sector turbulence.

4. International Expansion Catalyzes Multiyear Growth and Diversification

Expansion in high-growth international markets like Japan and the continued recovery in Macau position MGM for long-term earnings diversification. In Macau, Q1 2026 net revenue grew 9% year over year, and daily mass gross gaming revenue surged 19%. MGM China maintained a market share above 15% and is making targeted investments in premium suites and gaming spaces—responding to evolving customer demands. In Japan, MGM Osaka remains on time and on budget for a 2030 opening, tapping a market of 120 million residents and 40 million annual international visitors. This project, already over 40% complete in foundational work, offers an early-mover advantage in Asia’s next great integrated resort market. Together, these segments insulate earnings from U.S.-centric cyclicality and create exposure to Asia’s rising middle-class consumer.

5. Innovative Customer Acquisition and All-Inclusive Offerings Expand Addressable Market

Creative product initiatives like all-inclusive packages at Luxor and Excalibur are driving incremental visitation and bringing new customer cohorts to MGM’s portfolio. Early in 2026, approximately one-third of bookings for these new packages came from first-time Las Vegas visitors—a remarkable figure that highlights MGM’s ability to attract value-seeking guests who might otherwise be deterred by fluctuating fees or complex pricing. Management reports strong guest responses and sees the potential for expanded rollouts across other properties. By diversifying the customer mix and boosting occupancy during traditionally weak midweek periods, this innovation not only enhances revenue stability but also builds a foundation for downstream monetization through loyalty and cross-selling opportunities.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Margin Pressures from Rising Labor and Infrastructure Costs Threaten Profitability

Margins remain under pressure even as revenue recovers, posing a challenge for consistent earnings growth. In Q1 2026, despite group and digital strength, both adjusted and GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles) profitability declined year over year due to escalating labor costs, infrastructure investments, and higher brand fees (notably in Macau). Las Vegas and regional operations saw increased expenses without corresponding revenue leverage, resulting in EBITDA declines at the segment level. If wage and cost inflation across resort operations continue outpacing top-line growth, MGM risks sustained compression of company-wide margins, which could undercut the positive impact of digital growth and limit the effectiveness of share repurchase programs.

2. International Recovery Remains Vulnerable to Macro and Regulatory Risks

Macau and Japan expansion carries significant uncertainties tied to regulatory environments and international travel patterns. While MGM China posted a 9% year-over-year revenue increase in Q1 2026 and increased its market share, Canadian business was noted as being down 30–40%. Furthermore, profitability in Macau is now impacted by the doubling of the brand fee to 3.5%. The Japan integrated resort in Osaka, though on track, does not open until 2030 and remains exposed to changes in Japanese gaming policy, construction delays, and geopolitical tensions in East Asia. A slowdown or reversal in Asia’s travel and entertainment recovery could meaningfully delay the realization of anticipated returns from these sizable investments.

3. Accelerated Competition and Regulatory Hurdles Could Limit Digital Upside

MGM’s digital business momentum faces risks from increased competition, rising customer acquisition costs, and shifting regulation. Though BetMGM reported its first quarter of profitability in early 2026, it also reduced its full-year revenue outlook owing to stiffer competition and slower-than-expected new market openings. The digital segment’s market share in sports betting and iGaming, while high in core U.S. regions, now rests on ongoing investments in technology, content, and marketing—all in a sector with rapidly evolving legal and tax frameworks. Issues such as new tax regimes in Brazil, regulatory slowdowns in additional U.S. states, and escalating user incentives industry-wide could dampen both revenue growth and the hoped-for path to digital segment profitability by 2027. Any missteps here would lower the diversification benefits that investors are counting on from MGM’s digital transformation strategy.
