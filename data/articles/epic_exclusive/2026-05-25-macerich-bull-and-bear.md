Macerich Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 3:06 PM

Comments

The Macerich Company (NYSE:MAC) is one of the largest owners and operators of Class A regional malls in the United States, focusing its portfolio in affluent, supply-constrained markets. The company’s growth strategy centers on its multi-year “Path Forward” plan, which aims to elevate portfolio quality through extensive leasing, strategic acquisitions like Annapolis Mall, anchor backfilling, and disciplined portfolio refinement—all while managing leverage and maximizing growth in Funds from Operations (FFO) and Net Operating Income (NOI).

Potential Bull Cases
1. Strategic Leasing Transformation Promises Sustainable NOI Growth

Accelerating leasing momentum is set to transform revenue and occupancy fundamentals through 2028. As of Q1 2026, Macerich had completed 83% of its target to sign 1,000 new leases—roughly 25% of its total portfolio space—central to the Path Forward plan. This initiative aims to drive $140 million of cumulative “signed-not-open” (SNO) pipeline revenue and elevate physical permanent occupancy from 84% to 88–89% by 2028. The company’s robust pipeline, with 1.6 million square feet of leases executed in Q1 (over double the prior year’s new leasing volume), means future growth in Net Operating Income is highly visible and largely locked in. As SNO tenants commence operations, about 80% of that revenue is expected to directly flow through to NOI, delivering a multi-year growth engine that underpins rising FFO and improved cash flows.

2. Dominance in Class A Malls Supports Long-Term Pricing Power

Macerich’s concentration in irreplaceable, Class A mall assets ensures portfolio resilience and superior rent potential. Approximately 90% of forward-looking NOI is derived from Class A regional malls in affluent, supply-constrained markets where new supply is virtually non-existent. This dominant market positioning allows the company to cater to top-tier retailers seeking high-traffic, flagship locations, which has been validated by the introduction of luxury and Gen Z-focused brands such as Hermès, Tesla, and Uniqlo. Retailers’ preference for experiential and premium environments strengthens Macerich’s rent-marking ability and supports high sales productivity, with sales per square foot reaching a company record $941 in Q1 2026 for core assets. These dynamics provide defensive advantages and persistent rent growth potential, even amid macroeconomic headwinds.

3. Strategic Acquisitions and Redevelopments Are Immediately Accretive

The recent acquisition of Annapolis Mall highlights Macerich’s discipline in value creation and FFO accretion. In May 2026, Macerich acquired Annapolis Mall for $272 million at an initial yield of 10.5%, one of the highest seen for a dominant regional mall in recent years. The deal is immediately accretive, expected to contribute $0.04 per share to 2028 FFO, and fits the “Elevate & Transform” strategy by targeting assets that can be revitalized through remerchandising and new tenant signings. Anchor redevelopments and full leasing commitments for all 2.9 million square feet of previously vacant anchor space are projected to generate over $750 million in sales and unlock further in-line leasing demand. These moves enlarge NOI, add high-productivity centers to the portfolio, and demonstrate the company's ability to deploy capital with discipline for visible future growth.

4. Prudent Balance Sheet Management and Enhanced Liquidity Reduce Financial Risk

Active deleveraging and capital raises are improving financial flexibility and resilience. By Q1 2026, net debt to adjusted EBITDA improved to 7.76x (down a full turn from plan inception), with targets to reach low-to-mid 6x within a few years. The company completed over $1.3 billion in asset dispositions and, in May 2026, raised further equity through a 19.2 million share offering. Liquidity was bolstered to ~$780 million with a recently upsized revolving credit facility, supporting the Annapolis acquisition and strengthening day-to-day funding capacity. This balanced approach to funding—mixing asset sales, debt, and equity—limits refinancing risk and positions Macerich to weather economic shocks, all while supporting cautious, FFO-accretive growth initiatives.

5. Secular Demand Tailwinds from Gen Z and Retailer Omnichannel Strategies

Shifting consumer and retailer preferences create a structural runway for traffic and rental growth. The company benefits from robust demand from retailers seeking modern, experiential physical locations that cater to shifting consumer habits—especially from Gen Z, who now favor in-person shopping and experiences. Recent press coverage in Q2 2026 highlighted that Gen Z’s preference to shop, eat, and socialize at malls is reversing negative traffic trends seen over the past decade, with Macerich’s experiential, high-quality centers particularly favored. This, combined with a record-low tenant risk watch list and consistent rent renewals (90% of 2026 expirations already committed), suggests a durable, multi-year tailwind underpinning occupancy, sales, and rent-structuring that should enable Macerich to hit or surpass its 2028 FFO/share targets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Elevated Leverage Remains a Vulnerability Amid Macro Uncertainty

High leverage exposes the company to credit and refinancing risk if market conditions deteriorate. Despite recent progress, Macerich’s net debt to adjusted EBITDA stood at 7.76x at the end of Q1 2026—above sector averages and peer best-in-class levels. Only one loan (the 29th Street property) remains in default, but persistent high leverage may limit financial flexibility and raise borrowing costs, particularly if credit conditions tighten or asset values soften. While asset sales and equity raisings have reduced some risk, the company’s ability to rapidly deleverage further is contingent on successful execution of its disposition program and supportive capital markets. Should NOI growth underperform or interest rates rise sharply, Macerich could face challenges in meeting its leverage targets or in refinancing remaining maturities on favorable terms.

2. Back-End Loaded NOI Growth Is Vulnerable to SNO Pipeline Delays

The conversion of the signed-not-open pipeline is critical, yet not guaranteed, for the expected ramp in income. Much of the company’s projected growth in Net Operating Income and FFO through 2028 hinges on approximately $140 million in cumulative SNO revenue as new tenants commence rent payments. While 83% of the Path Forward lease-up plan is complete, the anticipated NOI uplift is heavily weighted toward future periods. Any delays—such as construction, permitting, or unforeseen tenant issues—could push back or reduce SNO conversion, particularly as weather and market factors already dampened Q1 2026 NOI by about 50 basis points. Should the leasing pipeline falter, or if new concepts underperform once opened, the company’s ability to meet its bullish multi-year financial targets may be compromised.

3. Consumer and Retailer Health Remain Key External Threats

Softening consumer spending or renewed retailer bankruptcies could blunt leasing and occupancy gains. The bull case assumes ongoing resilience in consumer demand and retailer expansion, especially given current trends driven by Gen Z and leading brands. However, the broader retail sector remains cyclical and sensitive to macroeconomic shocks, persistently high inflation, or shifts in discretionary spending. Any reversal in these trends—such as an economic slowdown, mounting retail bankruptcies, or a sudden pullback in store expansion—could reverse occupancy gains and compress rents, putting both current performance and future pipeline economics at risk. Portfolio exposure remains concentrated in retail, with only limited ability to diversify if market conditions abruptly change.
