AdvanSix (NYSE:ASIX), a chemical and materials manufacturer, saw sales growth this quarter but suffered a sharp profit drop as operating costs surged. The company produces nylon, fertilizer, and chemical intermediates used across industries from agriculture to automotive. AdvanSix released its first quarter 2026 results on May 8, 2026, covering a period marked by severe margin compression due to higher raw material costs and weather disruptions. Overall, the quarter showed progress in sales, but profit and cash generation weakened dramatically compared to the same time last year.

Notable Developments
Sales grew 7%, driven mainly by higher volumes in the Chemical Intermediates product line and higher prices in Plant Nutrients. Plant Nutrients made up 35% of total sales, with the segment growing 10% year over year.
Profitability deteriorated, with adjusted EBITDA margin dropping to 1.2% from 13.7%. The sharp drop was caused by higher sulfur and natural gas costs, winter storm impacts, and the absence of last year's one-time insurance benefit.
Free cash flow worsened to a $51.3 million outflow, reflecting lower profits, higher capital spending, and increased working capital needs. The company drew further on its credit line to cover operating requirements.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects improved performance in coming quarters, assuming input costs stabilize and planned cost savings take effect. Investment in growth and sustainability, such as new fertilizer initiatives and a Def (Diesel Exhaust Fluid) expansion project, is expected to support the business long term. Investors should keep an eye on margin recovery, free cash flow improvement, and the pace at which the company realizes benefits from tax credits and new projects.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter’s revenue increase came mostly from strong Plant Nutrients and Chemical Intermediates sales, offsetting flat results in Nylon and Caprolactam, which are used in fibers and plastics. Price gains in nitrogen-based fertilizers helped, but higher input costs weighed on overall profitability.

Adjusted EBITDA collapsed from last year due to several factors: higher costs for sulfur and natural gas, an $11 million hit from a severe winter storm, and the absence of a $26 million insurance payment that boosted earnings a year ago. Despite this, the company continued to invest in expansion projects and maintained its quarterly dividend at $0.16 per share.

Free cash flow declined as working capital requirements rose and capital expenditures reached $35.9 million, up slightly from the prior year. Management increased credit line use from $215 million to $270 million during the quarter to fund these needs.

What AdvanSix Does

AdvanSix manufactures nylon resins, fertilizers, and chemical intermediates from core chemicals at integrated plants, supplying markets including agriculture, construction, and automotive.
