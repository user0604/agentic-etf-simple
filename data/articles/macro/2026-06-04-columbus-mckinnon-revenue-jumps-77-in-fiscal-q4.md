Key Takeaways
Sales surged following a major acquisition, but margin pressure led to a significant net loss.
Revenue grew 77.3% year over year in the quarter.
Management expects sales and adjusted EBITDA to rise, but margin recovery and debt reduction remain key focus areas.

Columbus McKinnon (NASDAQ:CMCO) posted standout revenue growth thanks to its largest-ever acquisition, though the period came with sharply reduced profitability due to integration costs and non-cash charges. The leading maker of hoists, intelligent motion equipment, and precision conveyance systems reported its results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, on June 4, 2026. Overall, the quarter included a record jump in sales, but also a substantial net loss driven by one-time costs and margin compression.

Notable Developments
Revenue grew 77.3% to $437.8 million, mainly due to the completion of the Kito Crosby acquisition and a 7.3% rise in legacy sales.
Net loss widened to $238.2 million, reflecting a $200 million goodwill impairment and elevated deal and integration expenses.
Adjusted gross margin fell 2.5 percentage points to 32.7%, signaling pressure from integration costs and changes in product mix.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects fiscal 2027 sales of $2.05 billion–$2.12 billion and adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) of $390 million–$410 million. They are prioritizing synergy delivery from the Kito Crosby combination and aim to restore profit margins while focusing on debt reduction. Investors should pay closest attention to progress on margin recovery and the pace of synergy realization over the next twelve months.

Quarterly Highlights

Sales increased sharply as the newly combined operations brought in $437.8 million for the quarter. Orders also surged 68%, with non-U.S. sales up 89%, now making up 46% of total revenue.

Profitability dropped as merger accounting items and deal costs flowed through the income statement. The bottom line swung to a net loss of $238.2 million as the company recorded a $200 million write-down tied to goodwill, in addition to $36.8 million in inventory adjustments and $68.1 million of deal costs.

Margins were squeezed by both recurring and non-recurring factors. Adjusted gross margin, which strips out the largest unusual items, still dipped to 32.7% from 35.2% last year, and net cash from operations was negative due to one-off acquisition expenses, though underlying free cash flow normalized for these items improved considerably.
