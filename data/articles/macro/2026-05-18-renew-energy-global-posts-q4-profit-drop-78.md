Renewable power producer ReNew Energy Global (NASDAQ:RNW) grew its revenues during the quarter but experienced a sharp drop in profits, reflecting lower one-time gains and increased finance costs. The company is a major Indian developer and operator of wind, solar, hydro, and battery storage projects, with a growing solar manufacturing segment. The results, released on May 18, 2026, cover the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the quarter demonstrated continued operational expansion but also showed pressure on the bottom line from higher costs and fewer asset-sale gains.

Notable Developments
ReNew’s total renewables portfolio reached approximately 20 gigawatts by March 2026, with operational capacity up 16.6% year over year to about 12.6 GW (including battery storage systems).
Manufacturing revenues for external sales continued to surge, more than tripling year over year for fiscal 2026 and contributing significantly to the company’s overall adjusted EBITDA figures.
Net profit in the quarter was sharply lower than the same period last year due to higher finance costs, increased depreciation, and the absence of large asset sale gains.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management is guiding for up to 2.4 GW of new capacity additions in fiscal 2027 and expects adjusted EBITDA between 103 billion–109 billion Indian rupees (with INR 10 billion–12 billion from manufacturing). The company also highlights planned asset sales as a tool to support cash flows and pay down debt. Investors should watch upcoming solar manufacturing expansion, power sales trends, and how ReNew manages rising finance costs and leverage amid further growth.

Quarterly Highlights

The renewables portfolio expanded again this period, with new solar, wind, and storage facilities added. This growth helped drive a roughly 15% increase in consolidated revenue compared to the prior year quarter. Despite this, the operating profit improvement was not enough to offset sharply higher costs, leading to a much lower diluted EPS and negative cash flow to equity for the quarter.

The company’s manufacturing segment, focused on solar modules and cells, continued to scale, with revenue and profit from external sales more than tripling year over year for fiscal 2026. However, segment profit in the quarter moderated slightly compared to the prior-year quarter, indicating a less pronounced gain in the most recent period.

Net profit for the company as a whole was pressured partly by higher finance costs, which rose with additional projects coming online and increased borrowings. Unlike the prior year’s fourth quarter, this period did not include large profits from asset sales, highlighting the volatility such non-recurring gains can introduce to quarterly results.
