1. AI: The S&P 500's MVP

The S&P 500's recent rally looks far less broad once AI enablers are stripped out. A Goldman Sachs (NYSE: GS) index excluding AI-related stocks is slightly lower since late February, while the broader index is up about 10% and AI winners have surged more than 45%.

The concentration story: Bianco Research used a JPMorgan (NYSE: JPM) list of 41 AI-related stocks to show those names now account for nearly half of the S&P 500's market value. Macro strategist Jim Bianco called it the most concentrated single-theme market in 150 years, comparing it to the late-19th-century railroad boom that reshaped the U.S. economy.
Bubble math: Bianco wrote that the bigger question isn't whether AI is a bubble but where investors are in the cycle. His cautionary tale: those who exited after Fed Chair Alan Greenspan's December 1996 "irrational exuberance" warning would have missed a nearly 300% Nasdaq rally before the dot-com peak.
2. Marvell Soars on Huang's Trillion-Dollar Nod
★ NVDA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Marvell Technology (NASDAQ: MRVL) shares surged more than 25% after Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang called the chip designer the "next trillion-dollar company" at Computex in Taipei. Huang touted Marvell's networking and connectivity chips as essential to data centers, where computing tasks are spread across thousands of connected chips that need to share data quickly.

The connectivity moat: Marvell designs high-performance chips for cloud computing, AI, enterprise networking, 5G carrier networks, and automotive systems. Huang said disaggregated computing across massive AI clusters is "the reason why Marvell is so essential." First-quarter revenue hit $2.4 billion, and management forecasts continued growth driven by the data center business.
Nvidia's photonics push: Nvidia recently invested $2 billion in Marvell and has committed at least $6.5 billion since March to companies developing photonics technology, which uses light instead of electricity to transfer data more efficiently. The strategy aims to scale AI infrastructure without hitting the energy walls that come with copper-based connectivity.
3. Anthropic Files for IPO

Anthropic confidentially filed its IPO prospectus with the SEC on Monday, beating rival OpenAI to the milestone and setting up a potentially historic AI share sale. The start-up followed up Tuesday by expanding access to its powerful Mythos cybersecurity model to 150 additional partners across more than 15 countries.

Explosive growth backs the IPO: Anthropic's revenue run rate has ballooned to $47 billion, up from $10 billion in annual revenue last year. The company closed a funding round last week at a $965 billion valuation, topping OpenAI's $852 billion mark from late March. SpaceX has already filed its public prospectus and plans to debut next week, while OpenAI is readying its own confidential filing.
Mythos goes global: The Project Glasswing expansion adds industries like power, water, healthcare, and communications that weren't well represented at launch. Existing partners -- including Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL), Nvidia, Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT), CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD), and Palo Alto Networks (NASDAQ: PANW) -- have already revealed more than 10,000 high or critical security flaws since launch. Anthropic also said Monday it will offer Mythos access to the European Union.
