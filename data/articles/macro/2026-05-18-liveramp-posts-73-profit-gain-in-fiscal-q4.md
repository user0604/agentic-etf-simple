LiveRamp (NYSE:RAMP) advanced its profitability and recurring revenue, finishing the year with solid operational results as it moves toward acquisition. The data connectivity platform provider posted its fiscal fourth quarter earnings release on May 18, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31, 2026. Overall, the period was marked by strong margin expansion, robust cash generation, and notable strategic developments, though customer growth slowed and future outlook is now shaped by its pending merger agreement.

Notable Developments
Non-GAAP operating income increased 75% to $40 million, reflecting efficiency gains and higher gross profit on expanding revenue.
Annualized recurring revenue reached $545 million, up 8%, while subscription net retention improved to 107% versus 101% in the prior quarter.
Customer growth slowed, with only six net new direct subscription customers in the quarter and a sequential drop in accounts generating $1 million or more annually.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide forward guidance for the coming quarter or fiscal year in light of the planned acquisition by Publicis Groupe. With future results to be determined through a new corporate structure, the main focus for stakeholders will now shift to completion and integration of the proposed $2.5 billion transaction.

Quarterly Highlights

Subscription revenue and marketplace services both contributed to revenue growth, helping non-GAAP operating margin expand by 7 percentage points from the year-ago period. The company continued investing in its data collaboration and AI-driven platforms, including expanded partnerships with firms such as Unity and new integration with NVIDIA to accelerate artificial intelligence workloads.

Cash generation for the period remained strong, but both net cash from operations and free cash flow dipped slightly compared to last year's fourth quarter, coming in at $59 million each. On an annual basis, cash flow reached a record, supported by disciplined cost management and steady underlying demand.

Customer metrics present a point of caution. While annualized recurring revenue and net retention both improved, the number of direct subscription customers ended at 846, up just six from the prior year and only a marginal gain from the previous quarter. The high-value $1 million-plus customer cohort shrank sequentially by seven accounts, though it was up year over year.
