Ducommun Posts 226% EPS Jump in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Ducommun delivered record first-quarter revenue, driven by commercial aerospace strength and ongoing defense demand.
Adjusted earnings per share (non-GAAP) rose 226.1%, with margins and cash flow also improving year over year.
Management reaffirmed its longer-term profit margin goals, while warning of likely order slowdowns in commercial aerospace later in 2026.

Ducommun (NYSE:DCO) posted a business win as commercial aerospace rebounded and defense programs built momentum, leading to strong sales growth. The aerospace and defense supplier provides complex electronic and structural systems for commercial and military platforms, serving customers such as Boeing and Raytheon. In its first-quarter earnings release on May 12, 2026 (covering the three months ended April 4, 2026), the company recorded broad improvements while noting caution on future commercial aerospace orders. Overall, the period showed powerful margin and earnings gains, offset somewhat by signs of slower backlog growth ahead.

Notable Developments
Commercial aerospace revenue grew 18%, bouncing back after last year's decline, supported by higher production of single-aisle aircraft and rotorcraft.
Non-GAAP net income more than tripled, helped by improved margins and a $4.3 million drop in stock-based compensation expenses.
Remaining performance obligations declined 2.9% from December.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership restated its commitment to reaching an 18% adjusted EBITDA margin in fiscal 2027 and expressed confidence in longer-term defense demand. However, it cautioned that “destocking” by commercial aerospace customers will likely weigh on revenue growth through the rest of 2026. Investors should closely monitor order intake and remaining performance obligations (future backlog) as near-term demand could moderate.

Quarterly Highlights

Commercial aerospace operations delivered a sharp recovery as demand from Airbus and Boeing increased, reversing last year's declines. The company said this segment grew 18% from the prior-year quarter owing to strong demand for single-aisle jets like the A220 and 737 MAX, with rotorcraft also contributing.

Profitability improved across several fronts. Gross profit rose to $56.2 million as improved mix, higher sales, and disciplined spending resulted in higher margins. Much of the non-GAAP net income gain came from greater operating efficiency and a one-time reduction in stock-based compensation costs rather than higher revenues alone.

Despite these positives, backlog fell to $1.07 billion after several quarters of growth. The dip was mainly driven by softer commercial aerospace and military/space order intake, a warning sign that elevated growth may not continue at the same pace for the next few quarters.
