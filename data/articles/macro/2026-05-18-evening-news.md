1. Uber Supersizes Its Delivery Hero Stake

Rule Breakers rec Uber (NYSE: UBER) just upgraded its Delivery Hero (OTC: DLVHF) stake from 7% to 19.5% -- plus options on another 5.6%. No controlling stake planned "at this time," which in mergers-and-acquisitions (M&A) speak tends to mean never say never. The play is simple: DoorDash (NASDAQ: DASH), recommended in Hidden Gems and Stock Advisor, has been making moves in Europe via its Wolt unit, and Delivery Hero's 60-country footprint gives Uber a serious counter-punch.

Food fight heating up abroad: Uber has been on an overseas acquisition spree specifically to counter DoorDash outside the U.S., and Delivery Hero's global reach is exactly the kind of asset worth stacking up.
Delivery Hero's messy moment: Activist investor Aspex Management just forced out CEO Niklas Östberg, and the company is mid-"strategic review" -- translation: It's in play, and Uber just made sure it has a seat at the table.
2. Fianlimab Flop Sends Regeneron Down 10%

Regeneron (NASDAQ: REGN) is having a rough Monday, with shares down about 10% after its fianlimab candidate struck out in a Phase 3 melanoma trial, failing to beat Merck's (NYSE: MRK) Keytruda on progression-free survival.

No sugarcoating: Analysts at Citi (NYSE: C) yanked their buy rating, chopped the price target from $900 to $700, and flatly said the data doesn't support filing with regulators or assigning any commercial value to fianlimab.
Not all hope is lost: A separate head-to-head trial pitting high-dose fianlimab against Bristol Myers Squibb's (NYSE: BMY) Opdualag in the same indication is still running; Regeneron is going to need that one to go its way.
3. No China Deal Yet, but Huang Stays Bullish

"My sense is that over time, the market will open," Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang told Bloomberg Television on Monday at an event hosted by Dell (NYSE: DELL). Not exactly a victory lap, but the man is not sweating it. Huang flew to China with President Donald Trump last week and came back with little more than vibes -- and certainly no H20 deal. Chinese officials haven't budged on approving chip sales, even though the U.S. has already issued export licenses. Shares were down 1.33% on Monday. Nvidia is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers.

Why Beijing isn't rushing: China is busy building up its own chip champions, so throwing open the door to American silicon isn't high on the to-do list right now.
Earnings on deck: Nvidia is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2027 earnings after the market closes on Wednesday.
