Key Takeaways
Net sales and adjusted EBITDA came in stronger than management's outlook, but overall results deteriorated from a year ago.
Revenue fell 27.3% year over year, with margins significantly pressured compared to the prior period.
Management expects improved sales and losses for the next quarter, anchored by progress with commercial trucking customers.

Battery technology maker Dragonfly Energy (NASDAQ:DFLI) delivered a mixed start to fiscal 2026 as strong performance against management’s outlook helped offset continued weakness in its core markets. The company focuses on lithium battery systems for RVs, heavy-duty trucks, and other commercial applications, building its reputation on innovation and strategic partnerships. The first quarter report was released on May 14, 2026, covering results for the period ending March 31, 2026. Overall, the quarter featured better execution than planned but was marked by ongoing pressures on revenue, profit margins, and cash flow.

Notable Developments
OEM (original equipment manufacturer) segment revenue made up the majority of sales, but was down sharply year over year due to soft RV market demand.
Gross margin narrowed significantly to 17.6%, mainly due to lower sales volume and higher input costs.
Cash usage remained high, with cash and equivalents down by $9.6 million compared to the previous quarter, even as cost-control actions began to take hold.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management guides for revenue of about $13.2 million and an adjusted EBITDA loss of approximately $1.9 million for the next quarter, suggesting expectations for sequential improvements driven by commercial trucking orders and cost savings. The top priority for investors will be whether the company meets these sales and loss reduction targets, as continued heavy cash outflows would further limit financial flexibility.

Quarterly Highlights

OEM segment sales--mainly to RV and trucking manufacturers installing Dragonfly’s lithium batteries at the factory--were $5.8 million, remaining the largest single channel but down from the prior year. Recent large commercial orders, including a major fleet order from Stevens Transport, are expected to benefit results in upcoming quarters.

Gross margin, the share of sales left after direct costs, fell to 17.6% from 29.4% a year earlier as lower volumes resulted in less efficient use of production resources and higher per-unit costs. The company has launched cost reduction programs in areas like workforce and discretionary spending and expects the benefits to become more apparent in the coming quarters.

Cash balances ended the quarter at $8.6 million, boosted by financing actions taken after the first quarter of 2025, but operating losses led to a significant use of cash in the period. Maintaining liquidity will remain a key concern as losses continue and large customer wins are phased in.
