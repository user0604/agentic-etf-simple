Canadian Banks: Believe the Hype!

Jim Gillies

May 29, 2026, 8:53 AM

Comments (1)
10.0

I’ve hyped up the “Big Six” Canadian banks for American readers/viewers for a few years now, as well as recommended them via a particular ETF for members of Hidden Gems Canada.

I’m about to do it again.

This was fiscal second quarter earnings reports week for the “Big Six” and the scorecard looks like this:

Bank of Montreal (NYSE:BMO)(TSX:BMO) – Reported profit of $2.6 billion, with adjusted EPS of $3.67, up 40% year-over-year and well above expectations. Return on equity (ROE) of 13%. Raised the quarterly dividend 2.4% and now yields 3%
Bank of Nova Scotia (NYSE:BNS)(TSX:BNS) - Reported profit of $2.6 billion, with adjusted EPS of $2.02, up 33% year-over-year and well above expectations. ROE clocked in at 13.1%. Raised the quarterly dividend 3.6% and now yields 4.1%
Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce (NYSE:CM)(TSX:CM) - Reported profit of $2.5 billion, with adjusted EPS of $2.54, up 24% year-over-year and, would you believe it, well ahead of expectations. ROE of 16.4%. Did NOT increase the dividend so you’ll have to console yourself with the present 2.8% yield
National Bank of Canada (TSX:NA) - Reported profit of $1.2 billion, with adjusted EPS of $3.23, up 13% year-over-year and – shockingly - above expectations. ROE clocked in at 15.9%; raised quarterly dividend 6.5% and now yields 2.6%
Royal Bank of Canada (NYSE:RY)(TSX:RY) – The largest company in Canada by market capitalization reported quarterly profit of $5.5 billion, adjusted EPS of $3.90 - up 25% year-over-year and – tell me if you’ve heard this before - above expectations. ROE was 17.2%; quarterly dividend was bumped up by 7.3% and Royal now yields 3.7%
Toronto-Dominion Bank (NYSE:TD)(TSX:TD) - Reported profit of nearly $4.3 billion, adjusted EPS of $2.38, up 21% year-over-year – again well outpacing expectations. Return on equity (ROE) of 14.7%. Raised the quarterly dividend 3.7% and now yields 2.9%

Remember when tariff threats and mortgage implosions were going to ruin the Canadian economy? For those scoring at home, that’s $18.7 billion in quarterly profit for a market sector with several players older than Canada itself!

And – and this is the important thing – the Canadian banks have been doing this for decades.

They are dominant, well-capitalized, usually raise their dividends annually, if not semi-annually (not sure what CIBC’s excuse was this quarter). Every Canadian is interacting with them on a weekly, if not daily basis (today I’ve already grabbed cash out of a CIBC bank machine, checked my brokerage account with National Bank, and looked in on my kids’ education savings plan at Scotiabank; this is not a unique day).

These are the ultimate “widows and orphans” stocks here in Canada – except that these widows and orphans stocks just turned in average EPS growth of 26% while five of six raised their payouts. That bank ETF I mentioned? It’s delivered market smashing returns for Gems Canada members.

Have you listened in the past when I’ve pounded the table for a long-term position in the Canadian banks?

Are you listening now?
