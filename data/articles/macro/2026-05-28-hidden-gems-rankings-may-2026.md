Detailed Rankings
1. Tesla

Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) shareholders are roughly where they were three months ago as far as the stock price is concerned, although they've had to endure a pretty steep downturn along the way. The EV pioneer's production of 408,400 vehicles for 2026's first quarter was somewhat encouraging, as it was up from about 362,600 a year ago. Deliveries saw a more modest rise of roughly 21,300 vehicles. The company also returned to growth in financial terms, posting a 16% rise in revenue and adjusted net income gains of 56%. As the SpaceX IPO approaches, Tesla investors wonder whether Elon Musk's endgame will be to merge the two companies. That could produce a windfall for Tesla shareholders, but either way, we're comfortable with where we see the EV maker headed.
-- Dan Caplinger

2. Cloudflare

Cloudflare (NYSE: NET) has recovered some of its lost momentum over the past quarter, as investors now seem more confident that the cloud services provider will be able to keep growing at a healthy pace. Its early May release of first-quarter financials bolstered that bullish case, with revenue and current remaining performance obligations both climbing 34% year over year. Adjusted net income was up more than 60%, and Cloudflare also saw good gains in free cash flow.

A layoff announcement caused a sharp but brief stock decline, as shares bounced back quickly. Co-founder/CEO Matthew Prince has embraced an agentic AI-first operating model, and Cloudflare's newest collaboration with Anthropic to provide Claude Managed Agents on its platform is a step that we believe could lead to a stronger connection to AI-fostered growth in the years to come. -- Dan Caplinger

3. DoorDash

Earlier this month, DoorDash (NASDAQ: DASH) reported its first-quarter results, which some investors found wanting. Revenue of $4.04 billion climbed 33% year over year, marking the fourth consecutive quarter of accelerating growth. Unfortunately, diluted earnings per share fell 5% to $0.42. The top line was driven by total orders of 933 million, up 27%, and marketplace gross order volume (GOV) of $31.6 billion, up 37%. Management also reported record DashPass signups, strong customer engagement, and record monthly active users.

The company is investing in new features and streamlining its global operations to increase efficiency, resulting in a short-term hit to profits. The stock sold off on the news, but we think that's shortsighted and represents a buying opportunity for shrewd investors. -- Danny Vena

4. Aritzia

Aritzia (OTC: ATZAF) delivered record net revenue of $1.19 billion in Q4 2026 -- a 32.6% year-over-year jump that would make most retailers envious. Comparable sales grew 27.7% on top of 26% growth in Q4 2025, a stacked two-year performance that signals genuine brand momentum, not a one-quarter fluke. U.S. revenue surged 37.8% for the quarter, with full-year U.S. net revenue hitting $2.28 billion.

For all of fiscal 2026, revenue climbed 35.2% to $3.70 billion -- hitting the Fiscal 2027 revenue target management set in 2022, a full year early. ATZAF shares are up roughly 28% over the trailing year, and with management identifying a long-term opportunity for 180 to 200 U.S. boutiques against just 76 today, we see a long growth runway ahead. -- The Premium Team

5. MSCI

The growing popularity of exchange-traded funds and indexing is a boon for MSCI (NYSE: MSCI), a leading index provider. The company generates fees based on a small percentage of assets from any fund based on its collection of indexes, and it brings in substantial subscription revenue from its index data as well. Overall revenue rose 14.1% in the first quarter, while operating income surged 21.2%. Profit margins are incredible, with net income of $406 million on $850.8 million in revenue during the first quarter. MSCI stock is far from cheap, but this is an exceptional business that deserves a premium valuation. The passive investing trend appears far from over, and MSCI is a key beneficiary.
-- Tim Green

6. Kinsale Capital Group

Until 2025, Kinsale Capital Group (NYSE: KNSL) had never had a year in which its stock price fell, so it's understandable that some of its investors are jittery right now. However, Kinsale's 40% decline from its all-time high is due to cyclical weakness in the insurance industry, following some exceptionally strong years after the COVID-19 pandemic. Premium growth has declined and costs of claims have generally risen across the industry, and Kinsale isn't an exception. In the first quarter of 2026, gross written premiums declined by 0.5% year over year because the company is prudently being more selective in underwriting to maintain its class-leading profitability.

The bottom line is that Kinsale remains the best-in-breed specialty insurance company with a clear technology advantage. It still has a massive long-term market opportunity, and with shares trading at around 14 times earnings, Kinsale looks like a bargain for long-term investors right now. -- Matt Frankel

7. Cisco Systems

Cisco Systems (NASDAQ: CSCO) just delivered one of its best quarters ever, and the market noticed. In fiscal Q3 2026, Cisco posted record revenue of $15.8 billion, up 12% year over year, with product revenue climbing 17%. The real headline: total product orders surged 35% year over year, and Cisco raised its fiscal 2026 artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure order expectation to $9 billion, up from a prior target of $5 billion. Management is already guiding to at least $6 billion in AI hyperscale revenue in fiscal 2027.

A May restructuring -- cutting fewer than 4,000 jobs, or less than 5% of total employees -- sharpens focus on silicon, optics, and security rather than signals distress. Shares have gained 33% year to date, topping the Nasdaq's 14% advance. We think the AI networking supercycle is still early, and Cisco sits squarely at its center. -- The Premium Team

8. CrowdStrike

Earlier this year, CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD) stock fell on worries that agentic AI represented a significant threat to its business, along with many other SaaS companies, on the thesis that competing products could be built for a fraction of the cost, and potentially in mere months. The threat was a double-edged sword, too -- AI is becoming increasingly powerful.

Are we on the verge of AI beating AI-powered cybersecurity tools at their own game? Anthropic's Mythos, for example, uncovered tens of thousands of corporate and open-source security vulnerabilities in a matter of weeks. CrowdStrike, however, was one of a select few companies given access to Mythos, a reminder of its critical role in global cybersecurity.

Fast-forward a few months, and Mr. Market has shifted back to a more bullish stance. AI is a serious threat, but it's also a massive opportunity. CrowdStrike is set to report first-quarter earnings on June 3. -- Jason Hall

9. Meta Platforms

Meta Platforms' (NASDAQ: META) stock has seen some turbulence in recent months, despite strong growth from the social media giant. First-quarter financial results in late April featured a 33% rise in revenue that helped lift earnings by 62% year over year. Both ad impressions and price-per-ad metrics rose by double-digit percentages. Meta is aggressively pursuing AI opportunities, boosting its expected 2026 capital expenditure budget by $10 billion to a range of $125 billion to $145 billion. Like many of its peers, the company has also cut jobs, with its latest 10% reduction resulting in 8,000 layoffs. We expect CEO Mark Zuckerberg to accentuate the tangible results of Meta's AI efforts in the months and years to come. -- Dan Caplinger

10. Tractor Supply Company

Tractor Supply (NASDAQ: TSCO) has had a brutal 2026, with shares down roughly 40% year to date -- but the sell-off looks overdone. In Q1, net sales rose 3.6% to $3.59 billion, driven primarily by new store openings, while the company added a record 40 new stores with new store productivity in the 65% to 70% range. Diluted EPS came in at $0.31, down from $0.34 a year ago, as costs rose faster than sales.

The companion animal segment was the primary culprit, dragging comps by over 100 basis points. Still, four of five product categories were positive and digital sales grew at a double-digit rate. Management reaffirmed full-year guidance of $2.13 to $2.23 in diluted EPS. The company also refinanced its credit facility with a $1.30 billion unsecured revolving line in May. Its dividend increase earlier this year marked 17 consecutive years of increases. Shares are pressing 52-week lows near $29.42. We think the long-term bull case here remains very much intact. -- The Premium Team
