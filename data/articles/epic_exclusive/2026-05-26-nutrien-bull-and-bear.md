Nutrien Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 7:08 AM

Comments

Nutrien Ltd. (NYSE:NTR) is one of the world’s largest producers of potash and a major player in nitrogen and phosphate fertilizers, supported by a robust downstream agricultural retail network. The company’s growth strategy centers on cost leadership in fertilizer production, operational automation, structural cost reduction, and expanding higher-margin proprietary products, while optimizing its asset portfolio through divestitures and strategic reviews of underperforming businesses.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Cost Leadership and Automation Cement Potash Segment Profitability

Sustained low cash costs in potash drive margin stability and industry leadership – Nutrien’s potash segment remains the company’s largest and most profitable, underpinned by record sales volumes of 27.5 million tonnes in 2025 and controllable cash costs of $58 per tonne—well below the stated $60 per tonne target. The company has aggressively advanced mine automation, reaching 49% automation by 2025, which boosts productivity, safety, and scalability with minimal capital outlay. This cost leadership is particularly valuable as global potash demand continues to increase, with Nutrien maintaining a 19–20% market share in a market projected to ship 74–77 million tonnes in 2026. Sustainable low-cost production enables Nutrien to navigate market volatility, capture incremental market share, and monetize brownfield expansion opportunities at $150–$200/tonne, protecting margins even during inflationary periods and supply-side shocks.

2. Strong Upstream Pricing and Demand Tailwinds Bolster Earnings

Favorable fertilizer markets are boosting top-line and segment profits – The recent surge in fertilizer prices, notably due to disruptions from the Iran war and tight supply chains, has directly benefited Nutrien’s upstream operations. Benchmark potash prices rose approximately 20% year over year as of early 2026, while demand for nitrogen and phosphate also climbed amid global geopolitical uncertainty and robust crop cycles. The company’s Q1 2026 results spotlight this dynamic, with net earnings climbing to $139 million (up from $19 million YoY) and adjusted EBITDA reaching $1.11 billion—significantly above the prior year. Such operating leverage to cyclical tailwinds and global market imbalances reinforces Nutrien’s ability to generate substantial cash flow, enabling continued investment, dividend growth, and share repurchases.

3. Robust Capital Return Policy Underscores Financial Strength

Sustained dividends and accelerated buybacks demonstrate capital discipline – Nutrien remains steadfast in allocating capital to shareholders, raising its dividend for the eighth consecutive year and maintaining approximately $1 billion in annual dividend expense. The company’s board has authorized the repurchase of up to 5% of outstanding shares over the next 12 months, with buybacks running at roughly $50 million per month. In 2025, returns to shareholders increased 30% year over year. This disciplined capital return framework—supported by strong free cash flow even amid earnings cyclicality—signals management’s confidence in long-term value creation and operational resilience, while the ongoing reduction in share count improves per-share metrics without materially raising total dividend obligation.

4. Portfolio Optimization and Cost Reductions Enhance Earnings Quality

Systematic asset reviews and structural cost savings drive sustainable value – Management is actively rationalizing the portfolio, with roughly $900 million in divestitures of non-core assets and project cancellations in 2025, deploying proceeds toward short-term debt reduction (over $600 million year over year) and further capital returns. At the same time, Nutrien delivered over $200 million in cost reductions in 2025, cut sustaining capital expenditures below target to $2 billion, and improved working capital management. These actions underpin more stable and higher-quality earnings, position the business to navigate industry cycles, and allow management to focus capital on segments generating superior returns on invested capital, particularly in proprietary retail products and core fertilizer assets.

5. Downstream Retail Innovation and Digital Ag Expansion Accelerate Growth

Proprietary products and digital partnerships create structural retail earnings growth – Nutrien’s downstream retail segment now delivers adjusted EBITDA of $1.74 billion (2025), up $400 million since 2023 at a 6% compound annual growth rate, as proprietary products and targeted innovation become powerful growth engines. Proprietary product gross margin reached $1.2 billion in 2025 and is targeted for high single-digit growth rates in 2026, with 26 new product launches planned and strong international expansion opportunities. The recently announced partnership with BASF to digitize crop carbon intensity positions the company at the forefront of agricultural sustainability, while enhanced digital offerings and margin-accretive proprietary lines ensure resilience in the face of ag-sector cyclicality, supporting long-term retail earnings expansion across its core markets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Underperformance and Uncertainty in Brazil and Phosphate Challenge Portfolio Value

Structural headwinds in Brazil retail and phosphate persist despite cost actions – Despite incremental improvement, Nutrien’s Brazil retail business only reached break-even in 2025 and faces continued market headwinds and strategic uncertainty, with management signaling potential resizing or exit in 2026. Similarly, the phosphate segment remains structurally uncompetitive, posting ongoing negative or minimal margins due to elevated sulfur input costs and operating constraints, triggering a formal strategic review with outcomes ranging from restructuring to divestiture. Persistent underperformance and lack of clear resolution in these areas weigh on overall portfolio returns and could impede management’s ability to focus capital on core, high-return operations, potentially dragging earnings and valuation if progress stalls.

2. Geopolitical and Supply Chain Risks Threaten Earnings Stability

Tight global supply chains amplify the impact of shocks on cost and demand – Fertilizer markets are inherently volatile and highly sensitive to geopolitical disruptions, as evidenced by the recent Iran war, input cost inflation, and unpredictable weather patterns. Nutrien’s business is vulnerable to swings in agricultural cycles, supply chain interruptions, and shifting trade flows, including potential slowdowns in large importing countries or the entry of new capacity from players in the former Soviet Union (FSU), China, and Chile. While the company boasts industry-low controllable potash costs, operating near capacity leaves less buffer to absorb shocks, and unexpected supply or demand disruptions could rapidly erode margins and stall growth, as seen in periods of market oversupply or rapid commodity price reversals.

3. Nitrogen Segment Performance Constrained by Trinidad Plant Uncertainty

Trinidad nitrogen operations face ongoing gas supply and cost obstacles with unclear resolution – Nutrien’s Trinidad nitrogen facility, representing roughly 15% of segment volumes, has been idled due to persistent challenges in securing economically viable natural gas supplies. The plant contributed only 3% of segment earnings and 1% of cash flow in 2025 before exclusion from 2026 guidance, highlighting its limited current value and strategic uncertainty. If government efforts to secure adequate gas supplies falter, or if costly idling continues without a near-term outcome, the segment’s total volume and potential recovery may remain capped. This would leave the nitrogen portfolio smaller and more exposed to any price swings in core North American markets, potentially limiting future earnings upside and operational flexibility.
