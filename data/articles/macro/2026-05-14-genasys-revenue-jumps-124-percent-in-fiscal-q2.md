Key Takeaways
Genasys returned to profitability and reported gross margin over 63%.
Revenue more than doubled compared to the prior year period.

Genasys (NASDAQ:GNSS) returned to profitability in the latest quarter, driven by strong hardware deliveries and accelerating software sales. The provider of Protective Communications platforms for public safety and defense saw momentum in both its hardware and software businesses during the second quarter. The company's fiscal second quarter results were announced on May 14, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. Overall, the quarter featured strong growth, high margins, and improving profitability but also exposed continued reliance on major projects and outstanding receivables.

Notable Developments
Hardware revenue surged, powered by continued deliveries on the Puerto Rico Early Warning System and new defense orders such as a $9.0 million contract for the CROWS II program.
Genasys Protect software gained traction, landing five new municipal customers in California and new entries in Idaho and North Carolina, boosting population coverage above 25.5 million residents.
Accounts receivable swelled to $13.1 million, reflecting delayed cash collections from major projects, while the cash balance fell to $1.0 million and notes payable stood at $14.6 million.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated guidance for fiscal 2026, aiming for record revenue, gross margin above 50%, and net income profitability. The company expects to collect significant payments from Puerto Rico soon, with plans to retire its remaining debt and improve its balance sheet. The most important thing for investors to monitor is the timing and certainty of these project-related cash collections, as they will affect short-term liquidity.

Quarterly highlights

Hardware delivered the biggest revenue lift, with continued progress on the Puerto Rico project and new orders such as the CROWS II and Singapore Navy contracts. This led to a record-high gross margin, as hardware mix improved profitability in the quarter and supported an operating income swing.

Software showed clear momentum, with Genasys Protect expanding into new states and winning more municipal contracts. The addition of five new cities in California underscores the rising contribution from recurring software sales and geographic expansion, a strategic focus for the company.

Balance sheet metrics reflected the challenge of large project timing: accounts receivable increased, while cash dropped and notes payable remained high. Management intends to address this by collecting outstanding Puerto Rico receivables to retire debt and stabilize liquidity in the coming months.

What Genasys does

Genasys delivers hardware and software platforms that enable government agencies and organizations to send critical, real-time communications and warnings for public safety, defense, and emergency management.
