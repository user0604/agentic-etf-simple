Key Takeaways
Dingdong (Cayman) posted strong year-over-year growth but results were distorted by an accounting boost from its planned China business sale.
Revenue rose for the ninth consecutive quarter, reaching RMB5,892.7 million, up 7.5% year over year.

Dingdong (Cayman) (NYSE:DDL) delivered another quarter of growth, but headline profits were temporarily inflated by accounting changes related to its planned China business divestiture. The online fresh grocery retailer operates both within China and overseas, with most of its recent growth driven by the China segment. In its first-quarter 2026 report, released May 22, 2026, the company’s bottom line surged due to a one-time accounting effect, giving a boost to reported profits ahead of its business transition.

Overall, the quarter reflected ongoing top-line momentum and supply chain improvements, but the headline profit does not fully represent sustainable operating strength.

Notable Developments
The announced sale of the China business to Meituan resulted in an accounting reclassification, artificially boosting net income by about RMB138 million this period.
Gross margin remained flat at 29.9% year over year, while fulfillment expenses dropped as a percentage of revenue from 22.9% to 20.6%.
Overseas operations saw revenue surge nearly 200%, but losses in this segment also deepened, with the net loss reaching RMB71.4 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management noted continued revenue growth and expects "faster growth momentum entering the second quarter." However, after the sale of the China business completes, future reports will reflect only the much smaller, still-unprofitable overseas segment. The most important factor to watch is whether the company can bring overseas operations to profitability as the business transitions away from China.

Quarterly Highlights

The China business, still delivered by Dingdong in this quarter, generated nearly all company profits and showed steady revenue growth thanks to higher customer order volume and improved network density. However, the quarter’s net income figure was substantially boosted by the ending of depreciation and amortization charges following the held-for-sale reclassification: this added about RMB138 million, masking true operating profitability.

Supply chain and cost management initiatives continued to pay off. Fulfillment expenses fell 3.3% from the prior-year period and, as a share of revenue, dropped by over two percentage points, reflecting streamlining in logistics and delivery.

The overseas business posted strong revenue growth (up 195.2%), but losses widened sharply to RMB71.4 million. After the China divestiture, this segment is set to become the company’s sole operating arm and remains unprofitable so far.

What Dingdong (Cayman) Does

Dingdong (Cayman) operates online platforms for fresh food and grocery delivery, with a core business in China and expanding efforts overseas.
