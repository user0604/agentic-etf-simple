B.o.s. Better Online Solutions (NASDAQ:BOSC) delivered results in line with its own guidance, buoyed by a growing backlog but hampered by lower profits and sales compared to the same quarter last year. The technology-focused manufacturer provides automated robotics, RFID-tagging systems, and supply chain solutions to clients worldwide. Reporting first quarter 2026 results on May 28, 2026, the company faced margin challenges from currency swings and elevated costs. Overall, the quarter revealed steady demand and promising order growth, but profitability pressure persisted as expenses rose faster than cost savings.

Notable Developments
Backlog of future orders reached $31 million, up 29% from December 2025, supporting future sales visibility.
Orders from Indian customers grew nearly twentyfold, jumping to $3.3 million this quarter from $172,000 a year earlier.
Gross profit margin improved to nearly 25%, even as overall net income dropped to $765,000 from $1.35 million in the year-ago period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its revenue forecast for fiscal 2026 and now expects annual sales to surpass $51 million, but is holding its net income target steady at $3.6 million. This signals confidence in growing demand, especially from markets like India, yet underscores the ongoing impact of a weaker U.S. dollar against the Israeli shekel on costs. Investors should monitor the company’s ability to convert its growing order backlog into higher profits, especially as currency volatility remains a risk.

Quarterly Highlights

Order momentum was strong, with the company achieving substantial new business wins in India and growing its future sales pipeline. The Indian market accounted for $3.3 million in new orders, cementing its importance to the company’s strategic expansion goals outside its core Israeli base. The backlog of orders ended the period at $31 million, offering better earnings visibility for the remainder of the year.

The RFID (radio-frequency identification) division maintained stable revenue, bringing in $3.5 million. However, its profit margin remained modest. The company launched a new effort to transition the division’s solutions into Israel’s defense sector, reducing reliance on local retail, which has been sensitive to regional risk.

Gross margin increased to 24.9%, up by 1.0 percentage point year over year, attributed in part to better product mix and some operational improvements. That said, rising operating costs, especially from currency effects, caused operating income and net income to fall sharply compared to the previous period.
