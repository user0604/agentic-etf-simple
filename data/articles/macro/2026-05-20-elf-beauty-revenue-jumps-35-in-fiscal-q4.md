Key Takeaways
Solid business win as e.l.f. Beauty delivered robust sales growth, aided by acquisitions.
Revenue climbed 35% compared to the year-ago quarter.

e.l.f. Beauty (NYSE:ELF) continued its streak of sales gains, fueled by brand acquisitions and heavy marketing investments. The company, a prominent provider of affordable, vegan, and cruelty-free beauty and skincare products, reported its fourth-quarter results for the period ended March 31, 2026 (released on May 20, 2026). While sales rose at a rapid clip, profit margins and earnings per share faced notable pressure. Overall, the quarter offered both signs of strength and fresh questions about integrating new brands and managing costs.

Notable Developments
Revenue jumped 35% in the quarter, mainly due to strong contributions from acquired brands, including rhode and Naturium.
Net profit margins declined, with adjusted diluted earnings per share dropping 59%, impacted by rising costs and a one-time charge.
Balance sheet leverage increased, as cash nearly doubled, but long-term debt more than tripled year over year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management guided for slower sales growth of 12%–14% in fiscal 2027, down from the 25% seen in fiscal 2026, but expects adjusted profit margins to improve slightly.

Quarterly Highlights

Acquisitions played a leading role in top-line performance, as rhode far outpaced expected sales targets and required a $57.6 million fair value adjustment to acquisition-related obligations. While this boosted reported sales, it increased complexity in overall profit reporting.

Cost pressures continued to weigh on profitability. Adjusted EBITDA — used to measure ongoing cash operating profit — fell 28% year over year in the quarter, with heavy increases in marketing and staffing expenses. The quarter also saw the company’s adjusted net income and earnings per share decline even as revenue grew, underscoring the impact of higher spending and integration costs.

Cash levels at period end reached $289.7 million, up 94.9% from the prior year, but total long-term debt ballooned to $841.7 million — up more than threefold — reflecting the financing of recent acquisitions. The company’s asset-light supply chain model remains exposed to tariff risks, but management highlighted the potential for slight margin improvement if costs stabilize in the coming year.
