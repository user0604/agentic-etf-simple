Celestica Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

Celestica Inc. (NYSE:CLS) is a global leader in advanced electronics manufacturing with a pronounced focus on designing, engineering, and producing next-generation hardware for cloud, AI, and enterprise data center markets. The company’s growth strategy centers on moving up the value chain through high-value, codesign partnerships with hyperscalers and enterprise customers while securing multi-year supply and capacity contracts that underpin rapid revenue and profit expansion through 2027 and beyond.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Surging Cloud and AI Infrastructure Demand Fuels Historic Revenue Growth

Explosive, sustained demand from hyperscalers is driving exceptional top-line expansion – In Q1 2026, Celestica’s Connectivity & Cloud Solutions (CCS) segment accounted for 80% of total company revenue, reaching $3.24 billion—an increase of 76% year over year. Revenues are being propelled by hyperscaler adoption of next-generation networking switches (such as 800G and 1.6T platforms) and advanced AI/ML compute solutions. Management has raised 2026 revenue guidance by $2 billion to $19 billion—up 53% from the prior year—demonstrating both the pace and durability of demand as cloud and artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure investments accelerate across the industry. This dynamic, if it holds, ensures a solid foundation for continued outperformance relative to hardware peers.

2. High-Value Program Pipeline and Multi-Year Contracts Cement Growth Visibility

Deep program awards and long-term contracts lock in future revenues – Celestica’s awarded and in-development program pipeline is reported as the strongest in company history, with multi-year bookings tied to major hyperscaler customers now extending through 2028. Recent wins—including the AMD Helios rack-scale networking and 1.6T co-packaged optics Ethernet switch—will drive mass production into late 2026 and 2027. Approximately 65% of Celestica’s revenue comes from three top customers, and management reports that many long-term agreements with core customers include non-cancelable, non-returnable (NCNR) terms. This contractual structure reduces volatility and gives management unprecedented forecast certainty, in turn supporting ambitious expansion and operational investments with lower risk of abrupt program or demand reversals.

3. Margin Expansion Driven by Shift to Higher-Margin, Design-Intensive Services

Operating margins are reaching new highs as Celestica moves up the value chain – The shift toward high-value, custom systems is driving margin improvement. In Q1 2026, non-GAAP operating margin hit 8.0% (up 90 basis points year over year), and adjusted gross margin expanded to 11.3% (up 30 basis points), both at or above the high end of guidance. The Hardware Platform Solutions (HPS) business, a subset of CCS, showed 63% growth and now delivers higher operating leverage. As more engineering-intensive projects ramp up, the company is seeing sustainable profitability improvements that could support continued upward revisions to adjusted earnings per share—now projected to grow 68% to $10.15 in 2026. This mix shift to design and codesign projects differentiates Celestica from lower-margin contract manufacturers.

4. Disciplined Capital Allocation and Strengthened Liquidity Enable Aggressive, Risk-Managed Expansion

Robust balance sheet and expanded credit lines secure the capital for growth – In April 2026, Celestica refinanced and increased its revolving credit facility from $750 million to $1.75 billion and extended maturities to April 2031, creating over $2 billion in available liquidity. Gross debt to non-GAAP trailing 12-month adjusted EBITDA leverage ratio was 0.6, and Celestica was in compliance with all financial covenants. Elevated capital expenditures—$1 billion for 2026 and $1.5 billion projected for 2027—are directly linked to awarded programs and backed by strong business cases with anchor customers. Free cash flow remains healthy ($500 million projected for 2026), demonstrating disciplined financial management even amid rapid scaling, and providing flexibility to handle inventory builds, opportunistic investments, or shareholder returns as conditions warrant.

5. Rising R&D Intensity and Engineering Capabilities Deepen Competitive Advantage

Rapidly growing design headcount and innovation lock in customer stickiness – Celestica has materially increased its investment in research and development and boosted its design engineering team to about 1,350, up significantly year over year. This expansion enables codesign of complex hardware such as co-packaged optics Ethernet switches and liquid-cooled rack-scale AI systems with leading technology companies. The ability to deliver sophisticated, system-level projects not only increases attainable margins but also creates longer-duration, strategic relationships with customers. These capabilities establish Celestica as more than a contract manufacturer—it becomes an integral partner on the critical supply lines of AI and cloud infrastructure, positioning it to win in the current cycle and beyond.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Component Shortages and Input Cost Inflation Threaten Program Execution

Escalating supply chain constraints could limit growth and compress margins – Management acknowledges that component shortages—especially in custom silicon, memory, complex printed circuit boards, and optical parts—have worsened versus prior quarters. While Celestica has secured critical supply for near-term ramps, further bottlenecks could stall production, causing schedule slippage and missed revenue or margin targets. Rising input costs for silicon and memory are already capping gross margin expansion, and persistent or worsening shortages could force more expensive sourcing, eroding the benefits of strong demand and program wins.

2. Severe Customer Concentration Leaves Growth Vulnerable to Hyperscaler Demand Shifts

Abrupt changes in spending by key customers could destabilize performance – Despite multi-year NCNR contracts, Celestica remains highly exposed to a small number of hyperscaler and cloud customers, with its top three accounting for about 65% of total revenue (as of Q1 2026). If any of these technology giants reprioritize capital expenditure, delay major ramps, or move to alternate suppliers, Celestica’s growth trajectory and cash flow would be at risk. The possibility also exists that new hardware solutions (such as co-packaged optics) cannibalize legacy programs, compressing revenues unless additional pipeline wins offset substitution effects within these concentrated relationships.

3. Valuation Risks Mount as Market Expectations Move Ahead of Executive Guidance

Rapid upward earnings revisions leave little margin for error if growth slows – Celestica has raised its 2026 adjusted EPS outlook to $10.15 (up from $8.75 previously), representing a projected 68% year-over-year increase. The stock price has already reflected much of this optimism, experiencing both sharp rallies and volatility (including a 14% drop immediately following Q1 earnings). If unforeseen market or supply disruptions delay program ramps or earnings growth, there is a risk of multiple compression or rapid share price correction should actual performance fail to match the elevated outlook. Investors must weigh whether the current valuation appropriately accounts for execution risks and the potential for normalization in industry growth rates beyond the current AI/cloud hardware supercycle.
