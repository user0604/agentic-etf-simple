Key Takeaways
ABM Industries delivered another quarter of revenue and cash flow growth, but profit margins remained under pressure. Adjusted earnings per share rose to $0.90 on revenue up 8.4% year over year. Management reaffirmed the full-year profit outlook and expects revenue growth at the high end of guidance.

The facility services leader Abm Industries (NYSE:ABM) grew its revenue to a new second-quarter record, but its profit margins declined due to rising costs and operational challenges. The company, which provides janitorial, technical, aviation, and other solutions to clients across North America and the UK, reported results for its fiscal second quarter on June 5, 2026. Overall, the quarter demonstrated solid growth in sales and cash generation, balanced by persistent margin pressures.

Notable Developments
Revenue hit $2.29 billion, up 8.4% from prior year, driven by 6.1% organic and 2.3% acquisition growth.
Segment operating margin slipped to 7.3%, as newer contracts and ramp-up costs weighed on profitability.
Free cash flow improved to $22.4 million, supported by better working capital controls during the quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management kept its earnings and margin guidance unchanged and expects revenue growth at the upper end of the 4% to 5% full-year range. Margin recovery remains a priority, with leadership targeting improvement in the second half through cost and pricing efforts. Investors should monitor whether margin pressure eases as recent acquisitions are integrated and transformation programs advance.

Quarterly Highlights

The period saw the company post a solid increase in revenue, underpinned by record first-half new business bookings of $1.2 billion. Strong performance was especially evident in the Technical Solutions and Aviation segments, where demand for energy storage and datacenter services, alongside air travel trends, supported growth.

Despite this revenue growth, profitability challenges were clear as the segment operating margin declined by 0.6 percentage points to 7.3%. The slide was tied primarily to costs associated with new contracts in core segments like Manufacturing & Distribution and Business & Industry, as well as ramp-up costs in Aviation.

On the cash front, the quarter delivered improved free cash flow and operating cash flow figures. Management attributed this to better working capital management and continued investment in efficiency initiatives, such as the ongoing upgrade of the company’s enterprise resource planning systems.

What Abm Industries Does
