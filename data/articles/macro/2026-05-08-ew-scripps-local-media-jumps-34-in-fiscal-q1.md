E.w. Scripps Local Media Jumps 34% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 8:22 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Local Media profit climbed while Scripps Networks saw steep declines.
Revenue slipped with adjusted EBITDA down year over year.
Management looks to political ads and digital streaming for future gains.

Local media profits at television company E.w. Scripps (NASDAQ:SSP) jumped on the back of sports rights and early election-year advertising, but the company’s networks division posted sharp declines. The operator of local TV stations and national digital multicast networks released results for its first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 8, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed solid momentum in Local Media but ongoing profit and revenue pressure in Scripps Networks.

Notable Developments
Local Media segment profit grew 33.7%, helped by a 5% revenue rise and strong demand for live sports and political advertising.
Scripps Networks revenue fell 11.1%, and segment profit dropped nearly 28%, reflecting seasonal factors and digital transition efforts.
The company reduced its net leverage ratio to 3.9×, receiving $123 million from TV station sales and improving cash reserves to $84 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects Local Media revenue to continue growing at a low-single-digit rate in the next quarter, with Scripps Networks' revenue projected to decline about 10%. The company highlighted growing political ad bookings and the recent launch of its sports streaming channel as future growth drivers. Investors should pay close attention to profit trends in the Networks segment, which remains under pressure during the digital transition.

Quarterly Highlights

Local Media posted its strongest first-quarter segment profit in years, driven by rising advertising, especially from sports deals with National Hockey League teams and a ramp-up in political advertising. Segment revenue gained 5%, while expenses increased only 1.5%, leading to notable margin expansion.

Scripps Networks faced double-digit revenue declines, in part due to the absence of certain programming and the continued shift away from traditional broadcast toward streaming. This decline led to a nearly 28% drop in segment profit, despite recent launches such as Scripps Sports Network, a new free ad-supported streaming TV service.

On the financial front, the company sold two television stations, using the $123 million proceeds to pay down debt. Its net leverage ratio improved from 4.8× at the prior year-end to 3.9× at March 31, 2026, while cash and equivalents rose sharply.

What E.w. Scripps Does

E.w. Scripps owns and operates U.S. local television stations and national broadcast networks, with growing activities in digital media and streaming.
