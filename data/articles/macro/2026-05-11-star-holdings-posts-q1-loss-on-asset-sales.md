Star Holdings (NASDAQ:STHO) posted another quarterly loss as it marked down the value of its Safehold investment, but continued to return cash to shareholders and completed key loan collections. The company, a real estate asset manager currently winding down its portfolio, reported first-quarter 2026 results on May 8, 2026. Overall, the quarter saw steady progress in asset monetization but ongoing earnings volatility from its investment holdings.

Notable Developments
Star received repayments on two loans totaling $13.7 million, adding liquidity to support future shareholder returns.
A non-cash charge of $2.2 million related to Safehold shares contributed to the quarterly net loss.
The company repurchased 0.2 million shares for $2.0 million, demonstrating active capital return amid portfolio sales.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its strategy to maximize shareholder value through ongoing asset sales and loan collections, but did not offer new guidance for upcoming quarters. Investors should closely monitor the company’s progress in monetizing its remaining properties and the continued financial impact of its Safehold investment.

Quarterly Highlights

The period saw significant asset monetization, with repayments on a $10.6 million mezzanine loan in Asbury Park and a $3.1 million senior mortgage on a New York property. These loan collections are key for Star as it winds down its portfolio and builds cash for either distribution or debt reduction.

Earnings were dominated by a non-cash mark-to-market adjustment, as the value of the Safehold equity stake fell, costing Star $2.2 million for the period. This adjustment directly reduced reported EPS by $0.18, and continued swings in Safehold’s share price may remain an important influence on results.

Star also bought back 0.2 million shares, spending $2.0 million at an average price of $8.45 per share. This move highlights management’s ongoing commitment to returning excess capital to investors when funds from asset sales are available.
