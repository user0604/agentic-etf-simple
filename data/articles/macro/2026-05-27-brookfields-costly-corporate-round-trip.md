Brookfield's Costly Corporate Round Trip

My broad takeaway: What was the point?

Jim Gillies

May 27, 2026, 10:26 AM

Comments
9.5

Every so often I like to highlight silly investing stories – an attempt to remind myself (and the rest of y'all, particularly value guys like me) that the investing world can be absurd, messy, and, presumably, subject to competing interests perhaps not aligned with your own.

And no, I'm not talking about the pending SpaceX IPO and what it's probably going to do to retail investors.

Rather, I'm looking in the direction of Brookfield Corp (NYSE: BN)(TSX: BN), which this morning announced its "Corporate Simplification," which essentially amounts to a friendly merger/takeover of Brookfield Wealth Solutions (NYSE: BNT)(TSX: BNT) that is proceeding to its final stages.

My broad takeaway: What was the point?

To recap:

In December 2021 Brookfield spins out Brookfield Asset Management Reinsurance Partners (what a mouthful!)
Brookfield Reinsurance is structured to be "economically equivalent" to Brookfield...shares are exchangeable 1:1 out of the gate.
This effectively tethers Brookfield Reinsurance's price to Brookfield's price because otherwise an arbitrage opens up. If Reinsurance happened to drop, say, 10%-15% below the price of the mothership, why wouldn't you exercise your exchangeable option to convert to Brookfield proper? Of course you would do so...you're smart and can do math!
It is signaled that the exchangeability will be discarded after Reinsurance makes a few deals and the businesses start to diverge.
There's a random name change and rebranding a few years in; deals at Reinsurance, now "Brookfield Wealth Solutions," happen. The exchangeability between Brookfield Wealth and Brookfield proper never happens.
Brookfield and Brookfield Wealth Solutions then announce their intent to "simplify their corporate structure under one publicly traded company," by recombining after less than 5 years.

So I ask again: What was the point???

Separation was expensive. Coming together is expensive. Lawyers need to get paid. Investment bankers aren't cheap. Auditors like money in exchange for services. There's duplication of roles at two different companies, et cetera. For a company usually so smart (Brookfield), I question what they were thinking through this whole process.

The bottom line? You can get exposure to Brookfield AND all of its many spin-offs and satellites by just owning the parent company. This is now the second Brookfield entity (the first being commercial real estate entity Brookfield Property Partners – spun out in 2013, taken back under full Brookfield control in 2021) to be folded back into the mothership. Brookfield analysis is often fraught with complexity, but it's perhaps much simpler – just own the mothership.

Hopefully management here embrace that notion as well, rather than doing more costly and ultimately irrelevant spin-offs.
