Key Takeaways
Csw Industrials posted record revenue but faced margin and cash flow pressures.
Revenue rose year over year.
Management expects growth in revenue, adjusted EBITDA, and adjusted EPS for fiscal 2027.

Csw Industrials (NYSE:CSW) delivered record sales in the quarter, driven by acquisitions and a modest recovery in its core business. The diversified manufacturer focuses on building products and specialty chemicals, supplying sectors like HVAC/R (heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration), electrical, and infrastructure. The company released its fourth-quarter and full-year results on May 26, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31, 2026. Overall, the period showed growth in enterprise scale but notable downside in profit margins and cash generation.

Notable developments
Revenue hit a record high, rising 34% with $72 million from recent acquisitions and $6.4 million from organic improvement.
Profitability showed a split: adjusted EPS grew 21%, while reported EPS dropped 41% due to impairment and higher interest.
Free cash flow turned negative, swinging by nearly $30 million, as working capital increased to support large sales growth.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expressed “cautious optimism” for fiscal 2027, projecting further growth in sales, adjusted EBITDA, and adjusted EPS. Attention will stay on margin improvements and cash flow recovery as the integration of new businesses continues and as efforts continue to streamline costs and operations.

Quarterly highlights

Record revenue was achieved this quarter with most growth fueled by acquisitions, especially in the Contractor Solutions segment, which caters to HVAC/R (heating, ventilation, air conditioning, and refrigeration) and electrical markets. Organic sales in this area finally returned to growth, after several quarters of weakness linked to the broader housing market.

Despite strong sales, gross margin declined by 3.2 percentage points due to the lower margin structure of acquired firms and higher input costs, only partly offset by pricing actions. The quarter also featured a significant non-cash impairment, mostly in the Engineered Building Solutions segment, which contributed to the sharp drop in headline (reported) earnings.

Cash flow weakened, moving to an outflow for the quarter as inventories and receivables grew with higher sales and recent acquisitions. The company reported net debt of $842.7 million, at the upper end of its stated target range, after spending $1.0 billion on acquisitions during the year and returning $145.5 million to shareholders through dividends and buybacks. The quarterly dividend was kept steady at $0.30 per share.
