Centuri Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 3:06 PM

Comments

Centuri Holdings, Inc. (NYSE:CTRI) is a leading provider of utility and energy infrastructure services across North America, specializing in maintenance, modernization, and installation for gas and electric utilities. Its growth strategy centers on high-recurrence Master Service Agreements (MSAs), capitalizing on secular trends like grid modernization, electrification, and data center expansion. The company emphasizes operational discipline and geographic expansion to drive consistent revenue and margin growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Recurring Master Service Agreements Underpin Revenue Predictability and Resilience

A high proportion of long-term, recurring contracts shields the business from economic cyclicality and sharp revenue swings. In 2025, Centuri generated 78% of its revenue from Master Service Agreements, while its year-end backlog consisted of 82% work contracted under these same agreements. This structure allows the company to provide investors with strong visibility; as of year-end 2025, more than 85% of the next year's revenue was already covered by backlog. Such high renewal rates—100% of MSAs renewed in 2025—indicate deep entrenchment with regulated utility customers, creating a stable base for both near- and long-term earnings growth, even through periods of broader economic or utility spending volatility.

2. Record Backlog and Bookings Position the Business for Accelerated Near-Term Growth

Centuri’s record $6.5 billion backlog at the end of Q1 2026 sets a solid foundation for sustained top-line expansion. The company’s backlog increased 59% year over year to a then-record $5.9 billion in 2025, and surpassed $6.5 billion in the most recent quarter. With over $4.5 billion in annual bookings in 2025 (a 1.5x book-to-bill ratio), Centuri is converting industry tailwinds in grid modernization and electrification into a robust forward revenue pipeline. This backlog not only represents future sales but also indicates the quality and depth of customer demand for critical maintenance and modernization work, supporting management’s 2026 revenue guidance and multi-year growth outlook.

3. Margin Expansion Driven by Operational Initiatives and Segment Shifts

Recent margin gains suggest a structural improvement in profitability as Centuri executes on process enhancements and business mix shifts. In 2025, base gross profit margin (excluding storm restoration) expanded by 110 basis points to 8%, and adjusted EBITDA rose to $249 million, up from $238 million the prior year. Particularly notable were the Canadian operations, with revenue up 25% and gross margin jumping to 18.6%. The nonunion electric segment delivered a 51% revenue increase and improved margins by 260 basis points to 8.5%, fueled by better crew efficiency and scale. Sustained execution on these improvement initiatives—even during the seasonally soft first quarter of 2026, when gross profit surged 76% year over year—signals continued progress toward higher, more predictable profitability.

4. Secular Industry Tailwinds in Grid Modernization and Data Centers Create Multi-Year Demand

Exposure to the secular growth of electrification, renewables, and AI-driven data centers creates a durable demand tailwind. North American utilities are accelerating capital outlays for grid reliability, aging infrastructure replacement, and supporting new loads from data centers and renewable energy sources. Centuri highlighted a $13 billion opportunity pipeline, including over $1.3 billion of live data center projects in late-stage bids as of early 2026. The company’s strategic expansion—such as the recent offshore wind teaming agreement in Canada—diversifies its addressable market, adding resiliency and long-term revenue opportunities beyond traditional regulated utilities. These demand drivers are expected to sustain above-GDP growth in utility services over the coming decade, positioning Centuri to capture incremental share in expanding verticals.

5. Improved Capital Structure and Financial Flexibility Enable Strategic Growth Initiatives

Balance sheet improvements through equity issuance and debt reduction are enhancing Centuri’s ability to pursue growth and M&A. After fully separating from its parent in 2025, Centuri raised $251 million in equity, funding the Connect acquisition and reducing net debt to adjusted EBITDA from 3.6x at 2024 year-end to 2.5x by 2025 year-end, with a target of 2.0x by end of 2026. The shift to fleet leasing and sale-leasebacks improved free cash flow generation while supporting capital-light growth. These actions create headroom for future tuck-in acquisitions in targeted U.S. and Canadian markets, further expanding capabilities and geographic reach, and providing the financial strength to weather cyclical pressures or pursue opportunistic expansion.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Execution Risk in Margin Improvement and Gas Segment Seasonality

The company faces challenges in eliminating seasonal earnings swings and consistently expanding margins, especially in its gas business. Management has outlined a multi-year effort to reduce seasonality in the gas segment and deliver steadier margins throughout the year, but execution risks remain. In 2025, U.S. Gas gross margin held steady at 5.4%, despite only modest revenue growth, and leadership explicitly acknowledged it will take several years to fully “eliminate seasonality” and reach targeted margins each quarter. If operational initiatives underdeliver or unforeseen setbacks occur—whether from weather, bid discipline lapses, or labor bottlenecks—margin improvement could stall, preventing Centuri from achieving its multi-year earnings goals and disappointing investor expectations built into the current outlook.

2. Labor Availability and Cost Inflation Pose Ongoing Headwinds to Scaling Operations

Ongoing sector-wide labor constraints and input cost inflation may limit profitable growth if hiring and retention do not keep pace with backlog needs. Centuri is targeting 12–15% annual headcount growth to support expanding project volumes, especially in rapidly growing segments like nonunion electric and Canadian operations. However, tight skilled labor markets in North America, rising wage inflation, and competition for specialized utility crews create ongoing risks to both execution and profitability. Any inability to recruit, retain, or efficiently deploy enough workers could drive up costs or result in underperformance on key contracts, reducing margins and slowing backlog conversion into actual revenue.

3. Exposure to Utility Customer Capital Expenditure Cycles and Regulatory Delays May Slow Growth

Centuri’s reliance on regulated utility customers leaves the business vulnerable to capital allocation shifts and regulatory bottlenecks. While Master Service Agreements provide stability, the pace of utility spending can fluctuate in response to regulatory processes, rate case outcomes, and broader macroeconomic or political uncertainties. If utilities delay major infrastructure upgrades—whether due to funding constraints, changes in policy, or regulatory challenges—Centuri could experience slower new bookings, project delays, or reduced backlog conversion. These factors are largely outside management’s control and may create periods of muted growth despite favorable long-term industry trends.
