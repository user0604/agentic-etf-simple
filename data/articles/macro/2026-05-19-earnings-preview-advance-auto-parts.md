Earnings Preview: Advance Auto Parts

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 9:05 AM

Comments

Advance Auto Parts (NYSE:AAP), a major supplier of automotive replacement parts and accessories for both professional and do-it-yourself (DIY) customers, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings before the market opens on Thursday, May 21, 2026.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Advance Auto Parts reported net sales of approximately $2 billion, reflecting a 1% decline year over year. This drop was mainly due to the company’s strategic closure of over 500 corporate stores and 200 independent locations earlier in the year. Despite the lower sales, comparable store sales grew 1.1% in the quarter, marking a return to positive growth after three years of declines. The company’s adjusted diluted earnings per share (EPS) from continuing operations came in at $0.86, which beat analyst estimates of $0.42. Gross profit margin expanded to 44.2% of net sales, up significantly from the previous year, and adjusted operating income margin rose to 3.7%. The quarter included an extra operating week, contributing $132 million in net sales and $9 million in adjusted operating income. The company ended the year with an improved balance sheet, holding more than $3 billion in cash and a net debt leverage ratio of 2.4 times.

Positives
Return to positive comparable sales growth: After three years of declines, the company achieved a 1.1% increase in comparable sales, signaling improved execution and customer traction.
Significant margin expansion: Adjusted gross profit margin reached 44.2%, and operating income margin expanded by 870 basis points year over year, reflecting successful cost-saving and sourcing initiatives.
Pro business strength: Sales to professional customers grew nearly 4% in the quarter, outperforming the DIY segment and helping offset softness in consumer-driven categories.
EPS beat expectations: The company reported $0.86 in adjusted diluted EPS, more than doubling consensus estimates and reflecting operational improvements.
Negatives
Overall net sales declined: Total net sales fell 1% compared to the prior year, mainly due to the impact of widespread store closures.
DIY segment weakness: Sales to do-it-yourself customers declined in the low single-digit range, with management citing continued pressure on lower- and mid-income consumers.
Free cash flow remained negative: The company posted negative free cash flow for the year, impacted by restructuring-related cash outflows and reduced accounts payable.
Markdown headwinds from product transitions: The introduction of new brands and supplier changes led to higher-than-expected markdowns, which weighed on comparable sales for the quarter.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Comparable sales trends by channel: Investors should monitor whether growth in the professional segment continues to offset ongoing weakness in the DIY segment, as management expects both channels to contribute positively but with Pro outpacing DIY.
Gross margin performance and LIFO impact: The company guided for Q1 gross margin in the 44% to 45% range, with about $30 million in LIFO (Last-In, First-Out) expense expected to be a headwind. Watch for how merchandising and sourcing initiatives support margins despite these pressures.
Progress on supply chain and store productivity initiatives: Advance Auto Parts completed most of its distribution center consolidation and is now focused on simplifying operations and launching new labor and transportation tools. Updates on these efforts may indicate future cost savings and service improvements.
Early results from new loyalty program and owned brands: The recent launch of Advance Rewards (replacing Speed Perks) and the introduction of the ARGOS oil and fluids brand are intended to drive higher engagement and value for customers. Look for initial traction in customer adoption or sales mix shifts.
Cash flow and capital allocation: Management expects a return to positive free cash flow in 2026, with about $100 million targeted for the year. Watch for updates on working capital, restructuring costs, and capital spending, especially as the company increases investment in new stores and infrastructure upgrades.
