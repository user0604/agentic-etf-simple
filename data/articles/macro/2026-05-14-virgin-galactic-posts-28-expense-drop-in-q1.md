Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed that commercial test flights remain scheduled for the third quarter, with customer flights and revenue beginning in the fourth quarter of 2026. Quarterly highlights

Revenue was minimal again, consisting primarily of advance fees for seats on future suborbital flights, with no customer flights during the period. Cost control remained a focus, as non-GAAP operating expenses dropped to $57.8 million, supporting a smaller net loss and improvement in free cash flow versus the prior year’s quarter.

The company’s technology transition advanced, as its first next-generation spaceship moved from assembly to ground testing. Management confirmed that flight testing is on track for the third quarter, with preparations underway to support a ramp-up in flight activity once commercial operations begin.

On the financial side, the company ended the quarter with $251 million in cash and marketable securities, after equity issuance raised an additional $11 million, plus $52 million more obtained in April. External financing remains essential until substantial revenue begins, and the company continues to rely on at-the-market share offerings to support working capital.
