Record sales growth marked a milestone for Trio-Tech International (NYSEMKT:TRT), but higher costs continued to limit its profitability. Notable Developments
Semiconductor Back-End Solutions revenue nearly doubled, reaching $13.1 million, while Industrial Electronics also posted a solid 76% increase.
Overall gross margin contracted sharply by 11 percentage points, from 27% to 16%, due to a higher share of lower-margin, high-volume testing contracts.
The company maintained a strong cash position with $18.3 million at quarter-end and raised an additional $10 million in equity after the period closed.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management remains optimistic, projecting continued robust demand for semiconductor testing, particularly for advanced computing chips and electric vehicle (EV) applications. The expansion into new capacity in Southeast Asia is designed to support this growth.

Quarterly Highlights

The standout result for the period was the dramatic year-over-year jump in total revenue, fueled largely by the Semiconductor Back-End Solutions segment, which produces equipment and testing services for chip manufacturers targeting AI and automotive markets. This segment supplied most of the company’s growth, reflecting increased orders for burn-in boards and reliability validation of high-performance chips.

On the other hand, profitability metrics remained weak despite the sales surge. The gross margin rate declined by 11 percentage points compared to the prior year, as the company’s product mix shifted toward more high-volume but lower-margin testing services. Despite margin pressure, the company continued to make progress toward breakeven, with net loss attributable to common shareholders narrowing to $38 thousand from $495 thousand a year ago.

The company continues to pursue diversification and geographic growth, securing a large new facility in Penang, Malaysia, which will add 104,000 square feet of capacity. Trio-Tech’s cash balance and recent equity raise position it to support these investments even as working capital demands rise with expanding business activity.
