Key Takeaways
Fossil Group delivered stronger profitability but saw continued sales weaknesses. Adjusted EBITDA improved 59.3% year over year. Management reiterated its full-year guidance despite demand challenges in key categories.

Fossil Group (NASDAQ:FOSL) reported stronger profitability for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, but sales continued to decline. The global specialty retailer, known for its watches, jewelry, and leather goods, released its results on May 13, 2026. The period reflected steady execution on cost controls, but weak demand across Europe and direct-to-consumer channels offset operational gains. Overall, the quarter revealed persistent softness in underlying sales, even as expense discipline helped deliver better adjusted earnings.

Notable Developments
Direct-to-consumer sales declined 29%, while wholesale channel sales increased 5% at constant currency rates.
Traditional watch sales were essentially flat, but the Fossil brand dropped 17% year over year in constant currency.
Cost reductions led to a 9.9% decrease in selling, general, and administrative expenses, with inventory trimmed 14.3% compared to last year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management kept its full-year guidance unchanged, targeting a 4% to 6% sales decline with a return to growth by the fourth quarter and aiming for adjusted operating margins of 3% to 5%. The company signals confidence in its turnaround, but a close watch on sales trends, especially in the Fossil brand and direct-to-consumer segments, is warranted to assess recovery prospects.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter's profitability improved as operating expenses fell from the prior year and adjusted EBITDA increased. However, underlying sales contracted in most regions, with Europe showing a 14% decline in constant currency and Americas down by 3%.

The product mix shifted further toward traditional watches, with leathers and jewelry posting declines of 41% and 14%, respectively. Smartwatches contributed just $1.7 million, a marked decrease from last year's $4.0 million.

The company continued to close stores and reduce direct-to-consumer exposure, shrinking its global store count by 27 locations since the first quarter of the prior year. Inventory reductions and lower costs supported cash and liquidity, but debt increased to $195.3 million.
