McGraw Hill (NYSE:MH), a leading provider of digital and print learning solutions for schools and colleges, is expected to report earnings for its fiscal fourth quarter on Wednesday, June 3, 2026, after the market closes.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, McGraw Hill delivered revenue of $434.2 million, representing a 4.2% increase year over year. The company’s recurring revenue rose 14.8% to $357.5 million, and digital revenue grew 11% to $364 million. Adjusted EBITDA was $135.9 million, with a margin of 31.3%. The higher education segment stood out, growing revenue by 24% to $225.4 million, driven by continued market share gains, strong adoption of digital products, and favorable enrollment trends. The K-12 segment saw a 14.6% revenue decline, which management attributed to a smaller market opportunity this year. Following these results, management raised full-year guidance for revenue, recurring revenue, and Adjusted EBITDA. The quarter’s performance met or exceeded analyst expectations, as reflected in positive investor sentiment and the company’s decision to increase guidance.

Positives
Strong higher education growth: The higher education segment grew revenue by 24%, reflecting successful digital adoption and market share gains. This is significant because it demonstrates the company’s ability to outperform peers in a key market.
Recurring and digital revenue momentum: Recurring revenue rose to 82% of total revenue, and digital revenue made up 84%. This shift adds predictability and stability to future results.
Margin expansion and cash flow strength: Adjusted EBITDA margin improved to 31.3%, and cash flow from operations increased 12% year over year, supporting additional debt repayment and financial flexibility.
Raised full-year guidance: Management increased guidance for revenue and profitability, signaling confidence in ongoing business trends and execution.
Negatives
K-12 revenue decline: The K-12 segment’s revenue dropped 14.6% due to a smaller market opportunity this year. This highlights the impact of cyclical state adoption cycles and market timing.
International and professional segments flat: International revenue fell 1.8% and global professional revenue grew only 2%, showing limited growth outside the core U.S. education market.
