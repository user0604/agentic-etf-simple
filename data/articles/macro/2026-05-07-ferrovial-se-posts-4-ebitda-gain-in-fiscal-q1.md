Key Takeaways
Ferrovial Se reported mixed results as North American growth and a record order book balanced margin pressure and project delays.
Adjusted EBITDA increased 4.0% year over year, while revenue rose 1.9%.
Management remains focused on U.S. infrastructure projects, with construction margins expected to stabilize and strong future growth supported by the order backlog.

Ferrovial Se (NASDAQ:FER) delivered steady progress in its core North American businesses as project wins and solid execution offset cost pressure and construction delays. The global infrastructure developer and operator released results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, on May 7, 2026. Overall, the period showed a mix of growth in backlog and diversification, but also highlighted ongoing margin stress and weather-related impacts.

Notable Developments
The Highways segment posted 3.6% revenue growth and adjusted EBITDA of €235 million, with strong like-for-like gains in North America despite softer traffic in Texas.
Construction revenue reached €1,625 million, and the order book hit a record €17.6 billion, though the margin slipped to 3.1% due to higher U.S. bidding and technology costs.
Net debt rose to €6.1 billion as of March 2026, while liquidity excluding infrastructure projects increased to €5.5 billion from the prior year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its focus on U.S. infrastructure growth, highlighting strong positioning in upcoming express lane and airport projects. Construction margins are expected to remain near the long-term 3.5% target, supported by the robust order book. Investors should watch for short-term margin recovery and progress on major projects, especially the New Terminal One at JFK and U.S. highway developments.

Quarterly Highlights

The Highways division continued to perform well, with U.S. assets delivering double-digit adjusted EBITDA growth on a like-for-like basis. In Canada, the 407 ETR toll road saw traffic rise 7.1%, and a larger dividend is scheduled for the next quarter, reflecting strong cash generation from mature assets.

Construction operations achieved a record order backlog and healthy revenue, but higher bidding and technology investments in the U.S. weighed on margins. The North American subsidiary Webber helped offset weaker results from the Polish unit Budimex, which was affected by bad weather.

In the Airports division, the New Terminal One project at JFK reached 87% completion, with the first phase now targeted for fall 2026, a delay from earlier projections. The company announced a €400 million scrip dividend and ongoing share repurchases, signaling continued shareholder returns.

What Ferrovial Se Does

Ferrovial Se develops, constructs, finances, and operates transport infrastructure such as highways, airports, and energy projects, with a strong presence in North America and Europe.
