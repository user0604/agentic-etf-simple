PTC Therapeutics (NASDAQ:PTCT) capitalized on growing demand for its key medicines, setting a positive tone for the year. The biopharmaceutical company, specializing in rare disease therapies and drug development, published its first quarter 2026 results on May 7, 2026, covering the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter marked strong growth in new products, offsetting weaker mature franchises and supporting a higher outlook for the year.

Notable Developments
Sephience, the company’s new therapy for phenylketonuria (PKU), delivered revenue of $125 million in its first full quarter on the market and supported raising the annual product revenue outlook.
Mature products, including the Duchenne muscular dystrophy drugs Translarna and Emflaza, saw revenues fall steeply by 32% and 55% year over year, respectively, due to generic competition and shifting demand.
Pipeline progress continued with positive clinical results for votoplam (Huntington’s disease), and plans to start a new registration study for vatiquinone in Friedreich’s ataxia based on U.S. Food and Drug Administration feedback.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its 2026 guidance, now targeting $750–$850 million in annual product revenue and $1.08–$1.18 billion total revenue for the year, citing the strong launch results for Sephience. Expense projections remain steady. The most important thing for investors to monitor next is the pace of global expansion for Sephience and continued progress in the company’s drug pipeline.

Quarterly Highlights

The growth of Sephience drove overall product revenue up 47% from the prior-year quarter. This therapy generated $125 million in sales during the period, with 1,244 patients on commercial treatment as of March 31, and first sales made in Japan—the first of up to 30 planned country launches by year-end. In contrast, mature franchises like Translarna (ataluren) and Emflaza (deflazacort), both used for Duchenne muscular dystrophy, declined sharply amid increased generic competition and shifting market conditions.

Royalty revenue rose 29%, primarily from the Evrysdi (risdiplam) partnership with Roche, reflecting steady sales performance in spinal muscular atrophy therapies even though PTC recently sold a portion of its future royalty rights. R&D expenses fell by 11% on a Non-GAAP basis year over year, as the company continues to manage costs while advancing studies in its research pipeline.

Key pipeline updates included new clinical data for votoplam, the company’s Huntington’s disease candidate, showing a 52% slowing of disease progression at a 10 mg dose in an ongoing study. Progress also continued for vatiquinone in Friedreich’s ataxia, with a new open-label study launching soon as agreed with the FDA, marking a critical regulatory step. Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities totaled $1.89 billion at quarter-end, modestly lower than at year-end 2025.
