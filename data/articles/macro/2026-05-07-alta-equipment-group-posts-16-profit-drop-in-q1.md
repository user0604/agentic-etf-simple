Key Takeaways
Alta Equipment Group (NYSE:ALTG) reported declines in both revenue and profit as end-market demand stayed weak. Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) fell 16.4% year over year. Management lowered its full-year Adjusted EBITDA outlook but signaled some booking improvements and sees recovery potential in the second half.

Alta Equipment Group (NYSE:ALTG) reported another challenging quarter as sluggish demand led to revenue and profit declines. The leading distributor of construction and material handling equipment in the Midwest, New York, New England, Florida, Nevada, and parts of Canada released its first-quarter 2026 results on May 7, 2026, with the period exposing persistent top-line and earnings pressures. Overall, the quarter balanced emerging operational improvements with softer business results, resulting in a reset on short-term expectations.

Notable developments
Total revenue fell to $410.5 million, down 3.0% from the first quarter of the prior year, mainly due to softer market demand and a slow construction segment.
Adjusted EBITDA dropped 16.4% year over year, reflecting lower equipment sales and slightly higher selling and administrative expenses, though cash flow rebounded strongly with $20.8 million from operations.
Rental equipment sales jumped 44.5% as Alta took steps to optimize its rental fleet, while guidance for full-year profit was lowered amid ongoing end-market weakness.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reduced its full-year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance to a lower range, citing weaker first-quarter results as the main driver for the change. Leaders signaled optimism for a better second half, highlighting improved booking trends in Material Handling and easing tariff-related margin pressures in Master Distribution. Investors should monitor developments in customer demand and rental utilization as indicators of a more sustainable recovery.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue in the Material Handling segment fell 4.7% year over year, as both new and used equipment sales dropped and overall customer activity remained soft despite recent booking strength. March 2026 saw the highest monthly lift truck bookings since June 2023, suggesting possible future momentum in this segment.

Adjusted EBITDA, a key measure of profitability that excludes non-cash charges and interest, decreased 16.4% as the drop in business outweighed cost control efforts. Rental equipment sales climbed 44.5%, reflecting Alta’s active efforts to reduce the size of its rental fleet and generate additional cash during slow demand periods.

Net cash provided by operating activities rebounded to $20.8 million from a negative figure in the same period last year, a sign that tighter working capital and better inventory management are beginning to pay off. Management also highlighted that interest expenses fell by $2.4 million, helping to partially offset profit headwinds.
