Lululemon Stock Stagnates as Board Fight Rages

Talking directly to the lululemon board, I say, "Mate. You're a weathervane."

Jim Gillies

May 18, 2026, 9:18 AM

Comments (1)
9.9

Fools who know me know that I love a good activist investor fight. They're the types of situations that can, if assessed properly and you pick the "right" side, make you some pretty decent post-activism, oft market-beating profits.

And so, I feel like Christmas has come early with the heretofore low-simmering fight between lululemon athletica (NASDAQ: LULU) blowing up over the weekend.

The struggling athletic wear company has released a letter to shareholders defending its strategy, incoming CEO (who is taking the long and winding road getting here) and why their board nominees are good, good, good ... and those pitched by founder/former CEO Chip Wilson are bad, bad, bad.

Part of the fun of an activist/proxy fight is deciding who has more credibility. For me, so far, it's ... Wilson's side. That's because lululemon's interim leadership (no permanent CEO until September 2026) appears, to me, to be talking out of both sides of their mouth.

Consider first, their reliance on "appear to fear" (indulge me, Fools ... my daughter has been doing a section on "logical fallacies" in one of her classes, and it's a favourite topic of mine, so ...). Lululemon charges that Wilson has "outdated perspectives" and "troubling conflicts of interest" that will derail its turnaround plan. They also charge that Wilson has been attacking the company and board, damaging the brand and hurting shareholders.

Umm, guys, first, the fact that you HAVE a turnaround plan suggests that you, you know, need to turn around. Meaning ... your leadership that got lululemon to this place? Kinda suspect.

Second, quick quiz -- who is the largest individual shareholder of lululemon? That's right -- Wilson. So, he's allegedly hurt himself most for ... reasons?

Third, just common sense, how many of lululemon's customers do you think are paying attention to boardroom machinations?

But here's the biggest piece of (dark) humour I find here.

Until last week, lululemon was engaging in settlement talks with Wilson. They were offering to appoint two of Wilson's three board nominees (up from a prior offer of one of his nominees) and to appoint a third director not from his slate, but subject to his approval. They were also offering an advisory brand council that would include his third nominee for the board.

So last week, Wilson's nominees were good enough to sit on the board and influence the brand.

Today they are "less qualified nominees [who] would endorse his misguided perspectives, deprive the company of critical skills and expertise, and risk derailing our progress in an especially pivotal time for our business and organization."

Talking directly to the lululemon board, I say, "Mate. You're a weathervane. And your share price is basically where it was in 2018 while the market has tripled in the interim. Your fresh new CEO couldn't be bothered to join the company for nearly six months (and this was OK with you). You're going to have to do better than that for shareholders to back your side."
