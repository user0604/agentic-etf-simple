Hayward Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 3:09 PM

Comments

Hayward Holdings, Inc. (NYSE:HAYW) is a leading global manufacturer and marketer of pool equipment, specializing in smart, energy-efficient, and automation-enabled solutions for residential, commercial, and industrial pool markets. The company’s growth strategy centers on serving a large and continually expanding installed pool base through aftermarket sales, ongoing product innovation (notably its OmniX platform), margin expansion via operational excellence, disciplined capital allocation, and select international and commercial market expansion.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Aftermarket Dominance Shields Revenue and Margins from Cyclical Downturns

An installed base-driven model generates stable, recurring revenue regardless of new construction trends – Approximately 85% of Hayward Holdings, Inc.’s sales come from the aftermarket, serving millions of existing pools that require regular maintenance, repairs, upgrades, and replacements. This business model provides a resilient sales foundation, evidenced by Q1 2026 results where the company posted 12% year-over-year sales growth despite ongoing weakness in new pool construction. Because these revenue streams are largely non-discretionary, Hayward’s top line and profit margins gain visibility and defensive qualities, even when macroeconomic or housing cycles turn negative. This deeply entrenched position in the installed base creates a long runway for steady growth and margin expansion that is difficult for competitors or economic volatility to disrupt.

2. Pricing Power and Innovation Boost Revenue and Profit Growth

Robust price realization and a dynamic new product pipeline enable outperformance – In the first quarter of calendar 2026, Hayward posted 11.5% year-over-year sales growth; management guided that price realization for the full year is now expected to be roughly 4%, reflecting proactive price increases, including a discrete Q1 increase in response to specialty metal inflation. At the same time, sales of discretionary, higher-margin products such as automation upgrades, smart controls, and energy-efficient equipment outpaced core categories, thanks to strong market adoption of innovations like the OmniX platform. The company’s ability to innovate and pass through price increases—even as new pool construction remains flat—demonstrates strong value perception, brand loyalty, and pricing flexibility. These factors together underpin both revenue growth and margin resilience, even in inflationary environments.

3. Margin Expansion Demonstrates Operational Excellence and Cost Discipline

Improved profitability metrics reflect execution across pricing, cost control, and product mix – Hayward expanded its gross margin by 50 basis points to 46.5% and its adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) margin by 60 basis points to 22.1% in Q1 2026—both at or near record levels. This margin expansion was achieved despite cost pressures from inflation in key inputs such as specialty metals, freight, and resins, as well as tariffs. Management executed on targeted price surcharges, cost containment, and supply chain optimization, allowing Hayward to outrun inflationary headwinds and fund growth-oriented initiatives. Sustained improvement in profitability demonstrates management’s execution capability and gives the company financial flexibility for reinvestment or shareholder returns.

4. Financial Flexibility Allows Simultaneous Investment, Expansion, and Capital Return

Strong free cash flow conversion and a healthy balance sheet sustain multi-pronged growth levers – Hayward expects free cash flow in the region of $200 million for 2026, targeting conversion rates above 100% of net income and reducing net leverage to 2.4x from 2.8x year over year. This robust cash generation allows the company to invest in strategic capital expenditures such as supply chain automation ($40 million planned in 2026), pursue bolt-on mergers and acquisitions, and conduct opportunistic share repurchases (with $6 million executed in Q1 to offset dilution). These efforts ensure that Hayward can fund operational improvements and new product development while reinforcing balance sheet strength and offering potential for shareholder value creation.

5. Expanding Product Portfolio and Market Reach Drive New Growth Verticals

Commercial, international, and underpenetrated regional markets offer incremental upside – In Q1 2026, Hayward grew its international sales by 9% year over year, with European revenue up 14%, and continued to make progress in commercial pool and industrial flow control markets (up nearly 20% and low double-digits, respectively). The company is targeting share gains in underrepresented U.S. regions (West, Southwest, Florida) by investing in expanded field sales, marketing, and new product conversions. Recent acquisitions such as ChlorKing add new technology and commercial customer channels. These multi-faceted growth initiatives help diversify Hayward’s revenue sources and position the company to capture incremental share beyond its residential aftermarket core, extending its mid- and long-term growth potential.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Weakness in New Pool Construction May Limit Growth Upside

Flat or declining new build activity constrains future aftermarket expansion and headline growth rates – While the installed base currently provides strong recurring revenue, new pool construction has remained subdued, with management guiding to essentially flat activity until clear catalysts emerge. This not only reduces near-term top-line growth opportunities but also stifles the natural expansion of the total addressable aftermarket as fewer new pools enter the maintenance and upgrade cycle. If housing or consumer confidence deteriorates further, Hayward’s ability to grow its aftermarket through installed base expansion could be muted, especially as the next generation of pool owners are delayed or do not materialize at historical rates.

2. Inflationary Pressures and Tariff Risks Could Challenge Margin Sustainability

Persistent cost inflation and shifting tariff regimes threaten profitability and pricing power – Despite recent success in offsetting input cost inflation and tariffs through price increases and temporary surcharges, these conditions remain volatile. Section 232 and other tariffs, coupled with elevated costs for metals, resins, and freight, could compress margins if Hayward is unable to continue passing on increases or if demand elasticity rises. Management expects some margin compression in Q2 2026 before mitigation measures take effect, indicating potential near-term vulnerability. Prolonged cost escalation or intensified regulatory headwinds may diminish Hayward’s historic profitability gains and challenge its ability to fully defend margins in future quarters.

3. Macro Shocks or Consumer Slowdown Could Undercut Discretionary Spending

Exposure to consumer behavior and weather can dampen discretionary upgrades and replacements – While servicing the installed base provides some insulation, a major macroeconomic downturn, reduced consumer discretionary income, or extreme weather conditions (such as unusually cool summers in key regions) could significantly reduce spending on non-essential pool upgrades, automation, or even basic maintenance. The company’s Q1 2026 results already noted regional variability and referenced ongoing monitoring of dealer and distributor inventories. If consumer sentiment or end-market activity were to deteriorate quickly, Hayward’s revenue visibility—even in the aftermarket—could suffer, impacting both immediate sales momentum and the scope for margin and earnings upside.
