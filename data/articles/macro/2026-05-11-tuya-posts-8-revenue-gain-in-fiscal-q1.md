Tuya (NYSE:TUYA) delivered revenue gains as its expanding AI services offset weakness in hardware-linked products. The company, a leading provider of Internet of Things (IoT) platform services and AI-driven software, released its first-quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. The period showed stronger topline growth but declining non-GAAP profits and further margin compression, signaling both recovery in services and challenges in hardware and profitability.

Overall, the quarter saw revenue acceleration and solid liquidity, while cost pressures and gross margin declines remained key concerns.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 8.3% year over year, with continued acceleration in higher-value AI services, such as cloud-based recurring software products.
Non-GAAP net profit declined 14.9% despite revenue growth, as non-cash reductions in expenses from last year’s equity incentive plans did not repeat.
Gross margin fell by 1.6 percentage points to 46.9%, reflecting pressure from lower-margin hardware sales and pricing changes in the supply chain.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Tuya management described the operating environment as complex but highlighted “signs of normalization” and ongoing AI-led expansion as strategic priorities. The company will continue focusing on increasing recurring revenue from AI applications and attracting more developers. Investors should closely monitor margin trends and whether operating cash flow can recover as hardware segments face sales and cost headwinds.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue grew to $80.9 million, with strong contributions from AI application and services (up nearly 17% year over year), while the core platform-as-a-service offering also posted healthy growth. However, the smart home and robot product segment shrank nearly 7%, showing softer demand and ongoing hardware challenges.

Non-GAAP net profit slipped to $16.4 million, compared to $19.3 million the prior year. This reflects the impact of reduced share-based compensation last year, which had boosted profits at that time, while core expenses continued to rise—especially in research and development investments.

Registered AI developers surpassed 1.97 million, up over 9% from the prior quarter, signaling ongoing interest in Tuya’s ecosystem. Cash on hand remained solid at over $1.0 billion, but operating cash flow fell more than 30% from a year ago, a trend to track if margin and device segment challenges persist.

What Tuya Does
