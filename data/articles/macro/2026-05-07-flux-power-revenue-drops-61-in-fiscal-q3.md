Flux Power Revenue Drops 61% in Fiscal Q3

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:39 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Revenue fell sharply after a key customer paused capital spending.
Adjusted loss per share doubled year over year.
Management expects revenue to increase about 20% sequentially in the next quarter.

Battery maker Flux Power (NASDAQ:FLUX) reported a tough quarter as a capital freeze by its largest customer led to a steep drop in sales. The company, which develops advanced lithium-ion battery packs for industrial equipment, released its fiscal third quarter results on May 7, 2026 (for the three months ended March 31). Overall, the period highlighted significant short-term challenges, even as new customer wins and expanded sales efforts offered some positive signs.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped by 60.6% after the company’s largest customer imposed a capital freeze, significantly cutting sales volume for the period.
Operating expenses fell 30% from the prior year, showing cost-cutting in response to lower sales, but losses widened as expense reductions did not fully offset the sharp drop in revenue.
The company added new enterprise customers, including a $1.2 million battery order from a leading cargo airline, reflecting initial progress in diversifying its customer base.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now forecasts revenue in the next quarter to rise 20% sequentially, citing increased order activity and new contracts. Its main focus remains expanding relationships with equipment makers and securing new sales beyond its historical core customer base. A critical point for investors will be whether this rebound materializes and how the company manages its liquidity, given the reduced cash balance at quarter end.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter saw a substantial drop in sales following the pause in capital spending by the main material handling customer, with total revenue at about 39% of the prior-year period. Fluctuating orders and delayed decisions—partly linked to fuel price spikes late in the quarter—compounded the demand weakness for established products.

While cost controls succeeded in driving a 30% cut in operating expenses, the decline in revenue led to gross margin erosion and a much wider operating loss. Adjusted EBITDA moved further into negative territory, meaning the business continues to burn cash even after expense reductions.

Despite these setbacks, new commercial traction provided some bright spots. The signing of a new large cargo airline customer and progress in original equipment manufacturer (OEM) partnerships expanded the company’s reach. Additional efforts included refining pricing structures and adding new sales hires focused on OEMs to reduce reliance on a small number of major buyers.

What Flux Power Does

Flux Power designs, manufactures, and sells lithium-ion battery systems for industrial and commercial applications like forklifts and airport ground support equipment.
