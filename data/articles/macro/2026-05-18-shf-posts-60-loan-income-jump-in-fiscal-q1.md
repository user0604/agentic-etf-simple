Notable Developments
Loan program income jumped 55.6% year over year as improved terms in the PCCU partnership took effect.
Account fee income declined 19.0% and investment income fell as customers shifted to lower-fee product options and rates dropped.
Operating expenses fell by 4.7%, reflecting cost control efforts, though compensation costs rose and one-time legal costs affected the figures.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management highlighted a stronger balance sheet and a more favorable regulatory climate as key supports for future growth. They point to improved economics from the PCCU partnership and expanded product offerings as major building blocks.

Quarterly Highlights

Loan program income surged, driven by the new PCCU agreement that increased Shf's share of loan interest income to as much as 65%, up from about 37% previously. This contributed a retroactive payment and will add to revenue for several years.

Although revenue rose modestly, account fee and investment income declined as more customers used money market accounts, which carry lower fees, and as the interest earned on cash balances decreased due to a drop in the Federal Reserve’s interest on reserve balance rate.

Operating expenses were lower compared to the prior year, thanks to reductions in professional service fees, non-cash stock-based compensation, and improved loan risk ratings, though core compensation and employee benefits increased year over year.

What Shf Does

Shf provides specialized banking, lending, and compliance technology solutions for cannabis-related businesses and their financial partners.
