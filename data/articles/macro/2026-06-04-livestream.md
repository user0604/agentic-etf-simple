Shares of CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD) were down nearly 10% pre-market after reporting another quarter of tepid free cash flow.

To be fair, the overall results look solid. Revenue grew 26% year-over-year and annual recurring revenue (ARR) also grew 26%. Operating losses thinned and operating margin improved from -10.8% to -2.2%.

More customers are adopting more of the company’s modules for defending against cyberattacks, which is a good thing. But there also remains a huge cash cost for this growth.

On the surface, free cash flow looked good. If not for the cost of dealmaking, CrowdStrike would have produced $173 million in organic FCF, up from $33.3 million a year ago.

Unfortunately, all that and more got eaten by $881.4 million spent on acquiring SGNL.AI and Seraphic. We have to count that spending as if its capex because CrowdStrike has become dependent on deals to fund its long-term growth.

You can see the cost on the balance sheet. Goodwill — the excess cost above fair value for acquisitions — has more than doubled over the past year, from $913.3 million to $2.27 billion.

So will CrowdStrike get back to funding growth organically? I don’t think we can say at this point. It’s still a good business. It’s just not the pure-play, cash-generating monster that it used to be.
