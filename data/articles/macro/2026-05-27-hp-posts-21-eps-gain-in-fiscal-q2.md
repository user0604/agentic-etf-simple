HP delivered solid operational progress but continued to face pressure from cost increases and mixed segment results.

HP (NYSE:HPQ) delivered notable improvements in operating performance while still contending with rising costs and uneven progress across its divisions. The global technology supplier focuses on personal computing devices, printers, and solutions for both consumers and businesses. On May 27, 2026, it released second-quarter fiscal 2026 results covering the three months ended April 30, 2026. Overall, the period saw higher revenue and profit on a non-GAAP basis, but some underlying challenges remain.

Notable Developments
Personal Systems segment revenue grew 13% year over year, while total hardware unit sales fell 7%, indicating a shift toward higher-value or premium devices.
Non-GAAP operating margin improved by 0.2 percentage points, reaching 7.5%, but costs related to restructuring and ongoing investments continued to pressure margins overall.
HP generated $0.8 billion in free cash flow for the quarter and returned $374 million to shareholders through dividends and share buybacks.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained full-year earnings and free cash flow guidance, with non-GAAP EPS now expected to range from $2.90 to $3.10 for fiscal 2026. The company cited strong execution so far this year, though ongoing cost inflation and volume declines require close observation. The mix of AI-enabled and premium products in the Personal Systems segment will be especially important for future growth.

Quarterly Highlights

Personal Systems, the business line that includes desktops, laptops, and workstations, delivered double-digit revenue growth. However, total unit sales declined, suggesting HP is focusing on higher-priced or more feature-rich devices instead of chasing outright volume.

The Printing division remained flat in revenue terms compared to a year earlier. While commercial printing sales held steady, consumer printing dropped 10%, and total hardware units in both segments declined. Supplies, including ink and toner, delivered a modest 1% increase, which is critical given its high profit margin.

HP's continued investment in artificial intelligence capabilities showed in product launches and upgrades, with mentions of new AI-powered personal computers and smart print solutions. These initiatives support the company's efforts to differentiate itself in the market. Quarterly dividend was $0.30 per share.
