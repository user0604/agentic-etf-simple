Key Takeaways

C3.ai (NYSE:AI) faced a difficult quarter as both sales and customer activity fell sharply. The enterprise artificial intelligence software provider develops platforms and applications that help organizations build and deploy AI-powered solutions. For its fiscal fourth quarter, released on June 3, 2026, the company reported deepening challenges and a major leadership reset. Overall, the period was marked by weak sales, narrowing margins, and urgent efforts to reverse its fortunes.

Notable developments
Revenue dropped more than half from the same quarter last year, with professional services revenue down 85% as new project starts slowed.
Profitability deteriorated, with non-GAAP gross margin down 32 percentage points and non-GAAP operating loss rising to $(54.4) million, up from $(31.2) million a year ago.
CEO Thomas Siebel returned and invested $68.8 million in shares, while the company booked $10.8 million in restructuring costs as part of a leadership reset.
Quarterly highlights

Subscription revenue, which is the main driver for the business, fell over 40% compared to the prior year as both new customer additions and expansion sales slowed. Professional services revenue declined even more rapidly, reflecting weak demand for implementation work and fewer new project starts.

Margins compressed significantly, with non-GAAP gross margin dropping by 32 percentage points as fixed costs remained high despite falling sales. Operating expenses stayed roughly flat, which amplified net losses and led to negative free cash flow for both the quarter and the full year.

The leadership shake-up included CEO Thomas Siebel’s return and personal share purchase, while restructuring added extra costs to the quarter. Despite a cash balance of $575.4 million at April 30, 2026, rapid cash usage and a lack of detailed customer or segment data leave the pace and prospects of recovery uncertain.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects continued top-line declines in fiscal 2027, with revenue guidance between $210 million and $240 million for the year, well below recent performance levels. Operating losses are projected to persist, with no immediate return to profitability in sight. Investors should monitor whether the company can stabilize subscription revenue and improve customer retention as restructuring efforts continue.
