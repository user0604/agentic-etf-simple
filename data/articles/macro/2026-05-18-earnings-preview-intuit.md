Intuit (NASDAQ:INTU), a leading provider of tax, accounting, and personal finance software, is expected to report its third-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings after the market closes on Wednesday, May 20, 2026.

Q2 FY2026 Recap

In the second quarter of fiscal 2026, Intuit delivered revenue of $4.7 billion, representing 17% growth year over year. Operating income rose 44% to $855 million, and diluted earnings per share reached $2.48, up 49% from the prior year. The company’s results beat analyst estimates for both revenue and earnings per share. Growth was broad-based, with strong contributions from both the Global Business Solutions segment—which includes QuickBooks and Intuit Enterprise Suite—and the Consumer segment, which covers TurboTax, Credit Karma, and ProTax offerings. Management reaffirmed its full-year guidance and highlighted continued momentum in AI-driven automation and customer adoption of new features across its platforms.

Positives
Strong revenue growth across core segments: Both Global Business Solutions and Consumer segments posted double-digit revenue increases, showing demand for Intuit’s business and consumer platforms remains robust.
Continued expansion in AI-powered services: The rollout of AI agents and automation features drove higher customer engagement and time savings, especially in QuickBooks and TurboTax offerings, supporting future pricing power and customer retention.
TurboTax and Credit Karma outperformed industry trends: TurboTax revenue grew 12% despite a 5% decline in IRS tax return volumes, while Credit Karma revenue increased 23%, reflecting strong adoption of personal loan and credit card features.
Operating leverage and margin expansion: Operating income and net income grew faster than revenue, reflecting disciplined expense management and efficiency gains from automation.
Negatives
Mailchimp growth remains challenged: Mailchimp revenue was down slightly year over year, and management now expects a return to double-digit growth only beyond fiscal 2026, highlighting ongoing struggles with smaller business customer acquisition and retention.
Higher marketing and staffing expenses: Increased investment in marketing and personnel, especially in support of AI initiatives and mid-market expansion, contributed to higher operating expenses, which partially offset margin gains.
What to Watch in Q3 FY2026's Report
Tax season outcomes and TurboTax assisted growth: Q3 includes the peak of tax season. Investors should watch for updates on TurboTax’s performance in the assisted segment and whether strong early engagement with AI-driven features translates into higher market share or revenue per customer.
Adoption and monetization of AI and human intelligence (HI) features: Management has highlighted the rollout of new AI and HI-powered experiences. Progress in customer uptake, willingness to pay for combined AI and expert services, and any resulting pricing actions will be key areas of focus.
Mid-market and Intuit Enterprise Suite momentum: The pace of new contract wins, especially in verticals like construction, and the impact of expanded direct sales and accountant partnerships will signal how well Intuit is penetrating this higher-value segment.
Mailchimp trajectory and turnaround efforts: Any signs of improved customer acquisition, retention, or product innovation in Mailchimp will be closely watched, given management’s cautious outlook for growth in this business.
Updates on AI partnerships and platform integration: Investors should look for details on the recently announced partnership with Anthropic and the integration of Intuit’s apps with third-party AI platforms, as these initiatives could influence future product differentiation and customer acquisition.
