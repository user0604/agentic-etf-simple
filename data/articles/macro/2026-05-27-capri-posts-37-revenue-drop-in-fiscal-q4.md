Key Takeaways
Capri posted a return to adjusted profitability but continued to face top-line challenges after the Versace divestment.
Revenue fell 3.7% to $796 million in the fourth quarter.
Management guided for low-single-digit revenue growth and about 40% higher earnings per share in fiscal 2027.

Capri (NYSE:CPRI) returned to adjusted profitability but continued to face revenue declines after selling Versace. The global luxury group, now focused on Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo, is working to reposition its business for growth. Reporting results on May 27, 2026, for the quarter ended March 28, management described the period as a cautious step forward, while noting the need for more consistent brand momentum.

Notable developments
Capri recognized a $40 million one-time tariff refund, which boosted gross and operating margins in the quarter, though this benefit is not expected to recur at the same scale.
Inventory levels at the end of March 2026 dropped to $581 million, a 17% decline from the prior year, signaling improved inventory management.
Michael Kors revenue continued to contract, dropping 5.5% for the quarter, while Jimmy Choo posted low single-digit growth but with a wider operating loss.
Management outlook and what to watch

Capri’s management projects a return to low-single-digit revenue growth and about 40% growth in adjusted earnings per share for fiscal 2027, targeting approximately $3.525 billion in revenue. The company plans further capital spending and a larger share repurchase. Investors will want to watch whether core sales at Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo can stabilize or rebound, especially without the boost from any one-time items.

Quarterly highlights

Persistent revenue declines in Michael Kors, which now accounts for more than 80% of Capri’s revenue since the Versace sale, remain a central concern. Michael Kors’ revenue for the quarter dipped to $656 million, with improvement in EMEA (Europe, Middle East, and Africa) offset by ongoing contraction in the Americas. The company cited new product launches and influencer campaigns but has not reversed the sales pressure in its largest market.

Jimmy Choo delivered $140 million in quarterly revenue, an increase of just over 5% entirely due to favorable currency effects. Despite the higher sales figure, Jimmy Choo posted an operating loss of $20 million and faces challenges in achieving sustainable profitability in the near term.

The quarter’s margin improvement was influenced by a $40 million tariff refund, which reduced costs and improved headline profitability. Capri’s net debt dropped sharply to $222 million as of March 2026, compared to $1.4 billion a year earlier—aided by the Versace divestiture and free cash flow of $134 million for the fiscal year. Store count fell by 46 locations over the past year, while inventory was tightly managed to support future product launches.

What Capri does

Capri is a global luxury fashion group operating the Michael Kors and Jimmy Choo brands, offering accessories, footwear, and apparel through its retail and wholesale channels worldwide.
