Beasley Broadcast Group (NASDAQ:BBGI) reported a headline profit driven by asset sales, while its main business continued to face challenges. The leading U.S. radio broadcaster and digital media company saw ongoing declines in its traditional audio segment, with digital gains only partly offsetting the drop. The company released its first quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 13, 2026, showing a period marked by revenue pressure but some progress in digital and balance sheet initiatives. Overall, the quarter featured non-core gains, but underlying business challenges persisted.

Notable developments

Total revenue dropped 12.9% year over year, mainly due to continued weakness in core radio operations and recent asset sales.

Profitability improved on paper because of a $12.5 million gain from selling Fort Myers stations, but cash profit metrics turned negative at $0.4 million.

The digital segment delivered an 18.2% increase in same-station revenue, but its operating margin fell from over 29% last quarter to 15.5%.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its focus on expanding digital offerings, maintaining cost discipline, and reducing debt, as seen in post-quarter refinancing actions. However, it did not provide a detailed outlook or financial guidance for the coming months. The key area to monitor is whether digital growth and cost controls can offset ongoing declines in traditional audio revenue and restore positive cash generation.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s core audio operations, which include traditional radio advertising, continued to face pressure, with same-station audio revenue declining by 12.9%. While expenses in this segment fell, the overall decline highlights persistent industry headwinds for radio broadcasters.

The digital segment was a relative bright spot, making up 25% of total company revenue for the quarter. On a same-station basis, digital revenue grew 18.2%, but the segment’s lower 15.5% margin compared to previous periods suggests either higher investment or changes in business mix.

Operating profit of $7.7 million and diluted earnings of $1.77 per share were mainly the result of gains from asset sales, not from core operations. Adjusted EBITDA turned negative, showing that day-to-day business profitability remains elusive despite positive headline earnings.
