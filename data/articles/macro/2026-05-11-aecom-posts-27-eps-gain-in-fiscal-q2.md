Key Takeaways
Aecom delivered record results but faced temporary weakness in cash flow as payment collections were delayed.
Adjusted EPS increased 27% year over year.
Management raised full-year guidance while reaffirming its long-term growth targets.

Aecom (NYSE:ACM) posted record operational performance this quarter, but short-term weakness in cash flow was a challenge due to delayed payments in its Middle East business. The leading global infrastructure consulting firm continued to see strong demand, raising guidance for the second straight quarter. The results, released May 11, 2026, cover its fiscal second quarter and reflect broad-based strength in its Americas business alongside ongoing margin expansion. Overall, the period featured robust profit and backlog growth offset by softer free cash flow.

Notable Developments
Backlog reached a record $26.2 billion, up 8% year over year, with the design pipeline also hitting all-time highs.
Operating cash flow dropped sharply to $4 million, compared to $191 million a year ago, mainly due to delayed collections in the Middle East.
The Americas segment delivered 5% net service revenue growth and record adjusted operating margins of 20.0%.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised full-year adjusted earnings guidance and reaffirmed targets for margin expansion and free cash flow, noting that Middle East payment delays have already improved in the third quarter. Investors should focus on whether cash flow metrics recover as promised and how quickly the record backlog in International translates to revenue and profit growth.

Quarterly Highlights

Backlog, which reflects the value of contracted future work, set a new company high. This indicator supports future revenue and signals continued strong customer demand, with the design-focused book-to-burn ratio—meaning projects won versus those completed—staying above 1.0 for a 22nd straight quarter.

Profitability remained a bright spot, as the segment adjusted operating margin reached new highs, driven by both steady growth in the Americas and disciplined cost control.

However, free cash flow turned negative in the period, highlighting pressure from longer-than-expected claim resolutions and delayed client payments overseas. Management reiterated its full-year cash flow goal, asserting that collections have since improved.
