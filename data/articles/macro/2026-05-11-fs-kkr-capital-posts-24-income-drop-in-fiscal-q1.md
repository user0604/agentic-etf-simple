Key Takeaways
Quarter marked by portfolio value erosion and rising credit stress.
Net investment income per share fell to $0.42 from $0.48, with steep losses posted.
Management announced capital actions and expects strategic moves to help stabilize results, but credit trends remain a key risk.

Persistent credit issues and asset markdowns weighed down results as FS KKR Capital (NYSE:FSK) contended with another difficult start to the year. The business development company, one of the largest lenders to private middle-market U.S. firms, reported its first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026, showing sharply reduced investment income and a major drop in net asset value. The period featured aggressive capital measures by management, but ongoing credit risk and portfolio challenges kept pressure on key performance metrics.

Overall, the quarter saw continued portfolio strain and deteriorating asset quality, even as new capital injections and buyback programs sought to steady investor confidence.

Notable Developments
Reported earnings per share swung to a significant loss of ($1.57), compared to a profit of $0.43 per share in the prior year quarter, driven by heavy net realized and unrealized losses.
Net asset value per share fell by approximately 10% to $18.83, reflecting further portfolio markdowns and an increase in non-accrual, or defaulting, loans to 4.2% of the portfolio at fair value.
Management announced a $150 million preferred stock investment from KKR, a $150 million share tender offer at $11 per share, and a $300 million repurchase plan, alongside a temporary reduction in management fees over four quarters.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management emphasized its focus on stabilizing results through immediate capital actions, highlighted by outside preferred equity and a large buyback program. Executives said these moves aim to bolster net investment income and maintain shareholder distributions for the near term, primarily through lower management fees. The most important area to watch is credit quality, as growing loans on non-accrual status raise concern about further portfolio losses in subsequent quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Earnings performance was pressured by a combination of portfolio markdowns and rising credit stress. Net investment income per share fell to $0.42, while net realized and unrealized losses, totaling $2.00 per share, contributed to sharply negative overall results. At the same time, new non-accruals and a further jump in leverage pushed the net debt-to-equity ratio up to 131% as of March 31, 2026, compared to 122% as of De. 31, 2025.

Portfolio metrics reflected a defensive stance: total investments at fair value decreased to $12.3 billion from $13.0 billion at the end of last quarter. The share of assets in senior secured loans, which take priority in borrower repayment, ticked up to nearly 60%, yet portfolio yield on all debt investments moved lower, falling to 8.7% from 9.3% at the end of last year, suggesting lower pricing power or more assets moving to non-income status.

Strategic capital actions marked the quarter. The company secured $150 million in new preferred equity from KKR and initiated a $150 million tender offer for common shares at a steep discount to book value. A $300 million share repurchase program was also authorized, and distributions held steady at $0.42 per share, but dividend sustainability now relies partly on a temporary 50% reduction in incentive fees provided by KKR over four quarters.
