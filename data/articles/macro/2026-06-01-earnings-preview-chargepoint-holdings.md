Earnings Preview: ChargePoint Holdings

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 1, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

ChargePoint Holdings (NYSE:CHPT), a provider of electric vehicle (EV) charging stations and related network software, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2027 on Wednesday, June 3, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2026 Recap

For the quarter ended Jan. 31, 2026, ChargePoint reported revenue of $109 million, which was at the high end of its guidance and represented 7% growth year over year. The company’s non-GAAP gross margin remained at a record 33%, and subscription revenue reached $42 million, up 11% from the prior year. ChargePoint’s operational discipline led to minimal cash usage from operations, better than planned. The company highlighted strong performance in Europe, with double-digit revenue growth, and continued improvements in reliability and customer satisfaction. ChargePoint’s results beat analyst consensus for revenue, though analyst sentiment turned more cautious after the report, with at least one major analyst lowering their price target.

Positives
Revenue at high end of guidance and beat consensus. ChargePoint’s $109 million in revenue exceeded analyst expectations, signaling strong execution despite industry headwinds.
Record gross margin and improving subscription mix. The non-GAAP gross margin held at 33%, and subscription revenue grew 11% year over year, supporting a more predictable and higher-margin business model.
Operational improvements in reliability and customer satisfaction. The company reduced the percentage of stations down to below 1% and achieved customer satisfaction scores of 8.5 or higher out of 10, which should help drive repeat business.
European segment delivered robust double-digit growth. Europe contributed its highest share of revenue since ChargePoint became public, benefiting from regulatory incentives and new products tailored for the region.
Negatives
Net loss remains significant. ChargePoint reported a GAAP net loss of $44.4 million for the quarter, underscoring ongoing challenges to reach profitability.
Inventory levels remain elevated. Inventory was $215 million at quarter end, slightly higher than the prior quarter, mainly due to foreign exchange and overhead capitalization, which may pressure future margins if not managed down.
North America EV market softness and policy headwinds. The company noted a slowdown in new EV sales in North America after the federal tax credit ended in Sept. 2025, which could impact demand for charging infrastructure.
Mixed analyst and investor sentiment post-earnings. Despite beating revenue estimates, at least one analyst lowered their price target, and the stock declined in the days following the report, reflecting market caution about the path to profitability.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2027's Report
Revenue guidance versus seasonality and analyst expectations: Management guided for Q1 revenue in the range of $90 million to $100 million, reflecting typical seasonal weakness. The current analyst consensus is $95.6 million. Investors should watch where reported revenue lands within this range and how management frames expectations for the rest of the year.
Progress on inventory reduction and working capital: Inventory remains high, and management has indicated a plan for gradual reduction as new product ramps continue. Any signs of accelerated inventory drawdown or improved cash flow would be positive signals.
Gross margin trends as new products scale: The company expects new AC and DC charging products to contribute higher margins as they ramp in the second half of the year. Watch for commentary on early gross margin impact and signs of margin improvement in Q1.
European growth and regulatory tailwinds: Europe was a bright spot in Q4, with double-digit growth supported by incentives. Investors should monitor whether this momentum continues and if management provides updated views on the European opportunity.
Status of product launches and partnerships: Recent initiatives include the ramp of the Flex AC product, next-generation DC chargers, and expanded partnerships with Eaton, Ford Pro, and RAW Charging. Updates on the timing and impact of these launches, as well as any new partnership announcements, will be important for assessing ChargePoint’s competitive positioning.
