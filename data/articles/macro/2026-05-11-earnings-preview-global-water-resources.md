Earnings Preview: Global Water Resources

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 1:03 PM

Comments

Global Water Resources (NASDAQ:GWRS), a regulated water and wastewater utility serving fast-growing communities in Arizona, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings after the market closes on Wednesday, May 13, 2026.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

For the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Global Water Resources reported revenue of $13.54 million and a net loss of $0.01 per share. Both figures missed analyst estimates, which had called for revenue of $13.73 million and earnings per share of $0.04. The company’s results reflected a year of significant capital investment, including the acquisition of seven water systems from the City of Tucson and major upgrades to existing infrastructure. These investments contributed to higher revenues but also led to increased expenses, particularly in depreciation, maintenance, and personnel costs. The company’s active service connections grew by 6.3% year over year, driven by both organic growth and the Tucson acquisition. However, operating income and net income declined sharply from the prior year, as regulatory lag delayed the recovery of these higher costs through rate increases.

Positives
Active service connections expanded: The company grew its total active service connections by 6.3% to 68,577, reflecting both organic growth and the completed Tucson water systems acquisition. This expansion supports future revenue growth.
Revenue increased year over year: Total revenue rose 5.8% compared to 2024, aided by new connections and higher rates in select utilities. This demonstrates the company’s ability to grow its top line even in a challenging environment.
Major capital projects completed: The recommissioning of the Southwest plant and infrastructure upgrades in Pinal County were completed, positioning the company for long-term growth and improved service reliability.
Regulatory progress made: The company secured approval for new revenues at its Farmers utility and advanced key rate cases for its largest subsidiaries, laying groundwork for future rate relief.
Negatives
Missed analyst estimates: Both earnings per share and revenue for Q4 2025 came in below consensus expectations, reflecting pressure on profitability and some operational headwinds.
Operating expenses rose sharply: Expenses increased 12.2% year over year, driven by higher depreciation, maintenance, and personnel costs, including those related to the Tucson acquisition and medical expenses. This outpaced revenue growth and pressured margins.
Net income declined significantly: Net income fell 48.9% from 2024, and diluted earnings per share dropped to $0.11 for the full year, down from $0.24. The decline was mainly due to regulatory lag and one-time asset write-offs.
Permit activity slowed: New building permits in the City of Maricopa, a key growth area, dropped 39% year over year. This slowdown may impact future connection growth if the trend persists.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Progress on rate case settlement and regulatory relief: Investors should look for updates on the April 2026 settlement filing for the Santa Cruz and Palo Verde subsidiaries. Any signals on timing or magnitude of new rates will be important for future earnings visibility.
Expense management and capital spending pace: Management indicated a focus on controlling expenses and slowing capital investments in 2026. Watch for evidence of lower cost growth and details on capital allocation priorities.
Connection growth trends and housing market impact: Monitor active service connection growth and commentary on trends in building permits, especially in Maricopa and the broader Phoenix area. These metrics are key for forecasting future revenue.
Integration of Tucson water systems: Updates on operational integration and performance of the acquired Tucson systems will help assess the success of this recent acquisition and its contribution to revenue and expenses.
Dividend sustainability and balance sheet health: The company continues to declare monthly dividends. Investors should review cash flow, debt levels, and any commentary on the ability to maintain or grow the dividend in the face of regulatory and expense pressures.
