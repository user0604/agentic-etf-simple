Key Takeaways
Getty Images delivered mixed results as it grew revenue but faced margin pressure and subscriber attrition.
Adjusted diluted EPS improved while adjusted EBITDA margin declined compared to the prior year.
Management maintained its full-year outlook, guided for margin normalization, and highlighted continued focus on enterprise and subscriptions.

Getty Images (NYSE:GETY) reported a quarter of both progress and setbacks as it made modest revenue gains but struggled with declining profit margins and ongoing drops in customer activity. The visual content leader continues to transition its business model towards more stable, recurring, subscription-driven revenue, relying heavily on major enterprise customers and its proprietary content archives. The company released these first quarter results on May 11, 2026, reporting for the three months ending March 31.

Overall, the quarter offered stability in headline figures but also underscored ongoing challenges with core creative revenue and customer engagement.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 1.1% year over year, with strong performance in the Editorial segment, which grew 11.0%, while Creative revenue fell 4.5%.
Adjusted EBITDA margin declined to 27.2%, down from 31.3% a year ago, with management citing higher cost of revenue and specific event coverage costs as contributing factors.
Free cash flow turned positive at $24.0 million, up from a slight outflow in the prior year period, driven by lower interest expense and improved working capital.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance, anticipating revenue between $948 million and $988 million and adjusted EBITDA of $279 million to $295 million. Executives expect profit margins to normalize toward 30% for the rest of the year, as the impact of one-off costs and revenue mix shifts diminishes. Key areas to watch are trends in core creative revenue and active subscription totals, as both remain under pressure.

Quarterly Highlights

The Editorial segment led growth in the period, rising 11.0% year over year, supported by event coverage such as the Winter Olympics. This offset a notable 4.5% decline in Creative revenue, which reflects the company’s longstanding challenge of maintaining growth in its broadest content offering.

Key performance indicators reflected broader competitive and structural challenges: total active annual subscribers dropped nearly 19% to 258,000, and the paid download volume slipped by 0.7% to 92 million. The annual subscriber revenue retention rate also declined to 90%, down from 92.7% in the prior year period, pointing to softer customer loyalty.

On the cost side, adjusted EBITDA margin experienced material compression, falling by 4.1 percentage points, a result that management attributed to both the content mix and higher costs associated with significant editorial events. Despite these pressures, cash generation improved, with free cash flow increasing sharply compared to the prior year.
