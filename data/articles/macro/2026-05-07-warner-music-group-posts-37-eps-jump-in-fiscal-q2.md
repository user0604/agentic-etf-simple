Key Takeaways
Warner Music Group delivered a business win with robust digital and streaming-driven growth in the quarter.

Warner Music Group posted a stronger quarter, as digital and streaming sales more than offset currency and licensing headwinds. The global leader in music content and publishing benefited from successful new releases and strategic investment in music catalogs, with the results covering the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (reported May 7, 2026). Overall, the period saw across-the-board gains in revenue, margins, and free cash flow, supporting management's financial and operational priorities.

Notable Developments
Revenue surged to $1.73 billion, led by recorded music streaming growth and international markets.
Profitability improved as adjusted operating margin expanded by 2.5 percentage points, reflecting strong cost discipline amid margin-focused initiatives.
The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.19 per share, while $650 million was deployed for catalog acquisitions in partnership with Bain.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects margin expansion at the top of its previously guided 1.5 to 2.0 percentage points for the full year, with ongoing focus on free cash flow and disciplined capital allocation. While no broad upward revisions were made to revenue or cash flow targets, catalog investment and digital platform momentum remain central. Investors should keep an eye on streaming revenue growth and how foreign currency and changes in licensing economics may affect future profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Digital and streaming platforms powered the quarter, with streaming generating approximately 68% of Warner Music Group’s total revenue. In recorded music, both subscription and ad-supported streaming hit double-digit growth, while physical music sales and artist services also rose, helped by new releases from Bruno Mars and Ed Sheeran.

Profitability advances were broad-based: adjusted operating income before depreciation and amortization climbed 31%, and free cash flow tripled from the prior-year quarter. Margin gains reflected continued efforts in cost savings, with a pronounced improvement in the recorded music segment.

Strategic expansion efforts advanced, as $650 million was invested in music catalog acquisitions through Warner’s joint venture with Bain. International revenue increased 12% in recorded music and 9% in publishing compared to the prior-year quarter, highlighting the company’s growing global reach and the effectiveness of its talent and repertoire development programs outside the U.S.
