Giddy Up With Boot Barn

Jim Gillies

May 15, 2026, 10:18 AM

Comments
10.0

It tickles me immensely that:

There is a company out there walking around with the handle Boot Barn (NYSE: BOOT), and
It’s doing as well as it is.

Long-time Fool 24 viewers might remember that I was literally introduced to Boot Barn on this very show by fellow Fool Emily Flippen. I recommended it to Hidden Gems Canada members soon after, and, assuming today’s premarket price holds up into regular trading, it’s delivered about 20% annualized returns to members ever since.

Why talk about Boot Barn today? Because it dropped Q4 and full FY26 results last night, and they were pretty great. Consider:

Full-year sales rose nearly 18% year-over-year
Same-store sales were a very strong 7.2% versus FY25, up 6.2% in-store and 15.3% online
Earnings per share were up 25% year over year
Cash flow from operations more than doubled — up 107% to $305 million

I focus on cash flow from operations over free cash flow (FCF) here because Boot Barn is still in growth mode. Most of its capex is going to new store growth. Boot Barn opened 80 new stores across FY26 — 10 more than originally planned, taking the total open to 539. The company has designs on 70 more stores in FY27, which would take it to roughly half of its long-term U.S. target of 1,200 stores. This might seem like a lot. It certainly did to me when first introduced to Boot Barn.

But that’s on me. I truly didn’t appreciate the widespread appeal of the “Western Industry” in America — think ranchers, farmers, “horse people” (if you know one, you’ll know what I mean), and those who embrace “western events,” such as rodeos, country and western concerts, and the lifestyle that such folks find attractive. For the Canadians in the room, Boot Barn also has a substantial following in the “Work Industry” — think folks, often “blue collar,” employed in the agricultural, oil and gas, manufacturing, and construction industries. These are people to whom Boot Barn is selling tools, and they’re seeking quality — it’s reminiscent of the original value proposition for Mark’s Work Wearhouse before Canadian Tire (TSX: CTC.A).

The economics of new stores are the primary driver here in the quest to more than double the store count. Net investment runs at about $1.7 million per store for fixed and working capital. New stores do about $3.2 million in sales in year one and pay back all investment in 1.8 years. With those numbers, Boot Barn would be silly not to push the growth pedal at this point in time.

The balance sheet remains debt free. The company is cash rich. The only uses for self-generated cash now are growth spending and share buybacks (which the company spent $50 million on in the quarter).

Some stories are just Peter Lynchian “the stock price follows the earnings” in nature. For the past three years since I made its acquaintance, that’s been Boot Barn. I expect this to continue for the next five to 10 years from here. Giddy up!
