Key Takeaways
Tango Therapeutics reported a business setback as revenue dropped to zero following the end of a key collaboration.
Earnings per share improved to a loss of $0.32 from $0.36 the prior year, supported by lower R&D spending.
The company signaled continued focus on advancing its clinical pipeline with a strong cash position expected to last into 2028.

Tango Therapeutics (NASDAQ:TNGX) faced a business loss this quarter as it recorded no revenue, marking a sharp reversal from past collaboration funding. The oncology-focused biotech uses synthetic lethality, a strategy seeking to exploit unique genetic changes in cancer cells, to develop new cancer therapies. On May 13, 2026, it reported earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, with management emphasizing resilience in its cash reserves and continued advancement in its key clinical programs even as it moves toward a development-only phase.

Overall, the quarter reflected both discipline in spending and the risk of relying almost entirely on future clinical trial outcomes for the next value drivers.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell to zero after the Gilead collaboration ended, highlighting full reliance on clinical milestones and cash on hand.
Research and development expenses dropped 8%, reflecting the end of lower-priority programs and cost savings on discovery activities.
Cash reserves rose 56.4% year over year to nearly $380 million, supporting operations through anticipated key data events in 2028.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management emphasized it has enough cash to support operations and key clinical readouts into 2028 but did not give specific financial guidance. Leadership remains focused on advancing vopimetostat and TNG456, two investigational cancer drugs, with several trial results expected this year. Investors should watch for initial phase 1/2 results in pancreatic, lung, and brain cancer studies in 2026, which could determine the company’s near-term prospects.

Quarterly Highlights

The complete loss of revenue was due to the end of the Gilead collaboration, putting pressure on the company to deliver clinical trial successes for future growth. This shift leaves the company dependent on its cash balance and the progress of its own drug development programs.

Research and development spending fell as Tango discontinued projects (notably TNG908) and prioritized resources towards vopimetostat and TNG456, its most promising synthetic lethality therapies aimed at specific genetic changes in cancer cells.

Rising general and administrative costs mainly stemmed from higher personnel spending as the company scales up for late-stage clinical development, with the cash position signaling the ability to continue funding research and operations as it aims for pivotal milestones in 2026 and beyond.

What Tango Therapeutics Does
