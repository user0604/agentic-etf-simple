Mercer International EBITDA Falls 83% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 5:05 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Mercer International reported a sharp operational loss as cost inflation and weak pulp prices pressured results.
Operating EBITDA dropped significantly year over year.
Management expects some cost stabilization, but continued liquidity focus and slow market recovery will remain crucial.

Mercer International (NASDAQ:MERC), a global pulp and wood products producer, struggled to contain rising costs and slow market recovery in the first quarter. The period, reported on May 7, 2026, saw mounting financial strain from higher fiber costs and only minor relief in pulp pricing. Overall, the quarter highlighted deepening financial vulnerability driven by difficult market dynamics and ongoing operational challenges.

Notable Developments
Total revenue fell approximately 3% from the prior-year quarter, mainly reflecting lower pulp prices, even as solid wood product sales rose approximately 7%.
Operating EBITDA plummeted to $7.8 million, weighed down by a $22 million non-cash inventory impairment and elevated fiber costs across both business segments.
Cash and liquidity levels declined sharply, and a key loan covenant was breached, requiring a waiver with compliance not expected until late in the year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects only modest pulp price increases and some stabilization of fiber costs in the coming quarter. It emphasizes ongoing cost-cutting efforts and close attention to liquidity, with hopes to regain covenant compliance by the fourth quarter.

Quarterly Highlights

The pulp segment saw revenue drop 11% and segment operating cash profits fall more than 80%, driven by a combination of lower sales prices and a significant $22 million adjustment to inventory value. Despite higher revenues in solid wood products, the segment reported greater operating losses as soaring fiber costs offset top-line gains and led to deeper negative margins.

Mercer delivered $11 million of savings through its “One Goal One Hundred” initiative during the quarter, and built a mass timber order book with data center projects now making up about 60% of the total pipeline. However, the price per unit for manufactured wood products declined sharply, indicating ongoing pressure on profitability even in newer growth areas.

Financially, Mercer’s cash position fell to $84.5 million, with aggregate liquidity just over $229 million after a sharp drop from year end. The company had to secure a waiver on its German revolving credit facility, highlighting the ongoing strain on its financial flexibility.

What Mercer International Does

Mercer International produces pulp for paper, renewable energy from biomass, lumber, and engineered wood products for construction.
