Key Takeaways
Kore Group reported stronger growth in its core Connectivity Services business, but saw weakness in IoT Solutions and rising losses.

Kore Group (NYSE:KORE) delivered solid expansion in its core Connectivity Services business, even as its IoT Solutions segment fell sharply and transaction expenses drove up losses. The leading provider of Internet of Things (IoT) connectivity and solutions reported first quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. Overall, the quarter highlighted both operational momentum in adding IoT connections and challenges in the Solutions business and bottom-line profitability.

Notable Developments
IoT Connectivity revenue was $56.2 million and made up 85% of total revenue.
IoT Solutions revenue dropped 47% year over year to $9.6 million, driving an overall decline in total revenue for the quarter.
Net loss nearly doubled to $28.5 million, primarily due to transaction-related charges and a change in fair value of warrant liabilities as the company moves toward privatization.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue financial guidance for upcoming quarters, citing the ongoing acquisition process by private equity firms. The pending $726 million transaction, which will take the company private, means public disclosures will soon become less frequent. The main focus for investors will be on how Kore Group maintains growth in its core connectivity business while addressing turmoil in the Solutions segment as the transition to private ownership approaches.

Quarterly Highlights

Growth in IoT Connectivity continued, as connections rose to 21.9 million and the segment contributed an increased share of overall company revenue. This reflects Kore Group’s strategic emphasis on delivering essential IoT connectivity that supports device deployment for customers in healthcare, fleet management, and industrial applications.

The steep fall in IoT Solutions, which includes products and analytics designed to generate value from device data, dampened overall performance and reduced the segment’s share of total revenue from 25% to 15%.

Most of the sharp increase in net loss traces to non-operating expenses, including $11.5 million related to the change in fair value of a warrant liability and $4.7 million of restructuring costs tied to the company's upcoming acquisition. Despite this, free cash flow improved, driven by stronger operational efficiency in the core business.

What Kore Group Does
