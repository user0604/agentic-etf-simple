SpaceX's $28.5T Dream Meets Q1 Reality

The expectations are of cosmic proportions, but if SpaceX is able to accomplish just some of what it hopes to achieve, the sky's the limit.

Lou Whiteman

May 21, 2026, 9:09 AM

Comments (2)
7.6

Of all the astronomical numbers (sorry for the pun) in the SpaceX IPO, the one that stood out above all others is $28.5 trillion.

That's the size of the total addressable market (TAM) SpaceX is pursuing, a figure that the company acknowledges is "the largest actionable [TAM] ... in human history." Breaking it down further, SpaceX sees a $370 billion opportunity in space-enabled solutions, a $1.6 trillion opportunity in connectivity (including Starlink), and a $26.5 trillion opportunity in AI. Of that AI number, $22.7 trillion of that opportunity is from enterprise applications.

By comparison, total U.S. nominal gross domestic product is about $32 trillion.

It is no surprise to see Elon Musk talking about sky-high expectations (again, sorry). And we should expect nothing else from a company that says its mission is to "build the systems and technologies necessary to make life multiplanetary" and to "understand the true nature of the universe."

The question for investors is, are these expectations out of this world? (Last time, I promise.) On the surface, the financials look rather unremarkable. The company lost $4.28 billion on revenue of $4.69 billion in the first quarter. Starlink revenue improved year over year, but the space launch business shrank. The AI business generated revenue of just $818 million and would have lost money even without the $2.4 billion in R&D costs in the quarter.

The AI addition has led to an uptick in spending. SpaceX lost just $4.9 billion in all of last year -- nearly the amount it lost in the first quarter of 2026 alone -- with investment in its xAI division accounting for most of the increase.

To my eyes, the space business looks fine, grading on the curve of an early-stage business. (The same curve I would apply to Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB) and others in the space sector.) The AI business is both a burden, given the need for spending there, and an opportunity, based on the company's TAM projections.

The ultimate question for investors: Is this all worth $1.5 trillion or more? On the surface, I would say no. Then again, a lot of hard questions could have been asked when Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) went public in 2010. Musk has always played the long game, setting out-of-this-world (there he goes again!) goals and allowing them to evolve over time as he grows into the targets.

Investors interested in buying in should buckle up and expect a wild ride. The expectations are of cosmic proportions, but if SpaceX is able to accomplish just some of what it hopes to achieve, the sky's the limit.
