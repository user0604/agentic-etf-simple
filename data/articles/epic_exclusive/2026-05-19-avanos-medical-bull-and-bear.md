Avanos Medical Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 11:06 AM

Comments

Avanos Medical, Inc. (NYSE:AVNS) is a medical device company specializing in healthcare solutions for enteral feeding, neonatal care, and pain management. Its growth strategy centers on innovation in Specialty Nutrition Systems, geographic and channel expansion, targeted M&A (mergers and acquisitions), and margin improvement via supply chain transformation. In recent years, the company streamlined its portfolio, exited lower-growth businesses, and invested in new product launches and operational efficiency to reinforce its market leadership and drive long-term earnings growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership and Sustained Double-Digit Growth in Nutrition Systems Power Long-Term Value

Dominant market positions and superior organic growth in the Specialty Nutrition Systems (SNS) portfolio underpin a robust, recurring revenue engine. In 2025, the SNS segment achieved over 8% organic growth year over year, with short-term enteral feeding posting double-digit organic gains. Brands like MIC-KEY, Corpak, and NeoMed continue to benefit from high clinical adoption and ongoing innovation, including the CORGRIP 2 retention system and the expanding CORTRAK platform. For Q1 2026, company-wide revenue reached $182.2 million, exceeding analyst expectations, with organic sales for strategic segments up 3.4% year over year. This consistent above-market expansion positions Avanos to capitalize on rising demand for enteral nutrition in the U.S. and internationally, providing a resilient foundation for future earnings.

2. Strategic Acquisition and Seamless Integration Expand High-Growth Neonatal Franchise

The successful acquisition and integration of Nexus Medical accelerate growth in the neonatal solutions market and create lasting synergies. In 2025, Avanos acquired Nexus Medical, bolstering its neonatal portfolio and capturing $5 million in additional revenue. Management expects Nexus to sustain double-digit growth into 2026 and beyond, leveraging Avanos’ established NICU (Neonatal Intensive Care Unit) salesforce and global distribution networks. The seamless absorption of Nexus highlights the company’s disciplined approach to M&A, with integration proceeding on-target and providing immediate commercial leverage. This approach not only diversifies the product mix in a fast-growing category but also positions Avanos to pursue future bolt-on acquisitions that enhance scale and profitability in core markets.

3. Supply Chain Transformation and Tariff Mitigation Sets Stage for Margin Expansion

Ongoing supply chain realignment and successful exit from China manufacturing will relieve tariff pressures and lift margins starting in late 2026. Tariff headwinds were significant in 2025 and early 2026, with $30 million projected in tariff-related costs for the year—up from $18 million prior. However, the transition of syringe production to Mexico and Southeast Asia is on track to be complete by June 2026. Management forecasts gross margin improvement from the second half of 2026, once tariff impacts roll off as U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) coverage rises and cost-of-goods sold normalizes. This shift is expected to yield expanded operating leverage, support sustained investment in R&D, and deliver durable earnings growth—a structural shift that could transform Avanos’ long-term profitability profile.

4. Portfolio Rationalization Sharpens Focus and Unlocks Higher-Return Opportunities

The exit from low-growth and underperforming businesses increases emphasis on high-margin, fast-growing core segments while reducing operational complexity. In 2025 and early 2026, Avanos divested its hyaluronic acid business, exited the IV therapy segment, and transitioned its GAME READY business to a more profitable channel model. These moves enhance segment operating margins, sharpen capital allocation, and enable deeper focus on scalable platforms in enteral feeding and neonatal care. The streamlined operating structure frees up cash for reinvestment and M&A, while supporting disciplined cost management and innovation in key growth areas. Such portfolio actions lay the groundwork for improved structural profitability and long-term shareholder value.

5. Healthy Balance Sheet Provides Strategic Flexibility for Growth and Resilience

Strong liquidity and conservative leverage ratios provide room for ongoing investment, M&A, and risk management regardless of macro conditions. As of December 31, 2025, Avanos reported $90 million in cash and only $100 million in debt, with leverage meaningfully below 1x EBITDA—well below peer averages for diversified medtech firms. The company generated $43 million in free cash flow for 2025 and delivered adjusted EBITDA at $87 million, exceeding expectations. This capital strength allows Avanos to weather shocks such as tariff fluctuations, invest in new technologies or markets, and opportunistically pursue strategic acquisitions without undue balance sheet risk. The company’s prudent approach to capital management makes it particularly well-positioned for resilience and growth in a dynamic healthcare landscape.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Tariff and Cost Pressures Threaten Sustainable Profitability Despite Revenue Growth

Ongoing exposure to tariffs and elevated input costs risk undermining margin progress and could offset the benefits of top-line expansion. In 2026, Avanos expects $30 million in tariff impact—two-thirds related to China-sourced products—representing a $12 million year-over-year increase. While the supply chain relocation out of China is expected to mitigate this, management guidance confirms these headwinds will compress margins at least through the first half of the year. The company posted a negative net margin of 10.4% in Q1 2026, suggesting that even with double-digit top-line growth, profitability remains fragile. If supply chain execution stumbles or cost inflation persists longer than forecast, achieving meaningful and sustainable margin expansion could prove elusive.

2. Slow Recovery and Mixed Performance in Pain Management & Recovery Segment Limits Upside

The Pain Management & Recovery (PM&R) segment continues to lag, with surgical pain and GAME READY revenues declining year over year, offsetting robust gains elsewhere. While radiofrequency ablation (RFA) solutions grew double digits in 2025, the wider PM&R segment’s normalized organic sales climbed just 2.3%, well below the SNS segment’s pace. Surgical pain devices are challenged by slow adoption and delayed reimbursement under the NOPAIN Act, while GAME READY revenue shrank after restructuring. This lack of consistent growth across all business areas makes Avanos more dependent on its nutrition portfolio; if PM&R does not reaccelerate, the company’s overall growth and diversification will remain at risk.

3. Acquisition-Related Legal Risks and Transaction Uncertainty Pose Near-Term Strategic Disruption

The pending acquisition by American Industrial Partners exposes Avanos to shareholder lawsuits, governance risk, and operational disruption that could distract management and delay strategic progress. Multiple law firms are actively investigating whether deal terms adequately protect public shareholders and scrutinizing possible insider benefits. Such legal reviews may delay deal closure, impose unexpected costs, or require renegotiated terms, creating uncertainty about the company’s governance and near-term direction. During M&A transitions, operational or financial focus can waver, and integration or regulatory challenges could distract management from executing on innovation and supply chain transformation, meaning material risks exist for near-term strategy and investor returns until the transaction’s resolution is fully clarified.
