Digimarc Posts 19% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 8:19 AM

Key Takeaways
Revenue declined as major legacy contracts expired, but the company made strong progress controlling costs.
Non-GAAP loss per share improved significantly year over year.
Management maintains a focus on subscription growth; new product revenues will be important to watch in coming quarters.

Digital watermarking provider Digimarc (NASDAQ:DMRC) had a mixed start to fiscal 2026, as revenue losses from expiring contracts outweighed gains from new business and improved profitability. The company specializes in digital security solutions for physical and digital products, with a growing focus on SaaS (software-as-a-service) models. The latest results for the first quarter, released May 12, 2026, show a company navigating a business transition and managing expenses tightly.

Overall, the quarter demonstrated better underlying profitability, even as revenue came under pressure from contract roll-offs.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped 19% from the prior-year period, largely because two large commercial contracts expired in 2025.
Cost reductions were effective, with non-GAAP operating expenses down by just over half from the year before.
Annual recurring revenue (ARR) ended the period at $15.0 million, up 9% compared to the end of the previous quarter but still 25% lower year over year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide new financial guidance for upcoming quarters, but reiterated its focus on growing subscription revenue and increasing gross profit margins. The most important metric to monitor going forward will be whether the company’s new products, such as secure gift card solutions, generate enough recurring revenue to offset the loss of legacy contracts.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue for the period fell because two major contracts accounted for $6.8 million of lost annual recurring revenue. While overall revenues were down, new commercial contracts added $1.8 million in ARR, providing a sign that new business efforts are gaining traction.

Profitability improved, with non-GAAP net loss shrinking to $1.6 million, driven by aggressive cost controls. Operating expenses, both GAAP and non-GAAP, were lower primarily due to reduced headcount and less spending on severance and compensation versus a year ago.

Cash management was another bright spot, as free cash flow usage shrank from $5.6 million a year earlier to $2.0 million in the period. However, cash and short-term investments decreased to $10.0 million as of March 31, 2026.

What Digimarc Does

Digimarc creates digital watermarking and security software for authenticating products and preventing fraud in both digital and physical contexts.
