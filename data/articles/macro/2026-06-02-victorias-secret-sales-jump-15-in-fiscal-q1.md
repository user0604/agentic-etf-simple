Key Takeaways
Victoria’s Secret & Co. posted a clear business win by beating its own guidance for both sales and profit. EPS surged to $0.60 from $0.09 year over year. Management raised its full-year outlook but flagged that tariff pressures will remain a major headwind.

Victoria’s Secret & Co. (NYSE:VSCO) delivered a strong quarter with sales and earnings rising well above its own projections thanks to revitalized brand momentum and operational improvements. The company, a top player in intimate apparel through its Victoria’s Secret, PINK, and Beauty brands, reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on June 2, 2026. Overall, the period reflected broad-based sales growth, higher profitability, and a more optimistic full-year outlook.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 15.3% to $1.56 billion, exceeding the top end of its own guidance range.
Comparable sales, a key retail metric that tracks revenue from existing stores and digital channels, rose 13%, a sharp swing from a 1% decrease a year ago.
The company repurchased $100 million in shares and raised its full-year forecasts for both sales and profitability.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased full-year 2026 guidance, now forecasting revenue between $7.03 and $7.13 billion and expecting profitability to outpace sales growth, even as tariff challenges continue. For the next quarter, it forecasts sales of $1.59 to $1.615 billion and operating income of $90 to $100 million. Investors should closely monitor whether the recent sales growth can be sustained as tariff pressures persist and year-ago comparisons get tougher in the back half of the year.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter’s double-digit revenue growth stemmed from success across channels: North American stores improved, digital sales grew to nearly 30% of the mix, and international segment revenue rose 45%, helped by European channel changes and higher organic demand in China and franchise markets.

Profitability improvement was achieved even as tariffs knocked about 1.75 percentage points off margins. Operating income more than doubled as management controlled costs and benefited from reduced promotions and more regular price selling.

Shareholder returns included $100 million in repurchases, with $150 million still authorized. Management also highlighted a strong pipeline for upcoming product launches across bras, undergarments, sleepwear, and beauty, aiming to keep the brand relevant and consumer engagement high.
