Vertical Aerospace Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 8:49 AM

Comments

Vertical Aerospace Ltd. (NYSE:EVTL) is a U.K.-based advanced air mobility company developing electric vertical takeoff and landing (eVTOL) aircraft for urban and regional air transport. Its core strategy focuses on certifying its flagship Valo aircraft by the late 2020s, leveraging partnerships with leading aerospace suppliers, building proprietary battery systems, and attracting major commercial and government customers to drive future revenue through both aircraft sales and high-margin recurring services.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Accelerated Certification Progress Puts Vertical Aerospace Ahead of Key Competitors

Vertical Aerospace’s disciplined regulatory approach has reduced certification risk and set the stage for earlier commercial entry. The company is nearing completion of its piloted transition flight tests under direct oversight from the U.K. Civil Aviation Authority, bolstered by published means of compliance and proactive regulatory “dry runs.” Management targets full certification for Valo by 2028 and the hybrid model by 2029. The timelines are likely ahead of those of most eVTOL peers, who often face less stringent but less globally portable oversight. This regulatory clarity and CAA collaboration could allow Vertical Aerospace to access global markets sooner and establish itself as a category leader in both civilian and defense applications, with significant first-mover advantage in hybrid-electric aircraft.

2. Proprietary Battery Technology and Battery-as-a-Service Model Provide a Durable, High-Margin Recurring Revenue Stream

Vertical Aerospace’s in-house battery system and annual replacement cycle underpin a robust, recurring revenue model with 40% targeted margins. The company’s proprietary battery packs not only allow its aircraft to meet full payload and range requirements at launch but also enable the Battery-as-a-Service business line. Each aircraft is expected to require battery replacements every year over a roughly 20-year lifespan, creating long-tailed, predictable revenue. Pilot production is operational, a new 30,000-square-foot battery facility is in construction, and the batteries’ second-life applications are already attracting third-party interest. This model smooths out cash flows and could sustain profitability regardless of cyclicality in new aircraft sales, positioning the company as both a hardware manufacturer and a high-margin service provider.

3. Blue Chip Airline Preorders and Strategic Partnerships Validate Commercial Demand and Ecosystem Credibility

Engagement from major airlines and industrial partners de-risks Vertical Aerospace’s commercialization and scaling efforts. The company has secured preorders and partnership interest from globally diversified, Tier 1 airlines—including American Airlines (NASDAQ:AAL) and Japan Airlines—complemented by new network agreements in strategic markets such as London, New York, Miami, and Saudi Arabia. Strong Tier 1 supply chain relationships, including Honeywell (NASDAQ:HON), Aciturri, Evolito, and Hyundai WIA, bolster Vertical Aerospace's ability to scale production efficiently and minimize certification risk through proven, aerospace-grade manufacturing. These relationships offer not only demand certainty for initial deliveries but also credibility with regulators, investors, and ecosystem partners at a critical phase of industry emergence.

4. Modular and Hybrid-Converting Aircraft Design Unlocks Multisegment and Dual-Use Market Potential

The Valo’s modular architecture and convertible hybrid platform expand addressable markets across civil, cargo, EMS, and defense. By enabling quick conversion from four to six passengers and providing distinct passenger–pilot separation, the Valo aircraft exceeds the flexibility of most peer offerings, which are often fixed at minimum viable specifications. The hybrid-convertible design paves the way for applications beyond passenger transport, including cargo, medical evacuation, and military operations. This differentiated product roadmap, including hybrid certification aimed for 2029, positions Vertical Aerospace years ahead of competitors in dual-use and longer-range sectors—potentially accessing higher-margin defense contracts and broader commercial market segments as battery technology and regulatory landscapes mature.

5. Government Support and Competitive Policy Landscape Secure Additional Nondilutive Funding Opportunities

Robust public-sector backing reduces capital risk and may attract further grants or co-investment as the company scales. To date, Vertical Aerospace has received approximately $100 million in U.K. government support and is a core participant in high-profile initiatives like the Future Flight Challenge and SESAR in the EU. The company is actively being courted by other European governments and U.S. states, presenting strategic optionality for securing additional nondilutive funding. Sustained public co-investment for advanced manufacturing and clean aviation infrastructure reduces the reliance on dilutive equity funding and strengthens the company’s financial runway as it approaches capital-intensive industrialization milestones.

Potential Bear Cases
1. High and Rising Cash Burn Creates Dilution and Funding Risk Before Revenue Realization

Vertical Aerospace faces acute funding risks as annual cash burn is forecast at $190 million to 200 million and available liquidity is not yet sufficient to reach certification or profitability. As of the March 2026 update, the company reported approximately $85 million in short-term liquidity and an at-the-market facility with $78 million remaining, equating to up to $160 million in access—still short of full commercialization funding needs. The business is prerevenue and expects continued negative free cash flow until at least 2028, meaning significant new capital must be raised. If funding is sourced at unfavorable terms or amid market dislocation, existing shareholders could be meaningfully diluted, or milestone timelines could slip, risking delayed commercialization.

2. Certification Delays or Regulatory Hurdles Could Erode First-Mover Advantage and Customer Confidence

Ongoing risk of technical or regulatory delays threatens timely entry to market and could disadvantage Vertical Aerospace relative to competitors. While the company has made steady progress on flight testing and critical design milestones, the sector remains subject to unpredictable factors—including weather, evolving global certification standards, and intense CAA oversight. Missed technical milestones or slippage in transition testing and type certification (currently targeted for 2028–2029) could undermine relationships with strategic partners and preorder customers, reduce commercial credibility, and allow better-capitalized or U.S.-based peers to leapfrog into key markets first.

3. Litigation Could Undermine Strategic Momentum

Active legal investigations may create new headwinds for Vertical Aerospace’s ambitious roadmap. Archer Aviation filed a patent infringement complaint against Vertical Aerospace on February 23, 2026, in the U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of Texas. Also, after Vertical Aerospace disclosed going-concern doubts in its 2025 annual report filed March 24, 2026—causing the stock to drop 18%—securities law firms Pomerantz LLP and Schall Law Firm launched investigations into potential investor fraud claims on behalf of shareholders. These are investigations at this stage, not yet filed class action suits, but worth flagging.
