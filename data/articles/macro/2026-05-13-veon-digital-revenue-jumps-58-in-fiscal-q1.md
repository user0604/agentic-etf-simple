Key Takeaways
Veon delivered strong digital revenue growth but continued to see declines in its overall mobile customer base. EBITDA rose 17.7% year over year, with digital revenue climbing 57.7%. Management lifted full-year revenue guidance but warned that pressures on the mobile user base and tax costs remain concerns.

Veon (NASDAQ:VEON) accelerated its digital transition but saw its traditional mobile business shrink. The leading emerging markets telecom and digital services group balanced solid digital segment expansion with lower overall mobile customers in several key countries. On May 13, 2026, it reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period showcased stronger top-line growth and higher cash flow amid persistent challenges in user retention and rising tax expenses.

Notable Developments
Digital revenue represented 25.2% of total revenue, rising sharply from 18.7% the prior year, reflecting a faster transition to digital services.
Mobile customers dropped to 150.5 million, a 1.3% year-over-year decline, signaling slowdowns in several core markets.
Free cash flow after leases and license costs increased by 73.4% to $246 million, supporting ongoing investment and buybacks.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 revenue growth target to 11–14%, driven by better-than-expected digital momentum in the first quarter. Earnings guidance for EBITDA remains at 7–10% growth, but management flagged risks from higher tax rates and market challenges in customer retention. Investors should closely watch how the shrinking mobile base could affect future growth even as digital revenues expand.

Quarterly Highlights

Digital segment revenue jumped 57.7% year over year to $303 million, making up just over a quarter of the company-wide revenue total. Digital gains came from financial services, ride-hailing, entertainment, and health solutions, with especially strong growth in Ukraine and Pakistan.

Despite digital user growth, total mobile subscribers shrank by 1.3%, with declines most noticeable in Ukraine, Bangladesh, and Uzbekistan. However, the number of 4G and multiplay (bundled service) customers increased, helping maintain average revenue per user at higher levels.

Veon’s free cash flow after leases climbed 73.4% from the prior year. This increase supported both network capital spending and ongoing share repurchases under its $100 million buyback program. Margins remained broadly steady due to disciplined spending, even as some countries, like Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, faced cost headwinds.

What Veon Does

Veon is a telecommunications and digital service provider operating across emerging markets, offering mobile, internet, and digital financial, entertainment, and business solutions.
