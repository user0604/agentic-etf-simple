Earnings Preview: Build-A-Bear Workshop

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 9:08 AM

Comments

Build-A-Bear Workshop (NYSE:BBW), a specialty retailer known for its interactive make-your-own stuffed animal stores, is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on Thursday, May 28, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Build-A-Bear Workshop reported total revenue of $154.5 million, which was slightly below analyst expectations. Earnings per share came in at $1.26, essentially in line with consensus estimates. The company achieved record annual revenue, surpassing $529 million for the first time, marking nearly 7% growth year over year. However, quarterly net retail sales were flat, and e-commerce sales declined due to lower online traffic and tough comparisons with the prior year. The company cited adverse weather and supply chain disruptions, including tariffs, as key challenges, but these were partially offset by higher average transaction values and growth in its commercial (wholesale) segment. The quarter also marked the announcement of a CEO transition, with Chris Hurt set to take over as CEO in June 2026.

Positives
Record annual revenue milestone achieved: The company delivered over $529 million in annual revenue for the first time, reflecting continued demand and successful expansion efforts.
Growth in commercial and wholesale business: Commercial revenue rose over 40% in the quarter, driven by wholesale orders including a major rollout to Walmart, which helps diversify revenue streams beyond core retail stores.
International expansion momentum: The company doubled its international footprint to 36 countries over two years, with 11 net new experience locations added in Q4 and further growth in partner-operated stores.
Shareholder returns increased: Build-A-Bear returned nearly $40 million to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases, and announced a 4.5% dividend increase for the next quarter.
Negatives
Flat net retail sales and declining e-commerce: Net retail sales were essentially unchanged, and e-commerce demand fell 13.6% in the quarter, reflecting weaker online traffic and fewer high-profile product launches compared to last year.
Tariff and cost pressures impacted profitability: Tariffs and related costs reduced pretax income and earnings per share, with gross margin down 140 basis points year over year in Q4.
Higher operating expenses: Selling, general, and administrative expenses increased as a percentage of revenue, driven by wage inflation, higher medical expenses, and increased marketing spend.
CEO transition creates near-term uncertainty: The planned leadership change, while part of a multiyear process, introduces some uncertainty as the new CEO takes over during a period of ongoing strategic shifts.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Traffic trends and retail store performance: Monitor whether the company can sustain the positive momentum seen from its record Valentine’s Day and recent new product launches, especially after early-quarter weather disruptions.
Tariff impact and cost management: Watch for updates on how newly announced tariffs and ongoing supply chain costs are affecting margins, and whether the company is able to offset these with pricing or efficiency measures.
Progress on digital transformation: Look for signs of improvement in e-commerce traffic and conversion following recent investments in website upgrades, marketing, and personalization, as well as efforts to adapt to changes in online search and digital advertising.
Status of new store openings and ICON Park launch: Check for updates on the timing and performance of new experience locations, especially the high-profile ICON Park store in Orlando, which is expected to open in the second half of the year.
Wholesale and licensing expansion: Assess growth in the commercial segment, including the rollout of Mini Beans and other products into new wholesale channels like Walmart, and any early results from outbound licensing initiatives.
