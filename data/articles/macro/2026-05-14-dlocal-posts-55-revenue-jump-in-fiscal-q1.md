Key Takeaways
DLocal delivered rapid growth in payment volumes but faced margin pressure. Revenue rose 55% year over year. Management maintained full-year guidance, highlighting confidence in continued expansion.

DLocal (NASDAQ:DLO) delivered another period of robust volume growth, even as margin and free cash flow came under pressure. The leading emerging-markets payments platform reported results for the first quarter (Q1) of fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026. Overall, the period saw strong top-line expansion but highlighted the ongoing challenge of balancing scale with profitability.

Notable developments
Total payment volume reached $14.1 billion in Q1 2026, up 73% year over year.
Gross profit margin compressed to 35%, down 4 percentage points from the same period last year.
Adjusted free cash flow dropped 63% year over year due to temporary working capital effects, with normalization anticipated later this year.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year outlook, expecting payment volume growth in the 50–60% range and gross profit growth of 22.5–27.5%. It projected cost improvements and better operating leverage in the second half as expenses from last year’s investment cycle taper off. Investors should closely monitor trends in gross margin and cash flow to gauge whether operational improvements materialize as intended.

Quarterly highlights

Payment volumes expanded rapidly, with broad-based gains in Africa and Asia, supported by continued onboarding of merchants in key markets like Nigeria, Mozambique, and Vietnam. Brazil’s growth moderated after an exceptional prior quarter, but overall volume growth remained well ahead of yearly guidance.

Margin pressure persisted, with gross profit as a percentage of revenue and of total payment volume declining compared to last year. This reflects the impact of processing larger-scale transactions for established merchants and expanding into new geographies, which typically offer slimmer fees per transaction.

Adjusted free cash flow declined sharply, driven by higher receivables and tax credit timing. Management explained these as working capital timing issues and expects improvement later in 2026, referencing a long-term track record of strong conversion of profit to cash.

What DLocal does

DLocal is a payments platform that enables global merchants to send and receive money across emerging markets through a single application programming interface (API).
