Zillow Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments

Zillow Group, Inc. (NASDAQ:Z) operates the leading online real estate marketplace in the United States, connecting buyers, sellers, renters, and real estate professionals through an expansive digital platform. Its growth strategy focuses on leveraging proprietary technology, artificial intelligence-driven products, and integrated transaction tools across for-sale, rentals, and mortgage segments to outpace broader housing market trends and deepen user engagement—even during cyclical downturns.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Platform Scale and Direct Traffic Accelerate Share Gains in a Flat Housing Market

Zillow’s unmatched platform reach and direct user engagement are translating into continuous market share gains even as U.S. housing transactions stagnate. The company’s traffic remains highly defensible, with approximately 80% of visits coming directly, and the daily active app user base more than doubling its closest competitor as of Q1 2026. Despite a U.S. housing market that was flat year over year in Q1 2026, Zillow’s total revenue rose 18% over the same period—reflecting its ability to profitably aggregate high-intent consumers and outperform industry transaction values for three consecutive years. This resilience in audience scale and engagement creates a self-reinforcing moat, supporting sustained market share growth against both incumbent and new digital competitors.

2. Rapid Growth in Rentals and Multifamily Drives New Revenue Streams and Long-Term Upside

The rentals segment is evolving into a meaningful revenue engine backed by clear market leadership and product innovation. In Q1 2026, rentals revenue surged 42% year over year to $183 million, driven by a 57% increase in multifamily revenue. The company held a category-leading 2.7 million monthly active rental listings and 76,000 multifamily properties on platform (a 38% jump from the prior year), attracting 36 million unique monthly visitors. Management reiterated a visible path to $1 billion-plus in annual rentals revenue, bolstered by high-return property manager packages and new analytics tools. With rentals revenue growing at a 32% compound annual rate since 2022, investors see a long growth runway as Zillow deepens penetration and monetizes both supply-side and demand-side value chains.

3. AI Innovation and Product Integration Are Unlocking Higher Conversion and Margin Expansion

Accelerated technology deployment, especially artificial intelligence and integrated workflows, is driving higher user and agent productivity and improving profitability. Early pilots of Zillow’s new consumer-facing AI Mode (live to about 5% of the audience as of Q1 2026) showed deeper, more substantive engagement from millions of users, boosting actionable conversion rates. Internally, engineers are 40% more productive thanks to generative AI tools—supporting faster rollouts of features like Buyability, Showcase, and Zillow Pro. On the agent front, adoption of the Follow Up Boss CRM rose 70% since acquisition, with Showcase listings on 4.3% of new for-sale inventory. These innovations are expected to drive both revenue and cost leverage, underpinning management’s outlook for EBITDA margin expansion and higher customer lifetime value starting in the back half of 2026.

4. Enhanced Markets and Cross-Segment Integration Strengthen Zillow’s Competitive Moat

The rollout of enhanced markets and seamless integration across buying, selling, and financing is increasing conversion rates and boosting monetization per user. By Q1 2026, enhanced markets accounted for 49% of all connections (up from 44% the prior quarter), moving toward the targeted 75% penetration. This strategy enables greater adoption of mortgage, software, and agent tools across the transaction lifecycle, regardless of macro conditions. The company’s “shop with pre-approval” and Buyability features (4.3 million enrolled users in Q1 2026, up from 3.6 million at year-end 2025) illustrate the cross-pollination that binds home shoppers, loan officers, and agents within a unified. digital workflow—improving close rates and wallet share. This integration creates switching costs for both consumers and professionals, safeguarding Zillow’s leadership.

5. Disciplined Capital Allocation and Strong Free Cash Flow Support Sustainable Growth

Shareholder returns and liquidity strength provide ongoing flexibility for investment and downside protection. In Q1 2026, the company generated $127 million in free cash flow (a 44% year-over-year increase) and repurchased $626 million in stock, reducing its diluted share count from 256 million to 240 million. Total liquidity stood at $1.3 billion (including an undrawn $500 million revolver), with ample room remaining under authorized buyback programs. These actions highlight management’s conviction in long-term value and provide a financial cushion for continued technology investment or opportunistic mergers and acquisitions. Disciplined expense growth, especially fixed costs indexed only to inflation, sets the stage for sustainable increases in margin and return on equity as operating scale grows and legal overhangs recede in the second half of 2026.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Legal and Regulatory Overhang Pose Material Risks to Future Profitability and Operations

Elevated legal expenses and ongoing regulatory scrutiny could persist, undermining margin expansion and strategic flexibility. As of May 2026, multiple law firms had launched federal securities class action investigations related to the company, tied to operational disclosures and practices. In Q1 and Q2 2026 alone, legal costs increased EBITDA expenses by $11 million and are expected to be a $20 million headwind quarter over quarter, depressing margins by roughly 200 basis points in the first half of the year. While management expects these costs to decline after the conclusion of the Federal Trade Commission trial, adverse regulatory outcomes, settlements, or mandated operational changes could limit product initiative flexibility, increase ongoing compliance expenses, or even erode core competitive advantages. This sustained legal uncertainty poses a real risk to both bottom-line results and investor sentiment.

2. Macroeconomic Sensitivity Means Revenue Growth Remains Vulnerable to Prolonged Housing Market Stagnation

Flat or declining housing transaction volumes—driven by high mortgage rates and weakened affordability—could cap near-term growth and pressure profitability. The company’s management candidly assumes no material improvement in housing market activity for all of 2026, as confirmed by the slow spring selling season and persistently elevated mortgage rates highlighted in both internal and external market reports. While the business has managed to outperform the market with 18% year-over-year revenue growth in Q1 2026, much of the current margin structure remains tied to transaction volume recovery or continued share gains. Extended stagnation or a further downturn in housing could dampen the high-margin For Sale segment and soften agent demand, capping upside and delaying progress toward long-term margin targets.

3. Slower-Than-Expected Execution on Enhanced Markets or Rentals Expansion Could Challenge Growth Outlook

Key strategic initiatives, such as scaling enhanced markets and achieving $1 billion in rentals revenue, have execution risk that could impede overall growth. By Q1 2026, enhanced markets comprised 49% of the company’s sales connections, with a medium-term target of 75%. Progress toward this benchmark—while encouraging—has no guaranteed linearity and requires continued product rollouts, agent buy-in, and operational support amid changing competitive and regulatory dynamics. Similarly, achieving rentals revenue of $1 billion depends on accelerating multifamily adoption, ongoing inventory growth (which hit 2.7 million listings in Q1 2026), and property manager retention. Any delays—whether from resource constraints, market fragmentation, or competitive responses—could result in growth rates falling short of guidance, thereby disappointing investors accustomed to outperformance versus both digital and offline peers.
