Iren Posts 41% Margin in Fiscal Q3 AI Pivot

Key Takeaways
Iren’s AI cloud pivot gained momentum, but near-term financial losses deepened as it wound down Bitcoin mining.
Revenue reached $144.8 million, with adjusted EBITDA margin stable at 41%.
Management reaffirmed expansion plans, but cautioned that future growth depends on successfully deploying new GPU capacity on schedule.

Iren (NASDAQ:IREN) advanced its shift to AI cloud services with new contracts and data center expansions, but financial losses increased as it wound down its legacy Bitcoin mining business. The vertically integrated digital infrastructure company reported third-quarter results for the period ended March 31, 2026, on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed promising progress in AI, but also highlighted execution and cash flow challenges.

Notable developments
Iren signed a five-year $3.4 billion contract and a 5 GW partnership with Nvidia, advancing its AI cloud transition. The Microsoft and Prince George contracts bring annualized revenue under contract to $3.1 billion, with $3.7 billion targeted by the end of 2026.
AI Cloud Services revenue nearly doubled sequentially to $33.6 million, while Bitcoin mining revenue dropped sharply and led to a company-wide net loss of $247.8 million due to impairments and contract transition costs.
Cash and cash equivalents fell to $2.2 billion, reflecting continued heavy investment in new data center capacity and acquisitions in Spain and software platforms to support growth.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management remains committed to scaling AI data center capacity to 480 MW by year-end and targeting $3.7 billion in contracted annualized revenue by late 2026. However, this goal depends on on-time delivery and deployment of advanced computing hardware, making execution risks and capital management especially critical for investors to monitor in coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The AI cloud pivot accelerated as Iren signed major multi-year deals and expanded its pipeline, including the NVIDIA contract covering advanced Blackwell GPUs and planned data center expansions in Texas and Spain. These moves helped push contracted annualized revenue for AI and cloud services higher, though most of this pipeline is not yet reflected in reported revenue.

The quarter showed total revenue of $144.8 million, with a rapid rise in AI services offset by a drop in Bitcoin mining and large one-time impairment charges tied to retiring old mining hardware. Adjusted EBITDA, a key measure of core profitability, was $59.5 million at a 41% margin, unchanged from the last quarter but below levels typically seen in established cloud businesses.

Iren ended the period with $2.2 billion in cash after significant outflows for acquisitions and buildouts. The company noted ongoing financing needs, heavy investing cash outflows, and potential for share dilution under new NVIDIA equity options and convertible notes, which are important factors for investors to track as capital requirements remain high.

What Iren does

Iren operates renewable-powered data centers and provides infrastructure for both high-performance computing and AI cloud services, having shifted away from Bitcoin mining as its main business focus.
