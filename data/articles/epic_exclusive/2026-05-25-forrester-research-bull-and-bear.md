Forrester Research Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

Forrester Research, Inc. (NASDAQ:FORR) is a global research and advisory firm focused on providing actionable insights to businesses navigating digital transformation, AI adoption, and customer experience. The company is actively repositioning itself as an "AI research company", embedding proprietary research directly into client workflows—particularly through integrations like Microsoft 365 Copilot and Teams—to drive engagement, increase retention, and support long-term growth in a rapidly evolving enterprise landscape.

Potential Bull Cases
1. AI-Driven Product Integration Deepens Client Engagement and Expands Revenue Opportunity

Embedding Forrester AI into enterprise workflows is driving usage, retention, and new upsell opportunities. Integrating Forrester AI—now available within Microsoft Teams and Copilot—places the company’s proprietary data and expert insights directly where clients operate, amplifying value and convenience. In the first quarter of 2026, Forrester AI’s usage reached an all-time high, with overall usage up 55% year over year and prompt volume rising 65%, indicating surging client demand for actionable, workflow-embedded guidance. By meeting customers in their daily digital environments and embedding research as a seamless part of decision-making processes, Forrester not only strengthens its differentiation versus generic large language models but also creates multiple touch points for incremental upsell, win-backs, and price-insensitive renewals, enhancing the long-term revenue trajectory.

2. Successful Pivot to Subscription Research and Workflow Products Boosts Revenue Visibility

Forrester's rapid transition away from legacy consulting and toward scalable subscription products improves financial predictability. The company is executing a decisive exit from its strategy consulting segment by year-end 2026, focusing new investment and growth on recurring research offerings such as Forrester Decisions and AI Access. In Q1 2026, although research revenue contracted modestly (down 2% year over year), consulting revenue fell sharply (down 13%), reflecting the planned shift. This transition increases the portion of revenue derived from multiyear, high-retention contracts—now 72% of total contract value, up from 71% last quarter—thereby improving revenue visibility, reducing cyclicality, and allowing for more consistent investment in value-added digital products that can scale revenue without proportional cost increases.

3. Retention and Upsell Metrics Show Tangible Turnaround After Prior Weakness

Improved wallet and client retention rates point to a successful turnaround in customer engagement and account health. In the latest reported quarter, wallet retention rose to 89% (up 2 points sequentially and 3 points year over year), while client retention climbed to 78% (up 1 point sequentially and 5 points year over year). This marks a meaningful reversal following several periods of pressured retention and is largely attributed to effective post-sales programs, accelerated onboarding, and better alignment between customer success and sales teams. Notably, AI Access—now approaching 5% of total contract value and targeted to reach about 10% by year-end—is driving incremental upsell and facilitating win-backs, all while holding average contract values steady ($160,000 to $180,000 range year over year). These improvements are key predictors of future contract value stabilization and eventual growth.

4. Strong Balance Sheet and Cash Flow Allow Continued Investment in Innovation

The company’s robust liquidity provides a buffer and enables ongoing product development amid market volatility. Exiting Q1 2026, Forrester reported over $145 million in cash and only $35 million in debt, supported by free cash flow of $19 million in the quarter ($25 million when excluding one-time headquarters expenditures). The extension of its credit facility to 2029 and maintained access to capital offer the flexibility to invest in new research, product enhancements, and additional workflow integrations—even as near-term revenue softness persists. A strong balance sheet is a significant strategic asset in a period of rapid technology change, enabling Forrester to fund innovation, withstand economic headwinds, and execute on its pivot toward high-growth areas without resorting to dilutive equity issuance or disruptive cost-cutting.

5. Industry Tailwinds in Private AI Adoption Favor Forrester’s Trusted, Verticalized Content

Forrester’s focus on enterprise-specific, explainable AI aligns with secular growth trends in private and regulated AI deployment. Management estimates that 70% of future AI-related revenue in the enterprise sector will come from private, customized models—as opposed to generic, public solutions—due to data sensitivity, regulatory pressures, and intellectual property needs. Forrester’s unique positioning as both a technology partner and provider of curated, analyst-reviewed research makes it a primary beneficiary of these shifts. The company’s catalog of over 900 AI use cases, production of 420+ research reports last quarter, and a strong track record in vertical markets like banking, insurance, and government further underpin its status as the trusted advisor for organizations undergoing AI-driven operational overhauls. These industry dynamics could accelerate demand for Forrester’s differentiated offerings through the next five years.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Revenue and Earnings Declines Highlight Execution and Demand Challenges

Recent financial performance reveals ongoing pressure despite strategic repositioning. Forrester’s revenue in Q1 2026 fell 5% year over year to $85.5 million (GAAP), with research revenue down 2% and consulting tumbling by 13%. The company posted a non-GAAP net loss of ($0.04) per share, missing consensus by $0.16, and full-year 2026 guidance anticipates total revenue down 9–12% versus the prior year. While management points to sequential improvement in key metrics, these continuing top-line declines and negative earnings suggest that the pace of customer adoption and upsell is not yet sufficient to offset lost consulting revenue or fully stabilize the research business. Macroeconomic headwinds, slower client budget cycles, or delayed transformation projects could prolong or worsen this trajectory, jeopardizing confidence in a near-term turnaround.

2. Repeated Goodwill Impairments and Ongoing Restructuring Signal Structural Headwinds

The company’s history of large, recurring impairment and restructuring charges raises concerns about the staying power of its business model. Forrester booked a $10.8 million goodwill impairment in Q1 2026, following an even larger $83.9 million write-down in Q1 2025, alongside costs tied to workforce reductions and office closures. While management excludes these items from adjusted results for comparability, the pattern of ongoing asset write-downs and restructuring highlights the risk of underperforming prior investments and ongoing difficulties in achieving profitable, sustainable growth. If these non-recurring charges persist, they could erode investor trust, distract from operational execution, and mask the true underlying profitability of the business.

3. Contract Value Contraction Indicates Hurdles in Achieving Renewed Growth

Sustained declines in contract value (CV) reinforce questions about long-term demand for Forrester’s offerings. In Q1 2026, annualized contract value was reported at $285.3 million, down 3% year over year, following a 6% decline in the previous quarter. Despite improvements in retention and product usage, this persistent downward trend in the company’s primary recurring revenue metric indicates that recent product innovation and workflow integration—while promising—have yet to fully arrest client churn or reignite net new growth. If net contract value fails to inflect as projected, investor patience may wane and the investment thesis for a durable AI-driven turnaround could be materially weakened.
