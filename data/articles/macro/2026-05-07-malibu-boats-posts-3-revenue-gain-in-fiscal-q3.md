Malibu Boats Posts 3% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q3

Key Takeaways
Malibu Boats posted higher revenue due to the Saxdor acquisition but faced sharply lower profitability. Adjusted net income per share fell 24.3% from the prior year. Management guided for modest growth and a focus on integrating new operations in the coming quarter.

Malibu Boats (NASDAQ:MBUU) extended its top-line growth but faced heavier margin pressure and a swing to a net loss. The leading manufacturer of recreational powerboats benefited from its recent Saxdor Yachts acquisition, yet core segment volumes continued to decline. On May 7, 2026, Malibu Boats reported results for the third quarter of fiscal 2026 (the quarter ended March 31).

Overall, the period reflected resilient revenue offset by weaker profitability and ongoing cost challenges as the company integrates Saxdor and navigates cyclical industry softness.

Notable Developments
Revenue rose 3.1% to $235.7 million, helped by a $23.1 million partial-quarter contribution from Saxdor dayboats; total unit volume declined 12.4% over the same period.
Gross margin dropped to 17.5%, pressured by lower unit output and higher costs, while general and administrative expenses increased 60%, reflecting Saxdor acquisition costs and higher compensation.
Free cash flow improved over 80% year over year, reaching $16.0 million as capital spending stayed disciplined and cash flow from operations strengthened.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its fiscal 2026 guidance to account for acquired Saxdor operations, projecting combined net sales of $880–$886 million and adjusted EBITDA of $72–$74 million. The company expects legacy segment sales to be down slightly for the year, with Saxdor margins in the 10–11% range next quarter. Integration progress, cost control, and stabilizing volumes in existing segments remain key watchpoints going forward.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure for the quarter included contributions from its new Saxdor Yachts segment, which lifted the company’s average selling price but only partially offset declining sales volumes in the core Malibu segment. Growth in segments such as saltwater fishing and Cobalt runabout boats resulted entirely from higher average selling prices rather than increased unit sales, showing that product mix and pricing drove top-line gains more than demand.

Profitability continued to weaken, as adjusted EBITDA fell nearly 20% and gross margin narrowed by 2.5 percentage points year over year. General and administrative costs surged to $31.8 million, influenced by Saxdor acquisition expenses and higher compensation, while underlying unit softness weighed heavily on margins.

Despite profitability challenges, the business generated strong free cash flow and pursued share repurchases, with nearly 493,000 shares repurchased for $13.1 million in the quarter. Net debt rose moderately as the company used cash and new borrowing to fund the Saxdor acquisition.

What Malibu Boats Does

Malibu Boats designs, manufactures, and markets a portfolio of recreational powerboats, including performance towboats, saltwater fishing boats, and luxury runabouts.
