Key Takeaways
Ring Energy posted a net loss due to large non-cash charges, despite execution on cost control. Adjusted free cash flow dropped sharply to $0.2 million for the quarter. Management expects infrastructure investments to drive future production gains, but timing remains uncertain.

Ring Energy (NYSEMKT:REI) reported a difficult first quarter, with a net loss driven by major non-cash charges, even as it maintained strong control over operating costs. The independent oil and gas producer operates primarily in the Permian Basin, focusing on drilling and production efficiency. In its earnings release dated May 7, 2026, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, management noted that recent investments in infrastructure were yet to show full financial benefits. Overall, the quarter highlighted both solid operational execution and significant pressure from commodity price volatility and accounting impacts.

Notable Developments
Net income flipped to a loss of $220.6 million, mainly from a $162.1 million non-cash asset impairment and $77.0 million in unrealized derivative losses.
Cash flows were positive for the 26th straight quarter, but adjusted free cash flow fell to just $0.2 million, weighed down by higher capital spending.
Operating efficiency continued to improve: lease operating expense per barrel of oil equivalent dropped 12% compared to the prior-year quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management guided for modest volume increases and further progress on cost control for the remainder of 2026. It expects that current infrastructure and drilling investments will contribute to higher production and cash flow later this year or in 2027, but stopped short of raising guidance. Investors should watch for signs that these investments lead to a sustained improvement in output and free cash flow, as well as updates on debt reduction efforts.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s reported net loss resulted from non-cash charges tied to a downward revision of asset values and swings in hedging instruments, both linked to lower commodity price averages and market volatility. Excluding those charges, adjusted net income reached $7.4 million, which is down 31% from the same period last year. Underlying cash from operations stayed positive, but the cash flow after investments shrank sharply due to increased spending on projects and the timing of hedge settlements.

Operating efficiency improved further as lease operating expense per barrel of oil equivalent dropped by more than $1.40 year over year, beating management’s own cost guidance for the period. The company also redirected capital into infrastructure that will support longer drilling laterals—an effort aiming to boost future well productivity and reduce development costs. At the same time, production volumes were steady, with oil output increasing 2% and total equivalent production up 5% compared to the prior year’s quarter.

Leverage ticked higher, as management chose to accelerate capital spending rather than pay down additional debt in the quarter. Total liquidity stood at $160 million, while debt rose to $426 million. Despite large hedging losses on paper, approximately 72% of oil and 73% of natural gas production for the year remain price protected, a move intended to support future cash flows even if market prices weaken further.
