Encore Capital Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 7:08 AM

Comments

Encore Capital Group, Inc. (NASDAQ:ECPG) is a global leader in debt purchasing and recovery, primarily operating through its U.S.-based Midland Credit Management and European Cabot segments. The company's growth strategy is centered on acquiring charged-off consumer debt portfolios at attractive prices, leveraging advanced analytics and technology to boost collections efficiency, and maintaining disciplined capital allocation to maximize returns during favorable credit cycles in its core markets.

Potential Bull Cases
1. U.S. Market Leadership Enables Encore to Capitalize on Historically Favorable Credit Cycles

Dominance in the U.S. market positions Encore for sustained growth as credit conditions remain supportive. Recent results show that 87% of Q1 2026 portfolio purchases were in the U.S, where charge-off rates reached decade highs of 4%, and annualized net credit card charge-offs exceeded $54 billion in late 2025. This environment creates abundant, attractively priced supply for Encore to purchase, underpinning record collections of $718 million (+19% year over year) and robust earnings. As the leading buyer in the world's largest consumer debt market, Encore's scale and relationships allow it to access the best deals and weather competition more effectively than smaller peers, supporting its ability to generate high returns across the current and future credit cycles.

2. Technology and Operational Excellence Drive Superior Collections and Profitability

Ongoing investments in technology and process improvements have boosted collections yield and margin expansion. Encore's deployment of advanced analytics, machine learning, and digitized consumer engagement resulted in a record collections yield of 65.2% in Q1 2026 (up 2.6 percentage points year over year), with over 50% of new payments occurring digitally. The company exceeded its forecasted recoveries—global collections were 106% of its Expected Recoverable Collections (ERC) baseline. Flat headcount over three years, despite 39% growth in collections, showcases real productivity gains, allowing earnings per share (EPS) to double year over year (GAAP EPS of $3.86 in Q1 2026 vs. $1.93 in Q1 2025). These operational advantages may continue to generate excess cash flow and outperformance relative to competitors.

3. Strong Balance Sheet and Successful Debt Refinancing Extend Growth Runway

Effective capital management reduces risk and provides significant liquidity for opportunistic purchasing. In May 2026, Encore extended major debt maturities by issuing $750 million in new 6.625% senior notes due 2031, €325 million in European floating rate notes due 2033, and $550 million in senior secured notes due 2032. Net leverage improved to 2.3x at Q1 2026 (down from 2.6x a year prior), staying comfortably within the target 2-3x range. There are no significant maturities until 2028, giving Encore ample flexibility to capitalize on current market conditions, fund continued portfolio purchases, and maintain stability even if financial conditions tighten.

4. Operating Leverage and Efficiency Gains Translate to Improved Returns on Invested Capital

Substantial operating leverage is flowing through to higher profitability and investment returns. Operating expenses rose only 11% while collections increased 19% year over year in Q1 2026, resulting in improved cash efficiency margins (16.9%, up 2.6 pts) and a trailing twelve-month return on invested capital (ROIC) of 14.6% (up from 8.3% a year ago). As Encore grows collections without increasing headcount or expenses proportionally, incremental revenue increasingly drops to the bottom line. These efficiency gains validate Encore's investment in digital tools and disciplined cost control—and if the company maintains this trajectory, returns for long-term investors could remain well above industry norms.

5. Upwardly Revised Guidance Signals Confidence in Collection Performance and Earnings Power

Raised full-year 2026 guidance highlights management's optimism and visibility into future cash flows. Following Q1 outperformance, Encore now expects global collections to rise 8% to $2.8 billion, portfolio purchases to reach $1.4–1.5 billion, and GAAP EPS to grow 19% to $13 for full-year 2026 (up from prior consensus of $11.87). The ability to raise guidance—and consistently deliver ahead of consensus—demonstrates robust underlying business momentum and suggests Encore is well-positioned to outperform as long as current trends persist.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Heavy U.S. Concentration Exposes Encore to Credit Cycle Downturn Risk

Reliance on the U.S. market creates vulnerability to a reversal in consumer credit conditions. With 87% of portfolio purchases in Q1 2026 coming from the U.S. Encore's fortunes are closely tied to continued high levels of delinquencies and charge-offs. Should U.S. consumer credit health improve—reflected in lower charge-off or delinquency rates—portfolio supply would contract, pricing could rise, and returns on new purchases could be pressured. Given the company's consistent strategic focus on its home market, any moderation in U.S. credit stress could substantially reduce collections, revenues, and margin trajectory, diminishing the fundamental bull case.

2. Regulatory Complexity Around AI and Digital Collections May Hamper Execution

Heightened regulatory scrutiny and evolving rules on artificial intelligence (AI) usage in collections represent a material risk to operational efficiency. Encore is actively deploying digital and AI-driven tools to improve collections and reduce costs, but collections is a uniquely regulated activity. Management notes that using artificial voices and automating consumer engagement faces nuanced and evolving regulatory risk in both the U.S. and Europe. Slower adoption—or forced rollbacks—of digital enhancements due to regulatory enforcement would directly impact Encore's ability to sustain recent productivity gains, potentially eroding its competitiveness and margin profile if rivals are less affected or regulatory barriers persist longer than expected.

3. Collections Outperformance May Be Difficult to Sustain as Technology Matures

There is a risk that technology-driven margin and collections improvements will experience diminishing returns. Encore's recent results reflect the payoff from years of investment in analytics, digital payments, and process automation, which may be driving peak collections efficiency. Management itself acknowledges this is a "constant improvement journey," but also cautions that at some stage, returns from incremental technology spend could taper. If future investments underperform or early vintages prove more anomalous than enduring, Encore could see collections and profitability moderate, leaving current high expectations vulnerable and resulting in possible multiple compression if forward growth slows.
