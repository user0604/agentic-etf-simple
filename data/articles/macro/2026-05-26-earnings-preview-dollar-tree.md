Earnings Preview: Dollar Tree

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 9:08 AM

Comments

Dollar Tree (NASDAQ:DLTR), a major U.S. discount retailer operating more than 9,000 stores, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on Thursday, May 28, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Dollar Tree reported net sales of $5.5 billion, representing 9% year-over-year growth. Comparable store sales rose 5%, driven by a 6.3% increase in average ticket, while traffic declined 1.2%. Gross margin expanded by 150 basis points, and adjusted diluted earnings per share (EPS) increased 21% year over year. The quarter included a notable headwind from severe winter storms in January, which led to widespread store closures. Despite this, Dollar Tree’s results were at the midpoint of guidance for comparable sales and generally met analyst expectations for revenue, with a slight beat on adjusted EPS. However, adjusted EBITDA missed consensus estimates. The company completed the sale of Family Dollar during the year, making Dollar Tree a single-banner retailer focused on its core brand.

Positives
Gross margin expansion outpaced expectations: Gross margin improved by 150 basis points year over year, supported by favorable sales mix, lower freight costs, and better merchandise margins. This improvement is significant because it shows Dollar Tree’s ability to manage costs and improve profitability even in a volatile tariff environment.
Strong growth in household penetration: Dollar Tree U.S. households reached a record 102 million, with 6.5 million net new households added in Q4. This broad-based growth signals the company’s expanded relevance and customer appeal across income levels.
Multi-price strategy drove higher productivity: Multi-price offerings accounted for 16% of total sales in Q4, with about 2,400 additional stores converted during the year. Stores with mature multi-price assortments saw higher sales per square foot and larger basket sizes, supporting the company’s long-term growth plan.
Inventory management improved: Inventory was down 7% versus the prior year, while sales increased 9%. This led to more efficient operations and fresher assortments for customers.
Negatives
Traffic declined during the quarter: Comparable store traffic fell 1.2%, reflecting customer adjustment to recent price changes and disruptions from restickering. Although this decline was less severe than in past pricing cycles, it remains an area to watch.
SG&A expenses deleveraged: Selling, general and administrative expenses as a percentage of revenue increased by 70 basis points, mainly due to higher store payroll, incentive compensation, and one-time restickering costs. This offset some of the gross margin gains and put pressure on operating leverage.
Adjusted EBITDA missed analyst consensus: Despite a beat on adjusted EPS, EBITDA came in below expectations, which may have contributed to a negative initial stock reaction after earnings.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Comparable sales growth and ticket versus traffic mix: Management guided for 3% to 4% comparable sales growth in Q1, with a more balanced contribution expected from both ticket and traffic. Investors should watch for any signs of traffic turning positive after recent declines.
Progress and impact of multi-price rollout: The company continues to expand multi-price assortments across its stores. Look for updates on the percentage of sales from multi-price items and whether these stores continue to outperform legacy formats.
Gross margin sustainability amid cost pressures: Dollar Tree expects gross margin to be roughly flat year over year, with benefits from improved markdown performance offset by higher freight and fuel costs. Watch for commentary on the timing and impact of any tariff changes and fuel price volatility.
Store standards and operational improvements: Management has emphasized improvements in store operations, including staffing, inventory flow, and customer experience. Updates on progress here could indicate whether these efforts are translating into higher sales and profitability.
Capital allocation and share repurchases: Dollar Tree ended Q4 with $1.8 billion remaining under its share repurchase authorization and continued to buy back stock after quarter-end. Investors should look for any changes in capital allocation priorities or repurchase activity in Q1.
