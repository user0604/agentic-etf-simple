United Parks & Resorts (NYSE:PRKS) experienced a setback in its core theme park business as unfavorable weather and weaker international travel led to a dip in visitor numbers and revenue. The operator of SeaWorld and Busch Gardens parks faced steeper operating expenses and margin erosion in the first quarter reported on May 11, 2026 (for the period ended March 31, 2026). Overall, the quarter saw strength in per-guest spending and new attraction launches, but persistent cost pressures weighed on profitability.

Notable Developments
Attendance fell 5.0% to 3.22 million guests, mainly due to poor weather and reduced international visitors.
Total revenue per guest rose 2.1% to a record $86.43, driven by strong in-park spending.
Operating and general expenses increased despite lower attendance, pressuring profit margins and resulting in a larger quarterly loss.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management continues to project improved revenue and profit growth for the full year but acknowledges ongoing headwinds. It highlights strong advanced bookings and pass sales as positive indicators for the busier summer months. Investors should watch how these forward bookings translate into real visitor growth and whether expense controls materialize as promised.

Quarterly Highlights

Even as total attendance slipped below internal targets, in-park spending set a record, with guests spending more on food, merchandise, and experiences during their visits. This offset some of the revenue pressure from lower guest volumes but was not enough to prevent declines in overall revenue and profit.

Rising labor and marketing costs weighed on results, with operating expenses up over 6% from the previous year even as guest headcount fell. Severance and separation-related charges also contributed, reflecting ongoing business optimization efforts.

Advance indicators offered some optimism. Paid pass sales rose 10% in the quarter and group and Discovery Cove bookings were described as outpacing last year. However, cash reserves declined due to capital investment and share repurchases, and free cash flow, while improved, remained negative.

What United Parks & Resorts Does

United Parks & Resorts operates well-known theme and water parks across the United States, including SeaWorld, Busch Gardens, and Sesame Place, offering rides, animal experiences, and family attractions.
