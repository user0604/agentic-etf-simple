Atkore: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 5:08 PM

Comments

Atkore (NYSE:ATKR) manufactures electrical products and solutions primarily for commercial, industrial, data center, and renewable energy applications. The company’s growth strategy centers on sharpening its focus on core electrical infrastructure offerings, divesting non-core assets, optimizing its manufacturing footprint, and capturing secular growth trends in data centers and solar energy. Through operational efficiency and disciplined capital allocation, Atkore aims to deliver sustainable margin expansion and shareholder value.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Portfolio Rationalization and Strategic Focus Strengthen Core Growth and Margins

Atkore’s renewed strategic focus on core electrical infrastructure is set to drive margin expansion and operational resilience. Over the past year, the company has divested several non-core businesses, including its high-density polyethylene (HDPE) operations, the Tectron tube product line, and its Belgian surface protection business. Management disclosed in the Q2 2026 earnings call that, absent the now-divested HDPE unit, the core electrical business would have seen adjusted EBITDA margins roughly 150 basis points higher. This restructuring improves the company’s margin profile, allocates management attention to its highest-return segments, and positions Atkore to capture outsized gains from secular themes such as electrification, renewable energy, and data center expansion. As divestitures reduce exposure to volatile and lower-margin product lines, the business model becomes more predictable, setting a strong foundation for sustainable shareholder value.

2. Secular Demand Tailwinds from Data Centers and Renewable Energy Accelerate Growth

Atkore is directly exposed to high-growth end markets that are resilient even amid macro uncertainty. The company reported double-digit volume growth in data center-related products during the first half of fiscal 2025 and expects this strength to continue through 2026, fueled by rising investments in cloud infrastructure and mega-projects. Solar and utility-scale applications are also driving organic growth, with large-scale utility solar projects boosting specialty conduit and mechanical tube demand. Management highlighted that four of five product categories grew year-to-date, led by areas tied to these structural themes. The combination of industry tailwinds and Atkore’s expanded portfolio positions the company to outpace traditional construction peers and deliver mid-single-digit organic volume growth, as reaffirmed in forward guidance.

3. Operational Efficiency Initiatives Are Delivering Tangible Cost and Productivity Gains

A relentless focus on manufacturing excellence is translating to real cost savings and improved competitiveness. Atkore reported ongoing productivity improvements for both Q1 and Q2 of fiscal 2026, driven by plant rationalizations and sophisticated application of its “Atkore Business System.” The company is on track to achieve $10 million–$12 million in annualized cost savings from recent facility closures, possibly exceeding the initial target. Management stated that execution was timely and at an overall cost below earlier estimates. These self-help actions, including the 80/20 initiative to pivot capacity to higher-margin products, underpin margin expansion even in a challenging input cost environment and position Atkore to flexibly weather market volatility.

4. Strong Balance Sheet and Cash Generation Enable Strategic Optionality

Atkore’s conservative financial policies provide the company with greater strategic flexibility amid ongoing industry consolidation. As of the second quarter of 2026, the company had no major debt repayments due before 2030, and operating cash flow for the quarter was $19 million despite a $46 million timing delay in customer receipts, which was resolved after quarter-end. Atkore has maintained a steady quarterly dividend of $0.33 per share, representing a yield of roughly 1.8%, even after absorbing a $136.5 million litigation payout. This discipline signals management’s confidence in consistent cash generation, allowing Atkore to evaluate further growth investments, strategic acquisitions, or returns of capital without liquidity constraints.

5. Management’s Confident Guidance Reflects Underlying Business Strength

Reaffirmation of 2026 guidance despite major one-time charges highlights the resilience of Atkore’s operations. The company absorbed a substantial litigation-related charge in Q2 2026 but still maintained its full-year adjusted EPS outlook of $5.05–$5.55, indicating that normalized earnings power remains intact. Non-GAAP adjusted EPS for the quarter ($1.23) beat consensus estimates by $0.23, and management expects sequential improvement in sales, EBITDA, and earnings per share in both Q3 and Q4. This visibility is supported by robust customer backlogs and optimism among distributors for the remainder of the year.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Ongoing Legal and Regulatory Risks Could Pressure Earnings and Distract Management

Significant litigation liabilities present an unpredictable overhang that could disrupt Atkore’s financial trajectory. In Q2 2026, the company recorded a $136.5 million pre-tax charge to settle two out of three classes in a PVC pipe antitrust suit and is still addressing remaining legal exposures. While management has maintained full-year guidance, at least one law firm has initiated a shareholder investigation tied to disclosure and governance questions arising from this litigation. The potential for further regulatory costs or adverse court outcomes could depress future earnings, consume management bandwidth, and constrain capital returns, especially if similar issues recur.

2. Commodity Input Volatility Remains a Persistent Threat to Margins

A pronounced sensitivity to steel, copper, aluminum, and PVC resin costs challenges Atkore’s ability to sustain profit margins. During the second quarter of 2026, the company faced rising costs for key commodities — especially steel and copper — which led to spread compression in cable businesses that account for approximately 17% of sales. While Atkore has raised prices to offset some input inflation, margin pressure from commodity swings can be acute and subject to timing lags. Persistent volatility in these markets, or an inability to pass through cost increases rapidly, could undermine adjusted EBITDA and challenge the reliability of margin forecasts.

3. Strategic Review Could Lead to Uncertainty or Suboptimal Outcomes

Management’s ongoing strategic review introduces potential risks if portfolio changes fail to create long-term value. Atkore’s board has established a committee to actively evaluate further strategic alternatives — including asset sales and facility closures — and has not committed to a timeline for resolution. Industry peers have seen mixed outcomes from similar processes, particularly if asset divestitures disrupt continuity or if the company erodes scale advantages in its core business. Extended uncertainty from this review, or pressure from shareholder activists, could distract leadership or prompt decisions that deliver near-term optics at the expense of strategic durability, potentially weighing on valuation multiples and investor sentiment.
