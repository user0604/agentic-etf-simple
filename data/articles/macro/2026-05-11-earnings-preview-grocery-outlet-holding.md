Earnings Preview: Grocery Outlet Holding

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 3:03 PM

Comments

Grocery Outlet Holding (NASDAQ:GO), a discount grocery retailer operating primarily through independently run stores, is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings after the market closes on Wednesday, May 13, 2026.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

The company’s fourth quarter, which ended Jan. 3, 2026, saw net sales rise 10.7% to $1.22 billion, benefiting from an extra week in the fiscal year. Excluding this extra week, net sales grew 3.2%. Comparable store sales declined 0.8%, with a drop in average transaction size partly offset by increased customer traffic. Adjusted net income for the quarter was $18.7 million, or $0.19 per share, while adjusted EBITDA reached $68 million. These results missed analyst estimates for both revenue and earnings. The company reported a net loss of $218.2 million, largely due to non-cash impairment charges related to underperforming store closures and goodwill impairment. Management described the quarter’s performance as unacceptable and outlined several corrective actions, including a plan to close 36 underperforming stores and increased promotional investments to restore value perception among customers.

Positives
Net sales growth supported by new stores and extra week: Sales increased year over year, driven by store openings and the 53rd week, even though comparable sales fell.
Positive customer traffic trend: The number of transactions rose, indicating continued shopper interest despite basket pressure.
Improved cash flow from operations: Operating cash flow for fiscal 2025 more than doubled to $222.1 million, reflecting better inventory management and working capital improvements.
Early signs of turnaround from promotional investments: Management noted a 100 basis point improvement in comparable sales in February 2026 versus January, suggesting that recent promotional activity is resonating with customers.
Negatives
Missed analyst estimates for revenue and earnings: Both top and bottom line fell short of consensus expectations, contributing to a sharp share price decline after results.
Declining comparable store sales and basket size: Comparable sales dropped due to a lower average transaction size, reflecting challenges in maintaining the value proposition and product mix.
Significant impairment and restructuring charges: The company recorded $109.8 million in asset impairments and $149 million in goodwill impairment, mainly tied to store closures, resulting in a large net loss for the quarter.
Gross margin pressure from markdowns and promotions: Higher promotional activity and inventory markdowns reduced gross margin, which came in below internal targets.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Comparable store sales trend and basket size: Management guided for a comparable sales decline of 2.5% to 1.5% for the quarter. Investors should watch for any improvement in transaction size or traffic, especially as early promotional efforts showed some positive momentum in February and March.
Gross margin impact from increased promotions and store closures: The company expects gross margin to be pressured by both ongoing promotional investments and inventory liquidation from the 36 store closures. The magnitude and duration of this impact will be key to near-term profitability.
Status and effects of the 36 store closures: The closures, announced and underway, are expected to be completed by the end of Q2 2026. Monitor for progress updates, costs incurred, and any early signs of the expected $12 million annualized EBITDA improvement.
Progress on restoring opportunistic product mix: Management is focused on rebuilding the assortment of high-value, branded deals that drive customer excitement and larger baskets. Look for updates on opportunistic product mix and any related improvements in value perception or sales metrics.
Execution of store refresh program and new store openings: The company aims to refresh 150 stores by year-end and open 30 to 33 net new stores in 2026. Watch for progress on these initiatives, as well as any commentary on the performance of newly refreshed or opened stores, especially in the East where the company is piloting new approaches.
