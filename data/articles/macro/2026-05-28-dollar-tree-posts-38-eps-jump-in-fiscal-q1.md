Key Takeaways
Dollar Tree posted strong earnings growth and improved margins in the first quarter.

Discount retailer Dollar Tree (NASDAQ:DLTR) delivered a solid business win in its latest quarter, reflecting progress on its growth and modernization strategies. The company operates fixed- and multi-price discount stores across the U.S. and Canada. It reported first quarter results for the period ended May 2, 2026 (released May 28, 2026). Overall, the quarter showed meaningful improvement in profitability and cash flow, though store traffic remained a point to watch.

Notable Developments
Revenue reached $5.0 billion, up 7.2% from the prior year, with comparable store sales increasing 3.5%.
Operating income margin improved to 9.5%, supported by a higher average ticket and lower freight costs, though selling, general, and administrative expenses rose as a percentage of sales.
Free cash flow rose to $392 million, enabling $595 million in share repurchases during the quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year adjusted earnings guidance for fiscal 2026 to a range of $6.70 to $7.10 per share, reflecting confidence in continued margin gains and sales growth. Investors should watch for stabilization in store traffic and monitor cost pressures, especially in marketing and liability expenses, to ensure operational efficiency supports future profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter’s revenue growth was driven by a 4.5% increase in average ticket size, while same-store traffic declined by 1.0%. This pattern follows the previous quarter, as customers bought more per visit but visited stores slightly less often.

The rollout of multi-price store formats accelerated, now covering nearly two-thirds of locations. This contributed to larger baskets and a more relevant product mix, helping offset headwinds in customer visits.

Cost management and supply chain improvements led to significant gains in free cash flow, giving the company flexibility for share buybacks and continued investment in modernization, even as selling, general, and administrative expenses as a percentage of sales edged higher compared to the prior year.

What Dollar Tree Does

Dollar Tree operates a network of discount stores in the U.S. and Canada, selling low-cost everyday items and seasonal merchandise, both under fixed-price and multi-price formats.
