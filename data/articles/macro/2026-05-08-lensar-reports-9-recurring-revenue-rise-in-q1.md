Lensar Reports 9% Recurring Revenue Rise in Q1

Key Takeaways
Lensar reported a solid performance in recurring revenue, but new system sales dropped sharply.
Recurring revenue rose 9% year over year, now making up 94% of total revenue.
Management expects a gradual recovery in new system placements, with growth focused on recurring revenue streams.

Laser eye technology company Lensar (NASDAQ:LNSR) delivered a resilient first quarter as its recurring revenue streams expanded, yet new system sales faltered in the wake of a terminated merger. The company, known for the ALLY Robotic Cataract Laser System, continues to emphasize core business stability and technological strength following the end of a potential acquisition by Alcon. The earnings release, dated May 8, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, reflected both progress and persistent challenges as Lensar works to rebuild growth momentum. Overall, the quarter showed recurring strength countered by weak new placements and lingering strategic uncertainty.

Notable Developments
Recurring revenue climbed 9.2% to $12.6 million, now comprising 94% of total revenue as procedure volumes grew 3.3% year over year.
System sales revenue dropped sharply by 68%, and only 7 ALLY systems were placed in the period, signaling continued softness in new placements.
The company reported net income of $36.3 million, driven by one-time gains from the terminated merger and changes in warrant liabilities rather than core operations.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects a gradual recovery in new system placements over the next several quarters, with growth anchored by the strength of recurring revenue and expanding system utilization. The focus is on leveraging the substantial installed base as the main driver of financial performance while distributor relationships rebuild after merger uncertainty. Investors should watch for a turnaround in new system placements, as this will determine the path back to overall top-line growth.

Quarterly Highlights

Recurring revenue topped $12.6 million, making up the highest portion of total revenue in company history, thanks to rising cataract procedure volumes and higher system utilization. The ALLY Robotic Cataract Laser System continued to see market acceptance, but only 7 new systems were installed, with system sales falling considerably compared to the prior year. Net income swung to a positive $36.3 million due to merger termination income and a non-cash accounting gain, while adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) showed a minor loss, underscoring that underlying business performance remains stable but not yet growing on a cash basis.

Lensar maintained its regulatory compliance, with no new product approvals or setbacks reported for the period. The company's installed base grew 12% to 440 systems, but a lower backlog and slow pipeline restart highlight continuing challenges rebuilding post-merger momentum. Inventory increased to $23.1 million, a possible sign of slower system sales or preparation for future placements.

Operating expenses fell sharply compared to the prior year as acquisition charges disappeared, and the company's liquidity position remains steady with $13.5 million in cash, equivalents, and investments. Management stressed the importance of recurring revenues from procedures, leases, and services as the foundation for resilience, but acknowledged that accelerating new placements, particularly internationally, will be needed for meaningful growth.

What Lensar Does

Lensar develops and sells advanced laser systems, primarily for cataract surgery, that use proprietary technology to assist surgeons in improving patient outcomes.
