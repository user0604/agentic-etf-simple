Murphy USA Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 11:11 AM

Comments

Murphy USA Inc. (NYSE:MUSA) is a leading U.S. operator of gas stations and convenience stores, known for its “everyday low price” fuel strategy concentrated near Walmart locations and its expanding QuickChek network in the Northeast. The company’s long-term growth play centers on disciplined network expansion, relentless store-level cost control, robust loyalty programs, and targeted innovation to strengthen profitability and defend market share, especially in volatile fuel markets.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Sustained Fuel Margin Strength and Procurement Flexibility Drive Record Profitability

Murphy USA’s operational leverage in volatile fuel markets has produced exceptional margins and earnings outperformance in 2026, establishing a strong base case for future profitability. In the first quarter of 2026, the company’s earnings per share reached $7.28 on a GAAP basis, beating consensus by an enormous $3.89, while revenue jumped to $4.82 billion—both substantially ahead of expectations. Executive commentary emphasizes the separation of core “street” retail margins from short-term inventory-related windfalls. The company’s flexible sourcing strategy and supply chain discipline leave it well-positioned to benefit from ongoing price volatility and periodic market tightness. Should commodity turbulence persist through year-end, Murphy USA could continue to outperform industry peers on both margin and profit metrics, translating to strong ongoing cash flow generation.

2. Expanding Loyalty Base Fuels Long-Term Volume Growth and Customer Retention

Rapid increases in customer loyalty engagement signal resilience in traffic and support future volume recovery, providing a structural advantage as retail fuel competition intensifies. Management reported the highest monthly new loyalty sign-up total since 2022—600,000 in April—while active membership rose 8.5% year over year in March. Total in-store transactions surged 12%, reflecting sticky customer behavior even amid higher fuel prices. These figures indicate that the Murphy Drive Rewards and QuickChek Rewards programs are attracting both value-conscious new customers and lapsed users, supporting near-term volume stability and forming the groundwork for long-term share gains. As national fuel prices remain above historical norms, this loyalty ecosystem is likely to underpin traffic, defend margins, and provide valuable data for cross-selling and promotional initiatives as consumer behaviors evolve.

3. Robust Merchandise Performance Drives Earnings Diversification and Margin Expansion

Non-fuel merchandise operations have shown consistent strength, diversifying earnings and helping insulate the company from fuel volatility. In Q1 2026, non-nicotine merchandise sales climbed 2% year over year, while in-store margins improved by over 4% at Murphy USA-branded locations. Nicotine categories also remain a key profit driver, bolstered by robust manufacturer promotional support, but it is the resilience in non-nicotine, nondiscretionary offerings and successful margin management that signal a more stable income stream. Store-level innovation—including menu simplification, vendor partnerships, and a sharpened sales-centric culture—create further upside. This merchandising outperformance reduces reliance on cyclical fuel windfalls and positions the company for long-term profit growth regardless of commodity swings.

4. Disciplined Network Expansion and Capital Allocation Support Sustainable EPS Growth

A committed growth pipeline and conservative capital management ensure the company can consistently expand earnings capacity and return capital to shareholders. For 2026, Murphy USA reaffirmed its plan to bring 45 to 55 new-to-industry sites online and continues to invest in store raise/rebuilds. Strong free cash flow, bolstered by recent $500 million senior notes issuance, supports both growth projects and an increased quarterly dividend (now $0.64 per share, up from prior levels). Ongoing share repurchases further enhance per-share metrics. Notably, leverage remains low by sector standards, reducing financial risk while providing strategic flexibility. This disciplined, multi-pronged approach means even if fuel margins revert toward historical averages, the company can drive consistent EPS growth over multi-year horizons.

5. Operational Discipline and Innovation Agenda Enhance Structural Resilience

Well-controlled operating expenses and a pragmatic commitment to innovation make the business more resilient in downturns and better prepared for market evolution. Operating expense per store remained below full-year guidance in Q1 2026, thanks to optimized staffing, shrink reduction, and process innovation at scale. These self-help measures outperformed regional and industry benchmarks and are expected to continue as new stores are opened. Simultaneously, management is incubating new store formats, digital engagement tools, and foodservice offerings, signaling a strategic shift not just to defend legacy models but evolve them. By embedding continuous improvement mindsets and digital sophistication, Murphy USA enhances its ability to weather headwinds, protect margins, and realize new revenue streams in the years ahead.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Margin Windfalls Driven by Commodity Volatility May Rapidly Reverse

Much of the recent earnings surge is attributable to transient fuel market volatility, not structural improvements, making future outperformance questionable if the landscape normalizes. Executive commentary and analyst reaction to Q1 2026 results explicitly caution that outsized margins—especially inventory-driven gains and windfalls from Renewable Identification Numbers credits—can reverse as quickly as they appeared. If Brent and WTI crude prices stabilize or decline, retail margins may compress, eroding earnings as rapidly as they grew. Management declined to update full-year fuel margin guidance, citing “unprecedented” uncertainty, which highlights the risk that 2026’s record EPS is not sustainable, particularly if the macro environment moves against the company in the second half of the year.

2. Regional Competitive Pressures Threaten Volume and Margin Stability

Intensifying competition in key regions such as Colorado and Florida creates risk to both volume retention and margin defense, potentially undermining the company’s core low-price positioning. Management has cited heightened promotional battles and aggressive new store builds by rivals in select markets, forcing the company to deploy heavier discounting—at the expense of profitability—to defend market share. These pressures have already contributed to flat network fuel volumes in April 2026 and may persist or worsen if new entrants are not pushed out quickly. If competition escalates in additional regions, margin management will become more complex and earnings volatility could increase, potentially offsetting gains from loyalty initiatives or OpEx controls.

3. Execution Risk in QuickChek Turnaround and Merchandise Innovation Could Impede Earnings Growth

The ongoing QuickChek integration and reliance on continued promotional support in merchandise carry execution risk that could erode profit if category competitiveness or store turnaround efforts falter. The QuickChek banner, operating in the intensely competitive Northeast quick-service restaurant environment, is currently dragging on same-store sales growth. Although management is taking steps to simplify menus and reorient culture, there is no guarantee these strategies will deliver needed improvements. Concurrently, Murphy USA’s merchandise success is partly contingent on continued strong manufacturer support, especially in higher-margin nicotine categories. Any regulatory changes, reduced promotions, or operational miscues could shrink margins and leave the company exposed just as fuel windfalls dissipate, increasing the risk of sequential earnings declines if not well managed.
