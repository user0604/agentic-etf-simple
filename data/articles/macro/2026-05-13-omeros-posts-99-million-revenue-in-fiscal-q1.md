Key Takeaways
Omeros marked a milestone with its first commercial product launch and initial product revenue.

Management did not provide quantitative financial guidance, but EMA approval and U.S. revenue expansion are central to the outlook.

Omeros (NASDAQ:OMER) posted its first-ever product sales following the U.S. launch of YARTEMLEA, marking a pivotal shift from a development-stage to a commercial biopharma company. The commercial-stage biopharmaceutical firm specializes in therapies for rare and underserved diseases. The report, released May 13, 2026, covered results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, highlighting major operational changes but continued core losses. Overall, the period showed historic progress but ongoing dependence on a single product and lack of profit on an adjusted basis.

Notable Developments
YARTEMLEA generated $9.9 million in net product sales in its first quarter on the market, the company's inaugural commercial revenue.
Non-GAAP adjusted net loss narrowed to $17.1 million from $33.4 million a year ago, as operating expenses declined 22.0% to $27.3 million.
Cash and short-term investments totaled $135.3 million at quarter-end after the company repaid $17.1 million in debt and bought back 0.4 million shares.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide quantitative financial guidance for the year, noting it will wait for additional commercialization data before forecasting. The most important thing for investors is the progress of YARTEMLEA’s ramp—both in U.S. transplant centers and pending approval in Europe. The company targets product-level self-sufficiency in 2026 and overall positive cash flow in 2027, but this has yet to be demonstrated.

Quarterly highlights

The period marked a leap forward as YARTEMLEA, an FDA-approved therapy for a rare complication of stem cell transplantation, produced $9.9 million in net sales during its launch. Revenue far outpaced the prior year, as no product sales were recorded before fiscal 2026. The company also achieved substantial market access milestones, including the assignment of a permanent reimbursement code effective in July 2026, and saw payers grant broad initial coverage.

Operating expenses fell to $27.3 million, down sharply from $35.0 million, reflecting lower research spending following the sale of a pipeline asset to Novo Nordisk in late 2025. The resulting non-GAAP adjusted net loss, though smaller, indicates commercial sales have not yet made the overall business profitable. Notably, a large non-cash derivative gain drove reported GAAP net income higher, but this gain did not stem from commercial or operational activities.

Cash and short-term investments stood at $135.3 million by March 31 after the company paid down debt and repurchased shares. The balance sheet remains solid, aided previously by a large upfront payment from the Novo Nordisk partnership, but ongoing cash use signals a need for either growing product revenue or new milestone payments.

What Omeros does

Omeros develops and commercializes therapies for rare and underserved medical conditions, with an initial commercial focus on immune and endothelial disorders.
