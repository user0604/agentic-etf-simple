D-Wave Quantum Revenue Drops 81% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 7:16 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Record-breaking bookings marked a major commercial win for D-Wave Quantum this quarter.
Revenue declined to $2.9 million, down sharply year over year.
Management anticipates higher revenue in the year’s second half as large contracts convert into sales.

D-Wave Quantum (NYSE:QBTS) delivered a standout performance in new business activity, but recognized sales lagged far behind headline growth. The quantum computing technology firm achieved historic contract bookings but saw a sharp drop in reported revenue and a widening loss. The earnings release, issued on May 12, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, underscores both the opportunity and challenges facing this innovation-driven company. Overall, the quarter reflected strong future demand contrasted with current financial strains.

Notable Developments
Contract bookings soared to $33.4 million, about 20 times more than the prior year, driven by a major university system sale and a Fortune 100 cloud service contract.
Revenue fell by 81% compared to a year ago as most new deals have not yet translated into recognized sales due to multi-quarter implementation timelines.
Operating expenses swelled by 73% (on an adjusted basis), reflecting heavy investment in research, marketing, and integration following the Quantum Circuits acquisition.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide specific forward targets but repeated its expectation for stronger revenue performance in the second half of the year. The backlog of unrecognized contract commitments, known as remaining performance obligations (RPO), rose sharply and will be a key indicator for future realized sales as system installations progress. Investors should watch for signs that these substantial bookings begin converting into recognized revenue in the next two quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Bookings hit an all-time high at $33.4 million, largely due to a $20 million deal with Florida Atlantic University for a quantum computing system and a $10 million two-year cloud contract with a Fortune 100 company. This signals robust demand for D-Wave’s dual-platform offerings, including both quantum hardware and cloud-based services.

Revenue reported in the quarter remained subdued, largely because major new contracts are recognized over time rather than immediately. The sharp decline from a year ago reflects the absence of a large one-time system sale like the one that boosted last year’s result, with the current period showing deferred revenue stacking up for future quarters.

Operating losses widened meaningfully, with adjusted EBITDA loss growing more than fivefold year over year. The rapid increase in spending stemmed from expanding staff in research, sales, and other commercial efforts, plus significant one-time costs tied to integrating the newly acquired Quantum Circuits business.

What D-Wave Quantum Does

D-Wave Quantum builds and operates quantum computers, offering both physical systems and quantum-computing-as-a-service for enterprise and academic customers.
