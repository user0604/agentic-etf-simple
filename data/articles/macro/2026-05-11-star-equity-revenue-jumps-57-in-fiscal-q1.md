Key Takeaways
Star Equity delivered strong revenue growth but faced widening losses and operational hurdles.
Revenue jumped 57.1% from the prior year.
Management expects improved results later in the year but highlights ongoing challenges in profitability and integration.

Star Equity (NASDAQ:STRR) posted robust revenue growth but saw its losses increase as key segments underperformed internal expectations. The diversified holding company, operating across modular construction, business services, energy, and investments, reported its first-quarter results on May 11, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter revealed solid top-line momentum but disappointing profit trends and raised questions about the effectiveness of recent acquisitions and cost management.

Notable Developments
Total revenue surged 57.1% from the prior year, driven by contributions from recent acquisitions, especially in Energy Services and Business Services.
Profitability metrics declined, with non-GAAP EPS falling to a loss of $0.99 and adjusted EBITDA also widening to a $1.6 million loss.
Management cited project delays and macroeconomic factors for weakness in Building Solutions, while Energy Services stood out with improved profitability and new contract wins.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management stated its belief that ongoing investments and operational improvements will drive better results later in the year, though it did not provide specific financial guidance for future quarters. The company pointed to new business opportunities and cost-control efforts but acknowledged that segment-level performance and margin recovery need attention. Investors should watch for evidence of improved profitability and order capture, especially in Building Solutions and Business Services, as well as the realization of merger synergies.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue growth was the standout story for the period, climbing 57.1% year over year due mainly to the impact of the Alliance Drilling Tools acquisition and organic gains in the Business Services division. Energy Services became a clear bright spot, with revenue rising to $3.5 million and adjusted EBITDA doubling compared to pro forma results last year. However, Building Solutions faced declining revenue along with a backlog book-to-bill ratio below 1.0, which raises uncertainty about near-term growth in that segment.

Profit margins did not keep pace with revenue gains. While gross profit rose to $20.6 million, this 25.4% increase lagged well behind revenue growth, and overall gross margin dropped, reflecting higher costs and pricing pressure in several units. Both net and adjusted losses widened, highlighting operational challenges and the need for improved integration of recent acquisitions.

Management reported $2.6 million in annualized merger synergies and continued to reduce some corporate expenses, but overall operating and general expenses offset much of these gains. The company repurchased 70,424 shares for $0.7 million during the quarter while ending with $8.1 million in cash and cash equivalents. A quarterly dividend of $0.25 per share was declared for Series A Preferred stock.

What Star Equity does

Star Equity is a diversified holding company with operations in modular construction, global business services, specialty energy tools, and portfolio investments.
