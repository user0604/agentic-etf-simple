Earnings Preview: Workday

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Workday (NASDAQ:WDAY), a provider of cloud-based software for human resources and financial management, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2027 on Thursday, May 21, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2026 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, Workday reported total revenue of $2.532 billion, representing a 15% increase year over year. Subscription revenue reached $2.360 billion, up 16%. The company’s non-GAAP operating margin was 30.6%, and operating cash flow for the quarter was $1.28 billion. Workday ended the year with $5.4 billion in cash and marketable securities. The company’s gross revenue retention rate remained strong at 97%, reflecting high customer satisfaction and the mission-critical nature of its platform. These results beat analyst consensus estimates for both earnings per share and revenue.

Positives
Revenue and earnings beat estimates: Both total revenue and earnings per share exceeded analyst expectations, indicating solid execution and ongoing customer demand.
Strong customer retention and expansion: The 97% gross revenue retention rate and consistent net expansion rates demonstrate that customers continue to rely on Workday’s solutions, expanding their usage over time.
AI product momentum and integration: Workday delivered over $100 million in new annual contract value from emerging AI products in Q4, with annualized recurring revenue from these solutions now above $400 million. The integration of recent acquisitions like Sana and Paradox was completed quickly, and new AI-powered agents are moving into general availability.
Healthy cash flow and share repurchases: Operating and free cash flow grew year over year, and Workday repurchased $2.9 billion of its shares during the year, signaling confidence in its long-term outlook.
Negatives
Slower net new large enterprise deals: Some large enterprise deals, especially in government and healthcare, experienced longer sales cycles, which affected the volume of new business in Q4. While many of these deals remain active, the delay could impact near-term growth.
Sequential subscription revenue headwind: The Q4 results benefited from a one-time DIA contract, which will not recur in Q1 FY2027. This creates a sequential decline in subscription revenue, making Q1 growth appear softer.
Restructuring and workforce reductions: Workday incurred $303 million in restructuring costs for fiscal 2026 and announced an additional plan in Feb. 2026 to reduce its workforce by about 2% to align resources with strategic priorities.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2027's Report
Subscription revenue growth versus guidance: Management guided for Q1 FY2027 subscription revenue of about $2.335 billion, up 13% year over year, but sequentially lower due to the absence of the DIA contract. Investors should watch how actual results compare to this guidance and analyst consensus.
Pace of AI agent adoption and monetization: Workday is investing heavily in organically developed AI agents, with several now in general availability. The company expects broader adoption and increased revenue contribution, especially in the second half of the year. Progress on customer uptake and early usage metrics will be important.
Impact of Flex Credits consumption model: The new Flex Credits pricing model, which allows customers to pay based on usage, is being adopted by a growing number of customers. Investors should look for updates on how this transition may affect revenue recognition, customer expansion, and long-term growth.
International and mid-market momentum: Workday continues to invest in international expansion and has seen strong results in regions like Canada, EMEA, and Southeast Asia. Performance in these markets, as well as among medium-sized enterprise customers, will be key to sustaining growth.
Progress on restructuring and margin outlook: With restructuring plans announced for fiscal 2027, investors should monitor updates on cost savings, workforce alignment, and any changes to the company’s non-GAAP operating margin targets for the year.
