Moneyball Report Card: Hewlett Packard Enterprise

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 3, 2026, 6:00 PM

Comments

What the Hidden Gems Primary database scores can tell us about HPE.

For most of the last decade, the market has treated Hewlett Packard Enterprise (NYSE: HPE) as a legacy hardware story slowly being managed for decline -- a respectable dividend payer, not a growth company. The Moneyball database tells a more interesting story: a business in the middle of a genuine transformation, with a strategic vision that lines up squarely with where enterprise IT spending is actually heading.

Start with the top line. HPE earns a Superscore of 68, and the growth picture is the reason. Revenue jumped 14% over the trailing year, powered by a 51% surge in the Networking segment following the Juniper Networks acquisition. That deal didn't just pad the numbers; it repositioned HPE at the center of AI-driven networking, exactly the layer of infrastructure that hyperscalers and enterprises are racing to build out. The Growth score of 56 understates the optionality here, because new AI system orders and the GreenLake cloud platform's expanding Annualized Revenue Run-rate are both early stage engines still spinning up.

The technology fundamentals back the strategy. A Tech score of 80 and an AI score of 81 are among the stronger marks in the database, reflecting HPE's deep integration of AI across networking and hybrid cloud such as predictive analytics, intelligent traffic management, and AI-focused server solutions built for large-scale model training. Partnerships with Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) and CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD) plus private-AI deployments show a company co-developing with the market leaders rather than chasing them.

Then there's the other side of the ledger, and the database doesn't soften it. The Financial 1Y score of 57 captures a real tension: Revenue grew, but operating and free cash flow declined, margins compressed, and return on equity slipped as integration costs and non-recurring charges piled up. HPE even posted a GAAP net loss tied to a goodwill impairment. Strategic investment is expensive, and so far, it's showing up as cost before it shows up as profit.

The Governance score of 45 is the sharpest flag. The Juniper acquisition carries ongoing legal and regulatory scrutiny, the accounting involves highly subjective judgment, and executive compensation structures raise legitimate questions about alignment with shareholder interests. For a company asking investors to underwrite a multiyear transformation, those are not footnotes, they're material risks worth pricing in.

So the central question is whether the market's tired view of HPE has been overtaken by the evidence. The transformation is real, the AI and networking positioning is genuine, and the strategic logic holds. What's unproven is execution -- specifically, whether leadership can convert all this top-line momentum into durable profitability and clean up the governance overhang along the way. The vision is no longer the hard part. The follow-through is.
