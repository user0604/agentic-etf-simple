Kraken Robotics Surges on AUKUS Validation

When three navies agree to share standards, the company whose gear is already the standard tends to help write them.

Nicholas Sciple

Jun 1, 2026, 3:28 PM

Comments

Kraken Robotics (TSXV: PNG) shares are up more than 10% today on thesis-validating news announced over the weekend in Singapore.

On May 30, the defense chiefs of Australia, the UK, and the U.S. stood up in Singapore and named the first signature project under Pillar 2 of AUKUS, a trilateral naval partnership between the three countries. Out of everything the three governments could have led with, they chose payloads and enabling systems for uncrewed undersea vehicles. The official language defines payloads as the sensors and navigation a vehicle carries, and enabling systems as the kit that lets one country's gear talk to another's. First deliveries start in 2027, and the UK alone has committed 150 million pounds.

Now look at the very first purpose AUKUS named for the project: protecting critical national seabed infrastructure. That is exactly where we started with our Kraken thesis. Our November 2025 Motley Fool National Security recommendation of Kraken Robotics built the case around a wave of undersea sabotage, the Nord Stream pipeline, Chinese vessels cutting cables off Taiwan, and suspected Russian activity in the Baltic, with the reminder that more than 90% of global trade moves by sea and the world's data and energy flow through undersea cables and pipelines. The whole thesis was that protecting that infrastructure would drive a UUV buildout. Three governments just made that mission their number one trilateral priority, with money attached.

The March add to Kraken deepened the case. Kraken brings sonar and batteries, the March acquisition of Covelya brings navigation, positioning ,and underwater communications, and together they become the one company that can sell naval integrators the whole mission-critical stack inside an undersea robot. Sensors. Navigation. Communications. Control standards. That is the AUKUS payload definition almost word for word. They did not fund the submarines. They did not even fund the drones. They funded the stuff inside the drones. The stuff Kraken and Covelya make.

That said, let's not get ahead of ourselves. This is not a contract for Kraken. Nobody named the company on May 30. The 150 million pounds is a national pot spread across many suppliers, and part of it buys weapons Kraken does not make. What changed is that three governments told the market the layer Kraken owns is the layer they intend to scale. And the order book was already moving before the announcement: Kraken product orders for 2026 now sit around 97 million, with another 165 million booked across Covelya.

The risks remain. The Covelya deal still has to close and could draw a UK security review. Integration is hard. Revenue is lumpy. And after a run like this, plenty of optimism is in the price.

But the conviction holds. Naval gear has to be qualified, and requalifying a supplier costs years program managers will not spend. Kraken's batteries are rated to 6,000 meters with no Chinese materials. And when three navies agree to share standards, the company whose gear is already the standard tends to help write them.

The November call was that protecting critical undersea infrastructure would power the UUV wave. AUKUS just put that mission first.
