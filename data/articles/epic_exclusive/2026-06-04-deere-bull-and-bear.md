Deere Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

7:07 AM

Comments

Deere & Company (NYSE:DE) is a global leader in agricultural, construction, and forestry equipment, as well as digital and financial solutions for land-based industries. The company’s growth strategy centers on advancing precision agriculture and automation, expanding connected services and recurring revenue, and leveraging its diversified product and geographic mix to manage cyclical swings. Deere invests heavily in R&D to maintain its competitive edge in smart equipment and digital platforms while navigating the shifting landscape of global agriculture and infrastructure demand.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Construction Segment Strength Offsets Downturn in Agriculture

Sustained construction demand is mitigating the impact of the agricultural slump. Deere’s Construction & Forestry segment posted a 29% year-over-year revenue increase in Q2 FY2026, outperforming cyclical norms due to robust infrastructure spending, warehousing, and a surge in data center construction. Over 80% of the segment’s annual production slots are filled, and the U.S. order book is up 60% since late 2025, the highest since April 2024. This provides a steady revenue base as agricultural markets bottom out, helping the company maintain enterprise-wide growth and margin stability even when its core farming customers are cautious about new equipment purchases.

2. Technology Leadership Enables Recurring Revenue Expansion

Deere’s aggressive smart ag investments are laying the foundation for higher-margin, recurring software and service income. The company continues to scale adoption of its Operations Center SaaS platform, precision tools like See & Spray (resulting in 50–60% herbicide savings), and connectivity partnerships such as the Starlink agreement. Deere reported a 10% year-over-year growth in engaged acres and a 25% increase in its connected fleet with over 12,500 JDLink kits sold in a single quarter. With renewal rates above 90% for digital customers in their second year of renewal, the company is positioning for recurring revenue to comprise at least 10% of sales by 2030. This digital transition promises higher, more resilient margins and greater customer lock-in over future cycles.

3. Inventory Normalization Sets the Stage for an Ag Replacement Cycle Recovery

Channel discipline and aging fleets suggest pent-up demand when cycle trough reverses. Deere reduced new high-horsepower tractor and combine inventories by over 50% from mid-2024 peaks, and late-model used inventories are down by 45% year over year. Dealer destocking and tight production planning have restored healthier inventory balance throughout North America and key global regions. As large ag fleets continue to age, machine hours accrue, and used inventories stabilize, the probability of a meaningful replacement demand surge in 2027 and beyond increases. This positions Deere to capture a significant volume uptick as macro conditions improve.

4. Margin Discipline and Cost Control Anchor Profitability Through the Cycle

Operational rigor is sustaining double-digit margins even during cyclical lows. Despite a sharp decline in large ag volumes, Deere delivered sector-leading equipment operating margins in Q2 FY2026: over 16.9% for total equipment operations, 20.6% in Small Ag & Turf, and 14.8% in Construction & Forestry. One-off tariff refunds boosted short-term figures, but underlying progress comes from focused price realization—1.5–2% price lift projected in FY2026. While tariffs remain a 3-point margin headwind, disciplined sourcing, freight cost mitigation, and an emphasis on high-value products are helping to structurally improve margins compared to prior sector troughs. These improvements make the company more resilient and enable record R&D investment even at the bottom of the ag cycle.

5. Diversification Across Segments and Markets Reduces Earnings Volatility

Business mix and global reach provide a buffer against regional/cyclical shocks. In Q2 FY2026, gains in Construction & Forestry and Small Ag & Turf absorbed much of the revenue loss from Production & Precision Ag, driving consolidated sales up 5% year over year to $13.37 billion (GAAP). While North American agriculture plateaued and South America remained volatile, share growth and double-digit segment margins were achieved even in pressured markets like Brazil. The diversified engine—combined with a rising contribution from financial services and digital—allows management to reaffirm FY2026 net income guidance of $4.5–5.0 billion despite uneven end-markets, supporting long-term growth ambitions through 2030 and beyond.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Weakness in Large Agriculture Could Delay Recovery and Depress Earnings

Persistent downturns in large ag demand may undermine mid-term growth and profitability. Large farm equipment sales remain under acute pressure, with Deere's Production & Precision Ag segment seeing a 14% sales decline year over year in Q2 FY2026. Lower commodity prices, high input costs, and elevated interest rates have squeezed farmer margins, especially in North America and Brazil. The channel is still working through dealer destocking, and management explicitly frames 2026 as only the “bottom” of the current cycle—leaving open the risk that replacement demand or commodity market recovery could be slower or weaker than expected, dragging on earnings for several years and impairing operating leverage as long as the agricultural malaise persists.

2. Right-to-Repair Lawsuits and Customer Pushback Threaten Deere’s Digital Strategy

Litigation, regulatory scrutiny, and reputational risks may constrain Deere’s digital recurring revenue ambitions. The company is facing multiple class action lawsuits over its repair practices, accused of restricting access to software and forcing customers to use high-priced dealer services. Farmers continue to express demand for basic, repairable equipment, fueling a boom in the “no-tech” secondary market and inviting new competitors. If Deere is forced by courts or legislators to open its software or significantly improve repair access, it could lose aftermarket revenue, weaken its competitive moat, or see digital adoption slow. This would undermine the high-margin, SaaS-driven growth strategy—and the related improvement in long-term profitability the company is targeting.

3. Competitive Pressure From Simpler, Lower-Cost Entrants Could Erode Market Share and Margins

Emerging rivals focused on affordable, user-repairable equipment may disrupt Deere’s traditional dominance. As farmers resist expensive, tech-heavy models and flock to simpler options, competitors—both established players and new entrants—are introducing low-cost, repairable tractors aimed at cost-conscious buyers. This trend is especially pronounced in North America, where the secondary market for older John Deere machines is booming. If these shifts gain traction, Deere may be forced to lower prices or offer less profitable configurations, risking a structural decline in market share and segment margins at a time when operational leverage and scale are central to the company’s strategic plan.
