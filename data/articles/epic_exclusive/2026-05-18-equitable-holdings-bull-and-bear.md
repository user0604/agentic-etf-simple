Equitable Holdings Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 7:12 AM

Comments

Equitable Holdings, Inc. (NYSE:EQH) is a leading U.S. financial services company offering retirement, wealth management, and asset management solutions through a unique integrated model. With more than $1.1 trillion in assets under management as of Q1 2026, the company is poised for transformational growth by acquiring Corebridge, which is expected to dramatically increase scale, distribution, and capital efficiency while targeting industry-leading profit growth through 2027 and beyond.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Corebridge Merger Creates an Industry Powerhouse With Accelerated Synergy Realization

The combination with Corebridge will create a dominant platform spanning retirement, wealth, and asset management in the U.S. The planned merger, advancing through integration with a new leadership team, will raise assets under management and administration to $1.5 trillion and expand the customer base to over 12 million. Management has explicitly targeted $500 million in expense synergies (6–8% of combined costs) and expects at least $100 billion of new asset flows into AllianceBernstein over several years, reinforcing non-GAAP earnings accretion above 10% by year-end 2028. This industry-defining deal vastly improves competitive positioning, supports a lower average cost of funds, and enables broad product and distribution expansion that competitors with narrower business lines cannot match, potentially driving superior profitability and valuation multiples relative to peers.

2. Sustained Double-Digit Earnings and Cash Generation Through 2027 Underpins Capital Return

Equitable’s consistent operating execution has translated into earnings and free cash flow strength, ensuring ample capital for shareholder returns and reinvestment. For the first quarter of 2026, the company reported non-GAAP operating earnings per share of $1.62 (up 25% year over year) and $1.1 trillion in assets under management, confirming robust underlying momentum. Management reaffirmed guidance to grow EPS above 12–15% compounded annually through 2027, with holding company cash generation targeted at $1.8 billion for 2026, rising to $4 billion annually post-merger. A payout ratio commitment of 60–70%, supported by substantial buybacks—$147 million in Q1 alone—gives investors a clear line of sight to continued dividend growth and opportunistic share repurchases, even as the company navigates large-scale integration and industry change.

3. Diversified Growth Engines Drive Organic Expansion and Margin Resilience

Multiple business lines—retirement, wealth management, and asset management—deliver diversified income streams and reduce cyclical risk. In Q1 2026, the retirement segment saw total sales rise 10% year over year, with registered index-linked annuity (RILA) sales up 14%, while wealth management net inflows reached $2 billion (a 13% annualized organic growth rate). AllianceBernstein delivered an 11% increase in operating earnings even amidst asset outflows in active equities and fixed income, and its private markets business achieved a 13% AUM increase to $85 billion. This breadth and scale enable the company to capture a larger share of industry flows, maintain pricing discipline, and generate attractive 15%+ internal rates of return on new business, while facilitating stable, high-margin growth across market cycles.

4. Strong Capital Position and Risk Management Inspire Confidence Through Market Cycles

A robust capital base and conservative investment approach protect the company against extreme market shocks and regulatory change. At the end of Q1 2026, Equitable reported a 475% National Association of Insurance Commissioners risk-based capital ratio—well above its 400% target—and $1.2 billion of holding company liquidity, comfortably exceeding the $500 million minimum. Updated stress tests indicate the company could weather a crisis more severe than the 2008 downturn and still maintain capital strength. Liability-driven investing, 95% investment-grade private credit exposure, and strategic reinsurance transactions further reduce volatility, while full hedging of equity and interest rate risks ensures earnings and statutory capital remain predictable. This enables management to focus on strategic growth and accretive M&A without compromising financial flexibility.

5. Product Innovation and Distribution Expansion Unlock Future Market Share Gains

Ongoing investments in product development and a rapidly expanding advisor and third-party network support the company’s long-term growth strategy. The company’s commitment to innovation is evident in its market leadership in RILAs, growing third-party and proprietary advisor distribution, and recent acquisition of Stifel Independent Advisors. Post-merger, the combined firm will have leading positions in fixed and indexed annuities and will expand its product offering to include solutions like indexed universal life, broadening client reach and deepening relationships. The ability to offer a comprehensive suite of retirement, insurance, and wealth management products through a wide and growing distribution network positions the company to broaden its partnerships with advisors and institutions as demographic tailwinds and regulatory evolution expand the U.S. retirement market.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Corebridge Integration Risk Could Delay or Dilute Expected Synergies

Integrating two large organizations brings execution and cultural risk that could limit near-term financial benefits from the merger. While management has highlighted $500 million in targeted expense savings and meaningful revenue synergies, achieving these goals requires operational and technology integration on an unprecedented scale. Missed milestones or unforeseen disruptions could dampen expected earnings accretion, particularly if prolonged or costly. Legal inquiries from shareholder law firms, typical in large deals, underscore transaction risk. Any delays or underperformance during integration could weigh on forecasted double-digit EPS growth and create uncertainty for investors tracking the multi-year transformation story.

2. Asset Management Headwinds and Market Volatility May Pressure Fee Income and Flows

Persistent asset outflows and market turbulence pose risks to growth in the asset management segment, especially amid shifting investor preferences and margin compression. In the first quarter of 2026, AllianceBernstein reported $7.1 billion in net outflows from active equities and taxable fixed income, despite overall AUM growth driven by private markets. Ongoing rotation out of higher-fee products or adverse capital market conditions could compress average fee rates and reduce the positive impact of anticipated $100 billion in post-merger asset flows. Market-wide declines or heightened volatility would not only impact asset management earnings but could also affect wealth management flows, testing the company's resilience just as it integrates major acquisitions.

3. Lower-Than-Expected Returns From Alternatives and Changes in Interest Rates Could Dampen Earnings

Alternatives income and net interest margin stabilization are crucial to maintaining segment earnings, but both face downside risks if macro conditions deteriorate. For Q1 2026, returns on alternative investments fell to 3.5% annualized, prompting management to lower full-year guidance below the previous 8–9% range. Higher interest rates support spread income and new business IRRs, but sudden declines in rates or credit event-driven spread pressure could erode margins in both the retirement and wealth management lines. While capital discipline and risk mitigation are strong, sustained underperformance in yield or asset returns could hamper the company’s ability to hit its high-end growth and payout targets.
