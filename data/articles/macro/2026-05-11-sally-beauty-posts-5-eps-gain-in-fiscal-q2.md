Sally Beauty Posts 5% EPS Gain in Fiscal Q2

Key Takeaways
Sally Beauty delivered stable results, reaching the top end of its guidance with strong cash flow.
Adjusted diluted earnings per share rose 5% year over year.
Management maintained its full-year outlook, signaling measured confidence amid ongoing retail pressures.

Sally Beauty (NYSE:SBH) reported steady sales growth and improved cash flow in its latest quarter, driven by digital expansion and solid performance in its main U.S. segment. The specialty beauty retailer serves professional stylists and do-it-yourself consumers through a large store network and e-commerce platforms. For its fiscal second quarter ended March 31, 2026 (announced May 11, 2026), the company met or slightly exceeded its own guidance for sales, profit, and comparable sales. Overall, the period showed consistent progress, with growth in digital sales and resilient profit margins, even as some business segments and cost controls produced mixed results.

Notable developments
Revenue reached $903.4 million, at the high end of management’s range, while comparable sales turned positive after a decline in the prior year.
E-commerce sales increased 13% to $108 million, now making up about 12% of total sales, reflecting ongoing digital growth.
Free cash flow rose by $12 million year over year, and the company’s total store count declined by 47 locations due to continued rationalization.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management narrowed its full-year sales outlook to $3.725–$3.75 billion, keeping other guidance unchanged. Leaders remain confident in delivering consistent, profitable growth through the second half of fiscal 2026. Investors should monitor expense control and the performance of the Beauty Systems Group segment.

Quarterly highlights

The main U.S. and Canada retail segment outperformed with 4.1% sales growth, while the Beauty Systems Group segment saw flat sales. The U.S. and Canada division posted a 2.5% increase in comparable sales.

The Beauty Systems Group segment, which serves salons and stylists, reported a small sales decline and negative comparable sales growth, although its operating margin improved. The number of business consultants dropped, and total store count continued to contract, highlighting ongoing challenges in this area.

Company-wide e-commerce penetration reached 12%, with global digital sales growing faster than in-store figures. Cash flow strengthened, supporting $25 million in share repurchases, but total operating margin remained under modest pressure due to higher selling and administrative costs.
