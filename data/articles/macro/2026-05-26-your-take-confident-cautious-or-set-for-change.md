Your Take: Confident, Cautious, or Set for Change?

Members weigh in on how they feel about their portfolio allocation now.

In today's Breakfast News, we asked, "How are you feeling about your current portfolio allocation as we end the month? Confident, cautious, or ready to make changes?" Members had a variety of opinions -- here are some highlights!

S

StanleyS

To put the following in context, we are a few years into retirement, we're pretty well off, but we're concerned about sequence risk and longevity risk.

We are highly confident that

We are well diversified
We have at least some investments that will hold up, if not do well, regardless of economic regime
Our financial needs for the next few years are covered (minimizing sequence risk)
We will beat S&P in any significant downturn We will make still make some money when the markets do well

We are fairly confident that

We should be fine financially if our investments can beat inflation
Our overall approach to asset allocation and investment selection is good enough (satisficing)
The current investments are more or less appropriate to the current times (markets, economies, etc.)
We'll make enough money to get us through retirement (avoiding longevity risk)
We might muck things up by trying to be fix what may not be broken

We are not confident about

What will happen in the world
What will happen in the markets
How our investments will actually perform

We are not particularly cautious but are prudent and alert; we are not looking to change but remain ready to respond to changing circumstances as may be appropriate.

T

TMFPuraVida

The market's intense focus on AI is pushing several stocks in my portfolio to new highs. It's exciting, but I'm also approaching this run with caution, having seen similar cycles play out before. I have strong conviction in AI's long-term impact, yet markets can become disconnected from reality during periods of euphoria.

I'm not planning to sell any of my AI holdings, even though I recognize the downside risk. However, given that AI already represents a meaningful portion of my portfolio, I've largely stopped allocating new capital to the highest-flying names over the past six to nine months. Instead, I'm directing fresh capital toward other areas of the market.

explodingrobots

That's good to hear. I'm generally doing the same. I too tend to let my high flying AI stocks go over 100% return before I add to them, in case any of them drop 50%, I haven't lost my principal. But, I'm a novice.

beatSPdreamer

Cautious. While the AI buildout seems unstoppable, and I don't want to miss out, there are multiple other concerns- high valuations, higher inflation and rates, low sentiment, continued uncertainty, etc. I think the most concerning to me, and it has been for years, is the debt- debt-gdp ratio at highest level since post-WWII. The market seems to look past most of these concerns. I am planning to de-risk my portfolio a little.
