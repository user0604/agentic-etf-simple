Dear Fools,

Earlier this week, the Strada Education Foundation released a survey that suggests adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) tools is likely to increase entry-level hiring in many organizations, rather than reduce it. This isn't the apocalyptic narrative we've been hearing about.

News reports about college students booing commencement speakers who talk positively about AI, along with unemployment concerns, may have you thinking AI is going to take all our jobs. Might AI lead to more jobs and more prosperity, instead? Perhaps a dystopian world with a permanent underclass is not inevitable, after all.

We prefer to take the optimistic view when we can. And in this case, we think optimism is justified. That's the way tech advances tend to go, historically. The Industrial Revolution brought more jobs and a higher standard of living. The rise of the internet created entire industries that didn't exist before, such as cybersecurity, which was a $200 billion market in 2025. We expect AI to follow a similar path.

Of course, it will be disruptive, and there will be bumps along the road. People and businesses will need to adapt. We believe AI can be a net positive in the world, though, and we're hopeful that many of the companies in the Epic Portfolio will be ones that use AI to improve our lives, rewarding their investors as they do so.

Stocks in Focus: Amazon and DoorDash

Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) recently announced it was expanding its Amazon Now service to dozens of cities across the U.S., aiming to reach tens of millions more customers by the end of the year. Amazon Now delivers things like groceries and household essentials to customers' homes in 30 minutes or less and complements other delivery options, such as same-day delivery and one-hour or three-hour delivery. This Amazon news has us considering the implications for other companies, including another Epic Portfolio investment, DoorDash (NASDAQ: DASH).

We think DoorDash management should be concerned about Amazon's plans in the sense that all competent management teams should always be concerned about competitive threats. "Only the paranoid survive," after all, as Intel (NASDAQ: INTC) founder Andy Grove notes. We don't think the threat to DoorDash is existential, though, at least not yet. Amazon has shown no interest, so far, in attacking DoorDash's bread-and-butter business: restaurant delivery. Not to say that it won't, eventually, but that's not the immediate concern.

The bigger issue for DoorDash is its efforts to expand beyond restaurant delivery to other types of deliveries, including grocery. When DoorDash started its push into grocery delivery, there were already other competitors in that space, including Instacart (MapleBear) (NASDAQ: CART). Amazon makes the space more crowded and brings one of the best logistics networks on the planet to the table as they step in to compete, but competition here isn't new to DoorDash. Instacart probably has more to worry about, since Amazon is hitting its core focus more directly.

While Amazon's new grocery push isn't great news for DoorDash, it's exactly what we want to see from Amazon. Amazon has spent years investing in its logistics operation. Last year, it announced a $4 billion effort to expand its rural delivery network, for instance. Now, the company is turning up the heat on competitors and turning these investments into new revenue opportunities, like grocery delivery, Amazon Now expansion, and even opening its logistics network to third parties (for a fee, of course). These business expansions are one reason we recently added to our Amazon position.

This situation also reinforces why we diversify across 50+ positions in the Epic Portfolio. Would we love to see a situation where both Amazon and DoorDash win? Of course. But in a scenario where one might win at the expense of the other, well, we're glad we at least have the winner in our portfolio, too.

Member Mailbag

Epic Portfolio member abmalmulla asks a very thought-provoking question this week, a variation of which is likely on the minds of many members. You can (and should) read the full post on our community boards, but to summarize, the core question they ask is, "What is the best way to use the Epic Portfolio for someone who already has an existing portfolio, and may not be able to buy all 50+ stocks due to personal restrictions?"

This is a great question. Most of us coming to the Epic Portfolio probably already have a portfolio of some sort. Further, many of us may face constraints that prevent us from buying all the recommended companies, whether due to employer restrictions, religious or ethical concerns, availability at our broker, or personal preference.

First, let's address the issue of constraints. We intentionally set up the Epic Portfolio with 50 stocks (with room to grow over time) to create a diverse portfolio that would be a buffer against both market volatility and emotional turmoil. But you don't need exactly 50 stocks to get those benefits. Forty-five probably does the job. Or 55. So, if you skip one, or two, or more, that's fine. Don't worry too much about it.

Focus on building a portfolio with enough companies to provide you with the buffer you need to stay invested in the market in times of volatility to produce market-beating returns over decades. If you find yourself needing to pass on multiple recommendations, we'd encourage you to fill some of the gaps with alternatives you're comfortable with.

That may be existing recommendations from other Fool services you already own, or an exchange-traded fund (ETF) you're comfortable with. Ultimately, the goal is to create a portfolio you are comfortable with and that delivers the results you are seeking.

Regarding transitioning an existing portfolio, don't feel you need to rush. We are just getting started with the Epic Portfolio, and we're in it for the long haul. It's perfectly fine to take it slow, learn about the companies, and buy in stages as it makes sense for you.

Some may be adding new money over time and want to buy as that money comes in. Some may have a large chunk of cash ready to go. Others may have existing positions they'd prefer to sell down slowly to transition into Epic Portfolio recommendations. We encourage everyone to embrace the Epic Portfolio and make it their own. If that means easing into it over time, that's fine.

Finally, we'd encourage everyone to head on over to our community, offer your own thoughts on abmalmulla's question, and ask questions of your own. We're here to learn and invest together. Asking and answering questions together is a great way to do that.
