Weekly News: Snowflake Burns Bright as Markets Surge

1. Investors Warm to Snowflake

Snowflake (NYSE: SNOW) has been caught up in the artificial intelligence (AI) debate, with investors questioning whether the emerging tech will make the company's business more potent or eat into demand. The company's results, including a 34% revenue boost in its fiscal first quarter, were well received by the market, sending the shares up 48% for the week. Product revenue grew faster than expectations, and management raised its full-year guidance due to accelerating demand from large enterprises. Snowflake also announced an expanded strategic collaboration agreement with Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) Web Services, committing $6 billion over five years to AWS infrastructure and Graviton chips to accelerate enterprise adoption of agentic and generative AI applications.

"CoCo had the largest driver to the increase in our forecast." Management said its AI product, Cortex Code (or CoCo), was responsible for the beat. More than 7,100 accounts are actively using CoCo, which only became generally available in February.
"The bull case here isn't just that AI is a tailwind. It's that AI is eating Snowflake's own cost structure simultaneously." Sanmeet Deo notes that Snowflake generated that growth while hiring just 17 people in the quarter, a clear sign of efficiency. He's cautiously optimistic but wants to see more of the same in the quarters to come.
2. Micron Crosses a Trillion

Memory chipmaker Micron Technology (NASDAQ: MU), which was valued at less than $350 billion at the start of the year, crossed the $1 trillion market cap threshold this week. The stock was up 29% for the week following analyst commentary on sustainable demand from AI hyperscalers. Micron's jump is the fastest ever from $500 billion to $1 trillion, with the stock needing just 48 trading days to double. That broke Tesla's (NASDAQ: TSLA) record of needing just 230 days between the two milestones.

"These are not normal times." On Motley Fool Hidden Gem Investing, Travis Hoium, Rachel Warren, and Lou Whiteman expressed some concerns about the sustainability of the rally but noted that the demand-growth story for Micron is real and is projected to sustain through at least 2029.
"It's a commodity. This is what happens with commodities." On Morning With the Fool, Jim Gillies offered a word of caution: For decades, the memory market has behaved similar to oil and other commodities, swinging wildly based on changes in demand. Typically, those swings have not lasted long.
3. Retail Shines Despite Uncertainty

It was a good week for retail earnings, with companies posting solid results despite fears that higher prices would weigh on consumer spending. Dollar Tree (NASDAQ: DLTR) grew earnings by 38% on a 7% jump in revenue, with management crediting higher average ticket prices and lower freight costs for the gains. At Best Buy (NYSE: BBY), revenue grew by just 2%, but earnings were up 11% on strong sales of entertainment products and a jump in operating margin. Shares of Best Buy and Dollar Tree finished the week up 26% and 23%, respectively.

"Technology is more important in people's lives than it has ever been." Best Buy CEO Corie Barry used post-earnings interviews to make the case that the company's products are less discretionary than investors assume. With big-ticket vacations and other luxuries growing more expensive, optimizing tech can be a relatively inexpensive way to splurge.
Two different playbooks: Alicia Alfiere offers an insightful look at how Dollar Tree and Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT) are winning over budget-conscious shoppers in very different ways.
4. SpaceX Adjusts Its Coordinates Ahead of Launch

The market has had about a week to digest Space Exploration Technologies' (SpaceX) S-1 filing, and the initial reaction has apparently been lukewarm despite the huge numbers involved. The company has reportedly cut its target valuation to at least $1.8 trillion, from $2 trillion, based on initial consultations with advisors.

What every investor needs to know: What part of the business is profitable? How much control will Elon Musk have? How much debt does SpaceX have? The answer to all those questions and more are in this guide to what is still shaping up to be one of the largest initial public offerings on record.
"At root, SpaceX will either be a triumph or failure of extra-terrestrial engineering." Tim Beyers believes that for SpaceX to work out for investors, the company needs to effectively create space-based data centers and other seemingly out-of-this-world technologies. The challenges are massive, but SpaceX has the pieces to make it work.
5. Okta Locks in on AI

Cybersecurity company Okta (NASDAQ: OKTA) grew revenue by 11% and free cash flow by 14% in its most recent quarter, with 98% of revenue coming from steady, recurring subscriptions. The company expressed caution about the quarters to come, what CFO Brett Tighe called a "prudent approach." But investors remain bullish, sending Okta shares up 34% for the week.

"We are also seeing the investments we have made in our partner initiatives take root as partner source bookings experienced a meaningful increase." On the post-earnings call, management discussed the success they are having in finding multiple avenues for growth. That's important because with net retention still at 107%, Okta has shown an ability to grow its business with customers once they establish a relationship.
"The biggest near-term tailwind for Okta going forward is in AI agents." Jason Moser believes that as AI continues to work its way into daily workflows, Okta is well positioned to benefit from the need to secure those workflows from new threats.
