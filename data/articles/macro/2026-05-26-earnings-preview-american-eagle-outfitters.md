Earnings Preview: American Eagle Outfitters

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 9:08 AM

Comments

American Eagle Outfitters (NYSE:AEO), a specialty retailer focused on casual apparel and accessories, is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings after the market closes on Thursday, May 28, 2026.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

The fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 marked a strong finish for American Eagle Outfitters. Total revenue reached a record $1.8 billion, representing a 10% year-over-year increase. Comparable sales rose 8%, led by a standout 23% growth at the Aerie and OFFLINE brands, while the American Eagle banner posted a 2% increase. Adjusted operating income was $180 million, up 27% from the prior year, and adjusted operating margin improved to 10.2%. The company exited the quarter with $239 million in cash and no debt, after returning $341 million to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases. These results beat analyst estimates for both earnings per share and revenue.

Positives
Double-digit sales growth and record holiday performance: The company achieved its highest-ever fourth-quarter revenue, driven by strong demand during the Thanksgiving and holiday season. This indicates effective merchandising and marketing strategies.
Aerie and OFFLINE delivered exceptional growth: Comparable sales for these brands rose 23%, showing robust customer demand and successful category expansion in areas like sleepwear and activewear.
Improved operating efficiency and margin leverage: Despite significant tariff headwinds, the company increased its adjusted operating margin and leveraged SG&A expenses, reflecting disciplined cost control and operational improvements.
Strong financial position with no debt: Ending the year with significant cash and no debt gives the company flexibility for future investments and shareholder returns.
Negatives
Margin pressure from tariffs and promotions: Gross margin declined 30 basis points due to approximately $50 million in tariff costs and increased promotional activity, particularly in denim at the American Eagle brand.
Restructuring and impairment charges: The exit from the Quiet Logistics business and other restructuring actions resulted in $85 million in charges, impacting reported earnings and signaling ongoing adjustments to the business model.
Flat comparable sales at the American Eagle brand: While Aerie excelled, the core American Eagle brand saw only modest growth, highlighting the need for further product and marketing initiatives to drive broader gains.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Comparable sales momentum across brands: Management guided to high single-digit comparable sales growth for Q1, with Aerie and OFFLINE expected to see double-digit gains and American Eagle in the low single digits. Investors should watch whether these trends continue, especially given early positive reads on spring categories.
Tariff impacts and potential refunds: The company expects about $30 million in tariff headwinds for Q1, but recent legal developments could change the outlook. Any updates on tariff refunds or changes to trade policy could affect margins and future guidance.
Advertising and SG&A expense trends: Planned increases in advertising are expected to drive SG&A up by about 10% versus last year. Investors should monitor how these investments impact customer acquisition, brand awareness, and expense leverage.
Store fleet optimization and remodel progress: The company plans to open 35 new Aerie and OFFLINE stores and remodel about 60 locations in 2026, while closing 25 to 30 lower-productivity American Eagle stores. Updates on store performance and productivity gains from remodels will be important for long-term growth.
Product innovation and category expansion: Both brands are introducing new product categories and testing new fits, especially in denim, sleepwear, and activewear. Early customer response to these initiatives and their impact on sales mix will be key to sustaining growth.
