Key Takeaways
Boot Barn reported another period of strong growth, marked by higher sales and expanding its store footprint.
Earnings per share increased 18.9%, while revenue rose 18.7% from the prior year quarter.
Management projects continued gains, but expects slower same store sales and margin pressure next year.

Western and workwear retailer Boot Barn (NYSE:BOOT) achieved robust expansion in its business during the fourth quarter, marked by notable increases in sales and earnings. The company expanded its physical presence and digital reach while maintaining broad-based category and channel growth. The earnings report, released May 14, 2026 for the thirteen weeks ended March 28, 2026, also signaled moderating growth rates ahead. Overall, the quarter capped a year of strong momentum but pointed to a more cautious outlook for the new fiscal year.

Notable Developments
Revenue reached $538.8 million in the quarter, up 18.7% from last year as the company added 25 stores in the quarter for a total of 539.
Gross margin slipped to 36.3%, a drop of 0.8 percentage points, mainly due to higher occupancy costs from new stores.
Same store sales grew 6.1%, with e-commerce leading at 14.1% growth, showing continued strength in omni-channel performance.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects double-digit revenue growth and plans to open 70 new stores in fiscal 2027, but forecasts a slower same store sales increase of 2–4% and slight margin compression. This shift reflects a more cautious view as compared to the recently completed year, given tougher prior-year comparisons and ongoing investment needs. Investors should closely monitor how ongoing expansion affects profitability and whether consumer demand remains steady across both store and digital channels.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure for the quarter surpassed the company’s stated outlook from the previous quarter, driven by consistent traffic growth in stores and robust e-commerce gains. Exclusive brands continued to lift merchandise margin over the full year, though incremental costs tied to rapid growth in physical locations caused modest pressure on gross margin in the quarter.

The company finished the period with 539 stores, adding 80 net new units over the year. Rapid store growth has helped drive total sales, even as higher operating costs from new locations and expanded payroll have put downward pressure on margins.

Same store sales, which measure sales growth in stores open at least a full year and are an industry benchmark for underlying demand, grew 6.1%. E-commerce sales stood out once again, with 14.1% same store sales growth as Boot Barn expanded its digital offerings and exclusive-brand online platforms.

What Boot Barn Does

Boot Barn operates retail stores and e-commerce platforms offering western and work-related footwear, apparel, and accessories, serving both rural and urban customers across the United States.
