1. China Tech Giants Cleared to Buy H200s

★ NVDA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

The U.S. has approved roughly 10 Chinese firms including Alibaba (NYSE: BABA), Tencent (OTC: TCEHY), ByteDance, and JD.com (NASDAQ: JD) to buy Nvidia's (NASDAQ: NVDA) second-most powerful AI chip, the H200, but not a single delivery has been made, according to Reuters. Distributors Lenovo (OTC: LNVGY) and Foxconn are also approved, with each buyer cleared to purchase up to 75,000 chips.

Why deals are stalled: Chinese firms pulled back after guidance from Beijing, with pressure mounting in China to block or tightly vet orders. The shift came partly after unclear U.S.-side changes. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick said last month the Chinese central government has held back purchases to keep investment focused on domestic chip development. China's pivot toward Huawei resulted in an eroding position for Nvidia, with CEO Jensen Huang warning the company's AI accelerator share in China has effectively fallen to zero.
Trump's revenue cut and Beijing's wariness: Trump negotiated an arrangement where the U.S. gets 25% of the revenue from the China chip sales, requiring the chips to pass through U.S. territory before shipping. The structure has fueled Chinese unease about potential tampering or hidden vulnerabilities. Huang joined Trump's China trip this week, raising hopes the visit could unlock the stalled sales.
2. Brookfield Sits on $2 Billion SpaceX Stake

Brookfield (NYSE: BN) disclosed it owns $2 billion of pre-IPO SpaceX shares, with half on its parent company's balance sheet and the rest held by affiliated entities. The disclosure came in Brookfield's first-quarter earnings materials and signals a sizable bet ahead of SpaceX's confidentially filed IPO, which could happen as soon as June.

What's at stake: SpaceX is targeting a valuation of more than $2 trillion in its listing, which would make it the largest IPO of all time. Brookfield has been an investor in Musk's various companies for years, including a $250 million check in 2022 to help fund his buyout of Twitter, now X. That investment was the largest Brookfield Growth had ever made before the unit was shuttered last year.
Other Brookfield AI bets: The firm also committed $500 million to humanoid robotics developer Figure and invested in Hark Labs. The disclosures reflect how mainstream asset managers are increasingly using private-market vehicles to gain pre-IPO exposure to AI and robotics names, locking in positions before public listings that could carry premium valuations.
3. Cerebras Set for Hot Debut

AI chipmaker Cerebras Systems was indicated to open roughly 90% above its $185 IPO price on Thursday, with shares yet to begin trading on Nasdaq as of midday amid massive demand. The company raised $5.55 billion in the year's largest IPO, valuing it at $56 billion on a fully diluted basis.

What's drawing investors: Cerebras builds large chips designed for fast inference, the part of the AI market focused on running models quickly rather than training them. Nvidia is seen as having a weaker grip on inference, though the chip giant reportedly paid $20 billion for Cerebras rival Groq's tech and talent. Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) also announced an inference product last month. OpenAI is already using Cerebras chips and holds 33.4 million warrants tied to compute delivery and a $40 billion valuation hurdle.
Broader IPO context: Arm Holdings (NASDAQ: ARM) and majority owner SoftBank (OTC: SFTBY) approached Cerebras about a buyout weeks before the listing. The IPO market has more than doubled this year to $27.4 billion in proceeds, with Elon Musk's SpaceX targeting a potential $75 billion offering. Fidelity is expected to hold 11% of Cerebras' voting power after the deal.
