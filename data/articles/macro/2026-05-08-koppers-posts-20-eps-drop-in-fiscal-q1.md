Koppers Posts 20% EPS Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 9:23 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Koppers posted mixed results, with strong cash flow but ongoing profit challenges in several key segments.
Adjusted earnings per share fell 19.7%, and revenue declined slightly year over year.
Management lowered its full-year profit forecast but expects stronger cash generation going forward.

Koppers (NYSE:KOP) managed to boost its cash flow, but gains were tempered by declining profits and uneven results across major business segments. The integrated wood-treatment and specialty chemicals manufacturer released its first quarter results on May 8, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. Overall, although cash generation improved, the period highlighted margin pressure and prompted management to revise its earnings outlook downward.

Notable Developments
Free cash flow swung from a negative result last year to $34.9 million, showing improved working capital and inventory management.
Performance Chemicals’ adjusted EBITDA margin rose to 18.2%, up from 16.6%, while the Carbon Materials and Chemicals unit’s adjusted EBITDA margin fell drastically to just 1%.
Koppers repurchased $29 million in common stock during the period, almost 50% more than in the prior year quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management cut its full-year adjusted earnings and EBITDA forecasts, citing pressure from higher oil prices and ongoing segment challenges. Still, it believes cash flow for fiscal 2026 will come in much higher than in the prior year, aided by better inventory control. Going forward, investors should pay close attention to segment profit recovery, especially in Carbon Materials and Chemicals, as well as the impact of further restructuring and cost controls.

Quarterly Highlights

The Carbon Materials and Chemicals segment reported a sharp decline in profitability, with adjusted EBITDA falling more than 90% and margins nearly erasing to 1%. This drop was mainly due to lower prices for key products and a shift away from certain chemical lines.

Performance Chemicals showed resilience, with revenue rising by 17.5% on higher volumes and an improved product mix, producing a margin increase. Management noted that cost pressure from inputs like scrap copper was partly offset by hedging strategies and volume gains with large lumber customers.

The Railroad and Utility segment's sales retreated by 6.4%, driven by reduced rail tie activity and business divestitures, though higher utility pole sales provided some offset. Price concessions to retain long-term rail contracts also pressured profits, resulting in a modest margin decline for the business.

What Koppers Does

Koppers is a global supplier of treated wood products, wood preservatives, and specialty chemicals for the railroad, utility, and construction industries.

For those tracking sector shifts, stay tuned as Koppers navigates ongoing transformation and margin recovery efforts.
