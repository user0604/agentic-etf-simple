Celsius: A Monster Quarter

A blowout quarter powered by Alani Nu helped Celsius soar, but the company’s core brand is no longer carrying the growth story alone

Sanmeet Deo, CFA

May 7, 2026, 10:43 PM

Comments
8.5

Celsius Holdings (NASDAQ: CELH) dropped a headline-grabbing number Thursday morning: first-quarter revenue of $783 million, up 138% year over year. Adjusted EPS came in at $0.41, crushing the $0.30 consensus by 37%. On the surface, this looks like a company on fire.

Zoom in, and the story gets more nuanced, in the best and worst ways.

The headline growth is almost entirely acquisition-fueled. Celsius bought Alani Nu in 2025, and that brand alone contributed $368 million, growing 60% on a pro forma basis. The original Celsius brand? Up just 6%. That's a steep step-down from the 50–100% growth rates that once defined this stock. Goldman Sachs analyst Bonnie Herzog pressed management on this directly, and while CEO John Fieldly pointed to SKU rationalization timing and upcoming limited-time offers like Electric Vibe as near-term catalysts, there wasn't a clean answer on when Celsius reaccelerates. Management also acknowledged cannibalization from Alani Nu but essentially told JPMorgan to "take us as a portfolio," a response that felt like a sidestep.

On margins, the CFO was refreshingly candid: gross margin of 48.3% improved 90 basis points from Q4, but aluminum costs, a tariff-adjacent, industrywide headwind, are the villain of the story. Q2 is expected to be a "sidestep" on margins, with improvement only materializing in Q3 and Q4. A return to the low-50s gross margin target could slip into 2027 if commodities stay elevated.

The bull case: Celsius now commands about 21% of the U.S. energy drink market. Its new role as PepsiCo's (NASDAQ: PEP) energy category captain is unlocking foodservice and non-tracked channels that didn't exist a year ago. Alani Nu's integration is complete, $50 million in synergies captured, and shelf space gains of 100%+ are still flowing through. This is a genuine platform company now, not a one-brand bet.

The bear case: When your flagship brand grows 6% and your CFO warns of a margin "sidestep," the original thesis requires a rethink. Retail shelf wins and LTO excitement are encouraging, but investors who bought Celsius as the next Monster Beverage (NASDAQ: MNST) need to sit with an uncomfortable question: Is the core brand losing its identity as it plays second fiddle in its own portfolio?

Watch Celsius velocity data in Q3. That's the real tell for whether this is a transition or a peak.
