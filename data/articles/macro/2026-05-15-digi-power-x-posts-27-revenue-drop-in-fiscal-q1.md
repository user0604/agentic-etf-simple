Key Takeaways
Digi Power X posted a setback as revenue dropped during its shift to AI infrastructure and GPU cloud services.
Adjusted EBITDA turned positive to $1.1 million, showing improvement from the prior year's negative result.

Digi Power X (NASDAQ:DGXX) reported a business setback as it shifted focus to artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure and GPU cloud services, resulting in a decline in core revenue. The developer and operator of AI-focused data centers and GPU cloud platforms signed a major 10-year colocation agreement and recorded its first AI-related revenue, but legacy operations wound down faster than new lines could offset in the first quarter. The earnings release, dated May 15, 2026 for the first quarter, highlights a significant transition period with improved liquidity but ongoing profitability pressures. Overall, the quarter reflects early execution on the AI strategy, but revenue contraction and a wider net loss underscore the costs of this transition.

Notable developments
Revenue fell by 26.9% as legacy business scaled down, while AI revenue streams were only just beginning to contribute at quarter-end.
Adjusted EBITDA returned to positive territory after excluding items like digital asset price swings and share-based compensation.
The company signed a $1.1 billion, 10-year AI colocation agreement and reported $73 million in cash, its strongest liquidity position yet.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects substantial AI colocation and GPU-as-a-Service revenue to ramp from recently signed contracts and from the NeoCloudz platform, aiming for as much as $300 million in revenue for fiscal 2027. The key area to monitor is the speed and reliability with which new AI capacity is deployed and customer demand is activated, especially as legacy revenue continues to decline.

Quarterly highlights

The revenue figure dropped year over year as the company transitioned away from legacy operations and focused capital on AI data centers and cloud GPU offerings. The period’s net loss widened due to upfront costs in building out AI infrastructure and increased headcount to support these technical projects.

Despite this, cash holdings grew rapidly, and working capital improved, leaving the business with no long-term debt and a much stronger balance sheet. Operational advances continued as the NeoCloudz GPU-as-a-Service platform launched and achieved its first revenue contribution just after the quarter closed, indicating that the next quarter may capture early commercial impact.

The large $1.1 billion AI colocation contract signals a long-term revenue anchor, further supported by $45 million in recent capital expenditures for the Columbiana site and related hardware. Year over year, net fixed assets increased 29%, highlighting the scale of ongoing investment in critical AI infrastructure.
