AbCellera Biologics (NASDAQ:ABCL) delivered a mixed quarter as pipeline progress and revenue growth came alongside a shrinking pool of partnered programs. On May 11, 2026, the company reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, with the period reflecting higher research activity but persistent negative cash flow. Overall, the quarter highlighted both meaningful execution within the clinical pipeline and continued operating losses as AbCellera pivots to an internal program focus.

Notable developments
Revenue nearly doubled compared to the previous year, driven mainly by higher research fees, though milestone and royalty income stayed low.
Operating expenses held steady, with research and development costs up slightly and sales, general, and administrative costs dropping significantly.
The number of partner-led programs with downstream rights dropped from 44 to 40 over the quarter, reflecting ongoing portfolio changes.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management continues to focus on advancing its lead clinical programs, especially ABCL635, which is expected to have a key Phase 2 efficacy readout in the third quarter. It reiterated that current liquidity should support ongoing operations and research plans. The most important factor for investors is the outcome of the ABCL635 Phase 2 trial, which could determine the pace of future progress and commercial opportunity.

Quarterly highlights

AbCellera Biologics recorded a strong year-over-year increase in revenue, mostly from higher research fees, but milestone and royalty payments remained minimal this period. This revenue boost and a significant reduction in sales, general, and administrative expenses helped limit the net loss.

The pipeline advanced, with ABCL635 moving into Phase 2 trials after showing favorable early safety and strong biomarker engagement in Phase 1. No new candidate was added yet, but management confirmed plans to select at least one more development program this year.

Despite the boost in research activity, the number of active downstream-participation partner programs declined, and cash balances fell moderately as expenses continued to outpace income. Liquidity is still ample, but continued cash burn and attrition in partnerships are areas to monitor closely.
