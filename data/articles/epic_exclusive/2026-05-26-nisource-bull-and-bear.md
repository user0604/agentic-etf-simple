NiSource Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 7:08 AM

Comments

NiSource Inc. (NYSE:NI) is a diversified, fully regulated utility delivering natural gas and electric services to around 3.8 million customers across six states, with a principal focus on Indiana, Ohio, and Pennsylvania. The company’s growth strategy centers on executing a $21 billion capital plan aimed at grid modernization, reliability enhancements, and an innovative expansion into powering hyperscale data centers through its Genco platform, all underpinned by constructive relationships with regulators and large-scale, long-term customer contracts.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Accelerated Earnings Growth Driven by Data Center Demand and Genco Expansion

NiSource is capturing a once-in-a-generation opportunity from explosive data center growth, supporting raised long-term earnings guidance. The company secured roughly 800 megawatts in long-term data center contracts with global leaders, including Amazon and Alphabet (Google), and maintains clear line of sight to a pipeline of an additional 3–5 gigawatts. As a result, management increased its 2023–2033 adjusted earnings per share (EPS) compound annual growth rate (CAGR) target by 100 basis points to 9–10%, exceeding many utility peers. This acceleration is already visible: Q1 2026 adjusted EPS reached $1.06, up 8% year over year and already covering 52% of the full-year midpoint guidance, confirming robust execution. The dedicated $7.6 billion in capital for Genco and data center-related infrastructure enables NiSource to consolidate its early-mover advantage in this highly coveted market.

2. Structural Regulatory Support Enables Predictable Returns and Rapid Cost Recovery

Constructive regulatory frameworks in all major jurisdictions provide reliable avenues for investment recovery and de-risk execution. NiSource’s operations span six highly supportive states, benefiting from legislative alignment (such as Indiana House Bill 1002 and Ohio Senate Bill 103) and mechanisms like capital project "trackers" and "riders" that allow timely cost pass-through to customers. These policies reduce reliance on frequent rate cases, streamline regulatory approval for new data center projects, and solidify NiSource’s ability to recover costs expeditiously—critical for sustaining multi-billion-dollar capital outlays. Recent settlements with the Indiana Utility Regulatory Commission, expedited review timelines (often under 120 days), and strong political backing have allowed NiSource to advance both modernization and large-load growth initiatives, supporting ongoing investment and earnings visibility.

3. Disciplined Capital Allocation and Conservative Financial Policy Sustain Growth and Dividend

Prudent funding strategies and operational discipline underpin both growth ambitions and shareholder returns. The capital plan is grounded in conservative financial metrics, with plans to maintain funds from operations (FFO) to debt within a targeted 14–16% range through 2033. NiSource funds growth through a balanced mix of cash from operations, new long-term debt, and modest annual at-the-market (ATM) equity issuance ($400–$600 million), limiting shareholder dilution while providing financial flexibility. Operations and maintenance cost growth are committed to stay flat through the planning period, and the board affirmed a 7%+ annual dividend growth rate with a 55–65% payout, reinforcing the company’s long history of reliable income and capital stewardship for investors seeking stability and yield.

4. Customer-Focused Innovation Produces Tangible Savings and Political Goodwill

Innovative regulatory and contractual models directly return billions to customers, strengthening NiSource’s social license to operate. The company’s Genco contracts provide $1.4 billion in quantified, contractually defined bill credits—about $124 annually per residential customer—delivered transparently via pre-approved regulatory mechanisms. Ring-fencing ensures that the costs and risks of serving large data centers do not fall onto ordinary retail customers, while also scaling benefits such as lower system costs and enhanced reliability. This customer-centric structure not only rewards households and communities materially, but also creates goodwill with regulators and policymakers at a time when public trust in utilities is crucial to sustaining long-term capital programs and rate approvals.

5. Operational Excellence and Digital Transformation Drive Productivity and Risk Reduction

Proactive risk mitigation and adoption of artificial intelligence (AI) deliver measurable improvements in safety, reliability, and efficiency. NiSource recently recorded the safest first quarter on record for employee injuries since at least 2016, reflecting disciplined execution and modern asset management. The rollout of AI-powered tools in permitting, invoicing, and supply chain optimization has improved productivity by over 20% in targeted functions. Large-scale leak surveys and infrastructure inspections further reduce environmental and operational risks. These investments in technology not only manage regulatory and insurance liabilities but also unlock cost savings and position NiSource as a leader in utility sector innovation, offsetting inflationary pressures and enhancing earnings quality over time.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Data Center Contract Risks Could Lead to Over-Optimistic Growth Assumptions

Heavy reliance on a small group of hyperscale customers introduces concentration and execution risk to earnings projections. While NiSource’s Genco contracts with Amazon and Alphabet underpin its raised long-term EPS growth guidance, the company’s trajectory is increasingly tied to the financial health, expansion plans, and continued demand from just a few technology giants. Any delay, cancellation, or renegotiation of these contracts could materially impact both growth and capital recovery. Although contracts are structured for full cost recovery and required credit support, execution risks around construction, data center hyperscale loads, or evolving technology needs may still expose the company to adverse adjustments, especially as projected Genco EPS rises from $0.25–$0.35 (2030) to $0.40–$0.60 (2033).

2. Regulatory and Political Risks Remain, Particularly Around Large-Load Growth and Fuel Mix

Shifting regulatory landscapes or political backlash could threaten constructive frameworks and timely cost recovery. NiSource’s successful execution of its capital plan—and especially its data center strategy—depends on ongoing support from state regulators and policymakers who must balance affordability, reliability, and environmental priorities. Any reversal in regulatory sentiment, such as changes to tracker mechanisms, forced retention of uneconomic legacy assets (as seen with federal mandates on the Schahfer coal plant), or political scrutiny of large-load deals, could slow approvals or introduce unrecoverable costs. While stakeholder relations are currently strong, evolving legislative or public pressure remains an ever-present risk for utilities engaging in aggressive expansion or novel rate structures.

3. Increased Capital Needs and Leverage Could Pressure Balance Sheet and Dilute Shareholders

The $28 billion capital plan over the next five years carries funding and dilution risks as interest rates and market conditions evolve. NiSource’s financing mix includes both significant new debt and $400–$600 million annual ATM equity issuance, already sized to fund the current pipeline but subject to market volatility. If additional capital needs arise—whether from cost overruns, regulatory delays, or unforeseen project demands—the company may be forced to issue more equity or debt than planned, raising the risk of leverage exceeding the targeted 14–16% FFO-to-debt range or diluting existing shareholders beyond historical norms. Sustaining dividend growth while funding outsized CapEx in a rising rate environment may ultimately prove challenging if capital markets or regulatory support deteriorate.
