Key Takeaways

Revenue rose 52% year over year.

Speech AI specialist SoundHound AI (NASDAQ:SOUN) reported sustained business momentum as enterprise wins and new product rollouts drove revenue gains, but profitability was weighed down by higher costs and greater cash use. The company, which develops voice and conversational AI for auto, device, and enterprise customers, released its first-quarter financials on May 7, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. Overall, the period was a mix of robust sales growth and heightened operating losses as SoundHound prepares for a major acquisition and new product integration.

Notable Developments
Revenue jumped 52%, with especially strong performance in the automotive and Internet of Things business excluding acquisitions (up 88% year over year).
Non-GAAP loss widened as higher expenses, including non-recurring vendor charges, outpaced top-line growth despite stable per-share loss.
Net cash used in operations rose 37% to $26.3 million, narrowing future financial flexibility if spending remains elevated.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $225–$260 million, highlighting broad customer wins and upcoming benefits from the planned LivePerson acquisition. However, the company acknowledged margin pressures and greater operating losses, making profit improvement and disciplined spending a top focus for the next several quarters. Investors should watch integration progress related to new products and acquisition milestones, as well as whether operational improvements materialize to curb losses and stabilize cash flows.

Quarterly Highlights

Top-line growth remained the story for the quarter, with sales lifted by new and expanded deals in auto, retail, and enterprise verticals; core revenue in automotive and IoT artificial intelligence climbed 88% year over year when excluding acquisition-related contributions. The period also saw the debut of OASYS, SoundHound's orchestrated agentic AI platform for rapidly deploying and evolving conversational agents—a technology the company calls a world first for self-learning AI integration.

Profitability, however, was challenged: non-GAAP gross margin dipped to 49.7%, impacted by vendor-related charges and modest cost inflation; losses by both GAAP and non-GAAP measures increased meaningfully, with adjusted EBITDA loss reaching $26.7 million, up 20% from a year earlier. Cash burn accelerated, with net cash used in operations rising to $26.3 million for the quarter, which, at this pace, lessens the company's cash reserves over the coming year.

The company remains debt-free with $216 million in cash at quarter end, and prepares to complete its pending acquisition of LivePerson, aiming to create a larger platform for AI-powered enterprise communications. Management reiterated expectations that the combined business will pursue at least $500 million in revenue opportunities and target faster progress toward profitability—though benefits will depend on successful integration and cost discipline in subsequent quarters.
