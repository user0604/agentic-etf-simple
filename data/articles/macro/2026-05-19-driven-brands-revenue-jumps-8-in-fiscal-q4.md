Key Takeaways
Driven Brands sharpened its focus on core North American service brands but faced continued financial reporting challenges. For the fourth quarter, revenue increased 8% year over year. Driven Brands (NASDAQ:DRVN) sharpened its North American focus and added new stores, but accounting issues remain unresolved. The leading automotive services franchisor and operator reported its fourth quarter results for the period ending December 27, 2025, on May 19, 2026. The quarter showed steady operational progress, but lingering restatement concerns and softer spots in franchised brands temper the outlook.

Notable developments
The quarter’s revenue climbed 8% to $460.1 million, with most growth from same-store sales gains and new store openings.
Take 5, the oil change and quick maintenance business, posted 3.7% same-store sales growth, marking its 22nd quarter of positive growth in this metric.
Driven Brands completed the sale of its international car wash unit in January 2026, sharpening its focus on North America and paying down debt with the €411 million proceeds.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects revenue to reach between $1.95 billion and $2.05 billion in fiscal 2026, with moderate same-store sales growth and around 160 to 190 net new store openings. Restatement-related costs are expected to weigh on profit measures for the coming year. Investors should watch for improvements in accounting controls and stabilization in Franchise Brands segment performance.

Quarterly highlights

The Take 5 brand, focused on quick oil changes, continues to drive growth, adding 60 locations in the quarter and delivering steady same-store sales gains. Segment revenue for Take 5 exceeded $300 million for the period, contributing significantly to overall company expansion.

Franchise Brands, including repair and paint shops, saw a 1% decline in same-store sales, highlighting ongoing headwinds in traditional categories.

Driven Brands made decisive moves to streamline its services, completing the divestment of its international car wash operations. The proceeds were allocated to debt reduction, resulting in a lower pro forma net leverage ratio of 3.3 times adjusted EBITDA. This narrower service focus now centers around oil changes, collision and glass repair, and automotive maintenance in North America.

A major financial restatement affected previous years and highlights the importance of improved internal controls. The company reported that these corrections did not stem from operational underperformance, but restatement-related costs are anticipated to impact near-term results. Management reports enhancements to its accounting processes, which will be crucial for restoring investor confidence and lender trust.

What Driven Brands does

Driven Brands operates and franchises a network of automotive service locations, offering oil change, repair, paint, collision, glass, and maintenance services primarily in the United States and Canada.
