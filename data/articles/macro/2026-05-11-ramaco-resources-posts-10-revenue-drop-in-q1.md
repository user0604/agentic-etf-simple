Key Takeaways
Ramaco Resources posted a larger quarterly net loss as metallurgical coal prices and margins weakened.
Revenue fell 10% year over year, while liquidity more than quadrupled.
Management reaffirmed full-year production and cost guidance and highlighted rare earths progress.

Coal miner Ramaco Resources (NASDAQ:METC) faced a tough start to fiscal 2026 as lower metallurgical coal prices and higher fuel costs led to a wider quarterly loss. The company, a U.S.-based producer of metallurgical coal used primarily in steelmaking, remained disciplined on spending and maintained robust liquidity. The results, released on May 11, 2026 (covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026), showed earnings pressures despite cost leadership and operational progress. Overall, the quarter highlighted ongoing market headwinds and challenges to near-term profitability.

Notable Developments
Revenue decreased by 10%, as tons sold and coal prices both dropped compared to the prior year.
Cash mine costs per ton sold remained stable at $98, but cash margins shrank due to higher diesel prices and weaker coal pricing.
Liquidity rose sharply to $488.8 million, aided by capital raises and retained cash, allowing active share repurchases totaling $37 million in the quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 operational guidance for coal production, sales, and cash costs per ton. Leadership highlighted near-complete sales commitments for this year and ongoing investment in rare earths initiatives. Investors should watch coal price movements and progress on the Brook Mine rare earths project as central to results in upcoming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Coal revenue declined, driven by a 6% drop in tons sold and average realized prices falling 7% compared to a year ago. As a result, the company posted a net loss for the period, with diluted EPS dropping from $(0.19) last year to $(0.30) this quarter and adjusted EBITDA turning negative at $(1.8) million.

The cash mine cost per ton sold stayed at $98, continuing Ramaco's first quartile industry positioning, but cash margin per ton slipped to $16 due to diesel price inflation and persistent metallurgical coal price weakness. The Elk Creek Complex, the company's main mining asset, produced slightly more tons year over year as smaller, higher-cost operations remained idled amid market pressure.

Liquidity finished the quarter at $488.8 million, up sharply from $118.4 million the prior year, bolstered by last year's capital raises and active share repurchases. The company continues to advance its rare earths project with updated feasibility work expected soon and pilot plant construction on track for future operation.

What Ramaco Resources Does

Ramaco Resources produces metallurgical coal for steelmaking and is developing critical minerals, including rare earth elements, at projects in the United States.
