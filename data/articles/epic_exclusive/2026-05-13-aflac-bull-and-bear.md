Aflac Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 1:18 PM

Comments

Aflac (NYSE:AFL) is a leading provider of supplemental health and life insurance solutions, with significant business operations in both Japan and the United States. The company’s growth strategy centers on driving innovation in medical and cancer insurance, expanding distribution channels, and maintaining a strong capital return profile through dividends and buybacks. Its success depends on balancing growth in new policies with disciplined in-force management and capital stewardship, all while adapting to changing macroeconomic and competitive landscapes.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Innovation in Japan’s Medical and Cancer Insurance Drives a Sales Renaissance

Recent product innovation is fueling a powerful sales rebound in the key Japanese market – In the first quarter of 2026, Aflac Japan achieved a 25.5% year-over-year increase in new policy sales, especially from the launch of fresh medical (Onsen Tallett) and cancer (Miraito) insurance products. This significant growth came across all distribution channels and demonstrates that focused product innovation can reinvigorate consumer demand, even in a mature market. If annualized new sales surpass the company’s target of ¥90 billion (compared to ¥74 billion for 2025 and targeting ¥80+ billion for 2026), management expects this will finally offset ongoing runoff from legacy blocks and mark a return to positive earned premium growth. This inflection would signify not just a rebound but a foundation for sustained, profitable expansion in Aflac’s largest earnings segment.

2. Group Business Momentum in the U.S. Unlocks New Avenues for Premium Growth

Expansion in group insurance products is offsetting headwinds in traditional channels – In the U.S., the company’s strategic emphasis on group product lines delivered robust results, with group sales rising 12.4% year over year in the first quarter of 2026. Notably, group life, absence, and disability insurance surged 25%, and property product sales leapt 52%. While the individual, agent-sold side of the business remained flat or slightly down, persistency rates stayed strong at 79.3%. This shift demonstrates that Aflac can grow its premium base through cross-selling and targeting employer clients, providing a diversified growth vector not reliant on the more challenging individual agent segment. The group focus capitalizes on evolving workplace benefits trends and could drive steady double-digit sales growth in a large U.S. addressable market.

3. Capital Strength Enables Ongoing Shareholder Returns Even Amid Uncertainty

Robust capitalization supports generous dividends and buybacks – The company began 2026 with leverage at 21.2%—well within its target range—and an Economic Solvency Ratio (ESR) of 227%, with risk-based capital (RBC) at about 560%. Regulatory capital and $3.4 billion in holding company liquidity (well above the $1 billion minimum) provide flexibility to both weather financial shocks and return excess capital. In the first quarter alone, Aflac returned $1.3 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends, sustaining its 43-year streak of annual dividend increases. This consistent capital return, coupled with ongoing capacity for tactical deployments, provides a reliable value anchor and signals prudent risk management even amid macro volatility.

4. Resilient Profitability and Margin Discipline Underpin Long-Term Earnings Power

Solid benefit and expense ratios reinforce a stable margin profile – Despite headline challenges such as runoff and currency headwinds, Aflac maintained or improved profitability in both major segments during the first quarter of 2026. Japan’s benefit ratio declined by 290 basis points to 62.9%—within company guidance—and its pretax margin rose by 320 basis points to 35%, reflecting cost control and favorable claims experience. Similarly, Aflac U.S. posted a benefit ratio of 47.2% (GAAP), at the low end of its 48% to 52% guidance range, with pretax margin holding firm at 20.4%. Management’s ability to manage claims, expenses, and reserves translates into enduring earnings power, helping offset slower top-line growth when needed.

5. Strong ESG Commitment and Industry Recognition Enhance Brand and Customer Trust

Visible ESG leadership and community partnerships create competitive advantages – The company’s recognition as the American Cancer Society’s 2025 Corporate Partner of the Year and its ongoing Check for Cancer Champions initiative underline a deep commitment to environmental, social, and governance (ESG) practices. This can boost its profile with both existing and potential clients.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Runoff and Paid-Up Policy Impact Could Prolong Earned Premium Declines in Japan

Continued runoff from the legacy policy block poses a structural headwind – Even with double-digit new sales growth in Japan, management acknowledges that underlying earned premiums fell 1.3% year over year in the first quarter of 2026, with total earned premiums in yen down 3.8%. This is because ongoing runoff, particularly from older paid-up policies, consistently drags down net premiums. The sales threshold to reach flat or positive premium growth remains challenging to consistently exceed. If current sales momentum fails to persist—or macro or demographic pressures intensify—it could take several years before premium growth in Japan turns positive, limiting the group’s ability to drive revenue or earnings growth from its largest business line.

2. Prolonged Yen Weakness Exposes Reported Results to Foreign Exchange Risk

Exchange rate volatility could undermine reported financial performance – With approximately 65% of group income and most debt denominated in yen, Aflac remains exposed to a weaker Japanese currency. In 2026, ongoing yen depreciation—despite some hedging—continued to pressure reported U.S. dollar earnings, capital ratios, and leverage calculations. While the company’s underlying economic value is hedged, translated results still factor into market valuations, analyst estimates, and credit ratings. If yen weakness persists or worsens, Aflac may continue to report lower dollar-based revenue and earnings, hampering stock price performance even if the underlying business proves resilient in local terms.

3. Plateauing Individual Agent Business in the U.S. Could Limit Overall Sales Growth

Stagnant agent-sold segment creates strategic challenges for U.S. growth – While Aflac’s group business is thriving, the agent-sold individual market—the historical backbone of its U.S. operations—remains flat or in decline, according to first-quarter 2026 results. Despite investments in agent recruitment and enhanced onboarding, new agent conversion and productivity gains have not translated into meaningful aggregate agent-driven sales growth. If the traditional channel cannot return to positive growth and group sales reach a natural plateau, Aflac’s ability to expand U.S. premiums could stagnate, exposing the group to competitive threats, channel risk, or margin erosion over time.
