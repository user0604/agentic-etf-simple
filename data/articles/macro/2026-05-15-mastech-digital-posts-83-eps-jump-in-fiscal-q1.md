Key Takeaways

Non-GAAP EPS rose 83% to $0.11 year over year.

Mastech Digital (NYSEMKT:MHH) saw its revenue fall as clients delayed information technology and artificial intelligence projects. The digital engineering and IT staffing provider reorganized into new Talent and Data & AI segments to sharpen its AI focus and streamline operations. In its quarterly update reported May 15, 2026 (first quarter of fiscal 2026), the company posted higher earnings despite lower sales. Overall, the period featured sharply reduced revenue but clear progress on profitability and cost control.

Notable Developments
Revenue decreased 15% from a year earlier, totaling $41.1 million, as both Talent and Data & AI segments posted double-digit declines.
Non-GAAP net income improved to $1.28 million, driven by lower operating costs and higher margins, even with reduced sales volume.
Bookings in Data & AI nearly doubled to $7 million in total contract value, suggesting potential for future growth despite current weakness.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management indicated that uncertainty in technology budgets persists due to geopolitical and market factors. They see ongoing caution around IT and AI investments, but report strong bookings in Data & AI as a positive sign. Investors should watch the billable consultant headcount and whether demand rebounds in the company’s key segments over the next few quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Cost control efforts paid off, as selling, general, and administrative expenses dropped over 25%, following the EDGE transformation initiative launched last year. Non-GAAP net income climbed sharply, and the firm returned to a GAAP profit, reversing a loss from the year before.

In the Data & AI segment, while revenue fell 21%, strong bookings activity of $7 million may point to improving future pipelines. Gross margin remained steady in Data & AI at 37.2%, indicating the company held its pricing discipline despite lower volumes.

Talent segment revenue declined nearly 12% as consultant numbers dropped by more than 20%, but the average bill rate reached a new high, lifting margins to 22.3%. This offset some of the pressure from lower headcount and helped stabilize overall profitability.
