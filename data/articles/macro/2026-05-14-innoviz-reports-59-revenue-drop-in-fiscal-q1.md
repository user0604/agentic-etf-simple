Innoviz Technologies (NASDAQ:INVZ) reported a significant business setback as project-based revenue fell and losses increased. The company is a supplier of solid-state LiDAR sensors and software for autonomous vehicles and smart infrastructure. On May 14, 2026, it released results for the first quarter ended March 31, showing a steep decline in revenue and a reversal from positive to negative gross margins. Overall, the period highlighted challenges in maintaining commercial momentum.

Business Overview
Total revenue fell 59% year over year in Q1 2026, as revenue from application engineering services dropped from $15.85 million to $2.89 million, while revenue from LiDAR sensors increased from $1.54 million to $3.90 million.
Gross margin reversed from a positive 40.2% last year to a gross loss, reflecting a less favorable revenue mix and higher production costs.
Operating expenses rose 18.5%, mainly due to higher research and development and administrative costs, which pushed the operating loss up 89%.
Quarterly Performance

Revenue from application engineering services, which had been the largest contributor, dropped over 80% compared to the prior year. This exposed the business to volatility when major contracts end or pause. LiDAR sensor hardware sales increased, but the gains were not enough to offset the loss in project-based work.

The negative gross margin resulted from both lower sales volume and a shift in revenue mix, with hardware sales currently carrying thinner or negative margins. Management had previously reported progress on gross margin improvement, but this quarter saw a sharp reversal.

Research and development expenses increased to $16.8 million, showing the company’s continued investment in LiDAR and AI-driven sensor technology. Combined with steady administrative and higher marketing costs, this led to a larger overall loss for the quarter.

What Innoviz Technologies Does

Innoviz Technologies develops and sells solid-state LiDAR sensors and related software, mainly for autonomous vehicles and smart infrastructure applications.

Looking Ahead

Management did not reaffirm its previous full-year revenue target of $67–73 million, which now appears difficult to reach after a weak start to the year. The company stated it has enough funding for at least 12 months. Investors should watch for updates on new program wins or signs of a rebound in both project-based and recurring revenues.
