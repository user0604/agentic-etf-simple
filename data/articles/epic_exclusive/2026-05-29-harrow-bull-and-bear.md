Harrow Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 29, 2026, 7:07 AM

Comments

Harrow, Inc. (NASDAQ:HROW) is a specialized pharmaceutical company focused on developing and commercializing ophthalmic medicines for conditions like dry eye disease, ocular anesthesia, and inflammation. The company’s growth strategy hinges on expanding its branded product portfolio—including VEVYE, IHEEZO, and TRIESENCE—launching new assets, scaling its sales force, and capturing market share in high-growth therapeutic areas through improved market access and innovative product launches.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership in Ophthalmology Enables Sustained Prescription Growth and Share Gains

Exceptional market share gains in core eye care segments – Harrow surpassed a key inflection in Q1 2026, with VEVYE becoming the top prescribed branded dry eye therapy, overtaking long-standing competitor XIIDRA by reaching a 14% branded market share as of March. Despite an 18% year-over-year contraction in the broader branded dry eye category, VEVYE achieved a 25% sequential increase in new prescriptions and an 11% increase in total prescriptions. These advances reflect not only strong underlying patient and physician demand but also the effectiveness of targeted commercial execution. As the overall dry eye segment has grown prescriptions by 20% year over year for two consecutive years, Harrow’s ability to take share in a growing market positions it to drive above-market revenue growth as industry conditions normalize and branded volumes recover.

2. Accelerating Pipeline Launches and Regulatory Milestones Drive Revenue Expansion

Multiple new product launches and reimbursements widen growth runway – Starting in the second half of 2026, Harrow expects to recognize revenue from the July launch of BYOOVIZ, a biosimilar to Lucentis, as well as the commercial rollout of BYQLOVI, a new topical steroid. The introduction of a permanent J-code for IOPIDINE 1% is projected to unlock a previously inaccessible market of approximately 1.5 million annual laser procedures and to materially improve reimbursement economics at average selling price plus 6%. In parallel, under-marketed assets like VERKAZIA and NATACYN are set to benefit from new dedicated commercial teams. This robust cadence of pipeline launches and reimbursement milestones is critical for risk diversification and adds visibility to achieving management’s reaffirmed full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $350–$365 million, with expectations of an even stronger second half as access and pricing tailwinds emerge.

3. Expanded Sales Force and Commercial Platform Are Translating Into Tangible Upticks in Demand

Scaled commercial infrastructure accelerates product adoption – Harrow doubled its VEVYE sales team and tripled the TRIESENCE field force in Q1 2026, adding over 90 new reps in specialized ophthalmic roles. Early in Q2, the company is observing higher daily prescription “highs and lows,” which management attributes directly to this expanded field force. With retina now representing over 80% of IHEEZO use and account penetration up nearly 50% year over year, this commercial investment is already facilitating deeper physician engagement and faster new account onboarding. This strategic shift creates a durable growth engine, supporting not only legacy product performance but also the successful scaling of new launches across Harrow’s broadening ophthalmic portfolio.

4. Gross-to-Net Pricing and Access Issues Have Been Proactively Addressed for VEVYE

Proactive management response to pricing headwinds restores revenue trajectory – The major $8 million Q1 2026 gross-to-net adjustment for VEVYE, driven by high-deductible patient dynamics and expanded coverage, was isolated and swiftly addressed through new business rules implemented in April. These included tighter caps on copay assistance and program refinements designed to restore sustainable net pricing going forward. Management projects that average selling price for VEVYE will increase by approximately 30% from Q2 onward, with early indicators showing no negative impact on demand or patient access. This rapid mitigation demonstrates risk awareness and protects both margins and top-line growth as insurance coverage matures and access bottlenecks are resolved.

5. Breadth of Portfolio and Strategic Acquisitions Enhance Long-Term Diversification and Optionality

Diversified ophthalmic pipeline reduces concentration risk and supports scalable growth – Harrow asserts that it now owns one of the largest U.S. portfolios of ophthalmic prescription medicines, spanning branded, specialty, and compounded segments. Active commercial pushes into previously underappreciated assets (such as VERKAZIA and NATACYN) and the integration of the G-MELT pipeline product following the Melt Pharmaceuticals acquisition broaden Harrow’s addressable markets. Management’s openness to future accretive deals and commitment to scaling multiple assets over a five-year plan add strategic flexibility, reducing single-product dependence. This diversification underpins the goal of achieving $250 million in quarterly revenue by the end of 2027, suggesting a platform well-positioned for both organic and inorganic growth opportunities.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Recent Revenue Volatility Exposes Execution and Modeling Risk in Pricing and Access

Persistent risk of unanticipated gross-to-net compression – The $8 million shortfall in Q1 2026 VEVYE reported revenue, tied to underestimating the mix of high-deductible patients and prescription copay buydowns, highlighted a key execution risk in a complex U.S. reimbursement landscape. Though measures were implemented to prevent recurrence, the event revealed gaps in claims tracking and modeling under new payer agreements that took two months to fully identify. Should similar challenges arise as Harrow onboards additional commercial plans and payer rules shift, the company’s top-line and gross margin trajectory could be further disrupted. Ongoing vigilance in access management and claims monitoring will be required to avoid future earnings shocks and maintain investor confidence.

2. Margin Pressure and Negative Adjusted EBITDA May Persist Amid Heavy Investment and Growth Pacing

Sustained losses highlight dependence on scale and rapid revenue ramp – Harrow reported an adjusted EBITDA loss of $12.7 million in Q1 2026, weighed by increased selling, general, and administrative expenses tied to sales force expansion and support for multiple pipeline launches. While management expects operating leverage to emerge as revenue accelerates, persistent negative profitability could strain the company’s capital position if anticipated sequential and back-half revenue growth falls short. In addition, the lagging effects of gross-to-net issues and high initial launch costs for new products present ongoing pressure on margins, raising questions around the timing and certainty of the projected inflection to sustained profitability.

3. Competitive and Legal Risks Could Hamper Long-Term Trajectory Despite Core Demand Strength

Market and regulatory headwinds present disruptive uncertainties – Despite VEVYE’s impressive prescription growth and share gains, Harrow operates in intensely competitive branded and generic ophthalmic categories. Large pharmaceutical peers and new biosimilars may engage in aggressive discounting or contracting, limiting further price expansion and pressuring share. Additionally, the recent gross-to-net incident has triggered a securities law investigation, reflecting the risk that disclosure practices and financial modeling are now under greater regulatory scrutiny. Any unfavorable outcome, or further negative surprises in external communications or access, could affect the company’s reputation and weigh on future capital access or partner relationships, even as the underlying demand outlook remains robust.
