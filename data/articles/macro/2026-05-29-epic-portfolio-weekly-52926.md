Dear Fools,

The S&P 500 is up 18% in only eight weeks. Since 1950, a two-month, 18% gain has happened only every 3.5 years, on average. Let's raise a glass to being happier and richer -- investing is rarely this much fun.

However, The Motley Fool is also on a mission to make the world smarter. Therefore, the smart thing to do right now is to give ourselves a reality check, remembering that sometimes investing is not fun at all. Indeed, the S&P 500 drops 30% or more -- a proper crash -- about once a decade. I've experienced how "not fun" those can be.

With the Epic Portfolio, we're targeting five-year returns. The last crash happened in 2020, suggesting the next crash will likely arrive before 2031. Considering it's 2026 now, we should prepare for a market crash before this portfolio hits its fifth anniversary. And one of the best ways to prepare is to continue soberly diversifying our investments across the best businesses for the long term.

Stock in Focus: MercadoLibre

We believe the stocks we recommend are among the best; otherwise, we wouldn't recommend them. But all businesses face risks. And there are multiple types of risk to be aware of.

Many of the top performers in the Epic Portfolio are now facing valuation risk -- they're objectively expensive. What I don't like about valuation risk is that there's nothing the business can do about it. The valuation is controlled by market sentiment, which can change.

One of the poorer performers on the scorecard so far is Latin America's MercadoLibre (NASDAQ: MELI). But MercadoLibre isn't facing valuation risk. In fact, it hasn't been this cheap in nearly 20 years. By contrast, it's facing execution risk. But given this company's history of executing at a high level, that's a risk I'll gladly embrace here.

To illustrate its past execution, MercadoLibre has grown revenue by over 30% for seven straight years. Some studies have shown that growth is the best indicator of stock performance over the long term. This is logical. If a company grows revenue, then it becomes a bigger company. And if it's bigger, it's more valuable.

However, the studies on revenue growth have an asterisk we mustn't overlook. Growing into less profitable ventures can lead to underwhelming stock returns. And investors fear that MercadoLibre has arrived at this crossroads.

To this point, MercadoLibre's trailing-12-month (TTM) operating margin was 16% in 2023. As of the most recent quarter, it's dropped below 10% -- a 40% decline in under three years. The company is investing in logistics, offering free shipping, and increasing its lending business by a hefty amount, leading to lower profits.

This has happened before. In 2017, MercadoLibre's operating margin was over 20%. From there, it dropped off a cliff before turning negative in the early days of the pandemic. During this time, profits were down because of major investments in the business.

MercadoLibre's investments paid off with growth. From 2017 through the end of 2020, revenue jumped over 700%. From there, its operating margin was well on the path to recovery as previous investments paid off.

MELI Operating Margin (TTM) data by YCharts. TTM = trailing 12 months.

Today, I see MercadoLibre as being on a similar trajectory. Its operating margin in the first quarter of 2026 was down below 7%, yes. But the company's revenue grew by 49%, which was its fastest growth in four years. In other words, the investments lowered profits, but that paid off exactly how management expected: revenue growth acceleration.

In my opinion, MercadoLibre is the best value stock in the Epic Portfolio today. We'll be watching the loan book because this business requires different skills from its core operations -- to reiterate, there is execution risk. But in a stock market chock-full of valuation risk, I believe MercadoLibre stock offers some important, timely diversification.

Mailbag: What About Sterling Infrastructure?

We recommended Sterling Infrastructure (NASDAQ: STRL) on April 16. It's soared 67% since, making it the second-best-performing stock in the Epic Portfolio. As optimistic as we were, I'm not sure anyone was quite this optimistic.

In fact, Sterling Infrastructure stock already blew past Tom's five-year price prediction. For this reason, FlixFool asked (I'm summarizing) whether it was time to sell, keep holding, or readjust the price prediction higher?

Before I directly answer this question, let me briefly touch on price predictions. When Tom put down $733.19 for Sterling Infrastructure, he wasn't prophesying the highest price it would ever have. It represented one possible outcome out of many.

The price predictions in our recommendations are dynamic -- they change with time and new information. There's also a dose of personal opinion mixed in, which can be wrong or evolve. In short, price predictions change, and revisions can be both higher and lower.

To answer the question directly, I wouldn't sell Sterling Infrastructure stock simply because it passed the price prediction in the original recommendation. The price prediction simply doesn't carry enough weight to sell a stock.

Financial results from Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) and others since our recommendation show that demand for Sterling Infrastructure's services hasn't softened in the least. Indeed, revenue and backlog numbers point to a bright future. And we'll keep monitoring those metrics in the coming quarters.

Ongoing strong financial results for Sterling Infrastructure and in the AI infrastructure industry could lead the team to increase its price prediction for the stock. I'm not promising it will. But I know the team will revisit it at some point.

In my opinion, the best course of action is to keep holding Sterling Infrastructure. But let me clarify something: It could absolutely pull back from recent highs. And I wouldn't be surprised if it did.

It may seem foolhardy to keep holding a stock after a strong gain, especially if it's due for a pullback. But in my experience, it's more foolhardy to try to sell shares right before each near-term pullback. Stocks go up and down -- it's just how it is.

In short, I'd hold Sterling Infrastructure because I can't predict the near-term top. And I wouldn't know when it was time to buy back in anyway. This is why I adopt a five-year outlook as often as I can.

The beauty of running with a 50-stock portfolio is that even if Sterling Infrastructure stock underperforms for the next couple of years, there are 49 other stocks standing by to do the heavy lifting.
