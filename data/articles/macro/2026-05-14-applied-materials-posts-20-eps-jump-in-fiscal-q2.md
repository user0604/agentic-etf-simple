Key Takeaways
Record revenues driven by AI demand and strong execution.

Applied Materials (NASDAQ:AMAT) delivered a standout quarter, setting new revenue and profit records thanks to robust demand from AI-related chip manufacturing. The leading provider of semiconductor equipment, materials engineering solutions, and related services benefitted from surging orders for next-generation chip production tools, and strengthened its guidance for future growth. Reporting its results for the second quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026, it showed high growth but also a sharp drop in free cash flow and continued exposure to regulatory and geopolitical risks. Overall, strong earnings and upgraded guidance were weighed against cash flow and inventory build concerns.

Notable developments
The Semiconductor Systems segment drove most growth, with revenue rising 10% and non-GAAP gross margin climbing to 54.8%.
Free cash flow contracted sharply, falling 80% to $210 million.
The quarterly dividend was raised 15% to $0.53 per share, the ninth year in a row for a payout boost.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its full-year growth outlook for its semiconductor equipment business, pointing to continued strength in AI infrastructure and advanced chip manufacturing. Its guidance for the next quarter projects further acceleration, with revenue targeted at approximately $8.95 billion, plus or minus $500 million, and non-GAAP EPS around $3.36, plus or minus $0.20. Investors should watch free cash flow and working capital trends, as these will determine the company’s ability to convert earnings into cash and maintain capital flexibility.

Quarterly highlights

The Semiconductor Systems segment saw significant expansion, rising by 10% year over year, and now accounts for 75% of total revenue. Growth was strongest in DRAM chip equipment and advanced logic, both of which are crucial for emerging AI uses, while Flash memory equipment sales declined as a proportion of segment revenue.

Despite high earnings, free cash flow fell dramatically. This was due to larger investments in manufacturing capacity—capital spending (capex) increased—and a buildup of inventory to support higher future demand, pushing inventories to $6.34 billion compared to $5.92 billion at the end of fiscal 2025.

Applied increased its quarterly dividend by 15%, raising the payout to $0.53 per share, marking nine consecutive years of dividend increases. At the same time, management highlighted that geopolitical and regulatory risks, especially related to China, remain a key area to monitor moving forward.
