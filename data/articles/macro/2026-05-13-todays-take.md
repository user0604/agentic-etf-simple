1. Creating Order Out of Chaos

By Anthony Schiavone

I’m not sure there’s such a thing as a “crash-proof stock,” but a company that creates order out of chaos is probably a good place to look. These companies tend to have strong balance sheets that enable them to make counter-cyclical investments. They are liquidity providers in a capital-constrained market. Investor Richard Bernstein once wrote: “Perhaps the number one rule of investing is return on investment is highest when capital is scarce.”

Right now, I’d argue investment capital is not scarce. It is abundant. Cash-rich companies like Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE: BRKB) and Copart (NASDAQ: CPRT) would likely benefit in a market downturn. As Alice Schroeder wrote in The Snowball: Warren Buffett and the Business of Life, Buffett “thinks of cash as a call option with no expiration date, an option on every asset class, with no strike price.”

2. The Resilience Trio: Healthcare, Dividends & Big Tech Anchors

By Rachel Warren
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

When the market crashes, I believe an investor’s best friends are companies that provide essential services and have fortress balance sheets. I lean heavily into the healthcare sector where medical needs remain constant regardless of a recession. These companies often provide a reliable floor for a portfolio when cyclical sectors falter. By combining these with established dividend payers across various industries, you can generate a stream of passive income to reinvest when prices are at their lowest.

My core holdings also include cash-rich tech giants like Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOGL) (NASDAQ: GOOG). Maintaining a mix of healthcare, dividend gems, and resilient tech has allowed me to remain calm while others panic, and keep focused on my long-term investment goals rather than short-term market noise.

3. My Three Pillars of Safety

By Neha Chamaria
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

One of my biggest investing principles is to be able to sleep well at night through market crashes. Companies deeply embedded in our daily lives help me do that. Inelastic demand, essential infrastructure, and fortress balance sheets are my three pillars of defensive investing.

Utilities, for instance, fit that mold perfectly. So do essential service providers such as waste management companies. These are crash-proof industries because you and I will pay companies like NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE) for electricity and gas, and Waste Management (NYSE: WM) to pick up our trash, even in a recession. These companies are often regulated and generate durable cash flows. Of course, these stocks can still fall in a crash, but their stable underlying earnings and cash flows limit the drawdowns.

4. Holding Onto the Everything Store

By Yasser El-Shimy
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Ideally, all stocks in my well-diversified portfolio should be stocks I am prepared to hold through a crash. But if I were to name one, it would have to be Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN). This is a company that nearly touches all different facets of our life: shopping, cloud computing, semiconductors, entertainment, groceries, and even AI (through its stake in Anthropic). Amazon has over the past two decades laid the foundations of sustainable competitive advantages by investing in logistics, warehouses, data centers, and automation. I have held shares of Amazon through thick and for nearly 15 years, and it is the kind of business that I can sleep well at night owning during a market crash.

5. The Philosophy Buffett Shares with Wu-Tang

By Loren Horst
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Cash rules everything around me: It cushions portfolio declines, funds opportunistic buys, affords patience when assets are beaten down, and brings calm when the best action is inaction. All of these qualities of holding cash compound during market crashes, and Berkshire Hathaway’s (NYSE: BRKB) $397 billion in cash and short-term investments — nearly 40% of its market cap — more than doubles that of U.S. runner-up Amazon’s (NASDAQ: AMZN) $146 billion, according to data from TradingView.

Remember: The true return on cash isn’t the interest it generates. It’s the confidence to walk into a fire sale as a buyer, thinking rationally while others panic and accumulating as others are forced to liquidate. I eagerly await new CEO Greg Abel’s first strategic shot with Berkshire’s “elephant gun.”
