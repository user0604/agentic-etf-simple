Key Takeaways
Fermi continues to make progress on project infrastructure, but remains pre-revenue with no binding tenant agreements.
Net loss widened as expenses rose, with EPS at ($0.30) for the quarter.
Management aims to secure a binding tenant agreement within 90 days, keeping a cautious stance on capital outlays.

Fermi (NASDAQ:FRMI) delivered further construction and regulatory achievements in its latest quarter but did not resolve its most pressing challenge: the absence of customer contracts. The power infrastructure developer pursues massive-scale energy capacity for AI data centers, focusing on building out Project Matador in Texas. Released on May 14, 2026, for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, the results underscore operational progress, tempered by the critical need for tenant agreements. Overall, the period showed tangible infrastructure advances, but business viability remains uncertain until tenants are signed.

Notable Developments
Fermi secured a major 6 GW Clean Air Permit and filed for an additional 5 GW, supporting its buildout to potentially 17 GW of capacity.
The net loss reached $189 million, mainly due to $134 million in share-based compensation and $25 million in debt extinguishment expenses.
Cash and restricted cash totaled $243 million at quarter’s end, providing liquidity but dependent on restrained capital spending and future financing.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its target to sign a binding tenant agreement within 90 days, and will pace further capital spending to commercial achievement. Future project milestones and financial stability now rely on converting tenant interest into contracts. The most important item for investors remains the completion of a first tenant deal, as this will validate Fermi’s business model and unlock customer revenue.

Quarterly Highlights

Fermi advanced Project Matador, reporting infrastructure progress such as construction milestones, utility connections, and arrival of major Siemens SGT-800 turbines for future power generation. These physical steps align the company with increasing AI-driven demand for large-scale energy but do not yet generate revenue.

Financial results underscore Fermi’s pre-revenue status. The period’s net loss was predominantly driven by non-cash equity compensation, alongside costs for project buildout and repaying prior debt facilities. Raising $785 million in equipment financing and other commitments provided a liquidity cushion, but without operational cash inflows, ongoing spending depends on tight fiscal discipline.

Permitting progress was a highlight, as Fermi’s 6 GW air permit and participation in fast-track nuclear licensing programs set the stage for future growth. However, the lack of tenant contracts was again flagged as a key risk, and management set a new 90-day goal to reach a binding agreement. This builds urgency, especially as management had cited tenant engagement in the prior quarter but still missed the milestone this period.
