Earnings Preview: ARS Pharmaceuticals

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 9:03 AM

Comments

ARS Pharmaceuticals (NASDAQ:SPRY), a biopharmaceutical company focused on needle-free epinephrine for severe allergic reactions, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Friday, May 15, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, ARS Pharmaceuticals delivered its first full year of commercial results for neffy, its needle-free intranasal epinephrine product. The company reported $72.2 million in U.S. net product revenue for the year, with total revenue reaching $84.3 million. Net loss for the year was $171.3 million, reflecting continued investment in commercialization and infrastructure. The quarter’s results met analyst expectations for both sales and earnings per share, according to available consensus data. Management highlighted strong physician engagement, growing patient awareness, and ongoing efforts to expand insurance coverage and streamline prescription workflows.

Positives
Solid commercial uptake for neffy in first full year: Net product revenue of $72.2 million demonstrates meaningful physician adoption and patient demand, especially given the product’s recent launch and the maturity of the epinephrine market.
Broadening insurance coverage and reduced barriers: By year-end 2025, neffy achieved approximately 93% commercial coverage, with 57% of covered lives having access without prior authorization, improving patient access and reducing administrative hurdles.
Encouraging direct-to-consumer (DTC) engagement: Awareness campaigns lifted brand recall among caregivers and patients to about 55%, exceeding industry norms and building a foundation for future prescription growth.
Strong cash position supports future growth: The company ended 2025 with $245 million in cash, cash equivalents, and short-term investments, providing flexibility for ongoing commercial and development activities.
Negatives
High operating expenses drive significant net loss: Selling, general, and administrative expenses reached $230.1 million for the year, outpacing revenue and leading to a net loss of $171.3 million as ARS continues to invest heavily in commercialization.
Refill-driven market creates launch friction: Neffy’s growth has been slowed by the dominance of electronic refills for legacy auto-injectors, requiring new prescriptions and more education for adoption, which introduces early launch challenges.
Prior authorization still limits some patient access: Although coverage is broad, about 43% of covered lives still require prior authorization, which can slow prescription growth in high-volume categories.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Progress on unrestricted insurance coverage: Investors should monitor updates on negotiations with major pharmacy benefit managers, especially CVS Caremark, and Medicaid expansion, as broader access could drive prescription growth in the second half of 2026.
Impact of sales force expansion and territory realignment: The company is increasing its sales force from 106 to 150 in Q2 2026, funded by reallocating existing resources. Early signs of improved engagement and prescription volume in priority accounts will be important to track.
Adoption trends from Get neffy on Us and DTC campaigns: The virtual prescription and zero co-pay program, along with refreshed advertising, are designed to reduce barriers and accelerate patient conversion. Watch for updates on the percentage of prescriptions coming through these channels and any changes in patient or prescriber behavior.
Emergence of refill dynamics and back-to-school seasonality: As neffy’s installed patient base matures, the company expects refills to become a larger driver of revenue, especially heading into the summer and back-to-school period. Early evidence of this trend could signal more predictable, recurring sales.
Pipeline and international launch milestones: Interim data from the Phase IIb trial for chronic spontaneous urticaria is expected in the second half of 2026. Investors should also watch for updates on partner-led launches in Japan, Australia, and China, as well as regulatory progress in Canada and Europe.
