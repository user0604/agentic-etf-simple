Earnings Preview: Enovix

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 7:03 PM

Comments

Enovix (NASDAQ:ENVX), a company that designs and manufactures advanced lithium-ion batteries for smartphones, smart eyewear, and defense applications, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Wednesday, May 13, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

Enovix reported record fourth-quarter revenue of $11.3 million, up 16% year over year, with continued growth in defense and industrial battery shipments, especially for naval munitions. The company’s non-GAAP gross margin improved to 26% in the quarter, supported by higher volumes and operational improvements in its South Korea facility. The non-GAAP net loss per share was $0.14, which was better than analyst expectations. Enovix ended the year with a strong liquidity position, holding $621 million in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities. The company also authorized a new $75 million share repurchase program in February 2026. Management highlighted progress in smartphone battery qualification, smart eyewear commercialization, and operational leadership changes to support manufacturing scale-up.

Positives
Record quarterly revenue and margin improvement: Enovix achieved its highest-ever quarterly revenue and improved margins, which signals progress in scaling its manufacturing and meeting demand, particularly in defense and industrial markets.
Smart eyewear commercialization advancing: The company delivered over 1,000 battery packs to its lead customer and received a production purchase order, indicating early traction in a market expected to grow rapidly as AI-enabled devices become more popular.
Strong liquidity and capital flexibility: With $621 million in cash and new financing from convertible notes and warrant exercises, Enovix is well positioned to fund its commercialization plans and consider strategic opportunities without immediate need for additional capital.
Operational leadership strengthened: Recent appointments in manufacturing and engineering roles are expected to help improve production efficiency and support the transition to higher-volume output.
Negatives
Smartphone battery qualification delays: The company is still working to meet all cycle life testing requirements for its 100% silicon anode smartphone battery, particularly at the accelerated 0.7C discharge rate, which is a gating item for moving into commercial production with its lead customer.
Q1 FY2026 guidance below consensus: Management guided for Q1 revenue of $6.5 million to $7.5 million, below the analyst consensus of approximately $8.3 million, and projected a non-GAAP operating loss that is at the high end of expectations.
Ongoing operating losses: Enovix continues to report significant operating losses as it invests in product development, manufacturing expansion, and customer qualification programs. Profitability remains several quarters away.
Manufacturing bottlenecks persist: The laser dicing process in Fab2 remains a rate-limiting step, requiring further process optimization before full-scale production can be achieved for new products.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Progress on smartphone battery qualification: Investors should look for updates on the cycle life testing with the lead smartphone customer, including whether alternative qualification pathways or new testing protocols have been agreed upon.
Smart eyewear shipment milestones: Watch for confirmation of initial volume shipments to smart eyewear customers and any new customer wins, as this market is expected to provide earlier revenue growth than smartphones.
Defense and industrial order timing: Q1 revenue is expected to be lower due to seasonality in defense shipments. Updates on the timing and scale of new defense contracts or backlog conversion will be important for the rest of the year.
Fab2 manufacturing improvements: Monitor any reported progress in resolving the laser dicing bottleneck and increasing yield and throughput, as this will affect the company’s ability to meet demand and improve margins.
Capital spending and cash burn trajectory: Investors should review capital expenditures for Fab2 and Korea capacity expansion, and assess whether the company’s cash usage is tracking in line with its commercialization and production ramp plans.
