Zebra Technologies: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 10:49 AM

Comments

Zebra Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ:ZBRA) is a global leader in digitizing and automating frontline workflows for sectors such as manufacturing, retail, healthcare, and logistics. The company specializes in rugged mobile computers, barcode printing, RFID (radio-frequency identification), and advanced machine vision, increasingly embedding artificial intelligence and software into its offerings. Its long-term growth strategy centers on capturing a large and underpenetrated market for automation and asset visibility, supported by ongoing innovation, ecosystem partnerships, and selective acquisitions.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Rapid Growth and Upward-Revised Guidance Signal Sustained End-Market Demand

Management’s upgraded full-year outlook and strong Q1 2026 results highlight durable demand tailwinds. For the first quarter of 2026, the company delivered nearly $1.5 billion in net sales, a 14.3% year-over-year increase (4.3% organically), with both its Connected Frontline and Asset Visibility & Automation segments reporting robust growth led by mobile computing and machine vision. Sales momentum was broad-based across North America (up 4%), Asia Pacific (up 11%), and Latin America (up 10%). Reflecting this strength, full-year 2026 revenue growth is now guided at 10%–14%, with earnings per share guidance raised to $18.30–$18.70 (non-GAAP), comfortably above pre-quarter consensus. This underscores resilient demand across verticals — manufacturing, e-commerce, healthcare — and suggests the runway for automation and digitization adoption remains long.

2. Expanding Margin Profile Demonstrates Structural Profitability Improvements

Margin expansion amid cost pressures points to strong operating leverage and discipline. In Q1 2026, the company reported its highest adjusted gross margin in several years, up 80 basis points year over year to 50.4%. Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) margin expanded by 90 basis points to 23.2%. Leadership attributed these gains to ongoing productivity initiatives, favorable business mix, and effective cost-mitigation strategies. Despite headwinds such as memory inflation and rising freight costs, management expects to fully offset these pressures through price increases, restructuring, and sourcing efficiencies — supporting a full-year EBITDA margin forecast of approximately 22%. Margin resilience underpins the potential for improving free cash flow conversion and higher long-term returns.

3. Large, Underpenetrated Addressable Market Provides Long Growth Runway

The company’s $35 billion served addressable market remains only partially penetrated, especially in automation. Management highlights that roughly 75% of warehouses globally are still in the early stages of automation, while trends like rising e-commerce volumes and manufacturing modernization are increasing demand for real-time asset tracking and workflow digitization. This market remains highly fragmented and is expected to benefit from durable secular drivers: artificial intelligence adoption, supply chain visibility needs, and labor productivity constraints. Against this backdrop, the company’s diversified vertical exposure, embedded customer relationships (serving over 80% of the Fortune 500), and deep integration into business-critical workflows position it to capture organic growth of 5%–7% annually through the cycle, not including potential from future product innovation and M&A.

4. Innovation in AI, Machine Vision, and RFID Enhances Competitive Differentiation

Continued investment in advanced technology cements the company’s leadership and raises switching barriers. Recent launches include a new line of enterprise mobile devices and wearables with embedded RFID and AI, tailored for on-device processing and enhanced visibility. The asset visibility business saw strong double-digit growth in machine vision, positioned as a key engine for future expansion. Strategic investments in startups (such as Apera AI for industrial robotics vision) and tuck-in acquisitions (like Photoneo for deep learning) further build out the software and physical automation toolkit. These innovation efforts are complemented by a mature PartnerConnect ecosystem, boosting solution integration and ISV (independent software vendor) collaboration. Taken together, these initiatives increase the value proposition to enterprise customers while deepening the company’s technological moat.

5. Strong Balance Sheet and Active Capital Deployment Accelerate Shareholder Value Creation

Disciplined cash generation and aggressive share buybacks support long-term value and strategic optionality. In Q1 2026, the company generated $163 million in free cash flow, with guidance for at least $900 million and a ~100% free cash flow conversion rate for the year. Debt remains modest at 2.1x leverage, with $1.1 billion in available credit providing ample liquidity. Management has already repurchased $500 million of shares year-to-date, surpassing its prior commitment to return 50% of free cash flow to shareholders, and signals flexibility to scale buybacks further if valuations remain attractive. This capital return discipline, paired with M&A integration that is tracking to plan (notably with Elo Touch and Photoneo), augments both near-term returns and long-term strategic growth potential.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Memory Supply Constraints and Input Cost Volatility Could Pressure Margins

Operational confidence notwithstanding, memory supply and component cost inflation remain notable risks to profitability. The company continues to face headwinds from rising memory prices and supply constraints — a challenge acknowledged as a major watchpoint by management. While the leadership is confident in its multi-pronged mitigation strategy (including direct supplier co-planning, alternative sourcing, and contractual pricing), any deterioration in supply reliability or cost inflation could outstrip existing offsets and compress margins. Ongoing memory capacity constraints into 2027 add medium-term uncertainty, and any failure to adapt quickly could result in disrupted shipments, lower gross margins, or diluted operating leverage, especially in a high-growth environment.

2. Pace of Automation Adoption and Customer Refresh Cycles May Be Uneven

Growth projections for automation and digitization rest on the assumption of consistent customer adoption, but cycles may prove lumpy or prolonged. Despite management’s optimism about secular megatrends, the adoption of warehouse automation, machine vision, and RFID technologies is still in the early innings across many sectors and geographies. Customers may delay investment or refresh cycles due to macroeconomic uncertainty, shifting capital expenditure priorities, or volatile end markets. In particular, large-scale transportation and logistics refreshes are now projected to begin in 2027, suggesting a potential lull or plateau in certain verticals until then. If the expected acceleration in automation spending stumbles, forward estimates for mid-cycle organic growth rates and expanding margins could fall short, impacting valuation expectations.

3. Competitive Threats from Established Players and Agile Startups Could Erode Market Share

Intensifying competition in automation, AI, and asset visibility may threaten pricing power and growth momentum. As sectors rapidly digitize, both established enterprise technology providers and focused startups are investing heavily in overlapping areas — particularly machine vision, RFID, and industrial IoT (Internet of Things) platforms. While the company boasts a large installed base and integration into customer workflows, increasing commoditization, faster-moving cloud-based entrants, or tailored solutions from industrial automation giants could disrupt existing relationships or force price concessions. The constant need to innovate and integrate acquired technologies also brings execution risk; any faltering in product roadmap or customer service posture could open doors for rivals, slowing the progress already built into long-term forecasts.
