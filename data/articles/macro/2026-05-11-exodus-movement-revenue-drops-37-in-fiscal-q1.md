B2B swap partner volume reached $257 million, making up 22% of exchange volume and highlighting an ongoing push into business-to-business segments.
The acquisition of Monavate and Baanx closed just after quarter-end, expanding the company’s reach into card and payment infrastructure.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management provided no formal guidance for revenue or user metrics but is prioritizing integration of the newly acquired payment platforms. The company highlights ongoing investments in infrastructure and strategic partnerships. Investors should closely watch for updates on the financial contributions of acquisitions and turnaround in core user activity in future quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s core revenue line dropped sharply year over year, with operating expenses rising concurrently. Technology, development, and user support expenses increased 9% even as top-line performance weakened, contributing to the deepened net loss for the period.

B2B infrastructure is now more central, with business-to-business swap partners contributing over one-fifth of transaction volume, demonstrating some strategic diversification. However, this shift has not yet compensated for the steep decline in total processed volume or the flat line in monthly active users, which held at 1.5 million.

Exodus closed the acquisitions of Monavate and Baanx on May 1, aiming to boost its payments and card-issuing capabilities. Details on integration and financial results from these new segments are expected in future disclosures and will be key for understanding potential improvements in revenue stability and growth.
