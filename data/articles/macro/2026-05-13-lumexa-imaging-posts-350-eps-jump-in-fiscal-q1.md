Lumexa Imaging (NASDAQ:LMRI) reported a steady business recovery, with higher patient volumes and improved profitability after a challenging comparison a year ago. Notable developments
Revenue climbed 3.1% year over year as patient volumes improved and new imaging centers opened.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) more than quadrupled, rising to $0.18 from $0.04 last year as profitability improved.
Same-center advanced procedure volumes grew 5.6%, reflecting continued focus on MRI and CT scans as growth drivers.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its full-year 2026 guidance, targeting $1.045 billion-$1.097 billion in revenue and $234 million-$242 million in adjusted EBITDA. It expects seasonal weakness in this quarter to be offset by stronger volumes and margin recovery for the rest of the year. Investors should watch for margin improvement, cost control, and progress on new center expansion plans in coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The period saw the addition of four centers, including two tuck-in acquisitions and two new site openings, supporting the plan for at least 8–10 new de novo centers in 2026. Advanced imaging, including MRI and CT, continued to gain revenue share, now representing 31.1% of consolidated procedure volume, up 1.7 percentage points from the prior year.

Adjusted EBITDA, a measure of operating profitability, was nearly flat year over year, reflecting seasonal headwinds, higher costs from operating as a public company, and increased administrative expenses. While the margin narrowed from historical levels, management signaled confidence that lost volumes can be recovered across the rest of the year.

Management fees and other revenue posted a 4.8% gain year over year, while patient service revenue grew at a slower 2.6% rate. Cash and cash equivalents ended the period at $51.2 million, slightly lower than at the end of the previous year after investment in growth and typical seasonal patterns.

What Lumexa Imaging does

Lumexa Imaging operates a nationwide network of outpatient imaging centers providing MRI, CT, ultrasound, and other diagnostic scans to patients in a non-hospital setting.
