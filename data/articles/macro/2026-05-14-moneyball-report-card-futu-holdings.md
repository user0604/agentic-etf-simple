Moneyball Report Card: Futu Holdings

What the Hidden Gems Primary database scores can tell us about Futu Holdings.

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 2:45 PM

Comments

Futu Holdings (NASDAQ: FUTU) doesn't get much attention in U.S. investment circles, which is part of what makes it interesting. With a Superscore of 74 in the Moneyball Hidden Gems Primary database, this Hong Kong-based digital brokerage -- operating under the Moomoo brand in the U.S., Singapore, and Australia and Futubull in Asia -- has built one of the most profitable fintech platforms in the world at a valuation that doesn't obviously reflect it.

The financial profile is exceptional. Futu earns a 98 on Financial 1Y and a 94 on Financial 5Y -- among the highest pairings in the entire database. Revenue grew 68% in 2025, net margins approached 50%, and ROUNTA came in at 29%. Return on equity climbed to 28%. Over five years, the platform scaled from approximately $7 billion to $23 billion in revenue, and the balance sheet strengthened throughout, with the debt-to-equity ratio cut by more than half.

The Efficiency score of 99 tells the story clearly: Futu is an asset-light business running an extremely lean cost structure relative to the returns it generates. The PayScore of 3% is low in isolation, but the context matters -- a recent special cash dividend was widely read as management signaling confidence in sustained cash generation, and the company carries essentially no GAAP income tax burden.

The business model is distinctive. Futu built what it calls a "social brokerage" -- trading infrastructure layered on top of the NiuNiu community, a proprietary financial content and discussion platform with tens of millions of engaged users. Community creates retention, and retention creates the high switching costs that underpin the moat.

Product 5Y comes in at 84, reflecting consistent innovation over the full period and a deliberate expansion from a narrow Hong Kong equities platform into a global multi-asset brokerage serving more than 29 million users. The company has secured regulatory licenses across Singapore, Australia, the U.S., Canada, Japan, and beyond, which is an underappreciated competitive barrier -- each license represents months of regulatory effort that a new entrant would have to replicate from scratch.

The Optionality score of 86 reflects credible expansion beyond the core brokerage. The most developed adjacent business is wealth management; distributing third-party fund products through the platform shifts Futu toward recurring fee revenue rather than pure transaction dependency. Enterprise services (IPO distribution, ESOP management) bring in a B2B corporate client base that is entirely distinct from the retail focus. And Futu has recently entered cryptocurrency trading through partnerships, adding a new asset class to the platform at a point when institutional and retail demand for digital assets is growing. At a market cap of $19.6 billion, any of these scaling meaningfully would move the valuation.

The AI score of 77 reflects genuine integration. Futu's proprietary risk control systems and AI-driven investment assistants are core to the platform experience, not decorative. A dedicated technology committee and university AI research partnerships indicate organizational seriousness about the roadmap.

One flag worth noting: The database identifies a recent cessation of R&D investment as a departure from the company's historical innovation cadence. Whether this is a temporary harvest phase or a structural shift is a live question for the long-term thesis.

Governance is the other persistent concern, and the score of 25 -- matching Reddit's (NYSE: RDDT) as the lowest of the companies covered here -- reflects serious structural issues. Founder Leaf Hua Li controls the company through a dual-class structure that leaves minority shareholders with little practical influence.

The VIE framework introduces regulatory and enforcement risk that investors in Chinese-linked companies know well, and the database flags accounting inconsistencies within that structure -- specifically, service fee agreements that went uncharged for multiple years, which raises legitimate questions about intercompany transparency.

Most materially, Futu faces an ongoing inquiry from the China Securities Regulatory Commission regarding unlicensed cross-border operations, which has already resulted in removal from mainland Chinese app stores. Management characterizes the regulatory overhang as largely resolved; the database treats it as unresolved.

Leadership scores a 76, which is fair. The strategic pivot from a China-dependent business toward a genuinely global platform has been executed with real discipline, and management communicates clearly with data-rich operational updates. The governance discount is structural rather than a reflection of operational incompetence.

At $135.87 per share at the time of writing, the GARP score of 83 suggests the forward earnings trajectory still looks attractive relative to the current price -- particularly for investors willing to underwrite the regulatory and governance risks that have historically kept Futu trading at a discount to its financial quality.

The numbers here are hard to ignore. A 50%-net-margin business growing at 68% annually, trading at $19.6 billion, is not a profile the market typically leaves unattended. The discount exists for real reasons. The question is whether those reasons are permanent.
