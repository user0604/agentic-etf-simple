TMC The Metals Company (NASDAQ:TMC) secured a significant business win this quarter by advancing its regulatory agenda and finalizing a pivotal commercial partnership, even as rising operating expenses offset those gains. The deep-sea minerals developer operates the largest seabed critical minerals exploration program under U.S. and international regulations. On May 14, 2026, it released first quarter results for the period ended March 31, 2026, showing steady progress toward commercial nodule recovery, but continued financial losses and elevated non-cash costs. Overall, the quarter delivered regulatory and strategic milestones, yet also spotlighted ongoing financial discipline and capitalization risks.

Notable Developments
The company achieved “full compliance” status from U.S. authorities on its commercial recovery permit application, increasing its permitted area to 65,000 km².
General and administrative expenses more than doubled year over year, driven by higher share-based compensation and personnel costs.
Cash used in operating activities dropped sharply, suggesting improved near-term expense management despite ongoing non-cash charges.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management stated that existing cash and credit facilities, totaling about $164 million, are sufficient for operational and capital needs over the next twelve months. The main milestone to watch is completion of the U.S. regulatory certification process, currently expected by the end of Q1 2027. Any delay in regulatory approval could impact the timeline for commercial operations and future financing needs.

Quarterly Highlights

Regulatory progress was the headline achievement, with National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) granting full compliance on TMC’s U.S. permit application. This advances the company toward the first commercial seabed nodule production, now covering an expanded area and resource base of approximately 619 million tonnes of wet nodules.

On the operations front, a binding development and production agreement was signed with Allseas for the main offshore nodule recovery system. This contract confirms the production capacity and timeline, targeting system commissioning to begin in the fourth quarter of 2027, subject to regulatory approvals.

From a financial perspective, significant rises in exploration and general expenses reflect the scaling of technical and organizational activity needed for future commercialization. Much of the cost increase was from non-cash share-based compensation, leading to sharply reduced year-over-year cash outflows.
