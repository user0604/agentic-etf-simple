Key Takeaways
DraftKings reported another quarter of revenue and profit growth, despite a decline in active users. Adjusted earnings per share rose 66.7% year over year. Management maintained its guidance for fiscal 2026, with a focus on expanding core products and new markets.

DraftKings (NASDAQ:DKNG) posted another strong quarter, showing significant gains in both revenue and profitability, even as its active customer count declined. The leader in U.S. digital sports entertainment and gaming is increasing its focus on higher-margin products and new offerings. Management released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 8, 2026, delivering improved profits but with mixed trends in user growth due largely to changes in regulatory markets.

Overall, the period demonstrated robust financial progress, but also underlined the importance of expanding its customer base for future momentum.

Notable Developments
Revenue rose 16.8% as increased average user spending offset a decline in active payers.
Profitability improved, with adjusted EBITDA up 63.7% and net income positive for the first time in a first quarter.
Monthly Unique Payers dropped 4%, mainly due to the exit from the Texas Lottery market.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained its guidance for fiscal 2026, targeting $6.5 billion to $6.9 billion in revenue and $700 million to $900 million in adjusted EBITDA. It continues to invest in new technology and legal advocacy for market expansion. Investors should watch for recovery in user growth, especially as core categories such as Sportsbook and Predictions are prioritized for further rollout.

Quarterly Highlights

Profitability advanced sharply, with the adjusted EBITDA margin expanding as stronger revenue per user and improvements in sportsbook net revenue margin came through. The increase in ARPMUP, now at $131 per user, reflects more effective engagement and product mix even as total active payers declined.

The number of paying customers dropped to 4.2 million during the period, down 4% from the prior year, a change attributed to the company's withdrawal from Texas lottery markets; excluding that, payers in core categories increased by 2%. Revenue growth continued to rely more on spending by existing users than on net new customer wins.

Management kept guidance unchanged despite exceeding the internal run-rate needed for the upper end of its forecast, underscoring a cautious approach in light of ongoing regulatory and user acquisition headwinds. Investment in legal, advocacy, and product innovation--with $26 million spent on regulatory advocacy this quarter--remains a key part of its long-term strategy.
