Key Takeaways
Niu Technologies posted strong revenue growth on surging domestic e-scooter sales but saw profitability deteriorate as operating expenses climbed.
Revenue increased 33.4% year over year as the company sold 28.7% more e-scooters.
Second quarter guidance points to continued revenue growth, but international business remains a concern.

Niu Technologies (NASDAQ:NIU) rode robust e-scooter demand in China to revenue growth, but higher operating costs deepened its losses. The leading smart electric scooter maker reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 18, 2026, as it benefitted from new product launches with AI features and a focused brand push at home while its international operations continued to struggle. Overall, the quarter highlighted domestic momentum amid persistent overseas headwinds and heightened cost pressures.

Notable Developments
China e-scooter sales and units both surged, with domestic sales making up nearly 94% of total volume and up 35.4% year over year.
Adjusted net loss (non-GAAP) widened sharply to RMB 88.0 million as total operating expenses jumped 59.7%—well above the pace of revenue growth.
International markets saw a 32.4% drop in e-scooter units sold and ongoing issues with aged inventory, which put pressure on gross profit.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects second-quarter revenue between RMB 1,570 million and RMB 1,821 million, or a year-over-year increase of 25% to 45%. No volume or margin outlook was provided, and international recovery is not specifically discussed. Investors should watch how well emerging products and marketing efforts support consistent growth, and if the company can reduce losses despite persistent international shortfalls.

Quarterly Highlights

Strong demand for AI-integrated electric scooters in China, including new models built on the NIU AIOS operating system, propelled Q1 2026 unit sales to 261,624—up 28.7% year over year. Domestic franchised stores remained steady, and China now accounts for about 94% of all e-scooter sales, showing growing reliance on the home market.

Despite improved revenue per e-scooter and a stable gross margin, heavy investment in brand campaigns and intensified marketing—especially during the Chinese New Year period—pushed selling and marketing expenses up to almost 20% of revenue. Rising general and administrative costs, including large foreign exchange losses, further weighed on the bottom line.

International results continued to lag. E-scooter sales volumes overseas slipped by nearly a third. While the average selling price in international markets rose sharply, this did not compensate for the steep fall in units or the continuing need to write down inventory, echoing the prior quarter’s commentary about clearing aged stock before recovery is possible.
