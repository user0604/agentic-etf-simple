Key Takeaways
iHeartMedia reported a quarter of growth in digital audio but continued margin pressure in its legacy broadcast business.
Adjusted EBITDA declined 11.4 percent year over year as cash flow moved further negative.
Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 financial targets and unveiled additional cost saving measures.

Digital gains continued for iHeartMedia (NASDAQ:IHRT), but rising costs and tightening margins weighed on its first quarter of fiscal 2026. The country's largest owner of radio stations and a top publisher of digital audio and podcasts grew total revenue but saw profitability fall, challenging its balance of growth and efficiency. The earnings release, dated May 11, 2026, covers the first quarter and showed progress in digital and podcasting offset by a tougher operating backdrop.

Overall, the quarter mixed strong growth in digital segments with higher expenses and cash outflows, making future progress on profit margins and free cash flow a focus.

Notable Developments
Digital Audio Group revenue rose 18 percent driven by strong podcast growth, but its margin dropped to 26.5 percent from 31.4 percent.
The Multiplatform Group's Adjusted EBITDA margin fell by 5.3 percentage points due to rising operating costs and moderate revenue growth in radio.
Free cash flow moved further negative, with a $114.5 million outflow; management cited timing of receivables and higher interest payments as key drivers.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its fiscal 2026 goals for Adjusted EBITDA of about $800 million and free cash flow of $200 million, and announced an additional $50 million in annualized cost savings. Guidance for the next quarter calls for revenue growth in the low single digits and a return to higher EBITDA. The most important thing to watch is whether expense controls and cash generation improve as political advertising ramps up later in the year.

Quarterly Highlights

The Digital Audio Group, which includes podcasting and streaming, drove much of the company’s revenue growth. This segment benefited from a 26.9 percent jump in podcast revenue, even as its profitability stagnated due to higher digital content and marketing costs.

In the Multiplatform Group, which centers on broadcast radio, revenue edged higher compared to last year, but operating expenses increased faster. As a result, the segment’s Adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 14.8 percent to 9.5 percent, highlighting ongoing margin challenges from spending on marketing and trade initiatives.

Free cash flow stayed firmly negative as both higher cash use for daily operations and interest payments weighed on results. The company’s cash balance halved to $135 million compared to year-end, adding focus to liquidity and cost reduction as priorities.

What iHeartMedia Does

iHeartMedia operates the nation’s largest broadcast radio network and is a leading provider of digital audio and podcast content across multiple platforms.

Watch for continued updates on cash flow and margin trends in upcoming quarters.
