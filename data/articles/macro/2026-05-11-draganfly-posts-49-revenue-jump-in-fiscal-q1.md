Key Takeaways
Draganfly notched another quarter of strong sales growth, but continued to report sizable losses as costs outpaced revenue gains.
The company’s revenue rose 49.4% compared to the prior year, while gross profit improved 12.2%.
Management did not provide forward financial guidance, highlighting a need to focus on operational leverage and cost discipline in coming periods.

Drone maker Draganfly (NASDAQ:DPRO) delivered robust sales expansion for the first quarter but saw expenses escalate, keeping the business in the red. The company develops advanced unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), ground robots, and related software for use in public safety, defense, and industry. In results released on May 11, 2026, for the period ended March 31, Draganfly’s revenue continued to climb, though its operating losses widened on higher operating expenses and ongoing share dilution. Overall, the quarter reflected positive revenue trends but raised concerns about the sustainability of cost growth and ongoing losses.

Notable Developments
Total revenue grew nearly 50% year over year, driven by higher product sales to Canadian customers and early growth in services.
Operating expenses more than doubled, led by higher employee, office, travel, and research & development costs.
The company shored up its balance sheet with a major equity financing, raising its cash balance to $147.34 million but diluting existing shareholders.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue specific forward-looking financial targets in this quarter’s release. Executives previously highlighted plans to grow revenue through product expansion and new industry applications, but the current quarter’s sharp cost increases stand out. The single most important area for investors to monitor is whether rising expenses can be brought in line with revenue growth to support a roadmap toward profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Total revenue for the quarter advanced to $2.31 million, with the vast majority coming from drone product sales; service revenue also grew substantially but remained a small portion of the total. Most sales continued to come from the Canadian market, with only minimal US contributions. Management emphasized that research and development expenses, which increased by nearly four times compared to the prior year, supported the expansion of its lineup, including advanced first-person-view drones and ground robotics.

The company’s gross profit improvement lagged behind revenues, as gross margin (the percent of sales left after direct costs) narrowed due to rising inventory costs and a charge for obsolete inventory. Rapid increases in expense line items—such as office, travel, and personnel—help explain why total operating expenses rose to $7.96 million, far ahead of the pace of revenue growth for the period.

On the financial front, Draganfly’s cash reserves were significantly boosted by an equity raise that increased shares outstanding and contributed to a sharp rise in cash to $147.34 million. As a result, shareholders’ equity rose sharply, though the dilution is material when compared to the prior year’s share count. The company maintains that its cash supports continued investment and operational flexibility while it pursues growth across product categories and geographic markets.
