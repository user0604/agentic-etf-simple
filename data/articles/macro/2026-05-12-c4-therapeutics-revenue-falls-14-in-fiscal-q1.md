C4 Therapeutics Revenue Falls 14% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 7:16 AM

Key Takeaways
Gradual progress in pipeline execution, but collaboration revenue declined as partnerships were scaled back.
Net loss per share improved to $0.20, while revenue fell year over year.
Management reaffirmed cash runway guidance through 2028, highlighting cemsidomide data and new partnerships as key upcoming milestones.

C4 Therapeutics (NASDAQ:CCCC) advanced its core myeloma drug program but saw a drop in collaboration revenue as older partnerships wound down. The clinical-stage biotechnology company develops targeted protein degraders for cancer, aiming to address significant unmet medical needs. On May 12, 2026, it released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, showing a sharper focus on its lead program and a shift in partnership strategy. Overall, the period demonstrated steady operational progress, especially in clinical trial execution, but softer financial results as legacy collaborations scaled back.

Notable developments
The company advanced its Phase 2 and Phase 1b clinical programs for cemsidomide, including the first patient dosing in both trials during the quarter.
Revenue dropped 13.9% year over year, mainly due to the end of a Merck (NYSE:MRK) partnership, while a new collaboration with Roche (SIX:ROG) brought a $20 million upfront payment in May 2026.
R&D expenses decreased 9.2% as the company shifted resources away from CFT8919 in markets outside Greater China.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintained that its cash balance supports operations through 2028, assuming current spending levels. It emphasized upcoming clinical data for cemsidomide and the impact of new deals such as the expanded Roche collaboration. Investors should closely monitor progress in the cemsidomide trials and how effectively the company can secure additional partnerships or milestone payments to offset declining legacy revenue.

Quarterly highlights

The advancement of multiple cemsidomide trials in multiple myeloma, including the Phase 2 MOMENTUM study and a Phase 1b combination trial, was a core operational achievement for the quarter. These trials are part of the company’s effort to position cemsidomide as a future backbone treatment in this cancer type, with early data updates scheduled for later in the year.

Total revenue fell as previous collaboration agreements, particularly with Merck, concluded or narrowed in scope. Though near-term revenue is under pressure, the $20 million upfront from the new Roche collaboration (focused on degrader-antibody conjugates) and a $2 million milestone from the Biogen (NASDAQ:BIIB) partnership provided some offset and validate external interest in the company’s TORPEDO platform for targeted protein degradation.

The decision not to advance CFT8919, an EGFR degrader for lung cancer, outside Greater China reflects a narrowing of strategic focus and capital allocation. Management attributed this move to available clinical data and capital priorities, making the company’s fortunes more closely tied to the success of its core myeloma program in the near term.

What C4 Therapeutics does

C4 Therapeutics develops targeted protein degrader therapies designed to treat cancer by harnessing the body’s own protein recycling machinery to eliminate disease-causing proteins.
