Earnings Preview: StepStone Group

May 18, 2026, 9:05 AM

StepStone Group (NASDAQ:STEP), a global private markets investment firm that builds customized portfolios for institutional and private wealth clients, is expected to report its fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on Wednesday, May 20, 2026, after the market closes.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, StepStone Group delivered results that beat analyst expectations for both earnings and revenue. The company reported adjusted net income of $0.65 per share and fee-related earnings of $89 million, both representing significant growth from the prior year. Assets under management (AUM) reached $220 billion as of Dec. 31, 2025, driven by record fundraising activity and strong inflows across its private equity, infrastructure, private debt, and real estate strategies. The private wealth platform, which offers funds to high-net-worth and mass affluent individuals, continued to expand, ending the quarter with $15 billion in assets and over $2.2 billion in new subscriptions. Management noted that performance fees were particularly strong, especially from the Spring venture and growth equity fund, which benefited from high returns in artificial intelligence (AI) and technology investments.

Positives
Record fundraising and AUM growth: StepStone added over $8 billion in new assets during the quarter, resulting in its highest ever twelve-month fundraising total of $34 billion. This demonstrates strong demand for private market solutions and supports future fee growth.
Strong fee-related earnings and margin: Fee-related earnings rose 20% year over year, and the fee-related earnings margin improved to 37%. This shows that the company is scaling efficiently as it grows.
Exceptional performance fees from Spring: The Spring fund delivered a 39% return, which was a major driver of incentive fees and overall profitability. Most of this performance came from actual investment gains, not just markups, highlighting the fund’s ability to identify winners in the innovation and AI sectors.
Diversified fundraising across regions and strategies: Inflows were well balanced geographically, with two-thirds coming from outside North America. This diversification reduces reliance on any single region or product.
Negatives
High compensation and expense growth: Total expenses increased 16% year over year, mainly due to higher performance-based compensation and ongoing investments in conferences, technology, and headcount. This puts some pressure on margins even as revenues grow.
Reduced retroactive fee contributions: Retroactive fees, which can boost reported revenues, were much lower than the prior year. This means core revenue growth was strong but headline growth was less pronounced than last year’s quarter.
Legacy Greenspring performance drag: The company reported a negative contribution from legacy Greenspring carried interest allocations due to unrealized depreciation in certain fund investments. This offset some of the gains elsewhere.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report
Fee-related earnings trajectory and margin sustainability: Investors should watch whether StepStone can maintain or improve its fee-related earnings margin, especially as it continues to hire and invest in new initiatives.
Performance fee outlook for Spring and other funds: Management has indicated that Spring’s incentive fees may normalize after an exceptional year. The level of realized performance fees in Q4 will be important for understanding future earnings power.
Fundraising momentum and AUM growth: The pace of new asset inflows, especially in private wealth and international markets, will signal whether StepStone can sustain its record fundraising streak into fiscal 2027.
Progress on fund launches and new vintages: The company is in market with several new funds, including private equity co-investment, secondaries, and infrastructure vehicles. Updates on fundraising progress and activation of fee-earning capital will be key, as will any announcements about upcoming launches in real estate or growth equity.
Expense discipline and compensation trends: With expenses rising in Q3 due to conferences and hiring, investors should look for signs that cost growth is moderating or being offset by higher revenues in Q4.
