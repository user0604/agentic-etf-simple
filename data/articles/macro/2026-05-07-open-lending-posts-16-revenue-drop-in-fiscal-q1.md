Open Lending Posts 16% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:43 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Loan volume and profitability fell as new strategic initiatives take effect.
Revenue declined 16% year over year.
Management reaffirmed full-year loan and adjusted EBITDA guidance, expecting improvement in the second half.

Auto-lending software provider Open Lending (NASDAQ:LPRO) reported lower loan volumes and earnings as it continued transitioning toward higher-quality, lower-risk originations. The company, known for its data-driven lending platform and specialized risk analytics, released first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, for the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter highlighted ongoing volume and revenue pressure but progress in product and portfolio improvements.

Notable Developments
Certified loans fell 24% year over year to 21,064, though this landed just above the top end of management’s Q1 guidance.
Total revenue dropped 16% compared to the same period last year, with the largest decline in program fee revenue.
Management increased the share repurchase authorization to $50 million and reaffirmed full-year guidance for loans and adjusted EBITDA.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained its outlook for 100,000–110,000 certified loans in fiscal 2026 and $25–29 million in adjusted EBITDA. It signaled confidence in a second-half rebound, projecting a ramp-up in loan volume beginning in the next quarter. Investors should monitor volume growth, profit margins, and any financial impact from new products like ApexOne Auto and Project Red Rocks.

Quarterly Highlights

Loan volumes remain below last year’s levels as the company enacted tighter credit standards and pivots to higher-quality borrowers. Of the 21,064 certified loans, roughly 90% came from credit unions and banks—a strategic shift that management says boosts stability and loan quality. Profit share revenue per loan rose to $363 from $278 a year ago, reflecting this shift, but the overall revenue decline showed these efficiency gains have yet to offset lower total volume.

Total revenue fell to $20.5 million as both program fee and claim administration service revenue declined. Gross profit and adjusted EBITDA both decreased, and a net loss replaced last year’s net income for the period. The company cited higher operating expenses and some non-recurring charges—factors that together tightened margins while Open Lending invests in technology and business mix changes.

Open Lending extended and expanded its share buyback program, with $20.1 million remaining on the new $50 million authorization as of quarter-end. Cash and liquidity remained stable at $173.3 million. Management described the company’s risk tools, including Project Red Rocks and the new ApexOne Auto platform, as progressing on plan, though their financial contribution is expected later in the year.

What Open Lending Does

Open Lending operates a platform that helps automotive lenders assess and price loans for near-prime and non-prime borrowers, combining advanced risk analytics with default insurance to enable more efficient and secure lending decisions.
