Dear Fools,

Jensen Huang spoke, and the market listened. Our Marvell Technology (NASDAQ: MRVL) position nearly doubled this week after Nvidia's (NASDAQ: NVDA) CEO casually suggested it might become a trillion-dollar company.

Was he joking? Doesn't matter. Investors bought first and asked questions later. And Huang didn't stop there. Among his market-moving comments at the GTC Taipei conference, he sparked surges in the optical networking and system builder industries. As a result, Lumentum (NASDAQ: LITE) caught a double-digit tailwind.

At the same time, leadership concerns and sector rotation pressured some of our fintech and software holdings. Our returns were mixed this week, and we're trailing the S&P 500 by 7 points as of this writing on May 3.

That's to be expected from a 50-stock portfolio with just six weeks of history, of course. The market often makes unexpected moves in the short term, but we're here to build long-term value.

Stocks in the News

Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB) fell back to Earth after the recent SpaceX IPO furor. Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) CEO Jeff Bezo's Blue Origin ran a rocket burn test at Cape Canaveral that ended in an explosion so massive I heard it in the Tampa area. It felt like slow, rumbling thunder from a clear sky. The "anomaly" caused significant damage to the launch pad and set Blue Origin's launch plans back by several months.

The incident didn't directly involve Rocket Lab, but space stocks sold off en masse.

In other news, Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) (NASDAQ: GOOGL) is raising $80 billion,5 including a direct $10 billion investment from Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE: BRKA) (NYSE: BRKB) to fund its AI infrastructure buildout. When you're planning to spend up to $190 billion in a single year, even the Google parent needs outside capital.

While $80 billion is enough to rank among the top 200 companies by market cap, it's only 1.8% dilution for Google. The stock offering underscores the capital intensity of Alphabet's AI buildout.

One of our picks reported business results this week. Medtronic (NYSE: MDT) beat earnings estimates and posted its strongest revenue growth in a decade, but fiscal 2027 bottom-line guidance came in below consensus. That was still good enough for a 5% price surge, helped by a modest dividend increase and an AI collaboration with Nvidia.

Stock in Focus: Nu Holdings

Financial tech specialist Nu Holdings (NYSE: NU) is having the kind of year that makes you grateful for 50-stock portfolios.

This week, Nu announced CFO Guilherme Lago's departure, and the market reacted with a 12% price cut. The Brazilian neobank's stock is now down 32% year to date.

This C-suite move marks a significant transition for the company. Lago joined Nu in 2019, steered it through its 2021 IPO, and oversaw the company's explosive growth from less than 40 million customers to more than 135 million today.

Analysts and investors are uncomfortable with his exit even though his replacement comes with sterling credits. New CFO Rob Livingston was recently the CFO for Visa (NYSE: V) North America, and he also spent 18 years in the leadership team of Capital One (NYSE: COF).

Livingston's US background suggests that Nu is exploring expansion in the world's largest economy. There are other signs, of course. The company received conditional approval for a US bank charter in January. And Livingston will be based in the US, which tells you where management's attention is focused.

The negative market reaction makes sense. CFO transitions are often paired with structural business problems, and it seems even more risky to take that step in a period of ambitious growth plans with international overtones. Furthermore, Lago is not the only top-level executive to move away from Nu's C-suite in recent years. The company installed a new COO last year, following exits at the CPO, CTO, and CCO (Chief Credit Officer) posts in 2024.

At the same time, we're not panicking over our Nu pick.

For one, Lago is leaving on good terms, staying on as a "special advisor" for the long haul. Furthermore, heavy executive turnover can mean several things. In this case, it looks like a targeted strategy of building the kind of financial expertise needed for operations in the US.

The valuation has compressed significantly. The stock trades well below historical multiples, which means the market has already priced in quite a bit of skepticism. If Livingston executes well on the US expansion and the core business continues performing, there should be meaningful upside from these levels.

For now, we're holding and watching. Nu's next earnings report should provide commentary on the CFO transition and updates on US expansion timing.

This is execution risk in action -- the kind of risk you can manage by not betting everything on a single position. We own 49 other stocks for exactly this reason.

Mailbag

Epic Portfolio member higheredadmin22 is concerned that Lumentum and Marvell might not be the top dogs in the optical networking space. Higheredadmin22 notes that while Lumentum maintains a near-monopoly on 200G EML lasers today, Coherent's (NYSE: COHR) roadmap for much faster co-packaged optics could position it better for the 2030-plus timeframe.

This is an excellent question. AI data center networking is going through significant architecture upgrades, and Coherent will offer compelling products in the next generation.

Here's the thing, though: We don't need to pick the single winner. Many companies are positioned for success in the optical networking sector, and Lumentum stands out as a top pick in 2026.

First, architectural transitions usually take longer than anticipated, and multiple technologies coexist in the transition periods. Lumentum's dominant market position generates cash flow today while the co-packaged optics transition unfolds.

Second, we already hold Marvell Technology, which manufactures digital signal processors that work with optical components from multiple suppliers. No matter which optics specialist runs away with the next hypergrowth opportunity, Marvell's control chips will almost certainly benefit.

That's intentional portfolio construction.

We don't need Lumentum to dominate until 2030 and beyond. We need it to perform well over our investment horizon while other positions (like Marvell) capture different opportunities.
