Booz Allen Hamilton Holding (NYSE:BAH), a leading provider of technology consulting and cybersecurity solutions for government and commercial clients, is expected to report its fourth-quarter fiscal year 2026 earnings on Friday, May 22, 2026, before the market opens.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

Booz Allen Hamilton Holding reported third-quarter results on Jan. 23, 2026, that were in line with revised guidance and exceeded analyst expectations for earnings per share. The company delivered adjusted diluted EPS of $1.77, beating the consensus estimate of $1.27. Revenue for the quarter was $2.62 billion, representing a 10% decline year over year, mainly due to the impact of a prolonged U.S. government shutdown and a slower procurement environment. Despite these headwinds, the company demonstrated strong cost discipline and margin performance, with adjusted EBITDA of $285 million and a margin of 10.9%. Booz Allen Hamilton Holding also reported a record year-end backlog of over $38 billion, up 2% from the prior year, and noted growing demand in its national security and civil businesses.

Positives
Adjusted EPS beat expectations: The company’s adjusted diluted earnings per share exceeded analyst consensus, reflecting effective cost management and a lower tax rate.
Strong margin performance: Adjusted EBITDA margin held steady at 10.9% despite revenue headwinds, showing operational discipline in a challenging environment.
Record backlog and pipeline growth: Year-end backlog reached over $38 billion, with the qualified pipeline for fiscal 2027 up 12% year over year, indicating future growth potential.
Progress on strategic initiatives: The company advanced its transition to outcome-based contracting, launched the Velox Reverser cybersecurity product, and announced a strategic partnership with Andreessen Horowitz (a16z) to accelerate technology adoption in government.
Negatives
Revenue declined year over year: Total revenue fell 10% compared to the prior year due to government shutdown impacts and slower funding, which also affected headcount and billable expenses.
Continued weakness in civil business: The civil segment saw a 28% year-over-year revenue decline, reflecting ongoing challenges in government priorities and procurement delays.
Temporary shift in billable expenses: About $60 million in national security billable expenses were pushed from Q3 into Q4, which may affect quarter-to-quarter comparisons.
Cost reductions yet to fully impact results: Significant cost actions were completed, but the full benefit to profitability will not be realized until next fiscal year.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report

Margin trends and cost savings realization: Watch for any early impact from the $150 million annual cost reduction program and how margins hold up as spending patterns normalize in the fourth quarter.
Backlog conversion and new contract awards: The ability to convert record backlog into revenue and secure new awards, especially in national security and civil segments, will be key for future growth.
Progress on outcome-based and fixed-price contracts: Track updates on the shift toward outcome-based models, including the expansion of fixed-price work like the Thunderdome cybersecurity program, as these are expected to support margin expansion over time.
Execution of strategic partnerships and product launches: Monitor the rollout and customer uptake of new offerings such as Velox Reverser and the status of the Andreessen Horowitz partnership, as these initiatives are intended to drive innovation and open new growth avenues.
