Alliance Resource Partners Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 7:06 AM

Comments

Alliance Resource Partners, L.P. (NASDAQ:ARLP) is one of the largest coal producers in the eastern United States, with growing operations in oil and gas mineral royalties. The company’s long-term growth strategy emphasizes stable cash flows from its coal assets—secured by multi-year contracts—while investing free cash flow into expanding its oil and gas minerals portfolio. This diversification is designed to reduce earnings volatility as coal markets evolve and to position the business for resilience amid shifting energy demand and regulatory dynamics.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Diversified Royalties Portfolio Delivers Stable, Growing Cash Flows Beyond Coal Cyclicality

Strengthening oil and gas royalty income is transforming Alliance Resource Partners, L.P. from a traditional coal producer into a more resilient, cash-generative energy platform. In Q1 2026, oil and gas royalty segment revenue reached $41.3 million, a 14.6% year-over-year increase on a GAAP basis, while adjusted EBITDA rose over 15%. Record barrels of oil equivalent volumes (up 16.1% from the prior year) and management’s commitment to reinvest after-tax cash from royalties into further minerals acquisitions support a visible, long-tail earnings growth engine. This diversification cushions the company from coal market downturns and commodity price swings, and with full-year 2026 royalty volume guidance raised by approximately 5% after a strong start, unhedged exposure to rising energy prices could provide meaningful upside.

2. Strategic Contracting Positions Minimize Near-Term Volume and Pricing Risk

High forward contract coverage locks in cash flows and shields Alliance Resource Partners, L.P. from spot market volatility in the near term. As of Q1 2026, over 95% of projected coal sales volumes for the year are already secured at the midpoint of management’s price guidance. This disciplined contracting approach ensures revenue stability and allows the company to weather potential headwinds from softer spot markets or milder-than-expected electricity demand. The company’s customer base—primarily utilities seeking grid reliability and longer-term commitments due to data center growth and power market constraints—offers additional visibility. Ongoing requests for proposals from power generators indicate robust end-market demand, and successful incremental contracting could keep utilization and margins resilient for at least the next several quarters.

3. Cost Structure Improvement and Operational Visibility Support Margin Expansion Potential

Recent operational milestones enhance cost discipline and set the stage for improved profitability in the second half of 2026 and beyond. The completion of longwall moves at key mines—most notably Tunnel Ridge—has enabled Appalachia per-ton adjusted EBITDA expenses to fall by 10.8% year over year in Q1 2026, improving efficiency despite top-line pricing normalization. With no major production interruptions planned until 2027 and reduced reliance on higher-cost outside coal purchases, the company appears positioned to benefit from greater operating leverage as volumes normalize. Management expects coal segment margins to recover in the second half of 2026 as transitional cost headwinds abate and better mine sequencing takes effect, reinforcing free cash flow generation and supporting potential future capital returns.

4. Regulatory and Utility Policy Shifts Extend Coal Demand Visibility

Recent regulatory developments are extending the operational runway for U.S. coal power, directly benefiting Alliance Resource Partners, L.P. Key Environmental Protection Agency actions in 2026 have reduced compliance costs and increased operational flexibility for coal-fired utilities, leading many customers to announce resource life extensions into the next decade. As utility operators prioritize reliability—particularly for data center power and in regions lacking renewable alternatives—the company’s Illinois Basin and Appalachian coal is likely to remain an integral part of the generation mix. Management’s positive commentary and strong contract coverage reflect confidence that coal demand will remain stable for several more years, providing a tailwind absent in most other North American coal scenarios.

5. Conservative Balance Sheet and Prudent Capital Allocation Create Downside Protection and Optionality

A robust liquidity position and disciplined capital expenditure approach underpin downside resilience and position the company for strategic opportunities. As of March 31, 2026, the company’s total and net leverage ratios were just 0.73x and 0.69x trailing twelve months adjusted EBITDA, while total available liquidity stood at $431.2 million. The company’s measured approach to capital expenditures—with Q1 2026 capex above run-rate solely due to a one-off reserve purchase, and spending expected to moderate—supports healthy distribution coverage (1.0x in Q1 2026), but management has indicated no plans for buybacks or distribution increases until distribution coverage improves to the 1.2x–1.4x range. This financial flexibility provides a buffer against macro or industry shocks and allows management to capitalize on accretive mineral acquisitions as opportunities arise.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Ongoing Coal Pricing Normalization and Legacy Contract Roll-Offs Erode Profitability

Profit pressure from lower coal prices and less favorable contract mix is a significant risk to future cash flows and distribution stability. The average coal sales price per ton dropped by 6.5% year over year in Q1 2026, as legacy contracts signed during the 2022 energy crisis rolled off and were replaced by lower rates, particularly in the Appalachia region. This trend, combined with a 7.4% year-over-year price decrease in the Illinois Basin, could compress segment margins if cost savings or export arbitrage do not offset top-line declines. While most 2026 volumes are contracted, the company faces increased pricing risk for open positions in later periods, especially if summer demand is weak or the export market remains uncompetitive. Prolonged coal price normalization could hinder the company’s ability to achieve its targeted distribution coverage ratio and limit future capital returns.

2. Operational Uncertainty and Asset Impairments Undermine Consistency

Asset-specific challenges such as the Mettiki mine impairment introduce unpredictability into earnings and expose the company to further downside surprises. In Q1 2026, the cessation of longwall production at Mettiki resulted in a $37.8 million noncash impairment charge, highlighting the risks of operating aging or geologically complex coal assets. While increased production at Tunnel Ridge is partially replacing lost volumes, product mix shifts have lowered average realized prices and may not fully replace segment profitability. Persistent uncertainty around future Mettiki operations and the possibility of additional impairments could strain the coal portfolio’s earnings power and reduce investor confidence in long-term cash flow projections.

3. Reliance on Oil & Gas Royalties Increases Exposure to Volatile Commodity Markets

Growing dependence on unhedged oil and gas royalty income could introduce new earnings volatility as energy prices fluctuate. The company’s oil and gas royalties offset coal cyclicality and reached record performance in Q1 2026, but these earnings are highly sensitive to swings in oil and natural gas prices—markets that can move sharply based on global events or economic cycles. While recent conflict-driven price spikes provided a temporary boost, the lack of hedging means lower prices could rapidly erode the segment’s contribution to distributable cash flow. This exposure, combined with longer payback periods for mineral acquisitions, raises the risk that future market downturns or unsuccessful investments could offset gains from diversification and challenge the sustainability of distributions or reinvestment.
