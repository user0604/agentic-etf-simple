Endava Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

7:09 AM

Comments

Endava plc (NYSE:DAVA) is a global technology consulting and digital transformation services provider specializing in designing, building, and implementing modern, AI-driven solutions for clients across industries such as financial services, payments, insurance, and healthcare. The company’s recent growth strategy centers on a deliberate pivot toward AI-native delivery, integrating proprietary automation platforms and deepening sector expertise while forging strategic partnerships with leaders like Mastercard, NatWest, OpenAI, and major hyperscalers. By rapidly reskilling its workforce and investing in new vertical-specific offerings, Endava seeks to transition from traditional digital consulting to capturing secular demand for enterprise AI, even as it navigates near-term market turbulence and client budget pressure.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Rapid Growth in AI-Driven Revenue Mix Accelerates Margin Expansion

The company’s AI-native repositioning is showing strong traction, with AI-related revenues rising sharply from 5% of sales in Q3 FY25 to 15% by Q3 FY26, outpacing legacy business declines. This rapid mix shift is not only fueling top-line resilience in weak discretionary spending environments but also directly improves operating margin potential. Management projects continued growth in this segment, positioning Endava to outperform as clients transition from experimentation to AI operationalization at scale.

2. Enterprise Partnerships with Leading Tech and Financial Firms Open Large, Long-Term Opportunities

Endava’s strategic alliances with major players such as Mastercard, NatWest, Google Cloud, and OpenAI are embedding it into mission-critical digital transformation programs within payments, banking, and healthcare. These partnerships go beyond simple joint marketing—Endava is actively co-creating AI solutions, like the payments accelerator for NatWest and immersive marketplaces with Mastercard, that can unlock multi-year service revenues and serve as reference clients for broader industry adoption. The pipeline of new client pilots and marketplace launches suggests that as pilot projects convert, the company will be central to vertical AI deployment within regulated, complex industries. This ecosystem position could ensure sustained demand and pricing power as the transformation wave accelerates industry-wide.

3. Early-Stage Innovation Pipeline Enables Differentiated, Verticalized Offerings

Proprietary platforms such as Dava.Flow and PGX, along with sector-focused innovation like AI underwriting for insurance, differentiate Endava as clients seek turnkey, outcome-based digital transformation solutions. As of Q3 FY26, 10 new marketplace offerings are live, with Dava.Flow deployed in 12 client engagements, and the company has enhanced its rollout with policy-as-code automation and human-in-the-loop platforms. This rapid productization meets emerging client preferences for industry-specific AI solutions and helps reduce time-to-value. By offering accelerators proven to cut onboarding time by up to 30% in certain client deployments, Endava builds stickier, higher-margin client relationships and can outpace less-specialized consulting peers, strengthening competitive moats in high-growth verticals.

4. Strengthening Financial Foundation and Conservative Capital Allocation Prioritize Long-Term Investments

Despite top-line pressures, Endava ended Q3 FY26 with GBP 48.4 million in cash and cash equivalents and adjusted free cash flow of negative GBP 3.1 million, while borrowings increased to GBP 195.8 million. This prudent capital management enables the company to continue reinvesting in workforce upskilling and platform R&D, rather than being forced to cut back or take on dilutive financing. With a prioritized focus on AI investment and disciplined payout—no share buybacks were executed this quarter—Endava signals commitment to compounding long-term value, providing resilience and flexibility through market cycles.

5. Secular Shift Toward Enterprise AI Services Creates Massive Addressable Market

The global pivot to AI-driven automation, compliance, and modernization aligns directly with Endava’s deliberate strategy shift and positions the company to capture share of an expanding market. Clients increasingly demand secure, scalable, and regulated AI integration—trends acutely visible in payments, healthcare, and mobility. As organizations move beyond pilots to operationalize AI, Endava’s readiness is demonstrated by management reporting that over 75% of staff now use AI daily. With secular trends pointing to double-digit growth for enterprise AI services over the coming years, the company is well-placed to ride the next wave of digital transformation spending as macro conditions eventually normalize.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Extended Pipeline Conversion Times and Client Budget Caution Suppress Near-Term Revenue

Lengthened deal cycles and persistent client hesitancy on discretionary IT spending are weighing heavily on revenue growth and visibility. As of Q3 FY26, deal conversion rates lag historical trends, with average sales cycles stretching up to 5–6 months for AI-driven contracts, compared to 3–4 months previously. Enterprise clients, notably in banking and payments, continue to delay or scale back new project approvals, directly contributing to year-over-year revenue declines of 6–9% in key regions. With about 3% to 5% of Q4 FY26 revenue forecast still dependent on last-minute pipeline conversion, the risk of continued revenue misses and further guidance cuts remains high in the near term.

2. Margin Compression and Reskilling Costs Outpace AI Revenue Uplift During Transition

The company faces significant profit headwinds from necessary upfront investment in AI platform adoption, workforce retraining, and go-to-market expansion, outpacing the margin benefits realized so far from higher-margin AI contracts. Although Endava's AI-driven business has higher margins than its traditional digital transformation business, this remains a minority of overall sales, and the drag from underutilized staff and legacy business declines keeps overall profitability below historic norms. If the revenue mix does not shift more rapidly, Endava could struggle to restore operating leverage and risk disappointing on margin recovery targets.

3. Intensifying Competitive and Legal Risks Could Disrupt Execution and Investor Confidence

Endava faces mounting competitive threats from both large global IT firms and niche AI consulting upstarts, while also contending with an active legal investigation that could undermine stakeholder trust. The company’s smaller scale in AI revenue compared to major rivals like EPAM or Globant means it has less room for error should enterprise adoption remain slow, particularly as competitive pricing and bundled offerings become more prevalent. Recent news of a law firm investigating potential securities law violations by Endava adds another layer of uncertainty—such probes can create management distraction, investor caution, and market volatility, regardless of the eventual legal outcome. With legacy banking clients facing heightened competition from larger service providers, execution missteps or reputational hits could exacerbate client attrition and slow the turnaround in revenue growth.
