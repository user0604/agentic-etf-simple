Liberty Latin America Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 3:07 PM

Comments

Liberty Latin America Ltd. (NASDAQ:LILA) is a telecommunications provider operating across Latin America and the Caribbean, offering mobile, fixed broadband, video, and B2B services. The company’s growth strategy focuses on expanding higher-value postpaid mobile subscriptions, driving fixed-mobile convergence, investing in advanced network technologies (such as 5G and submarine cable infrastructure), and unlocking value through partnerships and cost efficiencies. Its diversified geographic footprint allows for selective growth, but it faces volatility from macro conditions, competition, and regulatory events in its core markets.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Postpaid Mobile Growth Supports Recurring Revenue and Margin Expansion

Accelerating postpaid mobile subscriptions elevate revenue quality and profitability – During 2025, the company added over 225,000 postpaid mobile subscribers—led by Costa Rica (+160,000 subscribers, up 16% year over year), with positive momentum unfolding in Panama and a return to net postpaid adds in Puerto Rico by Q4. This shift from prepaid to more predictable, higher-ARPU postpaid contracts supports lower customer churn and increases long-term lifetime value. In regions such as the Caribbean, fixed-mobile convergence penetration has reached 40%, providing a structural tailwind for ongoing upsells. As postpaid growth continues, it is expected to drive higher OIBDA margins and durable recurring revenue, which are essential for supporting future free cash flow and capital returns.

2. Major Infrastructure Investments Lay the Foundation for High-Margin Future Growth

Strategic projects in submarine cables and 5G propel long-term profitability – Significant investment in network upgrades and regional data infrastructure, such as the MANTA (5,600 km) and El Salvador submarine cable systems, positions the company as a key player in Latin American data transport by late 2027 or early 2028. Liberty Networks reported 21% adjusted OIBDA growth in Q4 2025 and 6% full-year wholesale revenue growth, benefits that are expected to scale as these projects come online. The first-mover rollout of 5G standalone in Costa Rica, already reaching 300,000 users, not only solidifies technological leadership but also enables richer product bundles and ARPU expansion. These capital-intensive undertakings are designed to unlock future high-margin, recurring revenues in wholesale, B2B, and advanced consumer segments.

3. Disciplined Cost Control and Capital Efficiency Drive Strong Cash Flow Recovery

Margin improvement and lower capital intensity enhance cash flow resilience – In 2025, adjusted OIBDA grew 9% year over year while OIBDA margins improved by 300 basis points, reflecting disciplined cost initiatives and operational efficiency. Property and equipment (P&E) additions fell to $640 million (14% of revenue), down from 16% in the prior year, demonstrating stronger capital discipline. As a result, adjusted OIBDA less P&E additions rose by 27% to $1.1 billion (24% of revenue), with Q4 adjusted free cash flow before partner distributions up 29% year over year. These improvements in core cash flow metrics provide flexibility to navigate market headwinds, fund growth initiatives, and ultimately support shareholder returns.

4. Deepening Strategic Partnerships and Technology Integration Enable New Revenue Streams

Collaborations with Amazon Web Services (AWS) and digitalization initiatives create competitive advantages – The recently announced integration with AWS goes beyond a simple reseller agreement, embedding AWS compute and AI capabilities within the company’s data centers in Panama and Colombia. This strategic partnership accelerates internal cost efficiencies, product innovation, and supports the rollout of managed cloud, AI, and resilience services for local business customers. With over-the-top (OTT) content integration into converged consumer offerings and the adoption of AI-powered customer service, the company is leveraging technology to capture higher-value B2B contracts and reduce opex, positioning itself favorably as enterprise demand for digital solutions in Latin America soars over the next several years.

5. Recovery and Stabilization in Key Markets Set the Stage for Shareholder Returns

Operational turnaround in challenged markets creates a platform for capital returns – The group is on track to reach pre-hurricane profitability levels in Jamaica by the end of 2026, with over 75% of fixed broadband customers already reconnected and mobile data volumes back above pre-storm figures as of early 2026. In Puerto Rico, signs of stabilization are emerging with improving net promoter scores and the first positive postpaid net add quarter since migration disruptions began. Management has reiterated its commitment to capital returns, stating that concrete plans for rewarding shareholders are likely to be announced in the second half of 2026 as free cash flow recovers, signaling confidence in the underlying trajectory and providing an additional catalyst for long-term investors.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Continued Structural Profitability Challenges Weigh on Bottom Line and Investor Confidence

Persistent GAAP losses and negative margins highlight unresolved financial pressure – For Q1 2026, the company reported a negative GAAP EPS of -$0.11, missing consensus estimates and resulting in a negative net margin of 11.2%. These ongoing headline losses—despite strong OIBDA performance—reflect the heavy burden of fixed costs, high depreciation/amortization, and interest expense from elevated debt levels. Persistent GAAP unprofitability can depress investor sentiment, restrict access to competitive financing, and may limit the pace or scale of future capital returns even if adjusted cash flow metrics remain robust.

2. Elevated Leverage, Particularly in Puerto Rico, Increases Balance Sheet and Refinancing Risk

High debt loads restrict financial flexibility and raise default or dilution risk – Consolidated debt reached $8.4 billion at the end of 2025, with a group net leverage ratio of 4.3x adjusted OIBDA; excluding Puerto Rico, leverage falls to the mid-3s, but LPR itself is highly leveraged, with segment net leverage near 8x and restricted sub leverage over 14x. Though 75% of borrowings are due after 2031, Puerto Rico may need further liquidity injections or liability management transactions to fund ongoing operations. Any deterioration in cash flows or failure to execute a clean segment separation could increase the risk of covenant breaches, asset sales at unfavorable prices, or shareholder dilution from additional secured or preferred financing—particularly if macro or market conditions worsen.

3. Macroeconomic, Regulatory, and Severe Weather Risks Create Persistent Operational Volatility

Diverse geographic exposure amplifies sensitivity to external shocks – The company operates in regions frequently hit by hurricanes, as evidenced by Hurricane Melissa in Jamaica, which forced the removal of 133,000 homes passed and will result in a $100 million adverse free cash flow impact in 2026. Regulatory setbacks, such as the blocked merger in Costa Rica and competitive pricing pressure in fixed broadband, have hindered revenue growth and required major pivots in strategy. Intense price competition, currency fluctuations, and government intervention remain ongoing risks in several markets. These factors may cause uneven performance across geographies, increased costs, or delays in recovery and profitability—ultimately threatening the pace at which the company can deliver on growth and shareholder return ambitions.
