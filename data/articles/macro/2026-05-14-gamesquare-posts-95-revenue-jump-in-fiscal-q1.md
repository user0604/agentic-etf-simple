Notable Developments
Revenue climbed to $14.5 million during the period, almost doubling from the prior year as new creator partnerships and acquisitions boosted sales.
Gross margin narrowed by 4.1 percentage points, falling to 38.4% largely due to changes in service mix and costs, even as proforma margin improved with TubeBuddy integration.
The net loss widened mostly from a $14.6 million fair value loss on digital assets, highlighting increased reliance on cryptocurrency holdings in the company's treasury.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance for proforma revenue of $85 million to $90 million, gross margin between 35% and 40%, and adjusted EBITDA above $5 million. It reported accelerating sales activity early in the next quarter, supported by recent talent additions and expanded partnerships. For investors, the most important factor to watch will be whether profitability and margin targets can be met amid ongoing digital asset volatility and execution demands from new integrations.

Quarterly Highlights

The period's almost 95% revenue growth was driven by the integration of TubeBuddy, new creators like "Steak," and deeper relationships with major brands in gaming and consumer goods. Revenue reached $15.8 million on a proforma basis, reflecting the impact of acquisitions and broader platform reach, though much of anticipated incremental revenue is still to materialize in future quarters.

Profitability improved on an adjusted EBITDA basis, narrowing the loss to $1.1 million from $2.6 million a year earlier, signaling stronger operating discipline. However, gross margin compression on a GAAP basis pointed to rising service delivery costs, while proforma margin remained higher at 43.1% as efficiencies from TubeBuddy began to show through.

GameSquare's digital asset strategy was a double-edged sword, with treasury assets of $35.9 million significantly buffering liquidity but a sharp $14.6 million loss from marking digital holdings to market. Cash stood at $2.4 million at the end of March, down from $4.6 million the prior quarter, as buybacks and integration costs absorbed cash resources.
