Key Takeaways
Vaalco Energy reported a challenging quarter marked by a steep financial loss as sales volumes dropped.
Earnings per share swung from an adjusted profit of $0.06 a year ago to an adjusted loss of $(0.45), while total commodity sales revenue fell 43%.
Management raised its full-year production and sales guidance, expecting stronger results as new projects come online.

Vaalco Energy (NYSE:EGY) faced a difficult start to the year, with lower sales volumes and a significant net loss. The independent oil and gas producer focuses on assets in West Africa, including Gabon, Côte d’Ivoire, and Egypt. For the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (reported May 7, 2026), it saw weak cash flow and higher debt as new drilling and operational projects increased costs. Overall, the period marked a low point as new production had not yet contributed to revenue.

Notable developments
Sales volumes dropped 36% to 12,157 barrels of oil equivalent per day, mainly due to the timing of government liftings in Gabon and no sales in Côte d’Ivoire.
The company posted a net loss of $93.8 million, driven by a $70.6 million loss on oil price hedging and a $22.4 million exploration write-off.
Vaalco completed the sale of its Canadian assets for $25.5 million, sharpening its focus on West Africa, while capital spending came in below guidance.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management kept its annual capital spending target unchanged and raised the midpoint of full-year production and sales guidance, citing stronger expected liftings and new projects. It expects results to improve in the second quarter as Côte d’Ivoire production resumes and Gabon sales timing normalizes. Investors should watch for the turnaround in cash flow as higher volumes start contributing to results.

Quarterly highlights

Sales volumes and revenue were affected by one-time timing issues in Gabon and the absence of Côte d’Ivoire sales, along with the exit from Canadian operations. These factors led to a sharp drop in key financial metrics, with adjusted EBITDAX, a measure of profitability before certain expenses, falling nearly 80% compared to the prior year.

Losses on oil price hedges played a major role in the bottom-line result. The expanded hedging program led to a $70.6 million charge, reflecting both changes in oil prices and realized trading losses. The unsuccessful exploration well in Gabon resulted in a $22.4 million expense, highlighting the risks of drilling new areas.

Operationally, the company brought new wells online in Gabon and started a new drilling campaign in Egypt, aiming for growth later in the year. The sale of Canadian assets further focused its portfolio on West Africa. The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.0625 per share.
