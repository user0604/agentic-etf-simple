Key Takeaways
Strong progress in high-performance computing (HPC) hosting offset major losses and rising costs.
Revenue was flat year over year as HPC lease income replaced bitcoin mining.
Management reaffirmed aggressive annual capacity targets but gave no updated margin guidance.

TeraWulf (NASDAQ:WULF) delivered on its transition to high-performance computing hosting, but large net losses and sharp cost increases weighed on results. The infrastructure operator, which is shifting away from bitcoin mining, reported results for the first quarter on May 8, 2026, reflecting both strategic milestones and serious financial challenges. Overall, the quarter highlighted major revenue mix improvements amid worsening profitability and capital intensity.

Notable Developments
HPC lease revenue reached $21.0 million, now making up well over half of the total, while bitcoin mining revenue dropped to $13.0 million.
Despite recurring contracts, the company posted a GAAP net loss of $427.6 million, far higher than the $61.4 million loss last year.
Cash and restricted cash swelled to $3.1 billion, supporting new campus buildouts and site acquisitions.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management restated its plan to add 250–500 megawatts of new contracted compute capacity annually, indicating continuing high investment and site expansion. The most critical factor for investors now is when the shift to recurring HPC revenue will yield clear, sustainable operating profits given current cost pressures.

Quarterly highlights

The period marked a turning point as HPC hosting generated the majority of revenue for the first time, with $21.0 million in lease income stemming from new long-term agreements at Lake Mariner. Repurposing legacy bitcoin mining capacity contributed to a sharp fall in digital asset revenue, down to $13.0 million from $34.4 million a year earlier.

Operating expenses soared, as total selling, general, and administrative costs more than doubled and the company booked major non-cash charges such as $216 million for warrant fair value changes, $101 million in stock-based compensation, and a $25.7 million asset impairment. These factors drove adjusted EBITDA to stay negative at ($4.1 million), only a slight improvement year over year.

Cash reserves rose dramatically to $3.1 billion, funded by recent capital raises and a new $250 million revolving credit facility. Property, plant, and equipment also jumped, reflecting ongoing large-scale campus and infrastructure investments necessary for the company’s multi-year expansion strategy.

What TeraWulf does

TeraWulf operates and develops energy-powered data centers specializing in high-performance computing hosting and digital asset (bitcoin) mining.
