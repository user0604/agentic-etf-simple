Gladstone Land AFFO Jumps 35% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 4:19 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Gladstone Land navigated a volatile quarter amid lower property holdings and a shift to participation-based farm leases.
Adjusted funds from operations per share rose 35.1%, while total operating revenue dipped slightly from the prior year.
Management expects most 2026 revenue and profits to be recognized in the fourth quarter, so investors should monitor Q4 outcomes.

Farmland owner and lessor Gladstone Land (NASDAQ:LAND) managed through a period of transition this quarter as it dealt with fewer farms in its portfolio and an increasing reliance on farm income tied to seasonal harvest outcomes. The company, which acquires and leases farmland to third-party growers, reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. For the period, stable dividend distributions and stronger cash flow measures offset a decline in total revenue and an overall net loss, highlighting continuing unpredictability in earnings timing.

Notable Developments
Farm portfolio shrank to 144 farms covering 98,688 acres, both down 4% from the prior year, reflecting ongoing property sales and some vacancies.
Adjusted funds from operations (AFFO) rose to $0.08 per share as participation rent revenue jumped nearly $4.5 million due to an early bonus payment on pistachios.
The common dividend stayed level at $0.14 per share, marking the 159th consecutive monthly payout.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management highlighted that most of this year’s revenue and earnings will be recognized in the fourth quarter, due to a shift to leases that tie payment to crop performance and pricing. They continue to describe these participation-based leases as temporary, planning to return to fixed rents when possible. Investors should watch Q4 closely, as late-year crop results are likely to determine full-year dividend coverage and overall profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

The number of farms and acres in the company’s portfolio declined compared to a year earlier, signaling continued property sales or repositioning efforts. Net investments in real estate dropped 5.1% year over year, and total assets fell to $1.20 billion, a 6.1% decrease.

The jump in participation rent income, mainly from pistachios, provided a boost to core operating measures like AFFO. However, this revenue is recognized only after processors finalize crop bonuses, which may cause most earnings to be recorded later in the year.

Dividend distributions remained flat at $0.14 per share for the quarter. Despite stable payouts, the coverage ratio is high, and the ability to maintain distributions for the year depends on strong results from Q4’s harvest-dependent revenues.

What Gladstone Land Does

Gladstone Land is a real estate investment trust that owns and leases farmland to independent farmers, focusing on a diverse mix of fruit, vegetable, and nut crops across the United States.
