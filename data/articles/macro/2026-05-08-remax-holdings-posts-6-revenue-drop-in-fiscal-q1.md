Key Takeaways
RE/MAX Holdings posted a challenging quarter as U.S. agent numbers and core recurring revenue declined.
Adjusted earnings per share fell to $0.16, and revenue decreased compared to the prior year.
With a pending merger and no forward guidance, near-term visibility remains limited.

RE/MAX Holdings (NYSE:RMAX) faced a difficult quarter as its U.S. agent count dropped and key recurring revenues declined. The leading real estate franchisor continued to experience pressure on its domestic business, along with higher costs tied to legal settlements and a pending merger. The first-quarter 2026 results, released on May 8, 2026, highlight significant headwinds and a period of transition for the company. Overall, the quarter reflected operational stress, with international agent growth only partially offsetting ongoing weakness in the North American business.

Notable developments
Recurring franchise fees and annual dues fell by 10.2%, with U.S. agent count down 4.8% while international agent numbers increased 6.7%.
Operating expenses rose 13% from the prior year, including $8.5 million in legal settlement and impairment charges related to an industry lawsuit.
The company entered into a definitive merger agreement with The Real Brokerage Inc., leading it to suspend earnings guidance and quarterly calls during the merger process.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide financial guidance for upcoming quarters due to the ongoing merger with The Real Brokerage Inc. The main focus for investors is now on the merger’s closing and how the combined company will address the substantial RE/MAX debt and agent retention in its core U.S. market.

Quarterly highlights

Core recurring revenue, which includes franchise fees and annual dues, declined and made up a smaller share of total revenue. The company attributed this decrease to changes in fee models and a shrinking U.S. agent count, partially offset by growth in broker fees. International agent growth remained a positive, with substantial year-over-year increases, but this was not enough to offset declines in the larger, higher-margin U.S. business.

Total operating expenses climbed to $78.1 million, driven mainly by legal settlements, higher event costs, and spending on technology initiatives. This cost pressure contributed to weaker profitability, with adjusted EBITDA down 19.3% and adjusted free cash flow worsening to negative $5.4 million for the quarter.

The announcement of the merger with The Real Brokerage Inc. marked a strategic shift. Management stopped quarterly guidance and earnings calls, reflecting the uncertain near-term outlook while the deal moves forward. The level of debt that Real Brokerage will assume is a key point of focus for the next period.

What RE/MAX Holdings does

RE/MAX Holdings is a franchisor of real estate brokerages and mortgage services, operating a network of independently owned offices and agents across the United States, Canada, and internationally.
