USA Compression Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 7:06 AM

Comments

USA Compression Partners, LP (NYSE:USAC) is a leader in providing natural gas compression services, operating one of the largest fleets in the United States. The company’s growth strategy centers on expanding fleet capacity, executing large-scale acquisitions like JW Power, leveraging in-house manufacturing, and capitalizing on secular tailwinds from the U.S. energy infrastructure buildout—including liquefied natural gas (LNG) export growth and surging data center power needs. With fixed-fee contracts and a Master Limited Partnership (MLP) structure, the company targets steady cash flows and disciplined capital allocation to support sustainable distributions and long-term growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership Drives Pricing Power and Revenue Growth

Leading market share and customer relationships enable record-high contract pricing and growth in core revenue metrics – In the first quarter of 2026, USA Compression Partners, LP set a new all-time high in average revenue per horsepower at $22.73 per month—a sequential increase of 5% and an 8% year-over-year rise. More than 90% of the nearly 110,000 new horsepower set to be added in 2026 is already contracted, reflecting robust demand and exceptional customer stickiness. This pricing leverage is amplified by fixed-fee contracts with Consumer Price Index for All Urban Consumers (CPI-U) escalators, providing reliable inflation pass-through. Sustained revenue growth, even amid cost pressures, directly strengthens distributable cash flow and supports a premium distribution yield, underlining why investor sentiment remains positive despite quarterly volatility.

2. Strategic Acquisitions Accelerate Fleet Scale and Geographic Expansion

Synergistic acquisitions fast-track fleet growth and deepen reach across critical basins – The closure of the JW Power acquisition in January 2026 expanded total fleet horsepower by approximately 1.037 million, lifting the company’s positions in key production areas such as the Northeast, Permian, and Gulf Coast. Management expects to achieve $10–20 million of annual run-rate synergies from integration efforts by year-end 2027, having already merged operations, reporting structures, and manufacturing systems. By achieving scale, the company is better positioned to win strategic contracts with large producers and respond nimbly to market shifts, supporting both top-line and bottom-line gains as integration milestones are hit throughout the next two years.

3. In-House Manufacturing Provides Strategic Flexibility and Supply Chain Resilience

Direct control over engine procurement lets the company outmaneuver industry-wide supply bottlenecks – With JW Power’s in-house manufacturing arm, the company now manages a capital program extending through 2028/2029, placing engine deposits several years in advance to secure supply. This flexibility matters as industry-wide lead times for large engines (such as Caterpillar 3,600 series) have tripled to as much as 150 weeks due to competing demand from power generators for artificial intelligence (AI) data centers. Because the company only commits a deposit (25–40% of skid cost), it can redeploy engines or resell them to third parties if market dynamics change, thus preserving capital discipline and maximizing return on investment even as the broader sector grapples with chronic supply chain delays.

4. Robust Cash Flow and Distribution Coverage Support Attractive Yield and Future Increases

Strong distributable cash flow (DCF) coverage and conservative leverage policy underpin a compelling and sustainable yield – In Q1 2026, the company reported a DCF coverage ratio of 1.72x, comfortably above its 1.6x target, supporting its $0.525 per unit quarterly dividend (a roughly 7.7% yield based on a recent $27.28 unit price). Net Debt to EBITDA improved to 3.74x in the quarter, hitting management’s near-term target, with guidance for leverage to trend lower by year-end. This balance sheet improvement, along with cautious distribution policy, provides both downside protection and potential for payout growth if performance remains strong over subsequent quarters. Investors increasingly prize such reliable, inflation-protected cash flows in a volatile macro environment.

5. Secular Energy Infrastructure and LNG Export Boom Fuel Multi-Year Demand Visibility

Massive U.S. LNG buildout and surging energy demand ensure a strong demand runway for compression services – Management projects that U.S. market demand will require over 10 million incremental compressor horsepower by 2030, driven by at least five new LNG facilities entering service in the next 24 months and sustained power requirements from new data centers. The company’s presence in every major U.S. basin, especially its outsized share in the Northeast and extensive Gulf Coast footprint, positions it at the center of this growth. With already contracted horsepower well ahead of prior years and multi-year procurement agreements with major customers underway, the company enjoys rare earnings visibility and is structurally aligned with some of the most durable trends in U.S. energy markets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Margin Pressure from Cost Inflation and Integration Dilution

Operating margins face headwinds from higher input costs and lower-margin acquisition mix – Q1 2026 net income per limited partnership unit was $0.27, $0.08 below consensus EPS estimates. While fixed-fee contract structures provide some inflation pass-through, not all cost pressures are immediately neutralized, and integration headwinds may linger. If these factors persist, they could erode future cash flow and constrain the company’s ability to raise distributions or fund growth without external capital.

2. Supply Chain and Equipment Lead Time Risks Could Disrupt Growth Plans

Extended equipment lead times and supply constraints threaten deployment efficiency and capital productivity – Lead times for key engine components have soared to 150 weeks, nearly tripling over the past two years due to surging demand from sectors like AI-powered data centers. While the company’s manufacturing flexibility partially mitigates this risk, execution still depends on timely access to new horsepower to meet contractual obligations and capitalize on market demand. Should global supply chains further deteriorate, or if alternative engine sourcing falls short of needs, growth rates and return on invested capital could face unexpected shortfalls, undermining investor confidence in multi-year expansion targets.

3. Commodity Price and Regulatory Volatility Could Impact Demand and MLP Structure

Volatile natural gas and oil prices, as well as regulatory or structural shifts, could destabilize cash flows and the investor base – The company’s outlook is closely tied to favorable natural gas prices and robust upstream activity, both of which are subject to commodity cycles and unpredictable geopolitical events (e.g, supply disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz). Any sustained downturn could depress compression demand, increase churn, or drive customers to lower-cost alternatives. Additionally, as an MLP, the company faces tax reporting complexities (notably with Schedule K-3) and the risk that regulatory or tax reforms could impact the relative attractiveness of the structure versus traditional corporations, potentially reducing long-term investor demand or increasing the cost of capital.
