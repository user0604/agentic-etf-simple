Odysight.ai (NASDAQ:ODYS) faced a setback this quarter as large defense customers significantly delayed purchase orders, leading to a steep shortfall in sales. The advanced AI-driven visual sensing technology company operates in aerospace, defense, and industrial monitoring markets. Its latest results for the first fiscal quarter, released on May 14, 2026, show that timing issues drove results well below the prior year. Overall, the quarter highlighted the company’s exposure to contract timing, even as management remains confident in the underlying business drivers.

Notable developments
Revenue fell approximately 96% year over year to $82 thousand for the three months ended March 31, 2026, as several key defense customers delayed purchase orders, creating a sharp year-over-year decline.
Gross margin held stable at 26% despite the decrease in total sales, reflecting the company’s product economics.
Operating expenses grew modestly to approximately $5.4 million, mainly due to investment in sales and marketing and new market expansion initiatives.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expressed confidence that delayed orders would convert to revenue later in 2026, keeping full-year business targets unchanged. The company highlighted an expanding roster of pilots and partnership activities, especially in the United States and Europe. Investors should pay close attention to backlog conversion and whether customer diversification efforts begin to offset concentration risk in the coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

This quarter, revenue nearly disappeared as major defense clients in Israel and the U.S. deferred orders due to operational priorities and regional geopolitical constraints. The $14 million backlog as of March 31 gives some visibility but management noted that customers can delay or cancel future orders at their discretion.

Gross margin stayed at 26%, illustrating the company’s ability to preserve its pricing model even as overall revenue plunged. Operating expenses increased to $5.36 million, reflecting continuing investments in expanding its U.S. and European presence, as well as higher share-based pay.

Odysight.ai continues to build future growth platforms with active pilots in aerospace and industrial sectors, including a key Cooperative Research and Development Agreement with the U.S. Navy and new European industrial deployments. The quarter also marked its dual listing on the Tel Aviv Stock Exchange, intended to broaden access for international investors.

What Odysight.ai does

Odysight.ai develops and sells AI-driven vision-based sensing technology for predictive maintenance and condition-based monitoring, helping aerospace, defense, and industrial customers track equipment performance and anticipate failures.
