Key Takeaways
Ondas capitalized on strong demand and recent strategic acquisitions to deliver record quarterly growth.
Revenue soared to $50.1 million, up 1,065% year over year, with gross margin improving substantially.
Management raised full-year revenue guidance but acknowledged ongoing operating losses and cash needs as integration efforts continue.

Ondas (NASDAQ:ONDS) surged past its previous targets as new product wins and acquisitions fueled a dramatic expansion. The defense and industrial autonomy company delivered breakthroughs in both revenue and backlog during the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (reported May 14, 2026). Despite rapid growth and early milestones in profitability at the product level, increasing operating expenses and continued losses underscored both the scale and challenges of this transformation.

Overall, the quarter marked a period of record growth alongside proven momentum in capturing new business and integrating recent acquisitions.

Notable Developments
Backlog reached $457 million, up sharply from $68.3 million at the prior quarter's end, mostly driven by recent acquisitions and new orders.
Product subsidiaries achieved positive adjusted EBITDA for the first time, although group-level adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $10.9 million.
Cash reserves climbed to $1.48 billion, primarily from a $1 billion capital raise completed in January 2026, providing ample capacity for further integration and investment.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased its full-year 2026 revenue target to at least $390 million, anticipating revenue growth from both organic operations and additional acquisitions. Adjusted EBITDA losses are expected to peak in the second quarter, then gradually improve as scale efficiencies emerge and integration advances.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue growth was fueled by large awards in key product segments—including counter-drone systems and ground robotics—that positioned the company as a multi-domain provider for defense, government, and commercial clients. Major contributions came from newly acquired businesses and from new contracts in high-growth markets like Europe and the Middle East.

Gross profit and gross margin both improved year over year, reflecting revenue scale, a more favorable mix of higher-margin products, and integration of acquired companies. However, elevated operating expenses—such as personnel costs, acquisition-related fees, and non-cash charges—continued to offset gains at the operating level.

The company achieved a milestone as its product businesses turned adjusted EBITDA positive sooner than previously projected. Even so, headline net income reflected almost entirely non-cash items, rather than ongoing business activity, highlighting the importance of monitoring the company's core operating trends going forward.
