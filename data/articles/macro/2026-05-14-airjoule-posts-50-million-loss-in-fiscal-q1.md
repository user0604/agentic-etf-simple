AirJoule Technologies marked another quarter of significant net losses as it continued to invest in product development and partnerships without yet generating commercial revenue. The leading innovator in atmospheric water generation and dehumidification met internal targets for product milestones and partnership development during the first quarter, as reported on May 14, 2026, for the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter reflected disciplined operational progress but underscored the ongoing risks and financial challenges before the company’s initial products reach the market.

Notable Developments
There was a significant net loss of nearly $50 million in the quarter, primarily driven by joint venture losses as the business ramped up operations.
The company reported achieving major technical milestones, including the completion of its first full-scale AirJoule Prime system and finalizing the design of the Core platform.
Cash reserves increased to $31.1 million at quarter's end after an equity raise in January, with management reiterating that cash should support operations through 2027.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its focus on completing remaining product certifications and preparing for the first commercial launches of the Core system in late 2026. The most important factor for investors to watch remains the conversion of active pilot programs and customer engagements into signed contracts and recurring revenue, particularly as the company transitions from pre-revenue to commercial sales.

Quarterly Highlights

Technical progress was evident as AirJoule completed the first AirJoule Prime system, a large-scale water-from-air generator aimed at data centers and industrial clients, and locked the design for the smaller Core platform. These technical achievements position the company for its initial commercial attempts late in the year or in 2027, but market readiness depends on completing ongoing product certifications and regulatory approvals, including UL safety certification and water-quality compliance.

On the financial front, the company absorbed a large net loss as upfront investments in its GE Vernova joint venture continued to weigh on results, and no meaningful commercial revenue was reported for the period. Research and development expenses decreased compared to the prior-year quarter, reflecting some cost shifting to the joint venture as product builds moved forward.

Liquidity improved after a January equity offering, with the company ending the period with $31.1 million in cash and $3.9 million at the joint venture, and no debt reported. Management reaffirmed guidance that this capital should support planned operations, joint venture activities, and the first commercial deployments through at least 2027.

What AirJoule Technologies Does

AirJoule Technologies develops energy-efficient systems that harvest water from atmospheric humidity and provide advanced dehumidification for commercial and industrial uses.
