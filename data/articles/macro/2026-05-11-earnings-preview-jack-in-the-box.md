Earnings Preview: Jack in the Box

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 3:03 PM

Comments

Jack in the Box (NASDAQ:JACK), a quick-service restaurant chain focused on burgers, tacos, and all-day breakfast, is expected to report its fiscal second-quarter earnings after the market closes on Wednesday, May 13, 2026.

Q1 FY2026 Recap

Jack in the Box reported first-quarter results for the sixteen weeks ended Jan. 18, 2026. The company delivered results that were broadly in line with management’s expectations and analyst consensus. Same-store sales declined 6.7% systemwide, with company-operated locations down 4.7% and franchise locations down 7.0%. The company completed the sale of its Del Taco business in December 2025, focusing now solely on the Jack in the Box brand. GAAP diluted earnings per share from continuing operations were $0.75, with adjusted operating earnings per share at $1.00. These results met analyst expectations for the quarter.

Restaurant-level margins declined to 16.1% from 23.2% a year ago, driven by higher food, packaging, and labor costs. Commodity inflation, especially in beef, remained a significant pressure point. The company continued to execute its JACK on Track plan, including debt reduction and restaurant refreshes, while also rolling out new marketing tied to its 75th anniversary.

Positives
Debt reduction progress: The company made a $105 million prepayment on its debt following the Del Taco sale, improving its balance sheet and reducing interest expense.
Early signs of sales improvement in January: After a slow start, sales trends improved in January, helped by new marketing tied to the 75th anniversary and product launches like the Chicken Supreme Munchie Meal.
Operational simplification and cost control: Management reduced selling, general, and administrative expenses by $4.1 million year over year, reflecting tighter cost discipline and the benefit of transition services income from the Del Taco divestiture.
Restaurant refreshes showing positive impact: Low-cost mini-refreshes at select restaurants delivered low single-digit sales lifts, encouraging further expansion of the program in key markets.
Negatives
Declining same-store sales: Systemwide same-store sales fell 6.7%, reflecting lower transactions and a challenging consumer environment, especially at franchise locations.
Squeezed restaurant margins: Restaurant-level margin dropped to 16.1% from 23.2%, pressured by 7.1% commodity inflation (mainly beef), higher labor costs, and increased utilities.
Ongoing Chicago market inefficiencies: Labor costs in newly opened Chicago restaurants remained elevated, with the company yet to activate full menu, digital, and 24-hour operations in that market.
Franchisee performance lagging: Franchisees experienced greater sales and margin pressure than company-operated locations, partly due to less participation in digital promotions and higher input costs.
What to Watch in Q2 FY2026's Report
Same-store sales trajectory and impact of weather: Watch for updated same-store sales trends, especially as management noted improvement early in Q2, but also flagged a winter storm that could impact results by up to 70 basis points for the quarter.
Margin recovery and commodity cost trends: Investors should focus on whether food and labor cost pressures, particularly in beef, begin to moderate as guided, and how this affects restaurant-level margins.
Progress on Chicago market turnaround: The company is targeting improved efficiency and the launch of digital and 24-hour operations in Chicago. Any update on this initiative could signal future sales and margin upside.
Status and results of restaurant refresh and reimage programs: Look for data on sales lifts from expanded mini-refreshes and any timeline or financial details for the broader reimage program, especially potential incentives for franchisees.
Debt refinancing plans and capital allocation: With additional debt repayments planned and refinancing likely in the coming months, updates on capital structure and use of real estate sale proceeds will be important for assessing financial flexibility.
