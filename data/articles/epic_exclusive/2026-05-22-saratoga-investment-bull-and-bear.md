Saratoga Investment Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 7:09 AM

Comments

Saratoga Investment Corp. (NYSE:SAR) is a business development company specializing in providing financing to lower middle market private companies across diverse industries. Its growth strategy is built on disciplined origination, a focus on senior secured first lien loans, and targeted sector shifts away from riskier software toward stable, recurring-revenue businesses, all while maintaining robust credit quality and a consistently high level of management ownership to align interests with shareholders.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Sustained Outperformance in Return on Equity Signals Superior Capital Allocation

Consistently high return on equity demonstrates durable operational excellence. For the fiscal year ended February 2026, Saratoga Investment Corp. achieved a 9.1% return on equity (ROE), surpassing both its previous year’s 7.5% and the business development company (BDC) industry average of 4.3%. Over the past twelve years, Saratoga’s long-term average ROE has been 10.1%, besting the 6.7% industry mean in 9 of 12 years. This outperformance suggests that management’s investment and expense controls are highly effective, increasing the likelihood of continued shareholder value creation even as market dynamics evolve. Higher ROE, relative to peers, signals that capital is being selectively deployed into accretive assets, which could support premium valuation and attractive long-term returns for investors.

2. Strong Asset Growth and Origination Pipeline Support Scalable Earnings Expansion

Double-digit asset growth enables future income generation. Saratoga posted a 13.4% increase in assets under management (AUM) in fiscal 2026, alongside $101.1 million in net new investments for the fourth quarter. Origination activity remained robust, with $135.1 million in new deployments during the fourth quarter alone. The deal pipeline has deepened, with six out of ten platform investments in fiscal 2026 stemming from new sponsor relationships. This influx of assets provides scale to offset potential yield compression and supports future net investment income (NII) growth, positioning the company to benefit if lending market conditions remain favorable or improve in the coming quarters.

3. Disciplined Credit Underwriting and Low Nonaccruals Limit Downside Risk

Exceptional credit quality reduces the risk of capital losses. At the end of Q4 2026, 96.8% of Saratoga’s portfolio was rated in its top internal credit tier, and the company carried a nonaccrual rate at cost of just 1.2%—less than one-third the BDC industry average. Portfolio markdowns remain modest, with only two investments on nonaccrual status and continued active management of challenged names. By emphasizing first lien lending (over 82% of the portfolio) and ongoing diversification across 43 industries, Saratoga enhances the resilience of its asset base, making it less vulnerable to cyclical downturns or sector-specific shocks. Superior credit metrics help preserve net asset value (NAV) through uncertain markets.

4. Dividend Stability and High Management Ownership Align Incentives for Shareholders

Reliable payouts and insider alignment make total returns more dependable. The company’s monthly dividend was maintained at $0.25 per share as of May 2026, equating to a 12.6% annualized yield at a $23.89 share price. Dividend coverage, though occasionally supplemented by spillover income, remains a stated management priority, with the Board reviewing sustainability each quarter. Notably, Saratoga’s management and directors own 11% of the company, one of the highest insider ownership rates in the BDC universe. This high alignment increases confidence that strategic and capital decisions will prioritize consistent total returns, making the shares potentially attractive for income-focused investors.

5. Strategic Portfolio Shifts Reduce Vulnerability to Disruptive Technology Risks

Active sector rotation mitigates exposure to high-risk software and artificial intelligence disruption. Management is executing a deliberate reduction in software and SaaS loan exposure, prompted by increased underwriting standards in light of accelerated technological disruption risk from artificial intelligence. The company’s realized track record in software lending is strong—35 realized deals produced a 14.4% internal rate of return over 13 years with no realized losses—but ongoing portfolio additions are now focused elsewhere. By shifting new capital toward secular growth sectors such as healthcare and education, Saratoga aims to sustain credit quality and NAV stability, potentially differentiating its portfolio through cycles where technology risk may impair peer BDC holdings.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Dividend Payouts in Excess of Net Investment Income Threaten Long-Term NAV Preservation

Persistent overdistribution erodes book value and may pressure future dividends. In fiscal 2026, Saratoga distributed $3.74 per share in dividends while only generating $2.32 per share in net investment income, covering just over 60% of distributions from current earnings. This $1.42 per share gap directly matched the $1.44 year-over-year decline in NAV per share. While management clarified this excess was drawn from previously undistributed NII, repeated reliance on such spillover suggests dividends may not be fully sustainable solely through recurring earnings. Without a rebound in investment income generation, continued overdistribution could necessitate future dividend cuts or lead to gradual NAV dilution.

2. Margin Compression and Competitive Pressure Could Stifle Future Earnings Growth

Narrower origination spreads threaten core profitability. Saratoga's latest results highlight that new investment spreads have tightened by as much as 200 basis points compared to loan repayments, reflecting increased competition for quality deals in private credit. While AUM growth partially offsets this, there is a tangible risk that future net investment income will decline if asset yields compress faster than asset growth or if further market entrants intensify price competition. Prolonged periods of margin pressure may also result in less flexibility to support current dividend levels or maintain above-industry ROE.

3. CLO and Structured Product Exposure Introduces Mark-Down and Nonaccrual Risk

Continued volatility in CLO (collateralized loan obligation) investments can impair returns and NAV. In Q4 2026, the company’s CLO F note was placed on nonaccrual and fully written down, with CLO and joint venture markdowns totaling $5.5 million. Although such assets now comprise a declining share of the portfolio, these losses illustrate the risk inherent in structured credit products—particularly when cash flows weaken or refinancing markets tighten. If legacy CLO assets deteriorate further or if market stress forces additional markdowns, equity returns and NAV could be negatively impacted, complicating the company’s efforts to deliver steady shareholder value.
