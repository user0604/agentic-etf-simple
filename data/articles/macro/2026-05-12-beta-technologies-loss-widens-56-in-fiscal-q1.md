Notable Developments
The company added over $375 million in commercial aircraft backlog, reaching a total backlog of $3.9 billion across 991 aircraft orders at the end of the quarter.
Operating expenses increased by 62% year over year to $138.8 million, mainly due to higher research and development and administrative costs.
Beta expanded its charging network by 16 new sites and reported over 139,000 nautical miles flown with its electric aircraft.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year revenue target of $39 million to $43 million, but widened its anticipated adjusted EBITDA loss range to ($355 million) to ($445 million) due to elevated investment in research, development, and production. Investors should closely watch the pace of aircraft deliveries and the company’s ability to control costs as capital outlays grow.

Quarterly Highlights

The company achieved an industry-leading role in the Federal Aviation Administration’s eVTOL Integration Pilot Program, being chosen in seven out of eight launch projects, and completed critical testing milestones for its CTOL (conventional takeoff and landing) aircraft and propulsion systems.

Although overall revenue grew, product-related sales fell 61% year over year while service revenue increased 29%. This shift in business mix led to a substantial reduction in gross margin compared to the prior year quarter.

Beta’s commercial momentum accelerated, with notable new partnerships including Surf Air Mobility and an expanded agreement for charger deployments with the Florida Department of Transportation. The cash position at March 31, 2026, was $1.59 billion, providing considerable runway for ongoing development and expansion.

What Beta Technologies Does

Beta Technologies designs and manufactures electric aircraft and charging infrastructure for commercial, cargo, and defense markets, using a vertically integrated approach from batteries to flight control systems.
