1. Inflation Reaccelerates

Inflation data took center stage this week. The Producer Price Index (PPI) for April surged 1.4% month over month (far exceeding the 0.5% estimate), bringing the year-over-year rate to 6% -- its highest increase since 2022. This followed a Consumer Price Index (CPI) report showing annual inflation at 3.8%, the highest level in nearly three years. Surging energy costs, driven by the Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz, are the primary culprit, with gasoline prices soaring 28.4%, according to CPI data.

Rate cuts in 2026 look unlikely: U.S. Treasury yields spiked in response to the inflation prints, with the 10-year yield hitting 4.49%, its highest level since July 2025. On Wednesday, the U.S. Treasury auctioned 30-year bonds at a yield above 5% for the first time since 2007. CME FedWatch is pricing a 99.2% probability of rates staying unchanged at the June Fed meeting. Markets now price less than a 3% chance of a rate cut at any Fed meeting in 2026.
Not just oil: Brent crude remains above $100 a barrel, more than 40% higher than before the Iran war began in late February, and the energy shock is working its way through everything from airfares to grocery bills. "Inflation is sticky and accelerating. The core reading confirms a deeper structural trend, especially in services," said David Russell, global head of market strategy at TradeStation. "The Hormuz crisis is aggravating the problem, but this goes way beyond oil."
2. Trump-Xi Summit: The Strait, Tariffs, and the H200 Breakthrough

President Donald Trump arrived in Beijing this week for a high-stakes summit with Xi Jinping, accompanied by Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) CEO Elon Musk, Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang, and Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) CEO Tim Cook. The headline market catalyst came fast: Shortly after their first sitdown, Reuters reported that Washington had cleared sales of Nvidia's H200 artificial intelligence (AI) chips to roughly 10 major Chinese tech firms, including Alibaba (NYSE: BABA), Tencent (OTC: TCEHY), ByteDance, and JD.com (NASDAQ: JD) -- a potential breakthrough for China's AI sector and a significant win for Nvidia, although no deliveries have been made so far. Xi and Trump also agreed that the Strait of Hormuz must remain open, offering some relief to energy markets.

The mood in Beijing: The CEO delegation left the Great Hall of the People in visibly good spirits. Musk called the talks "awesome" and later said "many good things" had been achieved. Nvidia's Jensen Huang said, "Mr. Xi and President Trump were incredible." Tim Cook simply gave a peace sign and a thumbs-up. Xi, for his part, told the group that China's door would "only open wider" for foreign businesses -- and for now, at least, Wall Street is taking him at his word.
"The Taiwan question is the most important issue in China-U.S. relations." According to a post on X by Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning, Xi warned that the Taiwan question remains the most sensitive fault line in the relationship, cautioning that mishandling it could put the entire U.S.-China relationship in jeopardy. That's a reminder that not everything that came out of Beijing this week was market-friendly.
3. An Impressive Earnings Season

Despite two hot inflation prints, surging Treasury yields, and oil stubbornly above $100 a barrel, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq notched fresh all-time highs on Thursday before retreating on Friday to close the week. Strong corporate earnings continue giving investors permission to look past macro concerns and geopolitical volatility. According to FactSet (NYSE: FDS), as of last week, 89% of S&P 500 companies have reported earnings, with a blended earnings growth rate of 28% -- the strongest since Q4 2021, if it holds. Meanwhile, 84% of companies beat earnings expectations, the highest positive surprise rate since Q2 2021.

Countercurrent beneath the surface: Not all index data is painting a rosy picture. On Friday last week, the S&P 500 hit a fresh record high, while 5.6% of its components simultaneously hit new 52-week lows. According to Hedgeye Risk Management, that combination has occurred only three other times in history: July 1929, January 1973, and December 1999 -- each of which came shortly before significant bear markets. The signal doesn't guarantee a bear market, but the historical comparisons are interesting to consider.
Valuations that leave little room for error: The Shiller cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings (CAPE) ratio now sits near its second-highest reading in history -- the only period that exceeded today's levels was November 1999. The key difference this time: The AI capital expenditure build-out is translating into real earnings growth now, with "Magnificent Seven" profits leading the index higher. For now, investors appear more focused on future AI demand than rising oil prices or inflation risks.
4. Wall Street Wants AI Profits Now

With the first-quarter earnings season nearly complete, investors showed growing impatience with companies unable to clearly monetize AI. The market is increasingly rewarding businesses demonstrating tangible revenue growth and infrastructure demand (see: Hidden Gems recommendation Cisco's (NASDAQ: CSCO) blowout quarter) while punishing companies with vague narratives, margin pressure, or unclear payoffs.

"This is a stock that demands conviction and time." Stock Advisor and Rule Breakers recommendation Doximity (NYSE: DOCS) fell 27% this week after warning on its earnings call that AI revenue contributions would remain "minimal" this year. Sanmeet Deo wrote that while Doximity's physician network moat is widening, the company's AI monetization strategy "requires patience the market doesn't have."
"Little room for error." Hidden Gems and Stock Advisor recommendation Wix (NASDAQ: WIX) plunged 32% this week after reporting disappointing Q1 earnings. Matt Frankel commented on Wix's "jarring earnings miss," noting "growing concerns" about AI competition threatening Wix's core business. "The market tends not to give the benefit of the doubt, even when the strategic rationale for the spending is sound."
5. Cerebras Goes Public: The Biggest AI IPO Ever

AI chipmaker Cerebras Systems (NASDAQ: CBRS) made its long-awaited Nasdaq debut Thursday under the ticker CBRS, raising $5.55 billion after pricing at $185 per share, implying a valuation of $56.4 billion, making it the largest AI-focused initial public offering (IPO) in history. Cerebras builds large chips designed for fast inference, the part of the AI market focused on running models quickly rather than training them. The stock rocketed out of the gate, opening 89% above the IPO price and triggering a brief trading halt due to the sharp price movement. Cerebras stock closed the week at $279.72 per share, 51% above the IPO price.

The hype cycle: Cerebras twice raised its expected price range before ultimately pricing above even the revised target. That kind of momentum-driven frenzy is exciting, but history offers a cautionary tale -- investors who bought Stock Advisor recommendation Snowflake (NYSE: SNOW) near its first-day close are still sitting on a loss nearly five years later.
"The new stocks will hit the markets like a tsunami." Cerebras' explosive debut adds fuel to the fire of speculation around a coming wave of AI IPOs, with companies like SpaceX, OpenAI, and Anthropic reportedly considering future listings. "A lot of money is concentrated in one area: AI," wrote Rule Breakers analyst Alicia Alfiere in her commentary on the AI IPO boom, adding that one venture capitalist and MIT research fellow compared the AI IPO pipeline to a "tsunami" that could pull capital aggressively away from other parts of the market.
