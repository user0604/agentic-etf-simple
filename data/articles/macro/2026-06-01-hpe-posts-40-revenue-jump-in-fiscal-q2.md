HPE Posts 40% Revenue Jump in Fiscal Q2

Key Takeaways
Hewlett Packard Enterprise marked a business win with record revenue and exceeded its own upgraded guidance.
Earnings per share more than doubled year over year as revenue grew sharply.
Management raised its outlook for fiscal 2026, projecting higher full-year growth and profitability.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (NYSE:HPE) posted a standout quarter, setting new revenue and profitability records as customer demand in both networking and cloud surged. The global technology provider, focused on networking, hybrid cloud, and artificial intelligence, released its financial results for the second quarter of fiscal 2026 on June 1, 2026. Overall, the period reflected broad-based strength — especially in networking and servers — while also flagging some margin pressure and inventory build that may warrant scrutiny.

Notable developments
Networking revenue soared to $2.7 billion, up 148% from a year earlier, led by the completed integration of the Juniper acquisition. Operating margin in networking fell to 21.6%, dropping more than three percentage points from the prior year.
Cloud & AI segment revenue reached $7.7 billion, growing 22.9% year over year. Server revenue climbed nearly one-third, while storage and financial services saw more modest gains.
Inventory increased to $9.0 billion from $6.35 billion six months earlier, due to strategic efforts to secure supply amid strong demand; management noted this could affect future working capital and margins.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 guidance, now targeting revenue growth between 29% and 33% and non-GAAP earnings per share of $3.35–$3.45. The company expects continued strong performance, especially in networking and AI-related platforms. Investors should monitor networking segment margins and inventory levels, as ongoing integration costs and higher inventory could influence profit trends.

Quarterly highlights

The networking division recorded a sharp revenue jump, driven by the first full period with Juniper’s operations included. While this boosted the top line, networking margins fell to 21.6%, with management citing integration costs and a shift towards routing products.

Cloud & AI, the largest business, posted solid results with server revenue up over 32%, as customers invested in AI-optimized and traditional infrastructure. Segment operating margin climbed to 12.4%, above the company’s own recent expectations and compared both year over year and sequentially.

Hewlett Packard Enterprise completed the previously announced sale of its H3C China joint venture, bringing in $1.36 billion in proceeds this quarter. R&D spending also rose to $922 million, highlighting a focus on platform innovation for cloud and AI offerings. The quarterly dividend was raised 7.7% year over year to $0.14 per share.
