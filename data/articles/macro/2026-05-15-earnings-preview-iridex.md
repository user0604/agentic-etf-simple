Earnings Preview: IRIDEX

The Motley Fool Team

May 15, 2026, 9:03 AM

Comments

IRIDEX (NASDAQ:IRIX), a company that develops laser-based devices for treating glaucoma and retinal diseases, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings on Tuesday, May 19, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, IRIDEX delivered strong revenue growth and marked progress in its cost-saving initiatives. The company reported total revenues of $14.7 million for the quarter, a 16% increase year over year. This performance was driven by higher sales in both the retina and glaucoma product lines. Operating expenses were reduced by 10% compared to the prior year, reflecting ongoing efforts to streamline the business. The company achieved positive adjusted EBITDA for the full year and generated positive cash flow from operations in Q4, a notable milestone. However, gross margin declined to 37% from 44% in the prior year, mainly due to increased manufacturing costs and tariff impacts. The net loss for the quarter narrowed to $0.01 per share. Overall, Q4 results met analyst expectations for top-line growth but missed on gross margin and profitability metrics.

Positives
Revenue growth across product lines and geographies: The company saw double-digit revenue increases in both glaucoma and retina segments, with notable strength in sales of PASCAL laser systems and Cyclo G6 glaucoma probes. This indicates broad-based demand for IRIDEX’s technologies.
Cost reduction initiatives showing results: Operating expenses declined by 10% year over year in the quarter, and by 22% for the full year. These actions helped the company achieve positive adjusted EBITDA and positive cash flow from operations for the first time in recent history.
Commercial execution and sales enablement: The use of MedScout, a sales software platform, helped target high-potential accounts, resulting in improved probe utilization and increased system placements. The company also converted significant leads from major industry conferences into sales.
Progress on manufacturing transition: The company began relocating administrative functions and is preparing to transition manufacturing to third-party partners, which is expected to drive further cost savings and improve gross margins over time.
Negatives
Gross margin decline: Gross margin fell to 37% from 44% in the prior year, due to higher manufacturing costs, tariffs, and inventory write-downs. This offset some of the benefits from revenue growth and cost reductions.
Continued net loss: The company posted a net loss for the quarter and full year, though the loss narrowed compared to 2024. Sustainable profitability remains a work in progress.
Geopolitical and macroeconomic headwinds: Sales in certain international markets, especially the Middle East and Asia, were negatively impacted by regional conflicts, trade tariffs, and currency fluctuations. These factors contributed to volatility and uncertainty in some business segments.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Revenue seasonality and Q1 softness: Management has indicated that the first quarter is typically the lowest for revenue, averaging about 22% of annual sales. Investors should watch for how Q1 sales compare to the $11.91 million analyst consensus and whether the company can maintain year-over-year growth despite seasonal trends.
Gross margin trajectory as manufacturing shifts: The transition to third-party contract manufacturing is expected to begin impacting costs in 2026. Watch for early signs of improvement in gross margin, or any further pressure from tariffs and supply chain costs.
Cost savings from relocation and restructuring: Relocation of general and administrative functions out of California is expected to generate $165,000 in quarterly savings starting in Q1. Updates on the timeline and impact of the planned headquarters move and further manufacturing outsourcing will be important.
Glaucoma and retina segment momentum: Trends in probe utilization, system placements, and adoption of the Cyclo G6 and PASCAL platforms will be key indicators of commercial execution. Any commentary on the impact of Medicare reimbursement changes and competitive positioning should be closely watched.
Cash flow and liquidity position: Management expects to continue generating positive cash flow in 2026. Investors should monitor cash usage in Q1, which is typically higher due to annual compensation payments, and look for updates on working capital and liquidity as the year progresses.
