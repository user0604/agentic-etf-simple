ServiceTitan (NASDAQ:TTAN) posted a standout quarter as its platform gained momentum across the trades sector, highlighted by strong early results from its new AI-driven Max platform. Overall, the quarter saw management raise guidance for the year, with continued margin expansion and rapid product innovation offsetting ongoing GAAP losses.

Notable Developments
Revenue grew 25% year over year, driven by broad adoption across subscription and usage-based products.
Non-GAAP operating margin expanded by 7.7 percentage points to 15.2%, reflecting improved operating leverage and strong customer retention.
Gross transaction volume rose to $21.7 billion, up 23%, showing continued momentum in both new and existing customer activity.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its forecasts for both revenue and operating income for the rest of fiscal 2027, now expecting up to $1.14 billion in revenue and $147 million in non-GAAP operating income. The company highlighted ongoing investments in platform innovation and scaling the Max platform in additional trades. Investors should closely monitor the pace and breadth of the Max rollout, as well as progress toward achieving consistent GAAP profitability and positive free cash flow.

Quarterly Highlights

Platform revenues, which include both subscription and usage-based fees, made up almost all of the total revenue and grew substantially year over year. Subscription collections benefited from existing customer expansion and strong retention rates above 110%, a key benchmark for software companies. Usage revenue, reflecting adoption of advanced and AI-driven features, outpaced overall platform revenue growth.

The company more than doubled the number of locations using its Max platform, a suite of AI and automation tools designed to streamline operations for trades businesses. Management expects to double Max deployments again in the next quarter, signaling robust demand but acknowledging that the rollout remains phased as onboarding is scaled.

Profitability improved materially, with the non-GAAP margin expanding and non-GAAP free cash flow losses narrowing compared to last year. However, GAAP losses remain, driven mainly by high stock-based compensation and elevated investment in research and development to maintain technology leadership.
