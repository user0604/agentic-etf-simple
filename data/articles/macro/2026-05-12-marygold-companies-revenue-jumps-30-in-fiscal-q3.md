Key Takeaways
Marygold Companies returned to profitability as its core ETF fund management business delivered strong growth.
Revenue rose 30.2% compared to the prior year quarter.
Management expects to close non-core business sales and focus on financial services for the year ahead.

Marygold Companies (NYSEMKT:MGLD) returned to profitability, reflecting major progress as it reshaped its business portfolio toward financial services. The diversified holding company underscored its focus on ETF fund management following restructuring efforts in its fintech and international segments. In its third fiscal quarter results released May 12, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31, management highlighted both revenue growth and strict cost control. Overall, the quarter demonstrated clear improvements with higher revenue, much stronger profits, and continued portfolio streamlining.

Notable developments
Fund management revenue jumped 55%, driven by an 81% increase in assets under management, now at $4.7 billion.
The company trimmed operating costs and completed the sale of its Canadian subsidiary, helping shift from a $1.0 million loss to net income of $222,000.
Marygold started the process to sell its New Zealand food and packaging businesses, aiming to fully concentrate on financial services.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its plan to divest remaining non-core subsidiaries within twelve months and center future growth around fund management and financial services. The most important item for investors to track is asset growth in the fund management segment, as changes in assets under management directly affect revenue. Investors should also watch for progress on selling the New Zealand subsidiaries, which could further simplify the business and improve margins.

Quarterly highlights

Fund management, primarily through ETFs (exchange-traded funds), became the clear engine of the business, now making up over 85% of company revenue, reflecting new focus and recent AUM growth. Higher asset levels came as investors increased commodity ETF allocations, especially during geopolitical volatility.

Strict cost controls, especially in fintech and administrative expenses, sharply improved company profitability. Several one-time items, including a gain from asset sales, helped support the bottom line, but cost reductions were the main driver.

The ongoing exit from non-core businesses, including the completed Canadian security sale and the planned New Zealand food and packaging sale, advanced Marygold’s transformation into a focused financial services group. Management expects these actions to improve performance stability moving forward.

What Marygold Companies does

Marygold Companies operates as a diversified holding company focusing on fund management, financial services, beauty products, and, through subsidiaries, food and packaging businesses it now plans to sell.
