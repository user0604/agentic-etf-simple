Target Hospitality (NASDAQ:TH) landed substantial new business in large workforce housing contracts but saw profitability fall due to higher costs and business mix changes. The leading provider of specialty rental accommodations and hospitality solutions focused on high-demand sectors like data centers and power, filed results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, on May 11, 2026. Overall, the quarter highlighted a period of transformation as legacy government contracts wound down and commercial opportunities scaled up.

Notable developments
Workforce Hospitality Solutions (WHS) segment revenue rose nearly four-fold to $23.6 million, as major contracts in AI infrastructure and data centers began ramping up.
Gross profit margin compressed sharply, with company-wide adjusted earnings falling over 50%, primarily due to investment in new communities and the sunset of higher-margin government contracts.
Total available liquidity stayed strong at $150 million, despite elevated capital expenditures of $45.5 million during the quarter to fund WHS build-outs.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management increased its fiscal 2026 guidance, now expecting $370 million to $380 million in revenue and $75 million to $85 million in adjusted EBITDA. High capital investment is anticipated to continue as the WHS segment becomes the largest growth driver. The biggest variable for investors going forward will be how smoothly the company transitions major new contracts from construction to ongoing operations, as this is key for margin recovery in late 2026 and beyond.

Quarterly highlights

The WHS segment showed the strongest growth in the quarter with its revenue jumping 353.8%, boosted by new long-term agreements in AI infrastructure and data center markets. These projects include multi-year contracts worth over $1.4 billion since January 2026, anchored by a $750 million contract for workforce accommodations at an AI data center.

Profitability, however, suffered as adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization, with certain costs excluded) more than halved compared to the prior year, driven by higher operating expenses for community build-outs and the loss of a key government contract. The government segment’s revenue and profit fell sharply, reflecting this strategic change in portfolio focus.

Despite higher costs, discretionary cash flow and available liquidity were both positive, thanks to contract structures that secure upfront customer commitments. Net leverage remained low at 0.6 times, giving the company room to manage its ambitious investment program.

What Target Hospitality does

Target Hospitality provides specialty rental accommodations and integrated hospitality services to workforce populations in sectors like energy, infrastructure, and government.
