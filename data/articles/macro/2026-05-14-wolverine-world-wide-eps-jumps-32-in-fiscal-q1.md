Key Takeaways
Wolverine World Wide posted a business win with double-digit growth in its largest brands and improved profitability.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) jumped 31.6% year over year.
Management raised its full-year profit outlook, though revenue guidance remains unchanged.

Wolverine World Wide (NYSE:WWW) delivered a stronger quarter as key footwear brands Merrell and Saucony drove gains. The global footwear and apparel marketer specializes in active lifestyle and work-oriented shoes, including well-known names across more than 170 countries. For the first quarter ended April 4, 2026 (reported May 14, 2026), the company outpaced its own top-line and profit guidance, thanks to robust international growth and disciplined cost control. Overall, the quarter marked notable progress, but performance varied by product and channel.

Notable Developments
Growth was driven by the Merrell and Saucony brands, with Saucony revenue climbing 20.1% year over year and Merrell up 12.7%.
Operating margin expanded 2.3 percentage points compared to the prior year period, helped by cost controls and improved sales mix.
The company ended the quarter with $120 million in cash and $280 million in inventory, both improvements in balance sheet strength.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its full-year profit forecasts, expecting higher operating margins and earnings per share but left revenue guidance unchanged for fiscal 2026. It explained this confidence by noting ongoing strength in core brands and cost discipline, even as tariff impacts persist. Investors should closely watch brand momentum at Wolverine and performance in the direct-to-consumer channel, as these areas lag peers and could influence results later in the year.

Quarterly highlights

The standout result was strong growth from the Saucony athletic footwear brand, which jumped 20.1% year over year, aided by recent launches and increased marketing. Merrell, an outdoor footwear brand, also outperformed, gaining market share and benefiting from global distribution strength. Together, these two brands make up the company’s “Active Group”, which accounted for over 80% of revenue and advanced 13.7% for the period.

Despite headline gains, gross margin stayed flat at 47.6% compared to the prior year, reflecting how ongoing U.S. tariffs offset product mix improvements and price increases. Management signaled that future margin gains could be harder to achieve if input costs or tariffs rise further, even as international business continues to expand rapidly.

The Work Group segment, including the Wolverine brand of work boots and apparel, remained challenged, with revenue essentially flat year over year and a 2.5% decline for Wolverine brand sales. Direct-to-consumer sales, which offer higher profit potential, increased just 3% and were flat when excluding foreign exchange gains, pointing to muted progress in digital and company-owned retail channels.

What Wolverine World Wide does

Wolverine World Wide is a global marketer and licensor of footwear and apparel brands focused on the active lifestyle, outdoor, and work categories.
