Key Takeaways
Palo Alto Networks posted strong revenue growth driven by demand for AI-powered cybersecurity and recent acquisitions.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) rose 6.3% year over year.

Palo Alto Networks (NASDAQ:PANW) delivered a business win this quarter as customers sought out its AI-driven cybersecurity solutions and recent acquisitions contributed meaningfully to sales growth. The leading cybersecurity platform provider reported its fiscal third quarter results (released June 2, 2026, covering the period ending April 30, 2026) that showed strength across major financial and operational metrics. Overall, the period featured notable topline growth and record bookings but highlighted ongoing pressure on reported profitability due to integration and acquisition-related expenses.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 31% year over year to $3.0 billion, including $388 million of sales from acquired businesses CyberArk and Chronosphere.
Next-Generation Security annual recurring revenue (ARR) reached $8.1 billion in the fiscal third quarter 2026, up 60%.
The company swung to a GAAP operating loss of $183 million as acquisition and integration costs, plus higher equity compensation, weighed on reported results.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year guidance on all major metrics, including total revenue, future contracted business, and cash flow margin. Executives pointed to “improving profitability across our businesses” and continued successful integration of recent acquisitions. For investors, the key variable to monitor will be the company’s ability to translate strong topline and customer momentum into improved profitability and sustainable operating margins over time.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter’s biggest growth driver was demand for next-generation security offerings, such as the Cortex and Prisma AIRS AI-powered platforms. These solutions help organizations automate threat detection and defense at scale, and contributed to the 60% jump in recurring revenue tied to new business models.

Another major factor was the inclusion of CyberArk and Chronosphere, which together added $388 million to sales and $1.6 billion to recurring revenue. Management reported that the integration of these acquisitions is ahead of schedule and helping strengthen its consolidated security platform approach.

Despite the strong revenue and bookings performance, costs rose sharply as research and development expenses jumped by 48.6% and sales costs climbed by 46.4% compared to last year. Acquisition and integration-related expenses were reflected in a swing to a $183 million GAAP operating loss, even as non-GAAP earnings and free cash flow set new highs.
