Earnings Preview: Torrid Holdings

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Torrid Holdings (NYSE:CURV), a retailer specializing in plus-size women’s apparel and intimates, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on Thursday, June 4, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Torrid Holdings reported net sales of $236.2 million and an adjusted EBITDA of $5.1 million. The company’s comparable sales declined 10% year over year, with a significant portion of the decline attributed to the temporary pause in its footwear business. Gross profit was $70.9 million, and the company ended the quarter with $20 million in cash and $31 million drawn on its credit facility. The quarter’s net loss was $8.1 million, or $0.08 per share. Torrid met its sales guidance and exceeded the high end of its adjusted EBITDA outlook for the full year, signaling early progress in its multi-year transformation plan. The company completed 85% of its planned store closures by the end of the quarter, optimizing its store footprint and cost structure.

Positives
Cost structure improvement is driving margin leverage: The store optimization program reduced operating expenses by $18.5 million in fiscal 2025, with further savings expected in 2026. This has supported EBITDA margin expansion and demonstrates effective execution of the company’s transformation plan.
Sub-brands are gaining traction and improving margins: Sub-brands generated over $70 million in sales for 2025, with plans to grow to $110 million in 2026. These brands carry higher product margins and are helping to attract new customers and reactivate lapsed ones.
Footwear reintroduction was successful in limited release: The company’s test of a reengineered footwear assortment sold out quickly, validating the new sourcing and pricing approach. This sets up a return to scale in the footwear category later in 2026.
Customer retention metrics are outperforming expectations: Most customers affected by store closures are migrating to nearby stores or online, resulting in improved profitability in the remaining store fleet and validating the omnichannel strategy.
Negatives
Sales and comparable sales declined year over year: Net sales fell 14.3% and comparable sales dropped 10% in the fourth quarter, reflecting ongoing demand challenges and the impact of the paused footwear category.
Gross margin contracted due to promotions and deleverage: Gross margin dropped to 30% from 33.6% a year ago, driven by higher promotions, product mix shifts, and lower sales volume spreading fixed costs over a smaller base.
Active customer count continues to decline: The number of active customers decreased to 3.44 million from 3.66 million the prior year, and net sales per active customer also fell, highlighting ongoing customer retention and acquisition challenges.
Net loss widened compared to last year: The company reported a net loss of $8.1 million for the quarter, larger than the $3 million loss in the prior year, reflecting the impact of lower sales and margin pressure.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Sales performance versus guidance and consensus: Management guided for Q1 net sales of $236 million to $244 million, with analyst consensus at $239.9 million. Investors should watch whether sales land within this range and if trends in core categories improve as expected.
Progress on store closure and optimization plan: The company planned to complete up to 30 additional store closures in the first half of 2026. Updates on the pace of closures and resulting cost savings will be important for margin outlook.
Footwear category reintroduction and timing: Footwear is expected to remain a headwind in the first half, with a full relaunch planned for June–July 2026. Investors should look for commentary on initial sales and inventory levels as the category returns to scale.
Customer file growth and reactivation efforts: Management has made customer file growth its top priority for 2026, focusing on reactivating lapsed customers and increasing purchase frequency. Watch for metrics or commentary on early progress in these initiatives.
Gross margin trends and promotional activity: With ongoing tariff pressures and a shift in promotional strategy (less reliance on Torrid Cash and more focus on opening price points), updates on gross margin direction and promotional effectiveness will be key to understanding the company’s path to margin expansion.
