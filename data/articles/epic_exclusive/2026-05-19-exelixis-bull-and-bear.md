Exelixis Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 7:11 AM

Comments

Exelixis, Inc. (NASDAQ:EXEL) is a biotechnology company specializing in oncology, with a growth strategy anchored by its flagship drug cabozantinib (marketed as CABOMETYX) and a robust pipeline led by next-generation asset zanzalintinib (ZANZA). The company’s approach focuses on expanding its presence across major cancer indications, maximizing current franchise value, investing in new therapies, and actively returning capital to shareholders through share buybacks as it prepares for an inflection point in its product lifecycle.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership and Share Gains in Core Oncology Franchises Strengthen Pricing Power and Cash Flow

Exelixis has reinforced its dominance in renal cell carcinoma and neuroendocrine tumors with record CABOMETYX sales growth and market share advances. In the first quarter of 2026, U.S. cabozantinib franchise revenues rose 8% year over year to $555 million, while global franchise sales grew 12.5% year over year to $764 million, reflecting the drug’s category leadership. CABOMETYX captured 47% of the oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) market basket during this period—up from 44% the prior year—and delivered a 14% increase in total prescriptions, outpacing broader market growth. These trends not only secure high-margin revenue in the short term but also provide the company with considerable financial flexibility as it funds new launches and pipeline development ahead of potential generic entry later in the decade.

2. Late-Stage Pipeline Advances Position the Company for a Smooth Transition Beyond the Patent Cliff

The imminent launch of zanzalintinib, beginning with colorectal cancer, offers a clear pathway to diversify and grow revenue as cabozantinib exclusivity wanes. The company’s new drug application (NDA) for ZANZA in combination with atezolizumab for third-line and later colorectal cancer is under review, with a Prescription Drug User Fee Act (PDUFA) date in December 2026. ZANZA is targeting a $1.5 billion U.S. market with later expansion into earlier disease settings and additional tumor types through seven ongoing or planned pivotal trials. This multi-indication development approach mirrors the “pipeline-in-a-drug” model and has the potential to establish ZANZA as the tyrosine kinase inhibitor of choice in several solid tumor settings throughout the 2030s, directly addressing investor concerns about revenue cliff risk.

3. Consistent Profitability and Cash Generation Enable Robust Shareholder Returns and Pipeline Investment

Strong profitability metrics and disciplined expense management continue to generate ample cash for both pipeline investment and shareholder capital return. In the first quarter of 2026, Exelixis reported GAAP net income of $210.5 million and non-GAAP net income of $232.8 million, with diluted earnings per share of $0.79 and $0.87, respectively—well above consensus estimates of $0.75. The company’s gross-to-net deductions, while rising to 30.2% due to higher Medicare and co-pay adjustments, have not prevented it from maintaining strong profitability and investing in R&D activity. This financial strength allowed the board to authorize a new $750 million share buyback in May 2026, following an earlier $430.8 million repurchase in Q1, which supports per-share valuation growth and signals management’s confidence in future cash flows.

4. Strategic Commercial Execution and Organizational Readiness Should Accelerate New Product Uptake

The company’s proactive franchise and sales force strategy is designed to maximize uptake for both existing and upcoming oncology launches. In the first quarter of 2026, Exelixis rapidly expanded its gastrointestinal-focused sales team, not only to capture further CABOMETYX opportunity but also to prepare for ZANZA’s commercial roll-out in colorectal cancer. By leveraging the same community-based infrastructure and experienced specialty oncology reps, the company is positioned to execute multi-indication launches efficiently and penetrate non-renal malignancies quickly. These preparations could drive rapid commercial traction for ZANZA, capitalizing on established relationships and organizational knowledge to accelerate revenue ramp as new indications are approved.

5. Multiyear Patent Protection Provides a Stable Platform for Pipeline Execution and Franchise Expansion

Durable intellectual property on cabozantinib through at least 2026–2030 gives the company a valuable earnings window to invest in growth initiatives. While management and investors are closely watching the eventual patent expiration, current projections and legal protections indicate generics are not anticipated before the later part of the decade. This provides a critical period during which Exelixis can continue to benefit from high-margin CABOMETYX sales, while accelerating the transition to its next-generation assets such as ZANZA. The window acts as both a revenue bridge and a strategic buffer, reducing operational risk as the company navigates multiple late- and early-stage clinical readouts throughout 2026–2027 and beyond.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Heavy Revenue Dependence on Cabozantinib Magnifies Patent Cliff and Generic Erosion Risk

The company’s overwhelming reliance on a single asset creates material vulnerability to patent expiration. Over 90% of Exelixis’s revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was generated by cabozantinib, which is set to lose exclusivity by early 2030. Despite ongoing pipeline efforts, the timing and magnitude of generic competition remain unpredictable; even a short delay in new product launches could translate to a sharp revenue decline as generics reach the market. This concentration risk is further elevated because the ZANZA franchise, while promising, is not yet established and faces significant regulatory and commercial hurdles before it can replace lost CABOMETYX earnings. As the patent clock winds down, investors face heightened uncertainty around both cash flow and valuation support.

2. Pipeline Execution Risk Threatens Transition to Next-Generation Revenue Streams

The success of the company’s transition strategy depends heavily on the timely approval and adoption of zanzalintinib and other pipeline assets. Clinical development in oncology is inherently risky, and while the STELLAR-303 trial for ZANZA showed a 20% reduction in risk of death in third-line colorectal cancer, mature subgroup data are still pending and regulatory approval is not guaranteed. Additionally, expanding ZANZA into earlier lines or additional malignancies requires positive pivotal results and favorable payer coverage decisions, both of which can face unforeseen setbacks. Any regulatory delays, negative trial outcomes, or competition from existing therapies could undermine the potential for ZANZA to fully offset post-CABOMETYX revenue declines, leaving the company exposed to a multi-year top-line contraction.

3. Increasing Pricing Pressure and Gross-to-Net Deterioration Erode Profit Margins

Regulatory and payer-driven rebate and discount trends are exerting sustained pressure on profitability metrics. In Q1 2026, the company’s gross-to-net deductions rose to 30.2% due to greater volume from the 340B program, increases in Medicare Part D discounts, and expanded co-pay assistance. This risk is compounded by a shifting U.S. policy landscape, including the Inflation Reduction Act and changes to Medicare Part D reimbursement design, both of which threaten to further restrict pricing power and compress margins in the future. Continued upward pressure on gross-to-net adjustments could challenge the sustainability of sector-leading earnings and cash flow, just as the company prepares for a high-cost pipeline transition and the loss of cabozantinib exclusivity.
