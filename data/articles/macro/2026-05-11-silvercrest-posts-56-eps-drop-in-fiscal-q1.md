Key Takeaways
Modest business performance as topline revenue was steady, but profits fell due to higher costs and outflows.
Adjusted diluted earnings per share dropped more than 55% year over year in the quarter.
Management expects continued expense pressure as international growth investments take time to translate into results.

Results for Silvercrest Asset Management Group (NASDAQ:SAMG) showed stable revenue despite a sharp drop in profitability and increased client outflows. The investment management firm, which specializes in high-touch service for wealthy individuals and institutions, reported results for the first quarter ended March 31, 2026 (released May 11, 2026). Overall, the quarter reflected ongoing investment for future growth, but important financial challenges such as reduced earnings and persistently elevated expenses.

Notable Developments
Revenue remained flat year over year at $31.4 million, as growth in assets under management was offset by higher client outflows.
Adjusted EBITDA margin dropped nearly 9 percentage points due to elevated compensation costs and new office openings in Asia-Pacific and Europe.
Discretionary AUM increased 1.8% to $23.1 billion, but net client flows were negative, driven mainly by institutional redemptions.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects compensation expenses and lower profitability to persist while it continues investing in talent and expanding internationally. It anticipates that these investments will support future growth but did not provide a specific timeline for when margins or client inflows will improve. Investors should closely monitor whether the new global offices begin to generate positive net flows and improved profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue held steady at prior-year levels, showing stability even as the company faced rising service costs and lower client inflows. Assets under management reached $35.7 billion, with a modest 1.1% increase from the prior-year period, but discretionary AUM growth was held back by net outflows.

Profit margins declined as the compensation ratio (the share of revenue spent on salary and benefits) rose to 67.2%, up from 60.2% a year earlier. This increase was due to continued hiring and costs associated with new offices in Atlanta and Singapore, as well as preparations for a Dublin location expected to open later in the year.

The company reported strong investment returns for proprietary global and small-cap equity strategies, such as a 36.7% one-year return for its Global Value Opportunity composite. However, despite this competitive performance, new client asset inflows did not yet materialize at the scale management had hoped for, resulting in higher net outflows during the period.
