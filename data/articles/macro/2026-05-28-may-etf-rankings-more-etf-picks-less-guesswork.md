Greetings, Fund Fools!

For years, our monthly ETF rankings have done one thing well: cut through the noise to surface the handful of funds most worth owning right now. But as investors navigate an increasingly fragmented investing landscape, including different brokerage platforms, different commission structures, and different fund availability, even good recommendations don't always translate into a clear next step.

Here's the thing: Where you invest matters almost as much as what you invest in. A Fidelity investor and a Schwab (NYSE: SCHW) investor might be looking at the exact same asset-class recommendation and have no obvious on-ramp. So starting this month, alongside our monthly ETF rankings, we're introducing a full fund family substitution chart covering Vanguard, iShares (BlackRock (NYSE: BLK)), Schwab, and SPDR (State Street (NYSE: STT)) across every major asset class we track. That means wherever you invest, you'll find a best-in-class recommendation.

The practical upshot is significant. If you're a Schwab brokerage customer who prefers their commission-free ecosystem, you get a direct Schwab ETF recommendation. Vanguard loyalist? Same deal. The four families we've chosen represent the dominant players in low-cost indexing, collectively covering the vast majority of retail investors' platforms.

Of course, the ETF universe is enormous, and there are plenty of worthy funds outside these four families. If you're evaluating one on your own, use the same lens we do:

How much diversification does it add to your total portfolio?
Does it complement a Motley Fool-style stock-picking approach rather than duplicate it?
What's the realistic likelihood it delivers positive returns over the next five years?
And, perhaps most important -- what's the expense ratio in comparison to its peer group?

A fund that clears all four bars is one worth owning, regardless of whose name is on the label.

And while that means we can't highlight every deserving ETF out there, our hope is that more comprehensive coverage means fewer gaps between our analysis and your portfolio. The best ETF recommendation in the world is only useful if you can actually act on it -- and now you can.

Our rankings below feature some of our favorite ETFs, but if you're looking for other solid funds in one or more of those asset-class roles, be sure to check out the ETF Fund Family Substitution Chart. Please note that there are several different low-cost ETFs in each asset class that could play an equally satisfactory role in an investor's portfolio, and this list is not exhaustive.

About the ETF Rankings

Our fund rankings are pulled from the investable universe of exchange-traded funds. Rankings are reviewed on a quarterly basis and are based on the following criteria:

Diversification offered in a total-portfolio context
Complementarity to TMF investing style
Likelihood of earning positive returns over the next five years
Fund expense ratio

Most investors can build a well-diversified portfolio with just a few carefully selected ETFs, so you won't need nearly as many fund investments as you would with stocks. You can use our rankings to identify the best funds to quickly build a portfolio that covers all the important corners of the markets.

Similarly, if you want to boost your exposure to one particular segment of the market (say, small caps or international), you can explore our Rankings and Substitution charts to identify funds that can get that job done. Be sure to consider your personal goals and risk tolerance when crafting any portfolio.
