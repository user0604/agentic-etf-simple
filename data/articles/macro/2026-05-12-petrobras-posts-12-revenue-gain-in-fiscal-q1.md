Petróleo Brasileiro (NYSE:PBR) sustained momentum in its core operations but continued to face narrowing margins and higher costs. The leading Brazilian integrated energy company posted its first-quarter results on May 12, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter delivered solid revenue growth with modest earnings gains, but expense increases squeezed profitability.

Notable developments
Sales revenue rose 11.7% to $23.5 billion, with gains in efficiency and upstream activity supporting the increase.
Cost of sales and selling expenses climbed, compressing gross margin by 1.1 percentage points to 48.2% and driving selling costs up 39% year over year.
Total equity increased by 12.7% to $85.5 billion, while the period-end dividend payable dropped sharply from $2.1 billion to just $22 million.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not issue forward-looking financial guidance or set out explicit new strategic priorities for the upcoming quarters. Future results depend on Petrobras’s ability to manage operating costs and deliver performance in both traditional and renewable energy. Investors should track future segment disclosures and watch for investments in renewables and any adjustments to the company’s dividend policy.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue grew compared to the same period last year, reflecting strong production and ongoing investment in upstream (exploration and extraction) activities. However, the cost of sales outpaced top-line growth, with cost increases rising and squeezing gross profit margins.

Higher selling expenses and increased research and development outlays contributed to profitability challenges, and management did not elaborate on specific drivers behind these costs. The balance sheet remained solid with total assets increasing to $238.7 billion and equity up to $85.5 billion at quarter’s end.

Dividends payable at the close of the period were substantially lower than at the end of last year, suggesting a sharp reduction in immediate shareholder payouts. Cash reserves and property, plant, and equipment increased, pointing to continued investment in core oil and gas infrastructure. The company’s results did not provide segment-level details or new data on renewable energy business lines, leaving questions about progress in the transition toward biodiesel, ethanol, and other sustainable initiatives. Regulatory and geopolitical factors remain watch points as Petrobras continues to operate in a complex Brazilian and global environment.
