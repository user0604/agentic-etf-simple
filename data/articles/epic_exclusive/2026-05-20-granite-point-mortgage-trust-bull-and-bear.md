Granite Point Mortgage Trust Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 20, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

Granite Point Mortgage Trust Inc. (NYSE:GPMT) is a real estate investment trust that originates, invests in, and manages senior floating-rate commercial mortgage loans, primarily secured by properties across the office, multifamily, hotel, and retail sectors in the United States. The company’s near-term growth strategy is centered on resolving legacy and high-risk loans, reducing its leverage and risk profile, and positioning for a return to new loan origination as market conditions stabilize. Management aims to leverage a cleaner balance sheet for future earnings recovery while exploring capital-light fee-based business lines for diversification.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Accelerated Legacy Asset Resolutions Restore Earnings Power and Confidence

Rapid progress on resolving troubled loans could unlock capital and improve financial metrics – Granite Point Mortgage Trust Inc. resolved approximately $175 million in legacy assets in the first quarter of 2026 and post quarter-end, including repayments and sales such as the resolution of a major retail loan and sales activity involving hotel loans. While these resolutions resulted in a realized loss of approximately $30 million, the impact was substantially offset by previously reserved amounts, but reported book value per share declined $0.24 sequentially. Completing these workouts not only frees capital for redeployment but also reduces earnings drag from nonaccrual assets, enabling higher distributable earnings per share once the capital is reinvested at target leverage. Such momentum—if sustained into late 2026—signals to investors and lending partners that the company is turning a corner, potentially paving the way for resumed origination, dividend sustainability, and improved valuation multiples.

2. Discounted Share Price Offers Compelling Value if Book Value Stabilizes

The deep discount to book value presents capital appreciation potential if asset quality normalizes – At March 31, 2026, Granite Point’s book value per share was $7.05, with its stock price trading at a significant discount to book. Historical recoveries in mortgage REITs—once credit risk is addressed—have often been marked by substantial mean reversion. If the company continues to resolve legacy issues and avoids further significant book value erosion, its shares could see material upside from current levels, even before earnings return to pre-disruption levels. This discount also provides a margin of safety for value-oriented investors, especially if new asset originations commence later in 2026 under improved credit conditions.

3. Strong Liquidity and Reduced Leverage Support Flexibility for Opportunistic Growth

High cash balances and low leverage position the company for efficient future capital deployment – Granite Point reported $44 million in unrestricted cash and a leverage ratio of 1.7x at the end of Q1 2026, well below its 3–3.5x target. This conservative balance sheet gives management important flexibility: they can fund additional workouts, pursue select new investments when the market stabilizes, or absorb unforeseen losses without being forced into distressed financing or asset sales. As market dislocation resolves and loan origination resumes—likely later in the year—the company will be able to scale up rapidly and capture attractive spreads, supporting both earnings recovery and portfolio growth.

4. Expense Control and Reduced Cost of Funds Will Expand Operating Margins As Legacy Headwinds Fade

Effective expense management increases margin leverage once interest income rebounds – Even as net interest income declined in Q1 2026 due to shrinking loan balances and high nonaccruals, when resolved capital is reinvested in new, higher-yielding loans, these structural improvements in cost discipline and funding expense will directly translate into improved net interest margins and distributable earnings per share. This creates an internal earnings lever that can help offset residual portfolio volatility and accelerate the path back to dividend coverage and growth.

5. Capital-Light, Fee-Based Ventures Could Deliver Diversified Earnings Streams

Strategic expansion into third-party asset management broadens revenue sources and dampens risk – Granite Point’s ongoing pursuit of capital-light origination and joint venture asset management shows credible commitment to new, less balance-sheet-intensive business lines. If the company successfully launches these ventures, annual earnings could increase by $2 million to $4 million within the first year, independent of the traditional loan book’s credit cycles. This diversification would help smooth earnings, reduce reliance on proprietary capital, and establish relationships with new institutional partners, strengthening the long-term business model and mitigating cyclicality in commercial real estate finance.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Credit Deterioration and Slow Asset Resolutions Threaten Book Value and Earnings Recovery

Ongoing downgrades and slow loan workouts may drive further losses and capital erosion – In Q1 2026, Granite Point’s weighted average risk rating deteriorated to 3.2, with multiple new loans migrating to Risk-4 and Risk-5 status, particularly in office and multifamily sectors facing persistent headwinds. Resolutions of problematic loans, while progressing, remain slower than management targets, and in several key cases have required transitioning assets to real estate owned (REO) status before a sale can be attempted. Continued exposure to nonperforming or impaired loans risks further credit provisioning, realized losses, and book value declines, potentially eroding the value proposition even at a deeply discounted share price. If market liquidity remains poor into 2027 or problem assets linger, recovery may prove elusive.

2. Uncovered Dividend and Diminished Earnings Raise Prospect of a Payout Cut

Sustained under-earning puts current dividend policy at significant risk – For three consecutive quarters ending March 31, 2026, the company’s distributable earnings per share (which was -$0.06 in Q1 2026) failed to cover its quarterly dividend of $0.05. Net interest income has shrunk from $17.3 million in Q4 2025 to $8.0 million in Q1 2026 due to loan portfolio runoff and nonaccruals. While management and the board express a desire to maintain the dividend to support investor confidence, they also acknowledge that continued lack of coverage—especially if asset resolution and redeployment are delayed—could force a reduction or suspension. A dividend cut would likely weigh further on valuation and sentiment.

3. Macroeconomic and Sector Weakness in Office Loans Could Prolong Recovery Timeline

Challenging external environment threatens pace and profitability of any turnaround – Broader commercial real estate fundamentals, particularly in the office sector, remain very weak through the first half of 2026. Wider spreads in commercial mortgage-backed securities and collateralized loan obligations have limited both new lending and the ability to resolve troubled loans efficiently. Price discovery and transaction volumes are muted, especially for secondary-market office properties. These macro headwinds increase the risk that the company’s legacy portfolio will take longer to clean up, with more aggressive provisioning or discounted resolutions required for the most problematic office and hotel assets. Any delay in market stabilization could postpone earnings rebound and origination regrowth, extending the period of depressed returns.
