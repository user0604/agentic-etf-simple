Sprinklr Posts 7% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Sprinklr reported solid revenue growth but faced margin pressures and a slowdown in profit.
Revenue increased 7% from the prior year, while non-GAAP net income per share slipped.
Management expects modest full-year revenue growth and continued investment in its AI platform.

Sprinklr (NYSE:CXM) delivered steady top-line growth as it continued its business transformation, but profitability declined due to a changing business mix and higher operating expenses. The enterprise software company specializes in AI-powered customer experience management tools for large organizations. On June 3, 2026, it released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (three months ended April 30), showing progress in revenue but ongoing pressure on margins. Overall, the quarter featured expansion in its service pipeline and future contract backlog, but with slower earnings and a reduction in non-GAAP operating income guidance.

Notable developments
Total revenue rose to $219.5 million, a 7% increase year over year, exceeding the company’s earlier outlook by about $3 million.
Non-GAAP operating margin fell to 14%, down 4 percentage points from the prior year, as operating expenses grew faster than revenue.
Free cash flow declined 18.5%, reflecting thinner profits and higher spend, but remaining performance obligations grew nearly 10%, pointing to a healthy future pipeline.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management guided for modest growth ahead, with full-year total revenue expected to rise just over 1% and non-GAAP operating income set to decline modestly from previous targets. The most important factor to watch will be the pace of subscription growth and the stabilization of profit margins as Sprinklr invests in its AI platform and partner-led services model.

Quarterly highlights

The revenue figure topped Sprinklr’s previous forecast, with both subscription and professional services segments contributing to growth. Subscription revenue reached the upper end of company guidance, suggesting continued demand for its Unified Customer Experience Management platform among large enterprise customers.

Profitability metrics weakened as non-GAAP operating margin slipped from 18% to 14%, driven by increased investment in research and customer service operations. Cash flow (free cash flow) was again positive at $65.8 million, but lagged the prior period primarily due to margin decline and higher capital outlays; share buybacks totaling $125 million were reflected in financing activities.

Sprinklr’s backlog of contracted business, measured as remaining performance obligation (RPO), climbed to $1.04 billion—up nearly 10% year over year—indicating future sales are locked in. However, investors will be watching for details on large enterprise customer wins and any updates on new AI-driven product launches, both of which were not addressed in this quarter’s release.

What Sprinklr does

Sprinklr provides software platforms that help large enterprises manage and analyze customer interactions across social, digital, and traditional channels using artificial intelligence.
