1. Alphabet Chases $85 Billion for AI as Stock Slips

Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) is doing something rare for a cash-rich giant: asking Wall Street for money. It plans to raise $85 billion in equity to fund its artificial intelligence (AI) build-out. The timing turns heads -- the stock just logged a fourth straight weekly drop, its longest losing streak in over a year. GOOG is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers. Shares were down 1% on Friday.

Big number, minimal sting: $80 billion would crack the 200 largest companies on its own, but as Motley Fool analyst Anders Bylund noted in a missive to Epic members, it's "only 1.8% dilution for Google." (That was before Alphabet nudged the target to $85 billion.) The raise lays bare just how capital-hungry Alphabet's AI build-out has become.
Beating the IPO stampede: With SpaceX, Anthropic, and OpenAI lining up mega offerings, Alphabet may want capital locked in first.
2. Will Investors Foot Meta's AI Bill?

Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) shares fell 5.5% Friday after the Financial Times reported that the company may sell new shares to bankroll its AI build-out. The move echoes Alphabet's $85 billion raise mentioned above. META is recommended in Hidden Gems, in Stock Advisor (Team HG), and in Rule Breakers.

Big tech's spending arms race: Meta, Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN), Google, and Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) are on track to spend more than $720 billion on AI chips and data centers in 2026, with more planned for 2027. Team Hidden Gems contributor Dan Caplinger recently wrote that "We expect CEO Mark Zuckerberg to accentuate the tangible results of Meta's AI efforts in the months and years to come."
Chasing fresh dollars: Zuckerberg is testing Meta One subscriptions across Instagram, WhatsApp, and Facebook and has floated a Meta cloud platform to help offset the bill. Meta still derives nearly all revenue from advertising.
3. Bitcoin Hits 2024 Lows

Bitcoin (CRYPTO: BTC) capped a brutal week, dipping as low as $59,099 on Friday -- its weakest since October 2024. At roughly $60,000, it sits more than 50% below its October 2025 record near $126,000. Three forces piled on: A hot May jobs report lifted Treasury yields, and Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) snapped a record 13-day outflow streak, with net assets sliding to $80.4 billion from $107.8 billion in mid-May.

Some bulls smell a bargain: With Bitcoin's once-tight tie to the Nasdaq now snapped, its dual identity is in question. This is Bitcoin's fifth visit to its 200-week moving average, and the prior four all saw buyers step in, setting a floor.
From Team Hidden Gems: "I think it's a good idea to buy some Bitcoin ETF -- just to start learning more about cryptocurrency," Motley Fool co-founder and CEO Tom Gardner has said. Bitcoin is recommended in Hidden Gems.
