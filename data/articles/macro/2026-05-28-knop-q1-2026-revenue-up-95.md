Notable developments
Depreciation expense increased to $41.9 million after the company shortened vessel useful lives, reducing both net income and operating income compared to last year.
The quarterly cash distribution to common units rose to $0.05 per unit, up from $0.026 in the previous quarter, but remains below historic levels.
Long-term charters were extended with Shell, PetroChina, and TotalEnergies, raising fixed contract coverage to 97% for the second half of 2026 and 81% for 2027.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expressed confidence in the fleet’s contract coverage, which is fully booked for the first half of 2026 and has high visibility into 2027. Further gradual increases in distributions are expected, but will depend on successful fleet renewal, dropdown acquisitions from the sponsor, and refinancing major debt. Investors should monitor progress on upcoming vessel drop-downs and refinancing efforts for debt maturing later this year.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue increased from the prior year, reflecting stronger charter rates and additional insurance recoveries. However, profit margins narrowed as depreciation rose after reducing vessel useful lives to 20 years, which sharply reduced both operating income and net income for the period.

Commercially, the company secured several important long-term charters with major energy companies in the North Sea and Brazil. This lifted the backlog to $857.9 million in contracted forward revenue and improved visibility for future utilization and cash flows.

Cash and cash equivalents grew to $92.7 million, supported by ongoing cash generation and insurance payouts for vessel incidents. The distribution to common unitholders was incrementally raised this quarter, but management remains cautious about larger increases until core earnings recover further.

What Knot Offshore Partners does

Knot Offshore Partners owns and operates shuttle tankers, which are specialized vessels used to transport crude oil from offshore production sites to land-based terminals under long-term contracts, mainly in Brazil and the North Sea.
