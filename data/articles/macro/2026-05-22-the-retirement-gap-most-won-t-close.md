The Retirement Gap Most Won't Close

Jim Gillies

May 22, 2026, 9:01 AM

Comments (1)
8.6

Every so often I like to remind myself, and, I suppose, now you, of the serious reason as to why you want to be a life-long and committed investor.

Using Canadian data here (I live here, after all, but I don’t think things are appreciably different for Americans), some sobering statistics:

Median net worth for middle aged (~35 yrs old) Millennials who own a house = $457,000
Median net worth for 65-yr olds = $914,000

Sounds good right? Not really, that’s because roughly 50% (and I have seen some sources as high as 70%) of Canadian household wealth is held in the form of real estate. Illiquidity is a thing.

The latest retirement survey out of Bank of Montreal (TSX:BMO)(NYSE:BMO) pegs the average Canadian’s “number” for retirement at $1.7 million. In the land of exorbitant real estate (British Columbia) that number of $2.2 million. (If you can retire in the Maritimes it’s around $928,000)

Those numbers and the median net worth numbers above are…somewhat apart.

Let’s make it worse.

Four of 10 Canadians do not think they can retire in comfort.
One in three Canadians save 0%-to-5% of their incomes.
Another third save <10%

These numbers are in-line with what the annual HOOPP (Healthcare of Ontario Pension Plan) survey has published.

Oh, and by the way, for the Americans in the room, roughly 60% figure they’ll outlive their savings…in spite of the fact that U.S. Social Security is more generous than the combination of Canadian CPP + OAS (which, at a max in 2026 pays around $27,000 annually for retires age 65-to-74; about $28,000 for those a year older; average total payment is around $19,000). This, of course, assumes that Social Security doesn’t dance with insolvency (a much-repeated fear, though as a Canadian, I’m never clear on the outcome that might emerge from “entitlement reform”).

At least the CPP is a real pool of money that cannot be touched by politicians…unfortunately, the investors managing it aren’t doing particularly well, for the last two calendar years the CPPIB (i.e. the “investor board” overseeing the CPP) have put up returns of 7.7% (2025) and 14.2% (2024), a far cry from the market total returns in those years of 31.7% (2025) and 21.7% (2024).

So, choices for golden years for many may boil down to:

Work longer. Maybe much longer (what if you don’t like your job? Or, if physical labour, simply cannot continue?)
Accept less. Can you and your spouse live on ~$50,000 in retirement combined? How about $40,000?
Sell your house in order to eat; assuming you have a house that is. Good thing reverse mortgages don’t exist. Oh…

Or, you know, you could be a regular and disciplined saver with a long-time mindset. Every paycheque, 10%-to-15% off-the-top, “paying yourself first”, with at least half deployed into low-cost index-tracking ETFs and the remainder into quality stocks for which you pay reasonable valuations.

Feather your own nests Fools – this will give you retirement options that many won’t have.
