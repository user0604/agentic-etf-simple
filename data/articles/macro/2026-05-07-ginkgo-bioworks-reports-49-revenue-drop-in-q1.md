Key Takeaways
Ginkgo Bioworks posted a period of transition as revenue contracted while cost controls improved losses.
Earnings per share loss narrowed year over year.
Management reaffirmed cash burn guidance and continues focusing resources on expanding Nebula, its autonomous lab product.

Ginkgo Bioworks (NYSE:DNA) reported a transitional quarter as revenue declined but operating losses improved compared to last year. The leading cell engineering and lab automation platform provider is focusing its operations on the scalable Nebula autonomous lab after divesting its biosecurity unit. The earnings release, dated May 7, 2026, covers results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and reflects ongoing efforts to contain costs and reposition the company. Overall, the quarter showed tighter expense management, but revenue contraction weighed on growth.

Notable developments
Revenue declined by 49% compared to the prior year quarter, reflecting program reductions and the loss of biosecurity business.
Cash outflows continued, but net loss from continuing operations improved by 8%, showing progress in cost control.
The company completed the sale of its biosecurity business and now operates as a single focused segment centered on the Nebula autonomous lab.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintained its full-year 2026 guidance for total cash burn between $125 million and $150 million, with no revenue or profit outlook provided. Leadership highlighted active investment to double Nebula's capacity and drive adoption across its product suite. Investors should watch for stabilization in recurring revenue sources and acceleration in customer uptake of new offerings, particularly Cloud Lab and Solutions.

Quarterly highlights

The completion of the biosecurity divestiture represents a major strategic shift. Previously, the biosecurity unit contributed meaningful revenue but required significant capital and exposed the company to government contract volatility. The business is now fully reported as discontinued operations, and future results will focus solely on the cell engineering and automation platform.

Despite ongoing revenue contraction for the quarter, the company improved its efficiency metrics. Both adjusted EBITDA and net loss from continuing operations saw year-over-year improvement, due mainly to reduced research and development costs (down 30%) and the absence of restructuring charges this period.

Adoption of Nebula, the autonomous lab product, is a central theme going forward. Management cited growing traction with partners such as Amazon and ProQR, as well as continued engagement with government agencies and pharmaceutical customers. The shift to a single reporting segment reflects the new commercial direction, though it limits detailed visibility into business line performance.
