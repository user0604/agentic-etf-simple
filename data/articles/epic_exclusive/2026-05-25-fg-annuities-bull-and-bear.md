FG Annuities Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

F&G Annuities & Life, Inc. (NYSE:FG) is a U.S.-based provider of annuities and life insurance solutions, specializing in indexed annuities, indexed universal life, and pension risk transfer products. The company’s growth strategy is anchored by leveraging the retirement demographic wave, expanding higher-margin fee-based and capital-light business lines, and strengthening its owned distribution network. Management is also focused on disciplined capital management and transparency to unlock long-term shareholder value.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Record Asset Growth and Demographic Tailwinds Propel Sustainable Expansion

Consistent double-digit asset growth, fueled by the U.S. retirement boom, positions the company for accelerating top-line gains. The company ended the first quarter of 2026 with nearly $75 billion in gross assets under management (AUM)—an 18% compound annual growth rate since 2019. This expansion is powered by a generational shift as over 4 million Americans reach retirement age each year through 2027, creating unprecedented demand for guaranteed income products. Core offerings like indexed annuities and indexed universal life (IUL) directly target this trend, supporting robust industry and company sales. This long-term demographic current is expected to provide a multi-year runway for organic growth and revenue stability across cycles.

2. Fee-Based and Capital-Light Strategies Drive Margin Expansion and Resilience

Strategic prioritization of fee-based, capital-light businesses is set to boost earnings quality and reduce risk. The company has shifted focus to high-margin, lower-capital lines such as flow reinsurance, owned distribution, and middle market life insurance, targeting a rise in the proportion of adjusted net earnings from fee business to 25% by the end of 2028 (from 15% in 2025). These fee-based revenues generate recurring income without the same balance sheet strain as traditional spread products, supporting higher returns on equity (ROE) and providing resilience against volatile interest rate or credit market swings. This transition should enhance both profitability and valuation compared to legacy life insurers.

3. Disciplined Expense Control and Operating Leverage Support Long-Term Profitability

Ongoing expense discipline and efficiency improvements create lasting operating leverage benefits. First quarter 2026 saw the operating expense ratio to AUM fall to 48 basis points, down from 60 at the end of 2024. Management’s target of reducing this ratio to 45 bps by 2027 represents a 25% cumulative improvement. These gains amplify profits as assets compound, allowing more of each dollar in sales to flow through to earnings.

4. High-Quality, Diversified Investment Portfolio Reduces Credit and Liquidity Risk

The company’s $53 billion retained investment portfolio displays strong credit quality and prudent risk management. With 97% of fixed maturities rated investment grade and well-diversified exposures across traditional bonds, structured products, private debt, and defensive mortgage loans, the portfolio has demonstrated resilience across market cycles. Efforts to maintain dual ratings coverage (64% of fixed income) and modest private-letter-rated assets signal conservative credit policies. Credit-related impairments have averaged just 6 basis points over the last five years, greatly limiting the risk of capital erosion due to credit events. This high-quality approach enhances financial flexibility and bolsters the company’s ability to navigate credit or interest rate volatility.

5. Active Capital Management and Shareholder Alignment Enhance Value Recognition Potential

Robust capital returns, buybacks, and transparent governance align management’s interests with long-term shareholders. In the first quarter of 2026, the company returned $67 million to investors through dividends and share repurchases, and it authorized a new three-year, $100 million buyback program. Management’s conviction that shares are undervalued is evident in these actions and ongoing exploration of strategic alternatives for its owned distribution business (Peak Altitude), which has generated $80 million in annual EBITDA. The continued stability of management and board, paired with a risk-based capital (RBC) ratio projected to remain well above the 400% target, underpins both capital adequacy and prudent stewardship. These factors improve investor confidence and could help unlock sum-of-the-parts valuation upside over time.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Alternative Investment Returns Lag Expectations, Potentially Pressuring Earnings

The alternative asset portfolio’s underperformance relative to modeled returns presents downside risk to recurring profitability. In the first quarter of 2026, actual annualized returns on alternatives were 8.3%, falling short of the 12–14% long-term expected return range the company now uses for planning. While this reflects prudent capital stress-testing, ongoing shortfalls could weigh on both adjusted net earnings and return on equity (ROE), especially if public equity or real estate markets remain muted. If alternative yield targets are not met, the gap with guidance could require greater reliance on spread income or fee growth to achieve management's profitability objectives, potentially introducing volatility and missed expectations.

2. Interest Rate and Credit Spread Sensitivity Remain Key Market Risks

Exposure to shifts in interest rates and credit spreads could pressure spreads, earnings, and regulatory capital adequacy. Despite strong asset-liability management, the company’s business model depends on generating a healthy spread between investment yields and credited rates to policyholders. In the first quarter of 2026, fixed income yield fell 16 basis points sequentially, partly due to one-time effects but demonstrating the inherent sensitivity to market conditions. A broad, sustained decline in rates or sudden spike in credit spreads could erode spread-based earnings and potentially increase policyholder surrenders or liquidity needs, particularly if competitors aggressively reprice or market volatility rises.

3. Execution Risks in Transitioning to Fee-Based and Capital-Light Model

The pivot toward a greater fee-based, capital-light business mix could face adoption, competition, or operational challenges. The shift from traditional spread-based products to more fee-oriented lines is central to management’s growth and margin narrative. However, successfully scaling flow reinsurance, owned distribution, and mid-market life products may require significant new client acquisition, investment in technology, and navigating competition from larger, more established players. If the company falls short of its goal to derive 25% of adjusted net earnings from fee-based business by 2028, the expected uplift to profitability and resilience may not materialize, leaving returns and valuation susceptible to legacy business pressures.
