Earnings Preview: Constellation Energy

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 9:02 AM

Comments

Constellation Energy (NASDAQ:CEG), a major U.S. supplier of electricity and clean energy solutions, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on Monday, May 11, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Constellation Energy delivered adjusted operating earnings per share (EPS) of $9.39 for the full year, exceeding the midpoint of its guidance range and marking the fourth consecutive year of beating its own expectations. The company’s acquisition of Calpine Corporation was completed in early January 2026, expanding its generation fleet and retail energy presence. Full-year operating revenues reached $25.5 billion, an 8.3% increase from 2024, driven by higher contracted energy prices and increased capacity revenues across most regions. However, GAAP net income attributable to common shareholders fell to $2.3 billion from $3.7 billion the prior year, mainly due to lower nuclear production tax credit (PTC) revenues and unfavorable unrealized losses on economic hedges.

Positives
Strong adjusted operating EPS performance: The company’s adjusted operating EPS beat analyst estimates and company guidance, reflecting disciplined execution and resilient core operations.
Completion of Calpine acquisition: The acquisition, completed in January 2026, adds significant natural gas, geothermal, and battery storage capacity, enhancing both scale and market diversification.
Growth in capacity and retail revenues: Higher contracted energy prices and capacity revenues, particularly in the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic, supported top-line growth and offset some headwinds.
Operational excellence in nuclear fleet: The nuclear fleet maintained a high capacity factor of 94.7%, indicating reliable plant performance and efficient operations.
Negatives
Lower nuclear PTC revenues: The company saw a significant drop in nuclear PTC revenues year over year, which contributed to the decline in GAAP net income.
Unfavorable economic hedging results: Net unrealized losses on economic hedges and equity investments negatively impacted earnings, reflecting market volatility.
Increased purchased power and fuel costs: Higher energy prices and transmission costs led to a 28.6% increase in purchased power and fuel expenses, pressuring margins.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Integration progress and synergy realization from Calpine acquisition: Investors should monitor updates on cost savings, operational improvements, and commercial opportunities as the integration of Calpine moves forward.
Execution of long-term clean energy contracts: The company has highlighted a robust pipeline of potential deals with data centers and commercial customers. Progress on new agreements, especially with hyperscalers and technology firms, will be important for future earnings visibility.
Status of the Crane Clean Energy Center restart: The restart, supported by a 20-year agreement with Microsoft, is pending regulatory approvals. Any updates on the timeline or regulatory progress could affect growth expectations.
Impact of PJM market reforms and FERC proceedings: Regulatory clarity around capacity markets and transmission services in the PJM region could influence contract structures, pricing, and the company’s ability to serve large data center loads.
Capital allocation and share repurchase activity: With a new $5 billion buyback authorization and strong free cash flow, details on the pace and scale of share repurchases, as well as ongoing investment in growth projects, will be key for shareholders.
