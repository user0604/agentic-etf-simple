Ares Management Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 15, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments

Ares Management Corporation (NYSE:ARES) is a global alternatives investment manager overseeing $644 billion in assets across credit, private equity, real estate, infrastructure, and secondaries as of Q1 2026. The company’s growth strategy emphasizes diversification across asset classes and geographies, scalable fee-centric earnings, and continual innovation in new products and channels, positioning the platform to benefit from secular shifts driving demand for private market investments and resilient income streams through market cycles.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Industry-Leading Fundraising Momentum Enables Outsized Asset Growth

Record fundraising capacity secures future growth – In the first quarter of 2026, Ares Management Corporation raised a record $30 billion in gross capital—up 46% year over year—demonstrating the company’s exceptional ability to attract new assets even in volatile markets. This industry-leading inflow outpaces most global peers and reflects robust demand from increasingly consolidated institutional allocators as well as fast-growing wealth channels. With $158 billion in dry powder, Ares is positioned to continue capital deployment at scale, supporting its goal of achieving above-industry asset growth and reinforcing recurring fee revenues for years to come.

2. Stable and Expanding Fee-Centric Earnings Model Underpins Predictable Returns

Growing management fees drive reliable income – Management fee revenue reached more than $1 billion for the first time in Q1 2026, up 22% year over year. Fee-Related Earnings (FRE) rose 26% to $454 million, while the FRE margin expanded 90 basis points to 42.4%. With the majority of revenue coming from long-duration, contractual management fees—rather than volatile performance income—Ares offers greater predictability and downside protection through cycles. This structure has enabled the company to meet or exceed its long-term guidance for 16–20% annual FRE growth, which is instrumental to consistent dividend payouts and long-term value creation.

3. Product and Channel Innovation Broaden Market Reach and Resilience

New fund structures and perpetual capital open untapped markets – Over the last two years, Ares has launched 14 new products and strategies, totaling $68 billion in new assets under management. These include both levered and unlevered direct lending vehicles, evergreen funds, and dedicated strategies for infrastructure and secondaries, all tailored to shifting investor preferences. The diversification across perpetual, closed-end, and retail-wealth offerings lets Ares capture opportunity across evolving demand cycles and channels, reduces dependence on any one distribution source, and helps sustain inflows even as traditional areas like U.S. direct lending experience temporary moderation.

4. Leadership in High-Growth Sectors Like Private Credit and Digital Infrastructure

Ares capitalizes on secular disintermediation trends – The company has positioned itself at the forefront of secular shifts transforming finance, including the continued rise of private credit, secondaries, and digital infrastructure. For example, Ares manages over $100 billion in credit dry powder (the largest among public credit managers) and is raising a global data center equity fund to target the estimated $900 billion third-party market addressing cloud and AI computing needs. These sectors are likely to see decades of expansion, granting Ares a first-mover advantage in capturing structural growth and new revenue pools unavailable to traditional managers.

5. Long-Term Track Record of Outperformance in Difficult Environments

Business model thrives during volatility and downturns – Ares has consistently delivered strong realized returns and asset growth during times of macroeconomic stress, evidenced by outsized growth during the Global Financial Crisis and COVID-19 volatility. With nearly 10% EBITDA growth in portfolio companies and high operational resilience, Ares' non-accrual ratios remain well below historical norms. This resilience—combined with the ability to deploy capital opportunistically amid market dislocations—suggests Ares is positioned to gain market share and enhance risk-adjusted returns even if conditions worsen, further reinforcing investor confidence in its strategy.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Dependence on Robust Fundraising Faces Potential Cyclical and Market Pressure

Fundraising and deployment could slow in adverse cycles – While fundraising has set record highs, it remains sensitive to shifting macroeconomic conditions, competing platforms, and episodic investor sentiment shocks. Slower transactional activity—such as muted direct lending in early Q1 2026 due to geopolitical turmoil—shows that deal flow and capital formation may become more volatile in a recession or prolonged risk-off environment. If institutional allocators or wealth channels scale back commitments, even temporarily, revenue growth and deployment could falter, potentially compromising future fee income and AUM expansion.

2. Performance Fee Volatility and Realization Timing May Impact Near-Term Earnings

Lumpy performance income can pressure distributable earnings – For Q1 2026, after-tax realized income was $1.24 per share (non-GAAP), missing consensus by $0.10 primarily due to lower-than-expected net performance income. Performance-based revenue is both seasonal and unpredictable, depending on fund realization schedules and broader market liquidity. Sustained periods of low exits or weak performance in certain strategies could depress overall profitability, placing pressure on dividend coverage if not counterbalanced by management fees, and resulting in nearer-term earnings volatility even as fundamental demand for alternatives remains strong.

3. Sector-Specific Risks, Valuation Sensitivity, and Geopolitical Uncertainty Remain Material

Portfolio exposures face external pressures and stress events – Although Ares maintains low concentration in high-risk sectors, the portfolio is not immune to adverse shocks. Credit and real estate exposures can suffer from higher rates, inflation, or a cyclical recession impacting asset valuations, interest coverage, or non-accrual rates. Geopolitical disruptions, such as ongoing tensions in the Middle East, have already dampened deal activity in some segments. Persistent valuation concerns in private credit or potential regulatory changes could trigger asset write-downs, reduce NAVs, or freeze new flows, undermining the confidence and predictability that underpin Ares’ growth model.
