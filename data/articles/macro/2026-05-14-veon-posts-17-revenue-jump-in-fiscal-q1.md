Key Takeaways
Veon boosted digital revenues sharply, strengthening its transformation into a digital operator.
Revenue rose 17.0%, driven mainly by digital business gains.

Digital momentum lifted results for Veon (NASDAQ:VEON), as its shift toward digital services delivered another strong quarter. The telecom and digital operator, active in emerging markets across Asia and Eastern Europe, posted robust growth in both sales and cash flow for the three months ended March 31, 2026 (results released May 14, 2026). Overall, the quarter reflected healthy digital expansion, balanced by ongoing shifts in its core telecom user base.

Notable Developments
Digital revenues grew 57.7%, now making up over a quarter of total revenue, with rapid increases recorded in Pakistan and Ukraine.
The company’s cash generation strengthened, as equity free cash flow after leases and licenses jumped to $246 million, a 73.4% increase year over year.
While overall revenue rose, the company’s total mobile subscriber count declined by 1.3% year over year, highlighting pressure in core telecom markets.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased its full-year revenue growth target to 11%–14%, citing ongoing digital expansion and multiplay customer gains. Full-year EBITDA growth guidance remains steady at 7%–10%. Investors should watch for execution in digital services across all markets, as well as the impact of new investment, especially in Pakistan, on future profit and user growth.

Quarterly Highlights

Digital revenue growth was a standout, with these revenues jumping 57.7% year over year to $303 million, buoyed by strength in financial services, entertainment, and new ride-hailing services. In markets like Pakistan, digital’s share of revenue climbed to 36.9%.

The company’s equity free cash flow, which reflects cash left after paying for capital expenses, leases, and licenses, surged by 73.4%. This figure indicates improved operational efficiency and the ability to fund future investments or shareholder returns.

Despite topline growth, total mobile subscribers fell to 150.5 million, a decrease of 1.3%. While multiplay users—those engaged in both telecom and digital products—are growing, the shrinking overall base will be an area to monitor for future quarters.

What Veon Does

Veon provides mobile telecommunications and a broad range of digital services—including financial, entertainment, and ride-hailing products—in emerging markets in Asia and Eastern Europe.
