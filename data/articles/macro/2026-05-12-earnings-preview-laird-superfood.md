Earnings Preview: Laird Superfood

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 3:03 PM

Comments

Laird Superfood (NYSEMKT:LSF), which makes plant-based coffee creamers and functional food products, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings on Thursday, May 14, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

The fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 marked a period of strong revenue growth for Laird Superfood. Net sales reached $13.3 million, up 15% year over year, driven by broad-based gains, especially in the wholesale channel. Wholesale sales surged 44% to $7.0 million, now making up over half of quarterly revenue. E-commerce sales declined 6%, but Amazon sales remained a positive offset to softness in the company’s direct-to-consumer website. Gross margin for the quarter was 34.1%, down from 38.6% in the prior year, mainly due to higher commodity costs and the absence of one-time settlement benefits. The company reported a net loss of $1.8 million, or $0.16 per diluted share, with the loss primarily due to acquisition-related fees and inflationary pressures. Adjusted EBITDA for the full year turned positive at $0.3 million, reflecting improved operating leverage as the business scaled. These results met analyst expectations for both sales and earnings per share.

Positives
Wholesale channel momentum continued: Wholesale sales grew over 40% in both the quarter and full year, confirming the success of the company’s shift toward retail distribution and expansion in grocery and club stores.
Successful relaunch of refrigerated creamers: The company reformulated and repackaged its liquid creamer, resulting in early signs of improved sales and better positioning with retailers and consumers.
Positive adjusted EBITDA achieved: For the full year, adjusted EBITDA was $0.3 million, a notable turnaround from a $0.7 million loss in the prior year, showing improved operational efficiency despite industry headwinds.
Navitas Organics acquisition completed: The acquisition, funded by a $50 million investment from Nexus Capital, added a profitable, complementary superfood brand and expanded the company’s scale and product reach.
Negatives
Gross margin contracted: Margins fell to 34.1% in Q4 from 38.6% the previous year, mainly due to higher input costs and the absence of prior-year settlement gains.
Net loss increased year over year: The Q4 net loss widened to $1.8 million, driven by acquisition-related costs and commodity inflation, even though core business losses were flat when excluding one-time items.
E-commerce softness persisted: Direct-to-consumer sales declined, partially offset by Amazon, highlighting challenges in driving growth through the company’s own website.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Initial Navitas Organics integration and contribution: Investors should look for details on how the newly acquired Navitas business is being integrated, its early revenue and margin impact, and any updates on synergy realization.
Gross margin recovery efforts: Watch for commentary and results on margin stabilization, especially as tariff headwinds ease and procurement contracts are renegotiated for the combined business.
Wholesale channel expansion and product distribution: Updates on new retail placements, distribution gains, and the performance of recently relaunched products like refrigerated creamers will be important for assessing growth momentum.
Progress on portfolio optimization: Management has indicated plans to focus on higher-margin products and discontinue the Picky Bars brand in Q2 2026. Investors should monitor any early signs of SKU rationalization and its effect on sales mix and profitability.
Operating cash flow and working capital trends: With accounts receivable and inventory normalizing after year-end, the company’s ability to generate positive cash flow and manage working capital will be key for financial health in 2026.
