Key Takeaways
Revenue dropped sharply as key gene therapy programs advanced, but financing extended the cash runway.
R&D expenses grew while net loss increased due to a non-cash accounting charge from warrant liabilities.
Management projects cash will fund operations into 2029, with major clinical milestones approaching in 2026.

Opus Genetics (NASDAQ:IRD) reported a sharp revenue decline as it advanced its gene therapy pipeline and secured new funding to support long-term operations. The clinical-stage biotechnology company develops gene therapies for rare inherited retinal diseases. Reporting results on May 12, 2026, for its first quarter of fiscal 2026, Opus Genetics focused on operational milestones and extending its cash resources. Overall, the period highlighted notable clinical progress, but financial pressures were pronounced due to lower collaboration payments and a large, non-cash net loss.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped by over 50 percent year over year, mainly reflecting decreased reimbursement from research collaborations.
Total net loss surged, largely due to a $51 million non-cash charge tied to changes in warrant liabilities, not cash operating losses.
Opus Genetics secured $40 million in new financing after quarter-end, extending its cash runway and reducing near-term funding risk.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects that its current cash, plus new financing, will fund operations into 2029, supporting multiple planned clinical milestones. The most important thing to watch is progress in late-stage gene therapy trials, especially for the OPGx-LCA5 program, as these will shape future opportunities.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue declined significantly as collaboration payments tapered off, and management cites ongoing dependence on research and development reimbursement from partners as the business model before commercial launches.

The net loss spike was almost entirely due to a one-time, non-cash accounting adjustment for warrant liabilities and was not the result of higher ongoing cash expenses for research or administration.

Gene therapy pipeline progress included new positive data for OPGx-LCA5, acceptance into an FDA acceleration path, and further advancement of OPGx-BEST1 and other clinical-stage programs, all funded by an expanded cash position.

What Opus Genetics does

Opus Genetics develops gene therapies for inherited retinal diseases using adeno-associated virus technology to target rare genetic mutations that cause vision loss.
