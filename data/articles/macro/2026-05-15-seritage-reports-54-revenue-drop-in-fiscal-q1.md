Seritage Growth Properties (NYSE:SRG) disclosed deeper financial losses as proceeds from asset sales slowed and impairment charges mounted. Notable Developments
Asset sales activity slowed, with the company reporting no assets under contract for likely near-term sale, even after receiving an $11.0 million post-period asset sale.
A $15.2 million impairment charge on one property reflected diminished expected sale values, along with a $5.2 million other-than-temporary impairment on an unconsolidated entity.
Seritage paid regular dividends to preferred shareholders ($0.4375 per share), but absent refinancing or accelerated asset sales, it warned of substantial doubt about its ability to meet future obligations.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management stated that liquidity is insufficient to cover operating expenses and debt service unless it completes asset sales or arranges refinancing before its term loan matures on July 31, 2026. The most important item for investors to monitor in the coming months is progress in securing asset sales or refinancing to address this looming liquidity shortfall.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue dropped by more than half due to a shrinking property portfolio, with reduced rental income now reflecting only 10 revenue-producing sites totaling about 0.8 million square feet. The decrease in regular income deepened net losses and highlighted the company’s vulnerability as more properties exited the portfolio.

Impairment charges totaling $15.2 million signaled that property values are falling below original expectations as current market conditions weigh on sales. Management also recorded a $5.2 million permanent loss on one unconsolidated property investment.

The company’s total cash position fell to $44.5 million, and management noted that committed sale proceeds are now very limited. With only 10 properties left and no signed deals expected to close soon, achieving future liquidity will depend on new sales or successful debt refinancing within the next year.
