Manitowoc Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 21, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

The Manitowoc Company, Inc. (NYSE:MTW) is a global manufacturer of cranes and lifting solutions serving infrastructure, energy, and construction end-markets. The company pursues growth and resilience through its “Cranes+50” strategy, which aims to expand high-margin aftermarket, rentals, and service revenues while investing in new product innovation, lean manufacturing, and geographic diversification.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Robust Backlog and Order Strength Point to Solid Revenue Visibility

Backlog growth and order momentum signal sustained demand and near-term topline stability – At the end of the first quarter of 2026, Manitowoc’s backlog rose to $940 million, up $146 million from year-end 2025 and $10.442 billion year over year, providing a firm base for revenue in upcoming quarters. Orders in Q1 reached $646 million, and April’s order run-rate accelerated to an estimated $225–250 million, above the recent quarterly average. Resurgent demand for all-terrain and tower cranes—especially with dealer inventories at a ten-year low in the United States and a 76% year-over-year surge in European tower crane orders—supports forward sales growth. This robust backlog offers the company a buffer against short-term economic swings, underpins management’s guidance confidence, and reinforces the likelihood of achieving 2026 revenue targets.

2. Recurring Higher-Margin Revenue Streams Enhance Profit Stability

Service, parts, rentals, and accessories make the business less cyclical and bolster margins – Manitowoc’s “Cranes+50” strategy is successfully shifting its revenue mix toward higher-margin, less volatile non-new machine sales. In Q1 2026, trailing twelve-month aftermarket, rental, service, and accessories revenue hit a record $696 million—an 8% increase from the prior year—while Q1 alone saw 3% year-over-year growth despite soft used equipment sales. Expansion of field service technicians (up by 50 to 567 in Q1), new service centers, and technology integration (e.g. ServiceMax asset management) all drive recurring and resilient business. This evolution reduces reliance on new crane cycles, improves gross profit consistency, and positions Manitowoc for steadier long-term earnings, especially as cyclical downturns eventually occur in core construction markets.

3. Strategic Investments in Product Innovation Attract Customer Demand

New model introductions generate customer excitement and long-term sales growth – Manitowoc’s recent product launches, including an 80-ton boom truck and an 800-ton all-terrain crane, have been met with strong operator and customer feedback at major industry showcases like CONEXPO. The upcoming field deployment of major new models in 2027 signals ongoing investment in innovation that can capture market share or support pricing power. By investing in accessory innovations and differentiated technology features (e.g, anti-intrusion panels, asset management), the company can address evolving customer needs, premiumize its offerings, and solidify competitive positioning, all of which provide a runway for both revenue and margin growth well beyond the current cycle.

4. Operational Excellence Initiatives Drive Cost Efficiency and Margin Upside

Continuous improvement under the Manitowoc Way and lean manufacturing supports productivity gains – Manitowoc’s recent operational campaigns, including safety-focused manufacturing changes (like eliminating over 250 hammers at its Katy Grove plant), process optimization through kaizen, and digitalization (with QR code implementation in maintenance), showcase a maturing lean culture. Improvements in real-time assembly feedback and kitting efficiency in logistics centers are already boosting productivity. As these initiatives continue scaling across manufacturing and aftermarket operations, they are expected to lower overhead, reduce scrap and service costs, and contribute to margin expansion, offsetting inflationary or tariff-related headwinds. Management forecasts that these benefits, now in the “early innings,” will become increasingly evident in the second half of 2026.

5. Strengthened Balance Sheet and Liquidity Enable Resilience and Investment

Improved cash flow, ample liquidity, and a recent credit rating upgrade support strategic flexibility – In Q1 2026, Manitowoc delivered $19 million in free cash flow, a $17 million year-over-year improvement driven by better accounts receivable collection. The company ended the quarter with $316 million in liquidity, and its net leverage was a moderate 3.1x. Standard & Poor’s upgraded Manitowoc’s corporate credit rating from B to B+ in April 2026, citing enhanced financial resilience and continued successful strategy execution. With these financial underpinnings, Manitowoc can invest in its rental fleet, service infrastructure, and R&D, while maintaining the flexibility to weather economic and regulatory shocks. This strengthens the company’s ability to convert backlog to cash and pursue margin-enhancing initiatives in the years ahead.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Tariff and Regulatory Headwinds Threaten Margins and Cost Structure

Ongoing tariff exposure and unresolved refund claims create significant earnings unpredictability – Manitowoc faces heavy exposure to U.S. steel-related tariffs under Sections 232, 301, and the Advanced Industrial Input Processing Assessment (AIIPA), with $18 million in Section 232 and $25 million in AIIPA tariffs paid through early 2026. While some relief may come if AIIPA refunds are approved, management remains in “wait-and-see” mode and has voluntarily disclosed potential calculation errors to U.S. Customs. Until these uncertainties resolve and as further tariff risks (especially Section 301) loom, the company’s cost structure and margin outlook remain vulnerable—management’s own guidance assumes no material policy change, but any negative regulatory developments could swiftly derail profitability, especially given tight Q1 margins and adjusted EBITDA of just $20 million (down 10% year over year).

2. Geopolitical Instability and Supply Chain Risks Jeopardize Order Fulfillment

Middle East disruption and global logistics threaten timely revenue conversion – Manitowoc is materially exposed to execution risk in key international markets, as highlighted by the 2026 closure of the Strait of Hormuz. This has effectively blocked crane deliveries into the Middle East, where the company has significant outstanding orders, including for large projects like Dubai Airport. Management has emphasized that the timeline for resumption of shipments is highly uncertain; prolonged disruption could delay, or even result in the loss of, booked revenues and profits. Broader project timing volatility—across infrastructure, energy, and mining end-markets—can be exacerbated by geopolitical tensions, shifting government budgets, or global economic shocks, limiting visibility and amplifying downside risks for investor returns.

3. Prolonged Margin Pressure May Undermine Earnings Recovery Despite Revenue Growth

Slim margins and missed consensus estimates reveal ongoing profitability challenges – The company reported a first-quarter 2026 net loss of $6.0 million (GAAP) and an adjusted net loss of $4.6 million, both totals representing only modest improvement from a $6.3 million loss a year earlier. Margins remained extremely thin, with net margin at just 0.32% and return on equity at 1.71%. The company also missed consensus earnings estimates by $0.17 per share. Although management reaffirmed full-year profit targets, single-digit margins suggest little room for error amid input cost volatility, macro uncertainty, or weaker-than-expected revenue from higher-margin services. If these challenges persist, expected second-half margin improvement may not materialize, undermining the bull case for near-term operating leverage and bottom-line growth.
