SelectQuote Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 1:24 PM

Comments

SelectQuote (NYSE:SLQT) is a technology-driven insurance distribution platform specializing in Medicare Advantage, pharmacy solutions via SelectRx, and life insurance, with a strategy focused on cross-selling, operational efficiency, and expansion into healthcare services. The company aims to leverage tech-enabled marketing, scalable operations, and local franchise initiatives to drive growth and improve profitability across its diversified segments.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Structural Operating Leverage Unlocks Robust Margin Expansion

Significant gains in operational efficiency and marketing productivity are propelling sustainable margin improvement. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, SelectQuote’s revenue-to-customer acquisition cost (Revenue/CAC) reached 6.7x, demonstrating a highly efficient cost structure. Marketing spend per approved Medicare Advantage policy fell 14% compared to two years prior, while agent productivity improved by 1% during the same period. These advances resulted in normalized adjusted EBITDA margins of 26% in the Senior segment even after excluding a one-time receivable adjustment. Efficiency gains are not limited to insurance, as the SelectRx pharmacy now achieves 30%+ shipping efficiencies at the new Olathe facility. This fundamental operating leverage sets the stage for improved cash flow and earnings power as the business scales.

2. Durable Recurring Revenue From Commissions Receivable and Pharmacy Membership

A large, resilient base of recurring receivables and growing pharmacy membership supports cash flow visibility. At the end of Q3 FY2026, SelectQuote reported nearly $1 billion in Medicare Advantage commissions receivable, significantly higher than its market capitalization, reflecting robust expected future cash flows. Persistency metrics are strong, with a recapture rate over 33% during two turbulent years in Medicare. Meanwhile, SelectRx membership grew 11% year over year to nearly 117,000, and prescription shipping volume increased 64% over two years. The company’s model, with 10-year assumed renewal terms and longstanding PBM contracts, creates reliable, high-quality earnings streams, helping insulate performance through market cycles and positioning SelectQuote as a cash-generative consolidator rather than a typical growth-at-all-costs distributor.

3. Expansion Into Healthcare Services Drives Diversification and Growth Optionality

Momentum in Healthcare Services (SelectRx) is broadening the company’s growth drivers and reducing dependency on Medicare distribution. Healthcare Services revenue grew 5% year over year to $199 million in Q3 FY2026, with further margin upside as the Olathe distribution center becomes fully utilized and the proprietary pharmacy management system comes online. SelectRx’s recurring EBITDA is now guided to a $40 million to $50 million run rate, positioning the company to capture a growing slice of senior health and pharmacy spending, while efficiency investments are already expanding gross margins. This diversification both increases SelectQuote’s addressable market and enhances resilience by spreading risk across multiple healthcare and insurance verticals.

4. SelectQuote Local Franchise Initiative Expands Reach With Minimal Capital

The nascent fee-based SelectQuote Local program offers scalable growth through local market penetration with limited balance sheet risk. By empowering local healthcare and insurance providers to leverage the company’s technology and marketing platform on a fee basis, SelectQuote can access new distribution channels and customer segments at a fraction of the up-front cost of traditional expansion. The pilot stage of SelectQuote Local leverages existing data analytics and operational know-how, potentially accelerating top-line growth and customer acquisition without meaningfully increasing fixed expenses. This strategic approach represents a compelling optionality play, as even moderate success could create high-margin ancillary revenue streams and deepen existing customer engagement across product lines.

5. Improving Industry Backdrop and Regulatory Trends Offer Tailwinds

Emerging stability in Medicare Advantage and Pharmacy Benefit Manager (PBM) markets could boost lifetime value (LTV) and profitability. After a period of disruption, medical cost trends in the Medicare Advantage channel are beginning to moderate, but payer reimbursement rates are not yet fully sufficient to cover increased costs. Regulatory changes to Medicare star ratings may provide further upside if carriers manage plan quality improvements. In the pharmacy segment, reimbursement rates have stabilized following new PBM agreements, and headwinds from recent legislation such as the Inflation Reduction Act proved less damaging to margins due to offsetting manufacturer refunds. If these positive trends continue, SelectQuote’s assumptions for policy persistency and LTV could be conservative, allowing for potential upward revaluation of receivables and higher future profits.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Dependence on Medicare Advantage Stability Poses Earnings and Cash Flow Risk

Heavy reliance on the volatile Medicare Advantage market makes SelectQuote vulnerable to regulatory or carrier-driven disruptions. The company’s high commissions receivable balance—nearly $1 billion as of Q3 FY2026—depends on policyholder retention and ongoing favorable renewal trends. Recent years have seen considerable turbulence from plan changes, reimbursement lag, and regulatory adjustments, which forced management to make material accounting adjustments. If negative surprise events, such as carrier exits, reimbursement cuts, or further benefit design changes, recur in the coming years, both receivable recovery rates and LTV assumptions could be too optimistic, directly impacting reported earnings, balance sheet integrity, and operating cash flow.

2. Sustainability of Margin Improvements May Be Challenged by One-Time Gains and Cost Pressures

Recent margin improvement is partly attributed to one-off receivable adjustments, which may mask underlying cost and competitive challenges. In Q3 FY2026, the Senior segment reported a 26% normalized EBITDA margin after excluding a $14 million positive adjustment tied to renewal expectations. Higher policy approval rates in the quarter may have been a product of timing effects rather than structural growth. Additionally, the core Term Life business continues to face market headwinds from shifting consumer media habits and intensifying competition, making it challenging to maintain or increase overall profitability. Management cautioned there could be elevated disruption in the coming year as carriers pursue target margin goals, which could affect profitability.

3. Execution Risks in Scaling Healthcare Services and New Initiatives Remain High

Ongoing investment and operational complexity in scaling SelectRx and launching new franchises introduce considerable execution and integration risks. While SelectRx’s Olathe facility operates at efficiency, it is currently underutilized—shipping less than 20% of total prescriptions and working one shift on less than half of facility space. Success depends on rapidly migrating business onto new systems and scaling membership volume, which could encounter unforeseen technical or operational hurdles. Similarly, the SelectQuote Local initiative is early and highly speculative, making eventual financial impact uncertain. Any missteps in these growth programs could lead to prolonged payback periods, added costs, or distraction from the established profit engines, weakening the investment thesis if projected cross-segment synergies fail to materialize.
