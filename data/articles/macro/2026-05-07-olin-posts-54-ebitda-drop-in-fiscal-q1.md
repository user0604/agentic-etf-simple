Olin Posts 54% EBITDA Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:37 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Olin reported a net loss as profitability fell sharply from a year ago.
Adjusted EBITDA fell 53.6% year over year in the quarter.
Management expects stronger earnings in the next quarter due to seasonality and higher demand.

Olin (NYSE:OLN) booked a quarterly net loss as it faced weaker pricing and higher costs across its core chemical businesses. The leading global supplier of chlor alkali products, epoxies, and ammunition declared results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed deteriorating profits despite sales growth in select areas, reflecting a tough period for its core operations.

Notable Developments
Profitability worsened, with the company recording a net loss of $83.0 million and adjusted EBITDA dropping by over half year over year.
Chlor Alkali and Vinyls sales fell 18.1% due to lower trading volumes and weaker prices, while the Epoxy segment posted both higher sales and smaller operating losses.
The Winchester ammunition segment grew sales by 21.2%, but rising raw material costs led to lower earnings in that business.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects a substantial sequential improvement in adjusted EBITDA for the next quarter, supported by stronger seasonal demand and higher expected pricing in chemicals and ammunition.

Quarterly Highlights

The company's core Chlor Alkali Products and Vinyls segment, which produces chlorine and caustic soda used in water treatment and manufacturing, saw revenue shrink considerably from the prior year. This contraction followed lower volumes and the wind-down of a trading joint venture, adding pressure to operating results.

Epoxy, a business unit making resins and coatings used in durable goods and construction, reported higher sales driven by European market strength and lower production costs. Although this led to a much narrower loss, product margins remained slightly weaker than the previous year.

Winchester, which supplies ammunition products for both military and commercial applications, saw strong revenue growth on increased military demand. However, input costs for key metals and materials jumped, squeezing segment earnings despite improved pricing actions.

What Olin Does

Olin is a global manufacturer of chemicals such as chlorine, caustic soda, and epoxy resins, and supplies ammunition for defense and civilian markets.
