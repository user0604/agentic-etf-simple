KT Posts 32% Profit Drop in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
KT reported profits falling (net income fell 31.5% year over year and operating income fell 29.9% year over year) and revenue slipping slightly (down 1.0% year over year) in the first quarter.
Earnings fell 31.5% year over year as margins compressed.
Management reaffirmed its dividend policy and focus on platform and cybersecurity growth.

KT (NYSE:KT) faced a challenging quarter as both profit and revenue declined sharply compared to a strong prior-year period. The leading South Korean telecommunications and digital services provider saw slower growth in wireless services, higher costs, and a tough comparison after last year's one-time real estate gains. The earnings release, published May 11, 2026, covers the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period was marked by lower earnings and continued investment beyond traditional telecom.

Notable developments
Wireless service revenue edged up 0.4% year over year, with wireless subscribers reaching 29.16 million, a 10.3% increase.
Operating expenses rose 2.3%, driven by labor costs up 4.6% and selling expenses up 9.1%.
KT declared a dividend policy targeting at least KRW 2,400 per share for fiscal 2026 and announced a Q1 dividend of KRW 600 per share.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its focus on platform-based growth in areas such as cybersecurity and network infrastructure, while maintaining a high shareholder return policy. The company did not change its full-year financial outlook in this release. Investors should monitor progress in digital services and cost control in the coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Wireless services saw subscriber growth, especially in the Mobile Virtual Network Operator segment, but average revenue per user slipped 0.2% and the customer churn rate rose to 1.7%. These trends point to price competition and some pressure on consumer revenue stability.

Media operations remained steady, with internet television (IPTV) revenue up 1.3% and broadband revenue up 1.8%. However, customer additions slowed, and content subsidiaries posted a modest 1.9% gain, reflecting a challenging market.

Digital and business-to-business (B2B) initiatives made limited progress, with B2B service revenue down 2.2% and cloud revenue nearly flat at 0.4% growth. KT continued to invest in technology infrastructure, but these areas have yet to drive significant company-wide growth.

What KT does

KT provides telecommunications, broadband, media, financial, and digital platform services primarily in South Korea.
