Fluent Posts 104% Commerce Media Gain in Q1

The Motley Fool Team

Key Takeaways
Fluent posted mixed results as strong growth in its Commerce Media Solutions business helped offset declines in legacy operations.
EPS loss narrowed to an adjusted $(0.19) per share as revenue dropped 19% year over year.
Management reaffirmed its target for double-digit full-year revenue growth in its core businesses, with an improved adjusted EBITDA loss.

Fluent (NASDAQ:FLNT) reported a mixed quarter as rapid growth in its Commerce Media Solutions segment helped counter planned declines in legacy business lines. The performance marketing company is shifting its focus from older “owned and operated” marketing tools to data-driven commerce media, aiming to build growth through technology and advertiser partnerships. The company released first-quarter results for the period ending March 31, 2026, on May 13, 2026. Overall, the period showed tangible progress in business mix, but persistent losses and funding risks remain important concerns.

Notable Developments
Commerce Media Solutions revenue more than doubled year over year, reaching $25.9 million and now accounting for 58% of total revenue.
Legacy owned and operated revenue contracted by 50%, with company-wide revenue falling 19% due to the absence of Call Solutions and ongoing segment transition.
Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $(3.6) million, while net loss narrowed year over year; cash flow benefited from a drawdown in accounts receivable.
Management outlook and what to watch

Leadership reiterated a target for full-year double-digit revenue growth from continuing operations and improved adjusted EBITDA performance for fiscal 2026. Management aims to boost gross margins in the Commerce Media segment toward the “mid-twenties” percent range as the business scales. Investors should monitor liquidity closely, as ongoing funding needs and a reliance on an uncommitted financing agreement create uncertainty about near-term cash coverage.

Quarterly highlights

The Commerce Media Solutions business posted 104% revenue growth from the prior year, expanding into new markets such as travel and appointment-based platforms. This growth followed a deliberate downsizing in the “owned and operated” (O&O) segment, which saw revenue drop by half and now represents a minority of the company’s business mix.

While overall media margin improved to 31% of revenue, gross margin for the Commerce Media line fell to 19% from 22% a year ago—well below management’s stated target. Adjusted EBITDA losses increased, with profitability pressure linked to both early-stage scaling costs and narrowed margins in newer contracts.

The period’s net operating cash flow turned positive, largely due to cash released from accounts receivable. Despite this, management flagged continued liquidity and “going concern” risk, with disclosed reliance on external, uncommitted funding agreements to support operations.
