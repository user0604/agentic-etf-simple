WEC Energy Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 21, 2026, 3:05 PM

Comments

WEC Energy Group, Inc. (NYSE:WEC) is a leading utility holding company serving over 4.7 million customers across Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, and Minnesota. The company’s strategy centers on sustained regulated rate base growth, major capital investments in grid modernization and renewables, and meeting rising electric demand from large-scale data centers and industrial customers. Management aims for long-term, high-single-digit earnings growth paired with a strong dividend policy, leveraging constructive regulatory frameworks and leadership in energy infrastructure transformation.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Accelerating Load Growth From Data Centers Will Fuel Sustained Earnings Expansion

Hyperscale data centers and tech-driven commercial activity are set to drive above-average electric load growth for WEC Energy Group in the second half of the decade. The company reported 1.3% year-over-year growth in weather-normalized retail electric deliveries in the first quarter of 2026, led by a robust 3% increase in large commercial and industrial volumes. Management forecasts 1.5% electric sales growth for the year and cites a significant pipeline of hyperscale data center projects—like the Vantage site with a forecasted 1.3 gigawatts of demand over five years, and potential to reach 3.5 gigawatts. This scale of incremental load is well above national utility averages and underpins rate base growth, positioning the company to capitalize on digital infrastructure megatrends as cloud, AI, and advanced manufacturing expand in its territory.

2. Regulatory Structures Provide Above-Average Returns and Revenue Visibility

Constructive regulatory environments in Wisconsin and Illinois enable above-peer returns with strong cost recovery assurance. The Wisconsin Public Service Commission recently approved a new Very Large Customer (VLC) tariff with an authorized return on equity (ROE) between 10.48% and 10.98% and a 57% equity ratio—metrics at the high end of the sector. The VLC structure protects both industrial and standard customers from cross-subsidization, while allowing WEC Energy Group to match capital investments directly with customer load. In Illinois, regulatory settlements and clear rate case cadence for infrastructure replacement provide further revenue certainty. These frameworks give management reliable cost recovery and investment incentives, supporting stable, forecastable earnings growth amid ongoing sector transformation.

3. Robust Capital Plan and Project Execution Position Company for Rate Base Growth

The company’s $37.5 billion five-year capital plan is purpose-built for rate base expansion and low-risk growth. WEC Energy Group’s focus is on “low risk and highly executable” investments, including $225 million in new solar facilities already entering service in 2026 and approvals for $730 million in additional solar and battery storage projects. Ongoing construction of new gas plants at Paris and Oak Creek remains on schedule, and management proactively extended the life of legacy Oak Creek units through 2027 to guarantee reliability and customer affordability during generation transitions. By 2030, an estimated 15% of assets will support very large customers, securing a strong foundation for regulated asset expansion and future cash flows.

4. Dividend Growth Track Record Remains Highly Competitive Among Utilities

A 23-year streak of consecutive annual dividend increases and a 6.5%–7% growth policy highlight reliable shareholder returns. In January 2026, the board increased the dividend by 6.7%, consistent with its stated goal and supported by a prudent payout ratio. This approach gives investors a combination of income stability and growth, even as peer utilities compete with climbing risk-free Treasury yields. WEC Energy Group also projects annual EPS growth of 7%–8% through 2030, with expectations for an uptick to the high end of that range beginning in 2028. The resilience of WEC’s payout policy—with disciplined capital management—stands out in a market environment where reliable total return is highly valued.

5. Balance Sheet Strength and Proactive Equity Funding Reinforce Growth Capacity

Disciplined capital structure management ensures flexibility for major investments without sacrificing credit quality. By issuing $455 million in common equity in the first quarter of 2026—nearly half the planned $1.1 billion equity raise for the year—WEC Energy Group is staying ahead of capital markets risk and regulatory expectations. With a targeted 57% equity ratio and commitments to fund incremental capital with 50% equity content, the company’s approach mitigates leverage creep as it embarks on its multi-decade infrastructure program. This balance gives the company the capacity to invest in grid upgrades, clean energy, and generation replacement while supporting investment grade ratings and lowering long-term funding costs.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Rising Interest Rates and Competitive Yields Could Pressure Utility Valuations

The persistent threat of higher long-term interest rates weighs on the relative appeal of utility stocks like WEC Energy Group. As 10-year and 30-year Treasury yields have risen through 2025 and into 2026, rate-sensitive sectors have faced valuation pressure and outflows. Utilities with steady but moderate growth—despite robust dividend histories—must compete with safer yields on government bonds, especially when Treasuries approach or exceed 4.75%. This dynamic increases the risk that total returns will lag higher-yielding alternatives, potentially leading to lower valuation multiples or a muted share price response even amid solid fundamentals and execution.

2. Execution and Cost Overrun Risk on Large-Scale Capital Projects Remains Material

WEC Energy Group’s ambitious $37.5 billion capital plan and large-scale renewable and gas infrastructure portfolio expose the company to significant project execution risk. Building new solar facilities, battery storage, and major gas plants on tight schedules can be challenged by supply chain bottlenecks, local permitting delays, or escalating construction costs—all issues that have recently impacted the broader utility sector. While management maintains that recent projects remain on track, any slippage, cost overruns, or construction delays could jeopardize expected rate base growth and trigger regulatory scrutiny. Proactive engagement with stakeholders and biweekly developer meetings help, but the sheer volume and complexity of infrastructure coming online through 2030 introduce unavoidable uncertainty.

3. Regulatory and Policy Changes in Key States Could Disrupt Growth Trajectory

Shifts in regulatory sentiment, particularly in Illinois, or policy-driven changes to energy transition pathways present potential headwinds for WEC Energy Group’s long-term earnings outlook. Although the company has achieved constructive settlements and tariff approvals recently, ongoing reliance on favorable commission outcomes for annual rate cases—especially in jurisdictions like Illinois with a reputation for challenging settlements—poses risk. Additionally, environmental policy shifts or federal regulatory changes affecting the economics of gas-fired and renewable projects could require adjustments to investment plans or result in stranded cost risk. Management’s current confidence in regulatory relationships is justified, but the inherent unpredictability of future policy settings cannot be ignored when assessing the durability of growth assumptions over the next decade.
