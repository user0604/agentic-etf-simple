Enact Holdings Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 3:07 PM

Comments

Enact Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ:ACT) is a leading provider of private mortgage insurance, supporting the U.S. housing market by enabling borrowers with modest down payments to become homeowners. The company’s growth strategy emphasizes disciplined risk management, advanced pricing analytics, and capital efficiency. Enact invests in proprietary technology like its Rate360 pricing engine and seeks long-term expansion through prudent capital allocation and innovation in underwriting and reinsurance.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Sustained Earnings Power Signals Durable Profitability Amid Cyclical Headwinds

Enact’s robust and resilient earnings profile underpins optimism for long-term shareholder value. In the first quarter of 2026, the company reported an adjusted operating income of $172 million ($1.21 per diluted share) and a healthy adjusted return on equity of 12.9%. This performance was an improvement over the $1.10 per diluted share posted in Q1 2025, despite ongoing industry-wide mortgage origination pressure. Such resilience indicates management’s effectiveness at offsetting cycle-driven slowdowns through expense discipline, credit risk management, and scalable operations. The consistency in both GAAP and non-GAAP profitability demonstrates stability, positioning Enact to weather market downturns and preserve returns for shareholders, a key competitive differentiator for insurers in a volatile sector.

2. Capital Strength and Generous Payouts Provide Downside Protection and Upside Optionality

Enact’s strong capital surplus and rising dividends highlight its ability to withstand shocks and reward shareholders. As of March 31, 2026, the company’s Private Mortgage Insurer Eligibility Requirements (PMIERs) sufficiency ratio was an impressive 162%, equating to $1.9 billion in excess capital. This surplus, bolstered by $1.9 billion in additional capital relief from credit risk transfer programs, supports management’s confidence in returning capital—seen in the Board’s 14% dividend increase and a full-year 2026 capital return target of approximately $500 million. Such robust capital management not only insulates Enact against unforeseen credit events but also enhances long-term return potential through repurchases and consistent dividend growth, even in uncertain market conditions.

3. Advanced Pricing Technology Secures Margin Stability and Competitive Edge

Proprietary analytics like Rate360 help protect Enact’s margins while adapting to shifting market risks. The company’s Rate360 pricing engine leverages machine learning to tailor premiums and manage risk across over 300 metro areas, enabling dynamic responses to geographic housing trends and borrower credit changes. For instance, customized pricing adjustments in riskier regions such as Florida and Texas offset local home price volatility, while real-time analytics support granular loan-level underwriting. During Q1 2026, management maintained a flattish base premium rate even as market mix shifted, demonstrating pricing discipline. This approach is likely to preserve profitability and risk-adjusted returns, setting Enact apart from competitors who may face margin compression in a volatile housing cycle.

4. Credit Performance Remains Benign, Supporting Lower Losses and Strong Balance Sheet

Stable credit metrics limit loss volatility and underpin consistent capital generation. In Q1 2026, total delinquencies fell 1% sequentially, with new delinquencies holding at 1.5%—in line with historical, pre-pandemic norms. Cures rose 13% quarter over quarter, and the cure rate remained elevated at 54%, well above previous multi-year levels. Even with a loss ratio rising to 15% (from 7% in Q4 2025, due in part to normalization and reserve releases), the portfolio’s high-quality credit mix—risk-weighted average FICO of 746 and only 1.2% of risk-in-force exposed to layered risk—has helped keep losses manageable. Such performance enables Enact to retain more capital and cushion shareholder returns, even as macro uncertainties persist.

5. Strategic Diversification Efforts Lay the Groundwork for Long-Term Growth

Enact’s ongoing investments in reinsurance and technology set the stage for growth beyond the core mortgage insurance market. The Enact Re platform continues to deliver risk-adjusted returns in a capital- and expense-efficient manner, while ongoing enhancements to analytics and artificial intelligence sharpen the company’s pricing and risk selection. Management’s focus on organic growth, disciplined investment, and openness to new strategic segments signals an intent to diversify earnings sources and reduce reliance on mortgage origination cycles. Such forward-thinking initiatives could help the company capture share and drive profit growth as the housing finance market evolves and as competitors with less diversified offerings struggle in the face of policy or demand shifts.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Mortgage Market Volatility Threatens New Business Growth and Revenue Stability

Enact remains exposed to the broader housing and mortgage cycle, which could curb growth if markets deteriorate. In the first quarter of 2026, New Insurance Written (NIW) totaled $13 billion, down 11% sequentially due to seasonality and higher rates, and persistency fell four points year over year as more loans became refinanceable. If macro conditions worsen—like a further rise in rates, job losses, or declining home prices—both new business and renewal volumes could suffer, resulting in lower premiums earned and pressure on profitability. As the company’s revenues are inherently linked to mortgage activity, a prolonged downturn could throttle top-line growth and squeeze margins despite operational resilience.

2. Rising Loss Ratios and Delinquency Normalization May Pressure Earnings if Housing Softens

Loss ratio normalization poses risk if credit trends worsen or if cures deteriorate from today’s unusually strong levels. While Q1 2026 delinquencies and cures compare favorably to pre-pandemic norms, the loss ratio rose to 15% from 7% in the prior quarter, signaling vulnerability to broader credit cycle shifts. If home price appreciation stalls, or if the labor market weakens, cure rates may fall and claim activity could accelerate, increasing losses and eroding earnings. Given the mortgage insurance sector’s lags to macro shocks, a delayed uptick in defaults could surprise to the downside, including higher-than-expected capital charges and weaker dividend potential.

3. Regulatory or Policy Changes Could Adversely Impact Risk Appetite and Capital Requirements

Potential changes in government-sponsored enterprise (GSE) policy or credit scoring adoption introduce uncertainty to Enact’s risk and capital frameworks. The phased rollout of new credit scoring models like VantageScore 4.0, pending clarification on interaction with PMIERs standards, could alter the risk profile or capital requirements for new originations. If GSEs modify eligibility or increase capital requirements, Enact may need to hold more capital or restrict business, potentially compressing returns. Although management reports operational readiness, any abrupt regulatory shifts—especially in stress scenarios—could force pricing or strategic pivots that reduce competitiveness or financial flexibility relative to the sector.
