Key Takeaways

Earnings per share loss improved to $(0.03) from $(0.08), while revenue slipped less than 1% year over year.

Sportswear brand Under Armour (NYSE:UA) continued its reset, with disciplined cost management helping control losses, but ongoing revenue declines and shrinking margins show challenges persist. The company focuses on high-performance apparel and footwear, targeting athletes through branded retail and partnerships. On May 12, 2026, it released financial results for the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, which ended March 31.

Overall, the quarter showed progress in cost control but highlighted deeper sales and margin pressures, especially in North America.

Notable Developments
North America revenue fell 7%, while international revenue rose 10% in the quarter, reflecting ongoing regional divergence.
Gross margin dropped to 43.1% (Non-GAAP), a decline of 3.6 percentage points, with management citing higher tariffs and cost pressures.
Direct-to-consumer sales grew 5%, driven by an 8% increase in owned-store revenue, but e-commerce was flat.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management forecasts another slight revenue decline in fiscal 2027, expecting low single-digit decreases in North America, partly offset by modest growth in Europe and Asia-Pacific. Most margin improvements in the year ahead will come from anticipated tariff-related refunds rather than operational gains. Investors should closely watch North America sales and the impact of increased marketing investments as the company seeks to regain growth there.

Quarterly Highlights

North America remained a key challenge, with quarterly revenue dropping to $641 million, down 7% from the prior year. International sales, however, climbed to $539 million, up 10%, led by Europe, the Middle East, and Africa, which increased 7%, and Latin America, up 22%.

Gross margin, which shows how much money remains from sales after the cost of production, dropped to 43.1% (Non-GAAP) as a result of higher tariffs on imported goods, increased product costs, and unfavorable pricing. This decline exceeded management’s earlier estimates and continues a trend from previous quarters.

Direct-to-consumer sales provided a bright spot as owned retail stores posted 8% growth, but e-commerce remained unchanged from last year and lagged industry expectations. Wholesale revenue dropped 3% in the quarter, continuing a longer-term decline.
