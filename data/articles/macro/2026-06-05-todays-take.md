THE QUESTION

What’s a stock that’s been on your watchlist for a long time but has never made it into your portfolio?

1. Uber Passes the Snap Test

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

For whatever reason, I still haven’t added Uber (NYSE: UBER) to my portfolio, despite the fact that I like the business and have enjoyed watching members benefit from my recommending the stock back in mid-2022. I think the battle between Lyft (NASDAQ: LYFT) and Uber has largely been answered, and I was really impressed to see in the most recent earnings call that Uber One members have now surpassed the 50 million mark (I must admit I was a bit skeptical that it would gain such traction).

Uber is now working on becoming basically an everything app, and that gives me a little bit of pause, but so far the company has shown its mettle, so it’s hard for me to doubt it will succeed. Maybe this Today’s Take is the kick in the pants I need to finally buy a few shares.

2. r/Redditstock

By Sanmeet Deo
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

It is common at our investment team meetings to discuss or pitch stocks formally or informally. A couple years ago, around September 2024, I did some brief research on Reddit (NYSE: RDDT), pitched it to our team, and placed it on my watch list. Reddit is widely known as a big Internet discussion board. However, with the advent of AI, it was licensing its vast trove of human conversations and data to the likes of Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOGL) as training data for Google’s LLM models. It seemed like a great idea, though I wasn’t sure how much juice it could squeeze from that orange.

Well, it was also beefing up the advertising business. Since then, the stock has gone from around $60 to above $180, a 3x return. I’m so happy I didn’t buy it (being sarcastic). A big regret in my investing is not being more aggressively buying my watch list stocks.

3. Not There Yet, But Could Be

By Neha Chamaria
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Some companies try to do too many things at a time and lose themselves in the game. Honeywell International (NASDAQ: HON) has been on my watchlist for as long as I can remember, but a bloated portfolio of a variety of businesses that keep canceling each other out has always held me back from owning the stock.

I’m glad I didn’t buy, because shares have gone nowhere in the last five years. Yet I never kicked the American industrial icon out of my watchlist, and that hunch could possibly pay off now. After listing its specialty chemicals business separately as Soltice Advanced Materials (NASDAQ: SOLS), Honeywell is now splitting its aerospace and automation businesses. Perhaps this stock will finally move from my watchlist to my portfolio.
