Key Takeaways
Enbridge delivered steady results with slight weakness in liquids pipelines offset by gains in gas utilities and storage.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) declined to $0.98 from $1.03, while distributable cash flow (Non-GAAP) increased 2.0%.
Management reaffirmed its full-year guidance, signaling stable expectations despite continued high leverage.

Steady but mixed performance defined the first quarter for Enbridge (NYSE:ENB), as strength in key utility and storage businesses was dampened by a notable drop in its liquids pipelines segment. The leading North American energy infrastructure company released its financial results for the quarter ending March 31, 2026, on May 8, 2026. Overall, the period balanced continued growth in regulated businesses with caution on profitability and debt levels.

Notable Developments
Liquids pipelines saw Adjusted EBITDA fall 12.1%, mainly due to lower Mainline tolls and the absence of prior-year gains from one-off litigation settlements.
Gas Distribution and Storage operations improved, posting a 6.8% increase in Adjusted EBITDA, driven by rate escalators and higher unregulated storage revenues in core Ontario operations.
The secured capital project backlog grew to $40 billion, with new wind and gas storage projects sanctioned, supporting long-term investment plans.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year outlook, keeping its 2026 Adjusted EBITDA and distributable cash flow per share guidance unchanged. The company plans to invest $10–11 billion annually in growth projects, maintaining focus on regulated and contracted businesses. Investors should monitor the leverage ratio, which remains at the upper end of the company’s stated range and could affect future spending flexibility.

Quarterly Highlights

The liquids pipelines segment produced $2.3 billion in Adjusted EBITDA, down from the previous year’s $2.6 billion, as lower rates and the lack of one-time gains impacted profitability. Mainline pipeline volumes stayed high at 3.2 million barrels per day, but cost and earnings-sharing negatively affected margins.

Gas Distribution and Storage, which covers natural gas delivery and storage for utilities, added $109 million of Adjusted EBITDA year over year. Growth was fueled by new rate structures in Ontario, Utah, and North Carolina, alongside strong demand in unregulated Ontario storage.

Enbridge expanded its renewable power portfolio by sanctioning a large wind project in Texas. Although Adjusted EBITDA for renewables declined $39 million compared to last year, the company continues to pursue growth through projects contracted to major technology customers.
