Leslie's Fiscal Q2 Shows Customer Growth and Margin Gains, but Losses Persist

Leslie's (NASDAQ:LESL) delivered encouraging signs from its transformation plan, with customer counts rebounding and margins improving. The leading U.S. direct-to-consumer pool and spa care retailer posted revenue growth and better gross profits in the fiscal second quarter (results released May 13, 2026, covering the quarter ended April 4, 2026). Still, net losses remain substantial, and full-year progress will depend heavily on the second half of fiscal 2026.

Overall, the quarter showed progress on operations and customer engagement, but profit metrics continued to reflect the uphill path toward sustainable financial health.

Notable developments
Revenue increased by 4.3% while comparable sales rose 6.6%, and total customer count expanded 8% year over year.
Gross margin improved by more than 4 percentage points as cost controls and newly launched pricing initiatives took hold.
Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed by $9.2 million, but net loss increased to $52.5 million as investment in pricing and transformation continued.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its full-year guidance for sales and adjusted EBITDA, relying on a strong seasonal lift in the second half. With store closures and most one-time charges completed, the focus turns to whether customer and sales gains can carry through peak pool season. Investors should closely watch sustained gains in customer counts and gross margin, as these metrics are key to the path back to profitability.

Quarterly highlights

The period reflected solid recovery in comparable sales and customer counts, showing that the “Price Drop” campaign and targeted marketing are drawing both new and returning shoppers. Leslie's reported its active customer file grew, reversing prior declines, and its direct-to-consumer leadership remained central to its business strategy.

The improvement in adjusted EBITDA loss, along with stable selling, general, and administrative expenses as a percentage of revenue, underscored efforts to manage costs and fund margin investments even as net income stayed negative.

Inventory dropped 21.7% from a year ago, supporting healthier working capital and reflecting the company’s focus on efficiency. Management reported that the planned round of store closures and associated impairment charges is essentially finished, shifting attention to operational improvements in remaining locations and digital channels.

Despite operational strides, liquidity and leverage are still concerns. Cash balances remained steady, but total liabilities continued to exceed assets, and leverage stayed high with long-term debt near $753 million. The ability to meet full-year sales and profit guidance will hinge on conversion of recent customer momentum into profitable summer season sales.

What Leslie's does

Leslie's operates a national chain of pool supply stores and a direct-to-consumer digital platform, selling chemicals, equipment, and services for pool and spa care to both residential and professional customers.
