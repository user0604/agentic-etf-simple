Earnings Preview: Vivos Therapeutics

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Vivos Therapeutics (NASDAQ:VVOS), a company that develops and sells oral devices and related services for sleep apnea and snoring, is expected to report its Q1 FY2026 earnings on Thursday, May 21, 2026. The company typically reports after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of 2025, Vivos Therapeutics completed its second full quarter following the June 2025 acquisition of the Sleep Center of Nevada (SCN). The company’s revenue for the full year 2025 grew by 16% to $17.4 million, driven by increased sleep testing services and treatment revenue at SCN locations. However, Q4 revenue was notably lower than Q3, primarily due to provider shortages. Gross profit for the year rose 17% to $10.5 million, with gross margin steady at 60%. The company’s operating loss widened to $19.9 million, reflecting higher costs from the SCN integration and business model transition. Net loss for the year was $21.2 million. The company also raised additional capital after year-end to support operations and maintain NASDAQ listing compliance.

Positives
Revenue growth from new model: The shift to a medical provider-focused model and the acquisition of SCN led to a significant increase in sleep testing and treatment revenue, which offset declines in legacy dental channel sales.
Stable gross margin: Gross margin remained at 60% despite higher costs, indicating the new revenue streams are not diluting profitability at the gross level.
Insurance access expanded: SCN operations achieved in-network status with several commercial insurers and Medicare, which should improve patient access and future revenue potential.
Operational learnings implemented: Management identified and addressed provider shortages that impacted Q4, adding redundancy to reduce future disruptions.
Negatives
Q4 revenue drop due to provider shortages: The company experienced an unexpected loss of several providers in Q4, which led to lower patient volume and revenue compared to Q3.
Higher operating expenses: General and administrative costs increased significantly, mainly from integrating SCN and related upfront investments, which contributed to a wider net loss.
Legacy revenue declines: Revenue from the company’s previous dental-focused model (VIP enrollments and appliance sales) continued to fall, creating a drag on overall results during the transition.
Ongoing capital needs and NASDAQ compliance risk: As of year-end, the company had low cash reserves and required additional equity financing to maintain listing requirements and fund operations.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Recovery in patient volume and revenue: Investors should look for signs that the provider shortages from Q4 have been resolved and that patient appointments and revenue are rebounding toward Q3 levels, in line with management’s expectations.
Impact of expanded insurance coverage: The effect of new in-network insurance agreements at SCN should begin to show in Q1 results, potentially improving both patient access and collections.
Progress on cost control and cash runway: Watch for updates on cost-saving initiatives and any further equity raises, as these are key to the company’s ability to fund operations and comply with NASDAQ requirements.
Status of new affiliations and expansion: The company has announced plans to expand its SO (Sleep Optimization) teams and pursue new affiliations with specialty medical groups. Updates on these initiatives and their early contribution to revenue will be important.
Developments in clinical studies and product pipeline: The Stanford University clinical trial for the DNA appliance remains on hold. Any updates on resumption or new clinical data, as well as progress on new product offerings or partnerships, could affect investor sentiment.
