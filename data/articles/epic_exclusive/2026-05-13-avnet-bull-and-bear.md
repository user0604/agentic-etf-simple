Avnet Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 3:07 PM

Comments

Avnet, Inc. (NASDAQ:AVT) is a global distributor of electronic components and embedded technology, supplying manufacturers across sectors such as industrial, networking, data centers, and artificial intelligence. The company’s growth strategy centers on expanding its portfolio through new product introductions (SKUs), advanced supply chain solutions, digital investments (notably e-commerce), and deepened demand-creation activity, targeting secular trends like AI and robotics while maintaining a disciplined approach to working capital and shareholder returns.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Share Gains Accelerate as Avnet Leverages End-Market Tailwinds in AI, Data Center, and Industrial

Double-digit revenue growth across key sectors highlights powerful secular momentum – In its third fiscal quarter of 2026, Avnet achieved 34% year-over-year revenue growth, with Electronic Components (EC) sales hitting record highs. Notably, the data center and artificial intelligence (AI) vertical now comprise 10–15% of EC revenue (up from 5–7% historically), reflecting strategic alignment with rapid technology adoption trends. Industrial, forming over 30% of EC sales, also rebounded sharply. The company’s broad exposure to high-growth sectors—spanning data centers, networking, and automation—positions it to capitalize on multi-year digitalization and reshoring cycles. Sustaining or expanding market share in these verticals creates substantial runway for ongoing topline expansion as customer demand for advanced semiconductors and related components intensifies.

2. Regional Expansion in Asia Fuels Revenue Growth and Deepens Supplier Relationships

Asia’s outperformance transforms Avnet’s global footprint and supply chain positioning – In Q3 FY2026, Asia contributed 49% of sales (up from 47% a year prior), with revenue in the region surging 39% year over year despite seasonally slow periods. This growth not only diversifies the company’s customer base and spreads geographic risk, but also cements Avnet as a preferred partner for leading-edge component manufacturers operating in fast-growing Asian markets. The scale of Asian operations enhances bargaining power, supply flexibility, and inventory sourcing, allowing the business to better serve global customers during periods of supply chain stress or lead-time extension, now affecting over half of product categories. Robust regional sales growth provides a durable engine for future company-wide expansion.

3. Strategic Investments in Digital, E-Commerce, and SKU Breadth Drive Customer Engagement and Resilience

Heavy investment in technology and inventory breadth positions Avnet for long-term market adaptability – In fiscal 2026, Avnet introduced approximately 130,000 new stock-keeping units, greatly expanding its offering in semiconductors and electromechanical components. Simultaneously, the company has enhanced digital platforms and e-commerce channels, especially within the Farnell segment, accelerating customer acquisition and improving operational agility. This digital and inventory scale not only enables the business to capitalize on sudden demand spikes, but also future-proofs operations against market volatility. Design-win revenue grew a robust 16% sequentially in Q3, evidencing the benefits of increased field application engineering and digital outreach. Together, these initiatives underpin sustainable growth, improved customer retention, and faster response times to evolving industry needs.

4. Strong Backlog and High Book-to-Bill Ratios Provide Multi-Quarter Revenue Visibility

Sustained demand pipeline points to continued short- and medium-term growth – Management reported well-above-parity book-to-bill ratios in all major regions, with a growing backlog as of Q3 FY2026. These indicators suggest that Avnet’s current sales run rate is likely to persist into subsequent quarters, supported by customers increasingly providing longer-term purchase commitments amid component lead-time extensions in over 50% of tracked categories. This visibility allows for better inventory planning, pricing decisions, and financial forecasting. As a result, investors can have higher confidence in near-term revenue stability and Avnet’s ability to navigate supply-demand imbalances, minimizing earnings volatility during potential industry slowdowns.

5. Operational Excellence and Disciplined Capital Allocation Enhance Long-Term Margin and Return Potential

Ongoing efficiency gains and robust capital returns strengthen shareholder value – Avnet increased adjusted group operating margin to 3.1% in Q3 FY2026 (up 40 basis points sequentially), and made significant strides in improving SG&A (selling, general, and administrative) leverage, aiming for a mid-60s percentage of gross profit. Inventory days ended at 77, exceeding management’s target of under 80, while return on working capital improved over 300 basis points quarter over quarter. The business maintains a solid balance sheet, with gross leverage declining to 3.6x (targeting 3x by year-end) and $1.7 billion in committed liquidity. Continued shareholder returns through dividends and share repurchases—$224 million returned year-to-date—underscore prudent capital management. These operational improvements provide margin expansion opportunities and support future investments, reinforcing long-term return prospects.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Reliance on Memory Price Increases Creates Risk of Unsustainable Growth Trends

Exceptional recent results were heavily driven by non-recurring pricing tailwinds – Approximately half of Avnet’s sequential revenue growth and a quarter of annual growth in Q3 FY2026 stemmed from surging memory prices, according to management. These tailwinds are expected to moderate, as price increases in memory and other components are unlikely to be repeated at similar scales. If volume growth does not accelerate to offset this normalization, headline growth rates may sharply decelerate. As pricing power recedes, gross margin could also come under renewed pressure, especially if macroeconomic headwinds temper end-market demand or incentivize channel inventory reductions by customers.

2. Expanding Exposure to Asia May Weigh on Margins and Heighten Geopolitical Risks

Revenue mix shift to Asia introduces structural and external challenges – While Asia accounted for 49% of sales in Q3 FY2026 and drove outperformance, the regional sales mix carries inherent trade-offs. Margins are structurally lower in Asia due to competitive pricing and cost structures, reflected in a group gross margin compression to 10.4%. Additionally, geopolitical and macro risks loom large, including supply chain chokepoints, currency volatility, and potential exposure to regional instability. Any escalation in trade friction, regulatory uncertainty, or broad-based economic slowdown across Asia could expose Avnet to outsized volume, pricing, or earnings volatility versus more diversified peers.

3. Intensifying Inventory and Working Capital Requirements Could Constrain Free Cash Flow and Flexibility

Rapid sales growth has absorbed significant cash, potentially limiting capital deployment options – To support an $800 million sequential sales uptick in Q3 FY2026, Avnet deployed an additional $168 million into inventory and absorbed a negative operating cash flow of $54 million. Ongoing investment in accounts receivable and expanded inventory breadth—while necessary for competitive positioning—creates risk that free cash flow conversion may lag if top-line growth slows or orders reverse. This could in turn restrict the pace of future share repurchases, dividend increases, or strategic acquisitions. Effective working capital normalization and continued discipline will be key to preserving Avnet’s balance sheet strength as market cycles evolve.
