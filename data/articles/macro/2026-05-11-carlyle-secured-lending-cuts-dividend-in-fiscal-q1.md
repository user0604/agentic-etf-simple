Carlyle Secured Lending Cuts Dividend in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Carlyle Secured Lending cut its dividend as portfolio value and net asset value slipped again.
Adjusted net investment income per share was stable compared to the prior year.
Management is focused on scaling joint ventures and improving origination, but near-term earnings may remain muted.

Carlyle Secured Lending (NASDAQ:CGBD) trimmed its dividend after another dip in its portfolio value and net asset value. The leading business development company, which invests in secured loans to U.S. middle market companies, reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. Overall, the period showed stable underlying earnings but signaled ongoing challenges for near-term growth.

Notable Developments
The company’s adjusted net investment income per share was $0.36 in both the first quarter of 2026 and the fourth quarter of 2025, indicating stable earnings power quarter over quarter.
Net asset value per share slipped to $15.89, marking the second straight quarter of decline.
The quarterly dividend was reduced to $0.35 per share, aligning payouts with current earnings levels.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management described its portfolio as stable and emphasized steps to scale its joint ventures, stating that expanding these partnerships should lay the groundwork for improved future earnings. The most important item to monitor is the company's progress ramping up new investment activity and joint venture contributions, as signs of earnings recovery depend on these efforts bearing fruit.

Quarterly Highlights

The company held adjusted net investment income steady at $0.36 per share, even as it operated in a tougher market for middle market lending. This measure reflects net profits from its lending business after expenses and gives a picture of how well its loan portfolio is performing.

Portfolio value and net asset value both declined during the period. The total fair value of investments was $2.3 billion as of March 31, 2026, and net asset value per share fell for a second straight quarter. These movements suggest the portfolio faced continued market-driven markdowns or more repayments than new loan activity.

The board announced a 12.5% year-over-year reduction in the quarterly dividend, bringing it to $0.35 per share for shareholders of record in the next quarter. Management said this change aligns the payout with underlying profits and aims for sustainability while still leaving room for possible supplemental dividends if earnings increase in later periods.
