Canadian Natural Resources (NYSE:CNQ) delivered steady operating performance as robust Canadian production offset a sharp decline overseas. The leading Canadian oil and gas producer reported its first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, covering the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter showed stable output and profitability, but higher spending slowed net debt progress and squeezed free cash flow.

Notable Developments
Company-wide production averaged 1,643,160 barrels of oil equivalent per day, up 3.8% from a year ago, led by records in Canadian conventional oil and natural gas.
Free cash flow dropped to $875 million from $1.86 billion last year as higher capital spending and abandonment costs offset stable adjusted net earnings.
The company raised its quarterly dividend by 6.4% to $0.625 per share, marking 26 consecutive years of increases for shareholders.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management kept its 2026 production and capital spending guidance unchanged, noting that first-quarter performance was in line with internal plans. It continues to target a net debt reduction milestone that would trigger higher shareholder payouts. Investors should monitor cost pressures in key business segments and any movement on the company’s $13 billion net debt goal.

Quarterly Highlights

Production growth was strongest in North America, where conventional oil output and natural gas volumes both hit new records. Average company-wide production rose, but this was partly offset by a sharp drop in international operations due to planned maintenance and asset retirements.

Profitability metrics held steady as adjusted net earnings reached $2.4 billion, but free cash flow declined more than 50% from a year ago. This was largely due to increased investment in asset maintenance and expansion projects.

Rising operating costs in oil sands mining and thermal in situ oil (a process where heat is used underground to produce heavy oil) contributed to margin pressure. Management flagged industry-wide cost inflation as a factor and emphasized continued focus on efficiency and capital discipline.

What Canadian Natural Resources Does

Canadian Natural Resources is a major oil and gas producer with assets in Western Canada, the North Sea, and offshore Africa, specializing in heavy oil, synthetic crude oil, natural gas, and related infrastructure.
