1. Software Stocks Stage Comeback

U.S. software stocks headed for a fourth straight session of gains Tuesday as investors reassessed AI disruption risks. The iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF (NYSEMKT: IGV) rose 1.1% in early trading to its highest level since January, even as chipmakers cooled off after the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index hit a record earlier this month.

Winners across the board: Workday (NASDAQ: WDAY), ServiceNow (NYSE: NOW), and Salesforce (NYSE: CRM) climbed between 3.7% and 4.3%. CrowdStrike (NASDAQ: CRWD), Okta (NASDAQ: OKTA), SailPoint (NASDAQ: SAIL), and Zscaler (NASDAQ: ZS) gained 1.2% to 2.5%.
Analyst split: Bank of America (NYSE: BAC) called ServiceNow "too entrenched" in enterprise workflows to challenge. The software ETF is still down 12.2% year to date.
2. Target Taps Walmart Vet

Target (NYSE: TGT) named former Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT) executive Jeff England as chief supply chain officer, the latest leadership move in CEO Michael Fiddelke's turnaround push. England joins from building materials supplier QXO (NYSE: QXO) and succeeds company veteran Gretchen McCarthy at the end of the month.

Turnaround plan: Fiddelke took over in February and committed roughly $6 billion to improve inventory, in-store experience, and delivery times. Target opened its first upstream "receive center" in Houston on April 29.
Earnings on deck: Target reports Wednesday during a heavy retail week. Investors want commentary on consumer spending amid high inflation and supply chain volatility tied to the Iran war.
3. Home Depot Reaffirms Outlook

Dividend Investor stock Home Depot (NYSE: HD) reaffirmed its 2026 outlook as customers stuck to smaller DIY projects in a tough housing market. First-quarter revenue grew 5% to $41.8 billion and beat expectations, but same-store sales rose just 0.6%, short of the 0.9% consensus. Shares were up 1% by midday Tuesday.

Consumer pressure: CFO Richard McPhail said fuel costs above $4.50 a gallon are weighing on shoppers, who keep deferring big-ticket categories like lumber, flooring, and lighting. Smaller projects like painting remain a bright spot.
Pro push continues: The Pro business outpaced DIY, helped by the 2024 SRS Distribution deal and this year's Mingledorff's HVAC acquisition. Digital sales rose 10%. Rival Lowe's (NYSE: LOW) reports Wednesday.
4. Musk and Altman Pivot to IPOs

Elon Musk lost his lawsuit against OpenAI CEO Sam Altman on Monday, clearing the way for both billionaires to focus on potentially record-setting IPOs. Musk's SpaceX, valued at $1.25 trillion after merging with xAI, could file its prospectus this week, while OpenAI eyes a debut later this year at an $850 billion-plus valuation.

Trial fallout: The jury ruled Musk waited too long to sue, and Musk plans to appeal. His attorneys spent weeks attacking Altman's character, raising questions prospective OpenAI investors will weigh.
Competitive backdrop: Anthropic is pressuring OpenAI on enterprise and AI coding, and just signed a major compute deal at xAI's Colossus 1 data center. Only Meta (NASDAQ: META) and Alibaba (NYSE: BABA) have ever debuted on U.S. exchanges with valuations above $100 billion.
