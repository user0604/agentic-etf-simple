American Financial Group Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 3:07 PM

Comments

American Financial Group, Inc. (NYSE:AFG) is a leading provider of specialty property and casualty insurance solutions in the United States. The company’s growth strategy emphasizes disciplined underwriting, expansion across specialized commercial lines, and consistent capital returns to shareholders. By focusing on niche markets and maintaining pricing rigor, American Financial Group aims to deliver strong, sustainable returns while actively managing risk and adapting to evolving market conditions.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Sustained Underwriting Excellence Supports Superior Profitability

American Financial Group’s ongoing underwriting discipline positions it for outsized returns in specialty insurance. Core net operating earnings per share jumped 36% year over year to $2.47 in the first quarter of 2026 (non-GAAP), and the company produced an annualized core operating return on equity of 17%. The specialty property and casualty (P&C) combined ratio improved markedly to 90.3% from 94% a year earlier, illustrating better-than-peer underwriting results. All three specialty segments—Property & Transportation, Specialty Casualty, and Specialty Financial—reported year-over-year growth in underwriting profit. These results reflect not only lower catastrophe losses, but also consistent pricing discipline and favorable reserve development. The ability to maintain strong margins even as competitors face margin pressure suggests American Financial Group can generate attractive profits through industry cycles, which underpins long-term value creation.

2. Persistent Pricing Discipline and Market Leadership Yield Reliable Premium Growth

Continued premium growth driven by pricing power highlights American Financial Group’s competitive advantage. Gross and net written premiums grew 6% and 3% respectively year over year in the first quarter of 2026, led by new business and a robust 5% average renewal rate increase in the P&C group (excluding workers’ compensation). Notably, the company has achieved overall renewal rate increases for 39 consecutive quarters, demonstrating impressive pricing power throughout cyclical markets. Even in areas facing competitive intensity, such as excess and surplus lines, renewal rates outpaced loss trends, supporting the maintenance of attractive margins. The crop insurance division—benefiting from stable-to-higher commodity prices and favorable quota share changes—also provides further momentum for net written premium growth in 2026. This persistency in premium increases reinforces American Financial Group’s position as a disciplined industry leader poised to compound earnings over time.

3. Conservative Investment Positioning Protects Capital and Enables Flexible Opportunity Capture

American Financial Group’s prudent investment approach offers stability and optionality for future growth. The investment portfolio remains defensively positioned, balancing yield and risk. Net investment income (NII) in the property and casualty segment grew 8% year over year in the first quarter of 2026, supported by a larger asset base and new fixed maturities yielding 5.25%. Alternative investments were a modest drag in the quarter due to a $13 million mark-to-market loss on collateralized loan obligations, but direct private credit exposure stands at only 1.5% of the portfolio, and broader private credit plus BDC exposure is under 5%. Management reassures investors of significant subordination and high credit quality in these holdings, reducing the risk of loss even in challenging economic environments. The company’s ability to redeploy capital from the pending $125 million pretax gain on the planned sale of Charleston Harbor Resort and Marina—either via fixed income, higher-IRR insurance businesses, or shareholder returns—gives it flexibility to enhance future earnings and limit downside volatility.

4. Robust Capital Generation Fuels Consistent and Value-Accretive Shareholder Returns

Reliable capital returns underscore American Financial Group’s commitment to rewarding long-term shareholders. In the first quarter of 2026, the company returned nearly $260 million to shareholders through a $1.50 per share special dividend, $0.88 regular quarterly dividend, and approximately $60 million in share repurchases. Shares were bought at just above $127, a level management considers notably value-accretive. Book value per share plus dividends, excluding accumulated other comprehensive income (AOCI), grew 3.1% for the quarter, reflecting the company’s preferred measure of value creation. With ongoing excess capital generation and an opportunistic capital deployment philosophy, American Financial Group has ample headroom for further acquisitions, special dividends, or buybacks, aligning management incentives with shareholder interests and fostering long-term compounding.

5. Strategic Investment in Technology Enhances Cost Efficiency and Performance Resilience

Continued IT investment advances American Financial Group’s operational excellence and adaptability. The company is making targeted investments in infrastructure for customer experience, security, and data analytics. These strategic technology projects drive modest upward pressure on expense ratios but are designed to enhance long-term efficiency and client service. Improved claims analytics, risk selection, and customer-facing systems should reinforce the company’s competitive advantage in high-margin specialty insurance lines. As new competitors emerge—often backed by less disciplined private capital—technology can help American Financial Group maintain robust underwriting results, quickly adapt to shifting risks, and navigate changes in the regulatory or client landscape more nimbly than slower-moving peers. This digital focus supports the sustainability of margins and profitability in the coming years.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Heightened Competitive Pressures and Softening in Key Lines Threaten Margin Sustainability

American Financial Group faces risk as competitive intensity in specialty lines increases and pricing power wanes. While management reports that overall pricing remains robust and that most businesses are matching or exceeding loss cost trends, the broader property and casualty insurance industry is beginning to see signs of heightened competition and some market softening, particularly in longer-tail casualty and excess liability lines. The ongoing influx of private equity capital into managing general agencies (MGAs) and managing general underwriters (MGUs), as well as market disruptions among fronting carriers, could exacerbate volatility and price discipline erosion over the next 12 months. If American Financial Group is unable to hold the line on pricing or faces unexpected loss cost escalations, margin compression and earnings volatility could result, especially in segments where its market position is more contested.

2. Capital Deployment Uncertainty from Large Asset Sale May Temporarily Weigh on Returns

Returns could be impacted if proceeds from the Charleston Harbor Resort & Marina sale are not optimally redeployed. The company expects a material $125 million pretax core operating gain from divesting a non-core real estate asset, with completion anticipated in the second or third quarter of 2026. Until clear visibility is provided regarding how these proceeds will be allocated—be it reinvestment in fixed income, share repurchases, or deploying capital into higher-return specialty insurance businesses—there is a risk that near-term income replacement may lag or introduce incremental risk. If reinvested at lower yields or into businesses that struggle to earn target returns, or if buybacks occur at less accretive valuations, there could be downward pressure on book value growth and earnings per share, especially during the transition quarters after the transaction closes.

3. Exposure to Social Inflation and Reserve Risk May Disrupt Strong Underwriting Track Record

Ongoing threats from social inflation and unexpected claim cost trends could challenge American Financial Group’s reserve adequacy. Even as the company reports a 66% year-over-year surge in underwriting profit for the first quarter of 2026 and improvements in combined ratios across all specialty segments, pockets of the specialty P&C marketplace—including commercial auto and excess liability—continue to face adverse jury awards and shifting claims trends. While ongoing rate increases and refined claims management have led to underwriting improvements, a single material reserve charge or unfavorable loss development in volatile lines could quickly undermine the company’s earnings trajectory and investor confidence. This risk is accentuated as the company maintains only moderate exposure to private credit and alternative investments, making underwriting discipline central to its overall financial profile.
