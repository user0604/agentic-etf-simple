Intellinetics (NYSEMKT:INLX) started the new fiscal year on a challenging note as a steep drop in professional services revenue and higher costs led to wider losses. The enterprise document management software provider, known for its cloud-based SaaS (Software as a Service) solutions and strong public sector presence, released its first quarter 2026 results on May 14, 2026, covering the three-month period ended March 31. Overall, recurring revenue held steady but could not offset the decline in project-based sales and increased expenses, resulting in weaker profitability for the period.

Notable developments
Recurring Software as a Service (SaaS) revenue was stable year over year at $1.54 million, even as management continued to focus on growth in this area.
Professional services revenue dropped by 14.3%, which accounted for most of the total revenue decline during the quarter.
The quarter included $430,000 in one-time costs related to the CEO transition, which increased both net loss and operating expenses.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its goal for double-digit SaaS revenue growth in fiscal 2026, calling this recurring segment the "core" of its strategy. The recent CEO transition has led to a renewed focus on improving execution and profitability. Investors should watch whether SaaS revenue accelerates after this flat quarter, as management’s guidance now depends on this area.

Quarterly highlights

The largest revenue decline came from professional services, which contracted by 14.3%. This project-based segment is subject to fluctuations and was the main driver of the overall revenue decrease for the quarter.

SaaS, which refers to cloud-based software sales that provide recurring, subscription-driven revenue, remained nearly flat compared to the prior year. This contrasts with previous quarters, where double-digit growth rates were achieved, highlighting a gap between management’s ambitions and current results.

Profitability weakened as gross profit fell 12.2% and losses widened, due to both reduced service margins and $430,000 in non-recurring expenses tied to the CEO transition. Adjusted EBITDA turned negative, reflecting pressure on the company’s margin profile.
