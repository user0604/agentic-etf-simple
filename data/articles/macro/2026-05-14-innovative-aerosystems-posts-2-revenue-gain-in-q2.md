Innovative Aerosystems Posts 2% Revenue Gain in Q2

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 9:16 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Revenue from core aviation markets offset military segment weakness.
Adjusted EPS dropped to $0.26, with revenue up 2% year over year.
Management reaffirmed its $250 million annual sales target and expects normalization in defense sales next quarter.

Innovative Aerosystems (NASDAQ:ISSC) managed to keep top-line growth moving forward by capturing strong demand in commercial and business aviation, even as military segment revenue dipped. The aviation technology provider reports results for its second quarter of fiscal 2026 (released May 14, 2026), when recent acquisitions and aftermarket sales boosted performance amid a transitional quarter for its key F-16 defense program. Overall, the period saw steady backlog growth and better cash flow, but margin pressure from increased R&D and acquisition-related costs weighed on profits.

Notable Developments
Revenue reached $22.4 million, as commercial and business aviation sales climbed and offset a $7 million year-over-year drop in F-16 defense program revenue.
Gross margin remained strong at 51.1%, slightly below last year, but operating expenses jumped 51%, mainly because of acquisitions and more than double the spend on research and development.
New acquisitions in the cockpit avionics and flight controls space are expected to contribute $10 million in annual revenue, and order backlog ended the quarter at $87 million, up over $7 million from a year ago.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained its long-term annual sales goal of $250 million and expects gross margin to remain in the mid-40% range. It forecasts a normalization of F-16 military program sales by the next quarter, now that approvals and facility transitions are complete. The most important factor for investors is whether acquisitions and integration spending yield the anticipated increases in non-defense revenue and profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

During the period, non-F-16 revenue surged 69% to $18.9 million, making up for the defense dip and reflecting stronger demand in retrofits and business jet aftermarket solutions. The drop in F-16 segment revenue was explained as a temporary post-transition lull—management says F-16 output should recover in the second half of the year.
