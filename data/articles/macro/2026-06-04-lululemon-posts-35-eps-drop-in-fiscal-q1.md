Key Takeaways
Lululemon delivered modest sales growth but saw a steep decline in profit. EPS dropped 35% year over year. Management cut full-year guidance, expecting flat to lower sales for 2026.

Lululemon (NASDAQ:LULU)’s latest quarter brought modest sales growth but a sharp drop in profit as it grappled with ongoing weakness in its largest market. The premium athletic apparel company reported results for its first quarter of fiscal 2026 (released June 4, 2026), with continued international gains unable to offset significant margin compression and sluggish North American demand. Overall, the period exposed pressure on key profitability metrics and prompted a sizable cut to the company’s financial outlook for the remainder of the year.

Notable developments
Revenue increased by 4%, mainly from strong international growth, while comparable sales rose 1% overall despite a 5% decline in the Americas.
Profitability declined sharply, with gross margin dropping by 4.1 percentage points and operating margin falling by 7.3 percentage points, reflecting continued markdowns and higher expenses.
Inventories rose 2% year over year by value but declined 4% by units, suggesting some improvement in inventory management but also ongoing discounting pressure.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management now expects full-year revenue of $11.0–$11.15 billion, down from its prior forecast, and earnings of $10.95–$11.15 per share, a cut from previous guidance of $12.10–$12.30 per share. The company also projects flat to lower sales in the next quarter and stressed the need for further action to address business headwinds. Investors should watch for signs of stabilizing demand and margin improvement in North America for any sign of a turnaround.

Quarterly highlights

Sales growth came mainly from international markets. Revenue outside the Americas jumped 22%, with China Mainland up 30% and Rest of World up 13%. In contrast, Americas revenue dropped 3%, and comparable sales in that region declined 5%.

Profit margins fell sharply. The gross margin was 54.2%, down from 58.3% a year ago, while operating margin declined to 11.2% amid high promotions and rising selling expenses.

Inventory values rose modestly, but units actually declined, showing that recent steps to control stock are starting to have an effect. However, heavy markdowns remain a concern, especially for North American stores, which continued to underperform.

What Lululemon Athletica Inc. does

Lululemon Athletica Inc. designs and sells premium athletic apparel and accessories for yoga, training, running, and other active lifestyles through company-operated stores and digital channels worldwide.
