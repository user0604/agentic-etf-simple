Array Digital: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 11:39 AM

Comments

Array Digital Infrastructure, Inc. (NYSE:AD) is a communications infrastructure company specializing in wireless tower operations, recurring tower rental income, and the monetization of legacy wireless spectrum assets. Having fully transitioned to a tower-focused business, the company's growth strategy includes increasing tower tenancy, optimizing operating margins, and leveraging non-core asset sales to fund shareholder returns. The company is currently evaluating a strategic buyout proposal from its largest shareholder, Telephone and Data Systems (TDS), which adds another layer of potential transformation to its story.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Record Tower Revenue Growth Demonstrates the Strength of Core Operations

Exceptional year-over-year gains in cash site rental revenue signal robust underlying business fundamentals. In the first quarter of 2026, Array Digital Infrastructure, Inc. reported 64% growth in normalized tower cash site rental revenue compared to the prior year, with a sequential increase in tenancy ratio from 0.95 in Q4 2025 to 0.96 in Q1 2026. This remarkable expansion is underpinned by strong collocation demand and minimal customer churn, with just one full-tower tenant loss in the quarter—well below industry averages. Sustained revenue momentum from top-tier tenants like T-Mobile, combined with the stability of long-term lease agreements, offers high visibility into future cash flows and supports thesis of durable operating strength.

2. Successful Spectrum Monetization Fuels Special Dividends and Creates Shareholder Value

Major progress in monetizing valuable wireless spectrum unlocks immediate cash returns for shareholders. By early 2026, the company had reached agreements to monetize roughly 70% of its wireless spectrum holdings, including closed deals with AT&T and T-Mobile and a pending transaction with Verizon. These transactions triggered a $10.25 per share special dividend in February 2026, with a further $10.40 per share payout proposed upon closing the next set of deals. Residual spectrum assets, such as C-band, are positioned as high-value 5G infrastructure with no forced sale timeline, creating option value for future monetization. This ability to convert dormant assets into tangible shareholder returns provides a compelling case for near-term and potential future capital distribution upside.

3. Consolidation with TDS Promises Strategic Synergies and Simplified Corporate Structure

The proposed TDS acquisition may streamline governance, deliver cost savings, and integrate complementary fiber and tower assets. Array Digital Infrastructure, Inc.'s majority owner, TDS, has proposed acquiring all remaining shares in a tax-free, all-stock merger, subject to the completion of further special dividends. Full consolidation could eliminate duplicative corporate overhead, unlock scale economies in procurement, and enhance the strategic interplay between TDS’s fiber expansion and Array’s tower footprint. Management anticipates $100 million in annual cost savings by 2028, with initial margin benefits already visible in 2026. By aligning the balance sheet and capital allocation under one umbrella, the combined entity can more efficiently deploy resources for growth and return of capital, supporting the long-term investment case.

4. Recurring Cash Flows from Legacy Partnerships Offer Stability Amid Industry Cycles

Ongoing equity income and cash distributions from legacy partnership stakes provide a stable revenue stream independent of tower segment volatility. Management has highlighted its reluctance to divest these partnership assets, given the consistency and attractiveness of recurring distributions. For instance, Q1 2026 saw elevated equity income due to prior-period adjustments, and distributions, although uneven between quarters, remain a predictable contributor to free cash flow. This revenue diversification helps buffer against potential tenant churn or sector-specific shocks, strengthening the company’s resilience and appeal to income-focused investors.

5. Execution in Fiber Buildout and Customer Penetration Bodes Well for Long-Term Growth

Strong fiber expansion and increased residential customer additions reinforce the growth runway beyond the tower business. The company delivered 40,000 new marketable fiber addresses in Q1 2026—nearly three times the previous year’s pace—with net residential fiber adds up 32% year over year and fiber revenue growing 13%. Enhanced sales channels, such as door-to-door and digital platforms, are driving deeper market penetration, with early presale cohorts achieving adoption rates in the low 20% range. These achievements position the business to capture rising demand for high-speed connectivity and 5G densification, supplementing its strong tower cash flows with recurring growth from broadband services.

Potential Bear Cases
1. High Tenant Concentration and Customer Churn Risks Threaten Revenue Predictability

Reliance on a small number of major wireless tenants makes the company vulnerable to leasing cycle volatility and strategic shifts by key customers. As of Q1 2026, revenue remains heavily dependent on the operational pace and lease renewals of T-Mobile, which has until January 2028 to determine its long-term site commitments. Management expects that only 800 to 1,800 of the original 2,015 T-Mobile-committed sites will remain following interim terminations. Meanwhile, the abrupt cessation of payments from DISH Wireless—leading to full revenue de-recognition since December 2025—underscores the financial impact a single customer’s distress can have. These concentration risks introduce uncertainty into future cash flows, particularly if industry consolidation or new technologies (such as satellite broadband) alter tenant strategies.

2. Legal and Regulatory Headwinds May Disrupt Strategic Initiatives and Destroy Shareholder Value

Ongoing shareholder investigations and potential class actions surrounding governance practices and transaction fairness could delay or derail value-creating plans. Multiple law firms have launched investigations into the fiduciary conduct of Array’s board after a 21%+ decline in share price since August 2025, tied to the TDS acquisition proposal and operational transitions. Legal proceedings, even if ultimately unsuccessful, can increase transaction costs, create management distractions, and delay execution of mergers, asset sales, or special dividends. Should litigation escalate into material settlements or regulatory action, the business could also face reputational harm or forced changes to transaction terms, diminishing the benefit of recent operational gains and asset monetization.

3. Uncertainty Around TDS Takeover Terms and Post-Merger Value Creation Complicates the Investment Thesis

The all-stock buyout proposal introduces deal risk, valuation questions, and potential dilution for current shareholders. The proposed TDS merger hinges on conditions such as the closure of significant spectrum sales and the payout of an additional $10.40 per share in special dividends. While marketed as an at-market premium, final valuation depends on broad shareholder approval, the trading value of TDS shares, and the strategic priorities set for the combined entity. Additionally, there is no clarity on whether future dividends or share buybacks will continue under TDS leadership. These uncertainties, combined with the possibility of shifting financial policies or strategic focus post-integration, inject ambiguity into potential upside realization for current Array Digital Infrastructure, Inc. shareholders.
