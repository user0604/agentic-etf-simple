Credicorp (NYSE:BAP) recorded a clear business win, with broad improvements in profitability, loan growth, and asset quality. The leading Peruvian financial group continued to strengthen its position across core markets, fueled by growth in digital channels and solid demand for banking and microfinance products. The results, released on May 18, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, showed broad-based momentum but noted persistent cost and insurance challenges. Overall, the quarter delivered strong progress in both core and new digital business lines.

Notable developments
Profitability hit a new high, with return on equity reaching 21.1%, well ahead of management’s full-year guidance of 19.5%.
Credit and deposit growth accelerated, with the loan portfolio up 8.2% and total deposits rising 13.3% from the prior year.
Operating expenses climbed 13.1% due mainly to technology investments, while insurance underwriting declined 9.1% as higher claims pressured results.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintained its full-year return on equity outlook of about 19.5%, expecting further loan expansion and a stable net interest margin. The group highlighted continued investment in digital banking, especially the Yape platform, while flagging the need for expense discipline. Investors should closely track operating costs and results from the insurance segment as these may affect earnings momentum in coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The quarter saw net income rise by 16.1% from the prior year, supported by both banking and microfinance results. Digital revenues made further inroads, as Yape’s monthly active users increased 14% and revenue per user grew by 65%, now representing 8% of group-wide risk-adjusted income.

Asset quality improved, with the non-performing loan ratio falling to 4.3%. This drop indicates that a smaller share of the loan book required special attention for late payments or defaults than a year ago, a key positive for any bank.

On the cost side, the efficiency ratio held steady at 45.8%, but total operating expenses rose sharply, mainly in support of digital growth and cloud operations for new offerings. Insurance underwriting results remained under pressure, down 9.1%, primarily from increased claims and softer car and commercial insurance premiums.

What Credicorp does

Credicorp is a diversified financial services holding company based in Peru, offering banking, microfinance, insurance, and investment management products throughout the Andean region.
