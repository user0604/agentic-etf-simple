Notable developments
Revenue increased 4.8% compared to the prior year, helped by growth in Rockies and Permian segments.
Profitability declined, with adjusted EBITDA down 5.8% and distributable cash flow lower by nearly 20% year over year.
The company repaid all accrued Series A preferred dividends and completed a $42 million equity raise, setting up for future capital returns.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance of $225 million to $265 million, citing new customer wins, accelerating well activity in key basins, and recent long-term pipeline contracts. The most important item for investors to monitor next is progress in ramping up natural gas volumes in legacy regions as protection from minimum volume contracts declines through the year.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue posted a 4.8% increase versus the prior year, driven by higher crude oil pricing and new wells connected in the Rockies and expanding Double E pipeline contracts. However, aggregate daily gas throughput declined 1.5%, as customer shut-ins and lower production in the Mid-Con and Piceance regions offset gains elsewhere.

Adjusted EBITDA margin decreased to 38.9% from over 43% a year ago, reflecting reduced earnings power from gas-linked contracts and greater commodity price exposure as minimum volume commitments rolled off. Strategic asset management remained in focus: the company executed a new 10-year crude gathering deal in the Williston and a firm 100 MMcf/d take-or-pay contract on the Double E pipeline, which moves gas from the Permian basin.

These commercial wins contributed to 37 new wells connected in the quarter, with 40 more targeted for the next period -- 20 of those in the Mid-Con region, where volumes remain under pressure. The company paid off all accrued preferred share dividends, placing it closer to reinstating a dividend for common shareholders. A $42 million equity raise and major refinancing of its Permian transmission joint venture also increased balance sheet flexibility, though total leverage remains above the company’s 3.5x target at roughly 4.2x.

What Summit Midstream does

Summit Midstream owns and operates pipelines and related midstream assets, gathering and transporting natural gas and crude oil for energy producers in several major U.S. basins.
