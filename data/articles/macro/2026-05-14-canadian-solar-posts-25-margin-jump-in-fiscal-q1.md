Notable Developments
Solar module shipments landed at 2.5 gigawatts, above the company’s internal range but down 64% from the prior year.
Gross margin rose to 25.1%, inflated by a one-time $93 million U.S. tariff refund, masking tougher underlying profit dynamics.
Energy storage shipments showed strength, up 142% year over year to 2.1 gigawatt-hours, while operating cash outflow improved from last year’s level.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects revenue in the next quarter between $1.0 billion and $1.2 billion, with a lower gross margin of 13–15% as the benefit from the tariff refund drops away. U.S. solar module and battery storage shipments are guided higher, but profit improvement depends on sustainable operational efficiency. Investors should watch whether margin quality and shipment volumes can recover without temporary one-offs.

Quarterly Highlights

Solar module shipments exceeded the top end of expectations but were down sharply from a year ago, reflecting weak global demand and the company's effort to prioritize profitability over raw volume. Energy storage solutions stood out, with shipments up more than two-fold year over year, a signal of strength in this business line and its importance as a growth driver.

The company’s headline gross margin surge was due almost entirely to a refunded U.S. tariff, not operational improvements. Excluding this non-recurring gain, profitability would have faced continued headwinds from high materials costs and intense competition, especially in solar modules.

Operational cash flow remained negative, but improved compared to last year’s first quarter. This was helped by careful inventory and payment management, even as project development revenues varied based on asset sales timing and reduced one-time benefits from earlier quarters.
