THE QUESTION

What company do you think is the best-run business in an otherwise mediocre industry?

1. The Unlikely Parabolic Commodity Stock

By Neha Chamaria
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Nucor (NYSE: NUE) is arguably the textbook example of a world-class business in an otherwise mediocre industry. Steel is an incredibly boring industry. It is also brutally cyclical and capital-intensive, and it has been plagued by decades of cheap foreign dumping.

Yet Nucor’s unique business model hasn’t just survived but has thrived even during downcycles. Nucor pioneered mini-mills, which use flexible, cost-effective electric arc furnaces instead of traditional blast furnaces to melt scrap into steel. The result? Nucor generates so much cash that it reduced its outstanding share count by nearly 30% and raised its absolute dividend per share by almost 50% over the past decade, with the stock delivering an incredible 540% total return. You rarely get to see cyclical stocks pull off moves like that.

2. A Little Extra Energy

By Matt Frankel, CFP®
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

The utilities sector is often dismissed by growth-oriented investors, and for good reasons. Most utility companies produce moderate, safe returns but aren’t particularly excited. NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE) is an exception.

The company operates one of the largest regulated utilities in the U.S., but the more exciting part is NextEra Energy Resources, which is the world’s largest renewable energy developer. It has a massive backlog of wind, solar, and storage projects and is a major beneficiary of the soaring electricity demand from AI data centers. Most utilities grow EPS at 4% to 6% in a typical year, while NextEra is targeting long-term earnings growth of more than 8%. With a three-decade history of increasing its dividend every year, NextEra could be an excellent combination of growth and income for a utility stock.

3. Membership Has Its Benefits for Investors

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I can’t say I get all that worked up for big-box retail. Retail in general is really tough, particularly in the grocery space. Margins are razor-thin, and competition is fierce. But Costco (NASDAQ: COST) figured out early on a way to capitalize on the opportunity by building what has become a membership model that has stood the test of time.

Just last quarter, the company reported 82.9 million total paid members, up 4.1% from a year ago, with the U.S. and Canada renewal rate an astounding 92.2%. Paying for a membership is a surefire way to convince one’s self to use it, and the value proposition Costco continues to provide is exemplary. Long-time shareholders have reaped the rewards for sticking with it.

4. From Near Bankruptcy to Best in Class

By Yasser El-Shimy
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Three years ago, Carvana’s (NYSE: CVNA) stock sat below $4, and bankruptcy was a real possibility. Today, it’s an S&P 500 constituent generating $20.3 billion in revenue at an 11% adjusted EBITDA margin — roughly 2.5 times the industry average. CEO Ernie Garcia calls it “the most profitable public automotive retailer in U.S. history,” and the numbers back him up: Carvana’s gross profit per unit of $7,182 more than triples CarMax’s (NYSE: KMX) $2,235.

The secret is vertical integration — owning reconditioning, logistics, and financing creates a compounding flywheel that rivals cannot easily replicate. While CarMax reported falling volume and a CEO shakeup in late 2025, Carvana increased its units 43% for the year. In the thin-margin and cyclical used car industry, that’s exceptional execution.

Yasser El-Shimy, PhD, MBA has positions in Carvana. The Motley Fool has positions in CarMax, Costco Wholesale, and NextEra Energy. The Motley Fool has a disclosure policy.
