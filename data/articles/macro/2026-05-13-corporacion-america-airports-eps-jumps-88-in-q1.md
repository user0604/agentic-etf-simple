Key Takeaways
Business win as strong growth across all core markets lifted margins and profitability.
EPS rose 86.6% year over year in the period.
Management highlighted ongoing investment and diversification but flagged persistent inflation and regulatory risk in Argentina and Brazil.

Airports operator Corporación América Airports (NYSE:CAAP) started 2026 on a strong note, notching higher profits as revenue grew in every major region and both aeronautical and commercial business lines. The company, which runs 53 airports in Latin America, Europe, and Eurasia, reported its first quarter results on May 13, 2026. The period was marked by broad-based revenue gains, expanding margins, and robust cash generation, though ongoing inflation and regulatory uncertainty, particularly in Argentina and Brazil, continue to present challenges.

Overall, the quarter showed continued operational momentum, with improved profitability matched by a solid balance sheet and new long-term concession extensions in Armenia and Ecuador.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 20.0%, lifted by both aeronautical ($277.8 million, up 17.4%) and commercial ($216.2 million, up 21.0%) streams, supported by a 7.0% increase in passenger traffic to 21.8 million.
Adjusted EBITDA rose 26.1% to $204.1 million, with margin expansion as operating expenses grew less quickly than revenue.
Significant concession extensions in Armenia (to 2067, with a $425 million capital commitment) and Ecuador (to 2032) strengthen the company’s long-term growth platform.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Although management did not provide formal guidance for the remainder of fiscal 2026, it emphasized continued focus on global diversification, infrastructure investment, and cash flow preservation. Investors should closely watch cost trends and regulatory developments in Argentina and Brazil, since rising inflation and shifting policy frameworks remain key risks to margins and cash flows.

Quarterly Highlights

Passenger growth and diversified revenue streams fueled gains, as total revenue hit $537.6 million and all key segments showed improvement. Commercial revenue saw faster growth (+21%) than aeronautical revenue (+17.4%), while construction service revenue jumped 37.2%, reflecting ongoing expansion projects.

Operating efficiency improved; costs of services rose 16.4%, less than the revenue increase, resulting in gross margin expansion from 34.9% to 36.9%. The combination of scale and cost discipline lifted operating income and contributed to robust net profit growth.

Regionally, Argentina remained the largest contributor, with revenue up 15.4%, while Brazil and Armenia posted increases of 31.7% and 40.0%, respectively. The company ended the quarter with $666.2 million in cash, and net debt to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization fell to 0.5 times, indicating financial flexibility.
