Grupo Aval Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 1, 2026, 7:08 AM

Comments

Grupo Aval Acciones y Valores S.A. (NYSE:AVAL) is Colombia’s largest financial conglomerate, operating leading franchises in commercial banking, pensions, asset management, and merchant banking (notably infrastructure). The company’s strategy blends market leadership in core Colombian financial services with geographic rebalancing, product innovation, and operational efficiency. With a multi-year roadmap stretching to 2031, Grupo Aval aims to deepen client relevance, drive digital transformation, and diversify income, offsetting cyclical volatility in banking with non-banking segments like infrastructure and asset management.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership Fuels Sustainable Revenue and Deposit Growth

Dominant market positions enable Grupo Aval to drive above-market loan and deposit growth, underpinned by trust and scale advantages. In the first quarter of 2026, the group grew gross loans 6% year over year to Ps 193.7 trillion and deposits 11.7% year over year to Ps 216.8 trillion. Savings deposits surged 17.8% in the same period, and Grupo Aval expanded its share in personal loans by 147 basis points. These figures highlight customer loyalty and the group’s ability to defend and grow market share amid competitive industry dynamics. Such strong organic growth in deposits both strengthens funding flexibility and allows for a more favorable funding mix, setting the stage for sustainable long-term growth even in volatile macro environments.

2. Strong Asset Quality Mitigates Credit Risk Amid Economic Cycles

Improving asset quality and prudent risk management strengthen resilience against macro headwinds. As of March 2026, loans past due over 90 days improved to 3.13%, down from 3.8% a year earlier, with a robust 137% coverage ratio. The group’s net cost of risk fell by 36 basis points year over year to 1.8%. These trends indicate progress in de-risking the loan book and benefiting from disciplined lending and underwriting standards. By maintaining a high proportion (90.3%) of loans in Stage 1 and investing in portfolio quality, Grupo Aval can better absorb external shocks, reducing the likelihood of material losses during periods of macroeconomic or political uncertainty.

3. Diversification into Non-Banking Segments Offsets Core Banking Cyclicality

Robust performance in infrastructure, asset management, and pensions provides valuable earnings diversification and counter-cyclical buffers. In the first quarter of 2026, merchant banking (mainly infrastructure) reported a gross profit of Ps 709.3 billion—up 40.1% year-over-year—while asset management subsidiaries such as Aval Fiduciaria delivered a 33% return on equity and captured over half of the market’s collective investment fund growth. This diversification lessens dependency on traditional banking net interest margins (NIM), which can be pressured by rate cycles or regulatory changes, and smooths overall group profitability. In periods when bank margins tighten, these segments can provide earnings stability and long-term value creation for investors.

4. Operational Efficiency Initiatives Drive Margin Expansion Potential

Cost discipline and digitization initiatives unlock structural efficiencies and protect margins. Despite a headline increase in operating expenses due to a one-off equity tax in Q1 2026, underlying administrative and other expenses rose 11.2% year over year and actually declined 7.3% quarter over quarter when the tax is excluded. The company’s efficiency ratio improved to 53.9%, or an even stronger 47.3% excluding the tax. Grupo Aval is deploying shared services, cloud migration, and procurement renegotiations, targeting COP 62 billion in 2026 operational savings—a 9% annual reduction in controllable spending. These efforts put the group in a position to expand margins further as technology-driven scale benefits accrue across its banks and fee businesses.

5. Strategic Acquisitions and Portfolio Rebalancing Strengthen Competitive Moat

The acquisition of Itaú’s Colombian retail banking business and the divestiture of Central American assets reposition Grupo Aval for local scale and focus. Completion of the Multi Financial Group (MFG) sale has sharpened geographic focus on Colombia and reduced foreign exchange and NIM sensitivity. The Itaú acquisition is set to add approximately COP 3.3 trillion in consumer loans, COP 3.2 trillion in mortgages, and around 277,000 new clients, bolstering the group’s domestic retail banking presence. By consolidating fiduciary units into Aval Fiduciaria and partnering with global asset managers, the company enhances its product offering and competitiveness. These strategic moves position Grupo Aval to capture profitable growth opportunities in its core market, increasing long-term shareholder value through focused scale and capability investments.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Regulatory and Tax Headwinds Threaten Sustained Profitability

Unexpected regulatory actions such as the one-off equity tax can materially suppress reported profitability and distort return metrics. In Q1 2026, the Colombian government imposed a single-year equity tax that reduced Grupo Aval’s net income by Ps 210.1 billion and increased operating expenses by Ps 311.7 billion, slashing return on average equity by about 467 basis points to 7.4%. Although management points to this as a one-off event, the risk of further regulatory measures—such as additional taxes, pension reform, or higher capital requirements—remains ever-present. Such moves, particularly if frequent or unpredictable, could undermine normalized profit growth and return expectations for shareholders, while reducing transparency around true sustainable earnings power.

2. Net Interest Margin Remains Vulnerable to Persistently High Funding Costs

Falling net interest margins highlight exposure to funding cost pressures and competitive loan pricing. In Q1 2026, Grupo Aval’s consolidated net interest margin (NIM) dropped 50 basis points year over year to 3.3%, while NIM on loans compressed to 4.4%. This was driven by a timing mismatch where deposit and liability costs repriced faster than loan yields, making it harder to fully pass through higher costs to borrowers. Management projects some stabilization, but further policy tightening and deposit competition in Colombia could constrain NIM recovery, especially as the deposit-to-loan ratio rises and retail funding becomes more expensive. Sustained NIM pressure would limit the group’s ability to grow profitability from its core banking business, even if loan volumes remain healthy.

3. Macroeconomic and Political Volatility in Colombia Could Disrupt Growth Plans

Grupo Aval’s fortunes are strongly tied to Colombia’s macro and political environment, which faces near-term headwinds and structural uncertainties. Rising inflation, policy rates of 11.25%, and uncertain political signals—especially around upcoming elections and possible pension reform—introduce risks to both lending growth and asset quality. Pension reform, for example, is projected to dampen long-term growth for the group’s pension manager (Porvenir), while economic slowdown could slow loan demand, pressure consumer credit costs, or trigger higher delinquencies, particularly in more vulnerable retail loan segments. These factors could delay or dilute the benefits of the group’s growth and efficiency strategies, potentially resulting in choppier returns than management’s medium-term targets suggest.
