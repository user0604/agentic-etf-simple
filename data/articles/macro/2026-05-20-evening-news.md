1. Jensen Huang's Money Printer Delivers

Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) just dropped a first quarter for the ages: $81.6 billion in revenue, up 85% from a year ago, with Data Center hauling in $75.2 billion all by itself. Oh, and Q2 guidance? $91 billion. NVDA is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Rule Breakers. Since its most recent rec in Hidden Gems, in May 2024, shares are up 134% -- 92 percentage points ahead of the S&P 500.

Ditching the penny: The quarterly dividend just jumped 25x -- from $0.01 to $0.25 -- plus an $80 billion buyback was announced. Epic member SharkyHeart summed it up: "fire sale price benefiting from naysayer momentum."
Jensen called it history: The CEO declared artificial intelligence (AI) factory build-out "the largest infrastructure expansion in human history" -- and with numbers like these, it's hard to argue.

2. Airbnb Adds Hotels, Cars, Groceries

Airbnb (NASDAQ: ABNB), recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers, is adding boutique hotels, car rentals, grocery delivery via Instacart (NASDAQ: CART), airport pickups, and luggage storage to its app. CEO Brian Chesky called the ambition building "an Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) for services" -- and eventually an AI-powered everything app for travel and living.

Revenue story backs the pivot: Q1 revenue hit $2.68 billion, up 18% year over year, with Q2 guidance set above analyst estimates. On a recent Scoreboard video, Motley Fool contributor Matt Frankel said that there's "not a lot to not like here" in terms of financials. He highlighted the company's "fantastic" margins and balance sheet.
Regulatory pressure adds urgency: Core home-rental markets face real headwinds -- Spain fined Airbnb 65 million euros (nearly $76 million), Barcelona is ending rental licenses in 2028, and New York has effectively banned short-term rentals since 2023.
3. Intuit Beats Big, Then Drops the Hammer

Intuit (NASDAQ: INTU), recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB), just dropped a quarter worth celebrating. Revenue was up 10% to $8.6 billion, full-year guidance was raised to $21.3 billion to $21.4 billion, a fresh $8 billion buyback was announced, and a 15% dividend hike was approved. Shares are down 14% in after-hours trading.

About that fine print: Intuit is cutting 17% of its workforce, a $300 million to $340 million restructuring charge landing mostly in Q4. The stated goal: move faster, get leaner.
AI glow-up: TurboTax Live revenue is tracking toward 36% full-year growth, with assisted-filing customers up 38%. Intuit's human-plus-AI model is becoming its biggest growth engine.
