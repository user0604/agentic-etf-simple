Detailed Rankings: Team Hidden Gems
1. BillionToOne

BillionToOne (NASDAQ: BLLN) delivered a Q1 2026 that turned heads: revenue of $108.4 million grew 84% year over year, beating the Wall Street consensus by more than 13%, while the company swung from a $4 million net loss to $18 million in net income. Gross margin expanded 9 percentage points to 73%. Oncology revenue more than quadrupled to $10.7 million, showing the Northstar liquid biopsy platform is gaining real commercial ground.

Management raised full-year 2026 guidance to $450 million to $465 million -- the second upward revision this year. The May 2026 launch of Unity Confirm, a non-invasive fetal cell confirmation test replacing riskier invasive procedures such as amniocentesis, opens a fresh growth lane. BillionToOne may be one of the most compelling stories in diagnostics today. -- The Premium Team

2. Tesla

Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) shareholders are roughly where they were three months ago as far as the stock price is concerned, although they've had to endure a pretty steep downturn along the way. The EV pioneer's production of 408,400 vehicles for 2026's first quarter was somewhat encouraging, as it was up from about 362,600 a year ago. Deliveries saw a more modest rise of roughly 21,300 vehicles. The company also returned to growth in financial terms, posting a 16% rise in revenue and adjusted net income gains of 56%. As the SpaceX IPO approaches, Tesla investors wonder whether Elon Musk's endgame will be to merge the two companies. That could produce a windfall for Tesla shareholders, but either way, we're comfortable with where we see the EV maker headed.
-- Dan Caplinger

3. Karooooo

Karooooo (NASDAQ: KARO) delivered an outstanding fiscal year 2026, with its Cartrack platform accelerating subscription revenue growth to 19% -- up from 15% the prior year -- despite headwinds from a strengthening South African rand.

Annual recurring revenue (ARR) climbed 18% in local currency and 38% in U.S. dollars to $325 million. Adjusted free cash flow surged 90% to ZAR 809 million, powering a 20% dividend hike. Total subscribers reached approximately 2.7 million, a 16% increase, with record net additions of 94,000 in Q4. The Karooooo Logistics segment -- a last-mile delivery platform -- grew revenue 29%, with 50% growth on a U.S. dollar basis. Management guided for 21% earnings per share (EPS) growth at the midpoint in fiscal 2027.

With shares still trading well below their 52-week high, we think patient Fools are being handed an excellent entry point into one of the most profitable small-cap software-as-a-service (SaaS) businesses on the market. -- The Premium Team

4. Sterling Infrastructure

Sterling Infrastructure (NASDAQ: STRL) delivered a quarter for the history books in Q1 2026. Revenue surged 92% year over year to $825.7 million, adjusted diluted earnings per share (EPS) soared 120%, and adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) margins hit a Q1 record of 20%. Management also secured the first phase of a semiconductor fabrication campus contract exceeding $500 million and raised full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $3.7 billion to $3.8 billion.

Combined backlog surged 131% to $5.2 billion -- multi-year visibility into artificial intelligence (AI) data center demand that few construction companies can match. Shares have rallied roughly 74% since our April recommendation, and we remain firmly bullish -- this story has years left to run.
-- The Premium Team

5. Symbotic

Symbotic (NASDAQ: SYM) delivered a strong fiscal 2026 Q2, and the market has gotten it wrong. Revenue hit $676 million -- up 23% year over year and above the high end of guidance -- while adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) of $78 million more than doubled the prior-year figure. The company achieved GAAP profitability for the second consecutive quarter, a milestone that shouldn't be taken lightly for a business still scaling fast. Software revenue surged 93% year over year, and the backlog climbed to $22.7 billion.

Yet the stock is down roughly 28% year to date, giving patient Fools a compelling entry point. We think the backlog, the software ramp, and deepening customer diversification -- including first deployments with Associated Wholesale Grocers -- make the long-term case stronger than ever.
-- The Premium Team

Detailed Rankings: Team Rule Breakers
1. Amazon

The day will come when Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) has no new worlds left to conquer, but don't expect that day to come anytime soon. The company is already the world's largest e-commerce provider, cloud infrastructure provider, and a leading contender in artificial intelligence and digital advertising. If that weren't enough, Amazon announced plans to offer its supply chain and delivery services to third-party customers, opening an entirely new profit center for the company.

Speaking of profits, in the first quarter, Amazon grew net sales 17% year over year to $182 billion, while its earnings per share rose 75% to $2.78. Not bad for one of the world's largest companies. Revenue from Amazon Web Services (AWS) continued to accelerate, up 28%. The announcement last quarter that it plans to spend $200 billion in capex this year wasn't well received by investors, but we believe the view is short-sighted. Andy Jassy said the company is "monetizing capacity as fast as we can install it." We think this allows forward-looking investors to get shares at a reasonable price. -- Danny Vena

2. Viking Holdings

Viking exhibits many of the traits we like to see in a Rule Breaker business. It is a clear first mover and top dog in its space, a leader in its niche of the cruise industry. Its brand strength, customer loyalty, and differentiation create sustainable competitive advantages we think competitors will find hard to challenge.

3. Alphabet

So much for the fears of Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) being disrupted by artificial intelligence. First-quarter revenue was up 22%, with ad revenue up 15% and services and Cloud revenue growing at a faster clip. Operating income was up 30%, the result of strong revenue growth, and operating margin improved to 36%. In short, Alphabet is winning across all of its ecosystems, including Google Search, YouTube, and Cloud, and the company's AI efforts are becoming a common thread across them all. Not only is the company a major provider of the infrastructure on top of which others are building and utilizing AI, but it has emerged as a serious player in AI innovation in its own right. -- Jason Hall

4. MercadoLibre

MercadoLibre (NASDAQ: MELI) reported another great quarter by most accounts. Total payment volume (revenue across its payments platforms) was up 55% and gross merchandise volume (the dollar value of e-commerce transactions) was up 42%, resulting in 49% revenue growth. It continues accelerating its finance business, with its loan portfolio growing nearly 90%. Yet at the same time, all that growth is impacting profitability. Net interest margin after losses (a measure of loan profitability and quality) was the lowest since at least 2023, and operating income and margin are contracting even as the business grows larger.

The empty half of the glass here says competition is increasing and MercadoLibre is focusing more on financing to drive the bottom line, and that's creating a lot of risk. And while competition is increasing, and finance is an increasingly large part of the business, a more glass-half-full analysis says the company is doing what it has done for decades: prioritizing investing in the things -- both real-world assets for e-commerce and a modern finance ecosystem -- that have made it the dominant leader in both categories in Latin America. Yes, the risk profile has changed, but we think the sell-off of the stock has also helped give investors more margin of safety and a nice opportunity to buy shares of this long-term winner. -- Jason Hall

5. Netflix

This year has been filled with drama for Netflix (NASDAQ: NFLX). After refusing to get into a bidding war, the company walked away from its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery (NASDAQ: WBD). The streaming pioneer delivered first-quarter revenue that grew 16%, while its EPS surged 86%, but the stock initially sold off on news that co-founder Reed Hastings would retire from the board in June.

Despite the sell-off, the future looks bright. At the advertising industry's annual "upfronts" event, Netflix revealed its ad-supported tier now serves 250 million monthly active users, up from 190 million in November. Of those, 80% are watching weekly, giving the company growing leverage with advertisers. Netflix is leaning into this new revenue stream, with plans to expand into 15 new countries in 2027.

This shows that, contrary to popular opinion, Netflix has a long runway for growth ahead. And with the stock trading at 29 times earnings, Foolish investors can pick up shares at a discount. -- Danny Vena
