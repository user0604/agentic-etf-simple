Key Takeaways
Red Cat delivered significant sales gains but widened its operating loss as costs outpaced profitability.
Revenue rose year over year.

Defense robotics provider Red Cat (NASDAQ:RCAT) posted massive sales growth but continued to register a steep loss as it expands its manufacturing and technological capabilities. The company, a maker of drones and autonomous maritime vessels for allied defense markets, reported results for its fiscal first quarter on May 7, 2026. Despite rapid expansion and order momentum, rising costs and cash burn were key features of the period.

Overall, the quarter demonstrated Red Cat’s ability to capture market demand, though the jump in expenses overshadowed margin improvement and strong revenue gains.

Notable Developments
Sales surged to $15.5 million, up over eightfold from the prior-year quarter, thanks to new defense contracts for Black Widow drones and expanded international reach.
Gross margin turned positive, reaching 12.7% compared to negative 52.1% last year.
Cash fell by $36 million in the quarter to $131.9 million, reflecting heavy investment in inventory and operations; inventory grew to $62.7 million, doubling in just three months.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Red Cat management raised its short- to medium-term annual revenue target to $150 million to $180 million, citing strong backlog and momentum in orders. Leadership highlighted improving margins and growing product diversity.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure reflects the success of winning new drone orders from both NATO and Asia-Pacific allies and a growing presence in Ukraine’s defense modernization efforts. Black Widow drones (a line of tactical unmanned aerial vehicles) secured landmark orders, while the Blue Ops platform (uncrewed surface vessels, or USVs) began ramping production and deployment.

While gross margin swung sharply positive from a year ago and improved nearly threefold from the preceding quarter, total operating expenses jumped to $29.3 million. Research and development and administrative costs more than doubled, leading to a widening operating loss and negative adjusted EBITDA.

Strategic partnerships and acquisitions were a major theme, with the purchase of Apium Swarm Robotics completed during the period and an agreement to acquire Quaze Technologies under review. These moves aim to enhance Red Cat’s capabilities in autonomy, AI, and wireless power for next-generation defense systems.

What Red Cat Does

Red Cat designs and manufactures advanced unmanned drones and autonomous maritime vessels for military and government customers, focusing on security, U.S. compliance, and technological innovation.
