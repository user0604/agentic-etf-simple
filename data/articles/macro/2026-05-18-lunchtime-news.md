1. NextEra Buys Dominion in $67 Billion Deal

NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE) is buying Dominion Energy (NYSE: D) in an all-stock deal valued at nearly $67 billion. The combination creates the world's largest regulated electric utility, with a $249 billion market cap, as the two companies race to power AI data centers.

Data center play: Dominion powers the world's largest data center market in northern Virginia, while NextEra plans to build more than 30 data center hubs across the U.S. to meet AI-driven electricity demand.
Generation muscle: The combined company will be the world leader in renewables and battery storage, the top U.S. natural gas generator, and second in nuclear power. Dominion shares jumped 9% on the news, while NextEra fell more than 6%.
2. Lululemon Fights Back Against Founder
★ LULU is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Lululemon Athletica (NASDAQ: LULU) issued its first major public response to founder Chip Wilson's proxy battle, calling his perspectives "outdated" and his board nominees "less qualified." Settlement talks fell apart last week, and shareholders will vote at the June 25 annual meeting.

Conflict concerns: Lululemon flagged that Wilson advises direct competitors Alo Yoga and Vuori, and noted his nominee Marc Maurer still holds a stake worth tens of millions in former employer On Holding (NYSE: ONON).
Stakes are high: Lululemon shares are down nearly 43% this year amid tariff pressure, weak U.S. demand, and a stale product lineup. Incoming CEO Heidi O'Neill, a longtime Nike (NYSE: NKE) executive, takes over Sept. 8.
3. Meta Trims for the Bots
★ META is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) starts its latest round of layoffs Wednesday, cutting roughly 8,000 jobs, or about 10% of its workforce. The company is also pulling 6,000 open roles as it shifts spending toward AI infrastructure.

AI capex surge: Meta lifted its 2026 capital expenditure guidance by as much as $10 billion last month, with spending now reaching as high as $145 billion. More layoff rounds are expected in August and the fall.
Stock lags peers: Shares are down about 7% this year and nearly 5% over the past 12 months, underperforming every megacap peer except Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT). Wall Street remains skeptical of Meta's AI strategy under chief Alexandr Wang.
