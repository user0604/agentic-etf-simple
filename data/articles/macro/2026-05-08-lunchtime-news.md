1. Progyny Beats With Buyback Push

Progyny (NASDAQ: PGNY) posted higher first-quarter revenue and profit while expanding its client base and completing a $200 million share repurchase program, signaling continued momentum despite the loss of a previously disclosed large client. Revenue rose 1.4% year over year to $328.5 million, or 12.2% excluding the lapsed contract.

Operational highlights: Net income jumped to $24.2 million, or $0.29 per diluted share, from $15.1 million a year earlier, while gross margin expanded to 25.3% from 23.4% on operational efficiencies and lower stock-based comp. Fertility benefit services revenue rose 1.5% to $209.4 million, and the company served 595 fertility and family building clients, up from 532 a year earlier.
Capital return push: Progyny repurchased more than 5.5 million shares for $116.4 million during the quarter, completing its $200 million authorization. The board is currently evaluating a new repurchase plan. CEO Pete Anevski said the early selling season is pacing ahead of last year, with new pipeline build "substantially favorable" versus a year ago.
2. Mythos Hysteria Was Already Here

Cybersecurity experts and artificial intelligence (AI) researchers told CNBC that the software-vulnerability discovery capabilities revealed by Anthropic's Mythos model can already be reproduced using existing models, including older ones from Anthropic and OpenAI. The finding cuts against the panic that sent global banks, tech giants, and governments scrambling last month.

What the research shows: Vidoc CEO Klaudia Kloc said current models have been powerful enough to detect zero-day vulnerabilities at scale for as long as a year, using a technique called orchestration that splits code analysis across multiple tools or models. Cybersecurity firm Aisle reproduced many of Mythos' headline results using cheaper models in parallel, suggesting scale and coordination matter more than the latest frontier model.
What's actually new: Anthropic argues Mythos' distinguishing capability is automating the next step, developing working exploits with little human input, a job that previously required skilled researchers. The release was limited to a few American firms, including Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL), JPMorgan Chase (NYSE: JPM), and Palo Alto Networks (NASDAQ: PANW). The Trump administration is now considering new government oversight of future models, while OpenAI granted limited access to its rival GPT-5.5-Cyber on Thursday.
3. AWS Outage Hits Trading Platforms

Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) Web Services (AWS) reported operational issues starting Thursday night that disrupted trading on platforms including Coinbase (NASDAQ: COIN) and FanDuel parent Flutter Entertainment (NYSE: FLUT). The outage stemmed from a thermal issue at a data center in Virginia, AWS' main U.S.-East-1 region.

Scope of the disruption: AWS said the issues are confined to a single Availability Zone in the region and that it's bringing additional cooling system capacity online to recover affected hardware.
Knock-on effects: FanDuel told users it was investigating technical difficulties tied to the broader AWS outage, with bettors complaining about lost wagers from being unable to cash out. Coinbase said failures in multiple AWS zones caused an extended outage of core trading services, though it later said the primary issue had been fully resolved. AWS accounts for roughly a third of the global cloud infrastructure market.
