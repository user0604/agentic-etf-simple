1. Why I Pass on the Most Valuable Brand in the World

By Loren Horst
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) is the third-largest company in the world, selling over 3 billion iPhones since what many consider a historic product launch 2007. Despite a reputation for sleek hardware and a tightly integrated ecosystem, I don’t own shares for two simple reasons.

First, my S&P 500 index funds offer enough exposure, roughly $6 out of every $100 tracking that popular index. Second, perhaps the simpler answer: I’m an Android guy on his second Pixel phone. It’s by no means an either-or decision, but I’ve owned shares of Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) the longest of any stock because I’m a huge fan, and I pass on many objectively great companies that I’m indifferent about as a consumer.

2. Brookfield: Bruce Flatt’s Brilliant Black Box

By Nick Sciple
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Brookfield (NYSE: BN) is one of the smartest private asset owners on the planet. CEO Bruce Flatt and his team have spent decades building a global infrastructure, real estate, renewables, and credit empire few can match in scale and operational depth. I have a hard time betting against them.

But I also have a hard time betting on them, and the reason is the accounting.

Brookfield reports through a tangle of affiliates, partnerships, fee-related earnings, distributable earnings, and mark-to-model fair values that I cannot independently verify. When I read a 10-K, I want to know what I own and what it earns. With Brookfield, I’m taking management’s word on a lot of inputs, and the structure almost forces it.

I’d rather own businesses where the numbers tell me the story.

3. Keeping the Can and Avoiding the Shares

By Asit Sharma
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

While most of my portfolio is tech-focused, I invest in many dividend stocks. I often encounter companies with hallowed brands and long track records that seem like prime dividend candidates at first blush – but there’s often a catch.

Take WD-40 (NASDAQ: WDFC): Publicly traded for more than 50 years, it enjoys household name status, excellent global distribution, and huge brand loyalty. But while cash flows are extremely stable, the business struggles to push top-line growth above 10% annually, and bottom-line growth rarely exceeds the high teens — traits I seek when evaluating dividend growth potential. I prize safety, but cash flow expansion is simply paramount when it comes to dividends. I admire this business and have a can of WD-40 around the house, but no shares in my portfolio.

4. Sunny Side Up, Stock Side Down

By Meilin Quinn
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

A company I love but won’t own is Vital Farms (NASDAQ: VITL). I buy its gorgeous eggs because the hens get acres of pasture to roam, grass and bugs to forage, and a life closer to what chickens should have. You taste the difference in the deeper orange yolks.

The stock is another matter. VITL is a premium brand with a commodity cost structure, which leaves it with scant pricing power. When conventional egg prices spike like they did during avian flu, the gap between these eggs and a regular dozen narrows, and VITL flies. When supply rebuilds and prices ease, like now, the gap widens and VITL has to offload excess inventory at wholesale prices.

Margins swing on an unpredictable chicken disease cycle, and the welfare standards that make the brand special keep costs structurally high.

5. The Chipotle Dilemma: A World-Class Business at a Premium Price

By Rachel Warren
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Chipotle Mexican Grill (NYSE: CMG) is a company I admire for its operational excellence and brand power. It mastered the art of throughput and maintained a premium image in an increasingly crowded fast-casual space. The company’s ability to push price increases while maintaining customer loyalty is also impressive.

My hesitation when it comes to scooping up shares is a function of its current valuation. While the business is financially stable despite short-term headwinds, the stock is priced for absolute perfection, leaving very little margin of safety for new investors. In a long-term lens, you want to buy great companies at fair prices, but right now, the market is asking for a massive premium. I’m keeping a close eye on the business for now.
