Gossamer Bio (NASDAQ:GOSS)'s latest quarter highlighted continued progress on its key clinical and regulatory goals, but losses widened as it ramped up investment and set out to reduce debt. The biotechnology company specializes in therapies for pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH) and related diseases. On May 18, 2026, it reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period saw meaningful growth in partnership revenue, significant clinical milestones, and a focus on refinancing, yet the company remains pre-commercial with a narrowing cash runway.

Notable Developments
The company secured an in-person Pre-NDA meeting with the FDA, keeping its timeline for potential regulatory submission intact.
Revenue from collaborations nearly doubled, fueled by increased activity in advancing seralutinib with partner Chiesi.
A major debt exchange offer was announced, aiming to cut convertible note liability from $200 million to $72 million if successful.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management projects its current cash balance will be enough to fund operations into the first quarter of 2027, which covers the anticipated window for regulatory review. The key focus remains on the outcome of the FDA meeting and the success of the debt exchange offer. Investors should closely monitor the company's cash use and capital structure as it moves toward a potential new drug approval.

Quarterly highlights

This quarter, Gossamer Bio advanced its lead drug candidate, seralutinib, achieving a milestone by confirming a summer meeting with the FDA to discuss potential approval. New imaging substudy results from the Phase 3 PROSERA trial demonstrated statistically significant changes across multiple disease parameters in PAH, supporting the drug's mechanism in a well-treated patient group. All findings remain exploratory, with further clarity expected after regulatory interactions.

Collaboration revenue, mainly from Chiesi, rose sharply versus the year-ago period, signaling continued partner engagement and reimbursement on shared clinical work. No details on new milestones were given, but management reiterated the role of strategic alliances in supporting the seralutinib program's progress.

Operating losses widened as research and development expenses climbed, mostly due to intensive Phase 3 trial activity. General and administrative expenses also rose because of severance and restructuring, while the company's debt exchange offer is designed to extend its funding window by reducing short-term obligations and providing more financial flexibility if accepted by creditors.
