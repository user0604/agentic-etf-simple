Key Takeaways
ECARX delivered significant improvements in profitability despite a sharp drop in total revenue.
Gross margin rose to 21.4%, and the net loss narrowed from the prior-year period.
Management reiterated its full-year revenue target but cautioned that margin pressure will increase in coming quarters.

ECARX (NASDAQ:ECX) reported clear progress on profitability but faced a major fall in revenue this quarter. The automotive technology provider focuses on advanced in-vehicle computing and cockpit electronics. The results, published May 19, 2026 for the first quarter, underline hefty cost controls and margin gains even as top-line sales fell due to portfolio changes and nonrecurring software revenues last year. Overall, the period shows a disciplined shift to higher-value products, with margin gains but a challenging revenue comparison.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped 22% due to a large one-time software deal last year and phased-out legacy businesses, but gross margin improved by 1.6 percentage points.
Total operating costs fell, with research and development expenses cut by nearly one third to support margin growth.
The company shipped more high-end Pikes and Antora technology solutions, which increased by about 73% year over year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained its full-year 2026 revenue forecast of $1.0–$1.1 billion, suggesting much higher sales are required in the rest of the year. Leadership signaled that gross margin could decline during the coming quarters due to higher memory costs. Investors should monitor whether ECARX can ramp up sales and maintain cost discipline as it aims for this ambitious target.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue dropped sharply from the same period a year ago, mostly from a $25 million nonrecurring software license and the company's decision to exit less profitable legacy products. Despite top-line weakness, gross margin reached 21.4%, driven by improved pricing and product mix. Adjusted EBITDA, which measures core operational profit excluding certain costs, marked its third positive quarter in a row, showing sustained cost controls as R&D and selling, general, and administrative costs decreased.

High-end solutions shipment growth drove technology momentum: shipments of premium Pikes and Antora computing platforms rose approximately 73% compared to the prior year. The company also highlighted four new vehicle model launches using its advanced stack, and key steps forward in international partnerships, including Volkswagen in Latin America and a new contract with a large Chinese automaker outside its historical core customer.

During the quarter, ECARX advanced its move into the global market by opening a fully operational Singapore office and announcing a strategic partnership with May Mobility, aiming to equip future autonomous fleets. The balance sheet reflects some pressure, with total cash at $70.1 million and increased borrowings, underscoring the company's need to sustain financial flexibility as it drives international growth.

What Ecarx Does

ECARX develops and supplies advanced automotive technology platforms, including digital cockpit solutions and vehicle computing hardware, for automakers worldwide.
