Full Truck Alliance Orders Jump 14% in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Full Truck Alliance’s business grew but profitability fell due to higher costs and shifts in service mix.
Fulfilled orders rose 14.3% year over year in Q1 2026.
Management guided for lower total revenue next quarter compared to last year.

Full Truck Alliance (NYSE:YMM) delivered higher order volumes and strong cash generation in the recent quarter, but saw profitability drop as cost increases and revenue mix changes weighed on results. The leading digital freight platform in China continues to expand its network of shippers and truckers, investing heavily in artificial intelligence to boost platform efficiency. The company announced results on May 21, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period showed robust platform activity but a setback in earnings, and management expects softer revenue in the upcoming quarter.

Notable Developments
Order activity continued to climb, with fulfilled orders up 14.3%, reaching 55.0 million in the quarter.
Profitability fell, as non-GAAP adjusted net income dropped 13.6% year over year and operating expenses rose sharply.
The company declared a quarterly cash dividend of US$0.0042 per share, for total Q2 payments of about US$87.5 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled that total revenue in the next quarter could fall below last year’s, guiding for RMB 3.07–3.17 billion versus RMB 3.24 billion in the prior-year period. Revenues from segments outside freight brokerage are still expected to grow, however. Investors should focus on the continued shift toward technology-driven services and how rising costs and regulatory pressures affect future profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

The platform handled 55.0 million fulfilled orders in the latest quarter, up from 48.2 million, as network usage and shipper activity both rose significantly. Management attributed this double-digit growth to ongoing improvements in AI-enabled matching and user experience, backed by steady investment in its technology infrastructure.

Revenue climbed 5.5%, but earnings were hit by higher general and administrative expenses, much of which resulted from substantially increased share-based compensation. Research and development spending also rose, especially with the consolidation of Giga.AI Technology into operations since July 2025.

Value-added services such as credit solutions and insurance saw revenue shrink due to changing regulations and decreased lending volumes, and the company noted a small rise in its non-performing loan ratio to 3.2%. Management highlighted ongoing transition to asset-light and more compliant credit models in response.

What Full Truck Alliance Does

Full Truck Alliance operates a digital platform in China that connects shippers and truckers for freight transportation, offering end-to-end services including freight matching, transaction processing, and value-added solutions like credit, insurance, and logistics tools.
