Cisco Systems (NASDAQ:CSCO), a provider of networking hardware, software, and security solutions for businesses and governments, is expected to report its third-quarter fiscal year 2026 earnings on Wednesday, May 13, 2026, after the market closes.

Q2 FY2026 Recap

Cisco delivered a strong second quarter for fiscal 2026, with both revenue and earnings per share growing at double-digit rates year over year. Total revenue reached $15.3 billion, up 10%, and diluted earnings per share rose 31% to $0.80 on a GAAP basis. Non-GAAP earnings per share were $1.04, up 11%. These results beat analyst estimates for both revenue and earnings. Growth was primarily driven by robust demand for AI infrastructure and campus networking products, while security revenue declined due to ongoing transitions within that portfolio. Operating income and margins also improved, reflecting disciplined cost management and efficiency gains. The company increased its quarterly dividend to $0.42 per share and returned $3 billion to shareholders during the quarter.

Positives
AI infrastructure and networking demand accelerated: Product revenue rose 14%, led by 21% growth in networking, as customers invested in AI infrastructure and campus upgrades. This growth outpaced expectations and was broad-based across geographies and customer types.
Strong order growth signals continued momentum: Total product orders increased 18% year over year, with notable strength from cloud and service provider customers. Orders from hyperscale data center operators were especially strong, supporting future revenue visibility.
Operating leverage and cost discipline improved profitability: Operating income grew 21% and operating margin increased by 2.3 percentage points, as Cisco managed expenses and benefited from lower amortization of acquired intangible assets.
Shareholder returns increased: Cisco raised its dividend and continued share repurchases, demonstrating confidence in cash flows and business outlook.
Negatives
Security revenue declined: Security product revenue fell 4% year over year, mainly due to lower sales of prior generation products and a shift in Splunk's business model from on-premises to cloud subscriptions, which dampened near-term revenue growth.
Gross margin faced pressure from memory costs: While productivity gains helped, higher memory prices and an unfavorable product mix reduced gross margin in the Americas and APJC regions.
Services revenue was slightly down: Services revenue declined 1%, reflecting lower support service sales, though this was partially offset by growth in professional services.
What to Watch in Q3 FY2026's Report
AI infrastructure revenue and order trends: Monitor whether Cisco can sustain or accelerate its momentum in AI-related networking and optics, especially as the company targets over $3 billion in AI infrastructure revenue from hyperscale customers for the full year.
Security portfolio transition progress: Watch for signs that newer security offerings (such as Secure Access, XDR, and AI Defense) are offsetting declines in legacy products, and whether Splunk’s shift to cloud subscriptions continues to weigh on reported growth.
Gross margin recovery and pricing actions: Assess how Cisco manages ongoing memory cost pressures and whether recent price increases and contract adjustments with partners are helping to stabilize or improve gross margins.
Campus networking refresh cycle: Look for updates on the pace of campus network upgrades, especially as customers replace older equipment and adopt next-generation solutions. This is a key multi-year opportunity for Cisco.
Status of announced joint ventures and partnerships: Track progress on the announced joint venture with AMD and HUMAIN for AI infrastructure, which is expected to begin operations this calendar year, and any updates on sovereign and neocloud customer wins or deployments.
