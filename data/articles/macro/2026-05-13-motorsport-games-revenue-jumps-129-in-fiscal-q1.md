Motorsport Games Revenue Jumps 129% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

Comments
Key Takeaways
Business win as core titles drive substantial revenue growth.
Management signals confidence but gives no specific outlook for the rest of fiscal 2026.

Racing game developer Motorsport Games (NASDAQ:MSGM) delivered robust revenue gains but saw a decline in per-share earnings due to rising costs and share dilution. The company specializes in officially licensed motorsport video games and simulation platforms. Reporting its results on May 13, 2026, for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, it posted another quarter of rapid sales growth, though profitability per share did not keep pace.

Overall, the quarter demonstrated strong top-line progress, driven mainly by its Le Mans Ultimate title, but higher expenses and a much larger share count held back net income per share.

Notable Developments
Revenue more than doubled, reaching $4.0 million as Le Mans Ultimate saw stronger player activity and new content updates.
Gross margin improved to 87.2%, lifting gross profit to $3.5 million, even as total operating expenses increased to $2.5 million.
Average shares outstanding jumped 71%, which, along with higher costs, drove EPS down despite flat year over year net income.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management describes the current financial position as stable, emphasizing “momentum” for core titles and highlighting expansion plans for new platforms such as consoles. There is no quantified financial guidance for fiscal 2026. Investors should watch for updates on the console release for Le Mans Ultimate and more detail on the monetization of its RaceControl subscription platform.

Quarterly Highlights

Le Mans Ultimate, a racing simulation game, provided the primary boost for sales. The company launched a major content update in March, setting a player engagement peak with more than 8,800 concurrent users and introducing new features aimed at future console releases.

Management attributes this to investment in supporting top titles and product development, including higher stock-based compensation reported at $375,000 versus none a year ago.

The company completed a $3.7 million repurchase and cancellation of all its Class B common stock just after the quarter, and secured a $3.0 million credit facility with Citibank to fund product investments. Cash reserves ended the quarter at $5.9 million, but dropped to $3.8 million post-buyback.

What Motorsport Games Does

Motorsport Games develops and publishes officially licensed motorsport video games, online racing platforms and simulation tools for racing fans and professionals.
