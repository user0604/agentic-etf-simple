Aritzia Dazzles While Vital Farms Wilts

One stock's results were excellent. The other feels like a bait-and-switch.

Jim Gillies

May 8, 2026, 10:09 AM

Comments
10.0

Often, what we bring to The Morning Show are success stories. A stock we have recommended reports excellent results and there is much rejoicing. I have one of those today, but I'm also going to offset that with one that has me wondering if management are willful idiots or just clueless.

Start with the positive -- Aritzia (TSX: ATZ)! The "Everyday Luxury" fashion house delivered its latest outstanding quarter (Q4-26 and full year FY26) last night. Consider:

Opened 14 new boutiques in FY26. Free cash flow (FCF) increased six-fold (important when you remember Aritzia is self-funding their expansion)
Revenue up nearly 33% for the quarter; annual revenue on a four-year 25% per year growth pace
Same-store sales grew almost 28% (!) for the quarter
Margins (gross, EBITDA, operating, net, FCF) UP -- FCF margin went from 2.9% in FY25 to 13.1% in FY26, expenses as percentage of revenue DOWN.
Revenue growth targets achieved a full one year early; cash-rich, debt-free balance sheet.
FY27 guidance essentially for more of the same -- 12 to 13 new stores, double-digit (likely starting with a "2") revenue growth, continued margin expansion, all growth self-financed, continued cash production.

I'm penning this before market open and so have no idea how other investors might take the quarter. But for my money (I'm both an owner, and have recommended Aritzia multiple times, most recently about six weeks ago) this was an outstanding report; the latest in an ongoing stream of them. Carry on Aritzia!

On the other hand ...

I don't mind stocks that plunge after an earnings report. Indeed, it's often opportunity to add shares of a quality company. But there are also occasions where the stock gets creamed upon earnings, you look at the earnings report and nod along saying, "Yeah, that makes sense." The "pinnacle of the species" is the company that botches their business multiple quarters in succession, taking a stock price beatdown each time. Which is how we get to Vital Farms (NASDAQ: VITL).

I recommended Vital Farms in Hidden Gems Canada in January at around $30; it's sub-$10 today. I'd argue that I (badly) overestimated the business, and particularly management, strength here. Vital has reported just two quarters since recommendation, both riddled with problems. Last quarter I commented on feeling "baited and switched" -- 2025 was an "investment" year with elevated capital spending; 2026 was to be a "harvest" year, where the benefits of those investments started to ramp up. Management (negatively) surprised us with capital spending plans nearly double that of the "investment" year; spending plans that simply cannot be met with self-generated cash.

This quarter they pulled the ever popular, "Sharply reduce guidance barely a quarter into the year," trick.

Expectations for 2026 were for revenue of $900 million to $920 million (21% growth), and adjusted EBITDA of $105 million to $115 million. After the Q1-26 report, strike revenue expectations to $775 million to $800 million and adjusted EBITDA to $0 to $10 million. Margins contracted. Expected quarterly profit turned into a reported quarterly loss. Management spoke of "anticipated changes in industry pricing and promotional dynamics", and then wasted $20 million on stock buybacks in the quarter when they had to know the impact on their results and pending likely guidance reduction. They also announced they're exiting the butter business (3.5% of 2025's revenue) which -- so what? The butter segment's contribution is less than the revenue guidance downward revision.

I suppose the "good" news is that Vital's management cut their planned capital spending for the year -- my source of "bait and switch" last quarter, in half. But this strategic flip-flopping and apparent lack of insight into their own business has left a really bad taste in my mouth. Increasingly thinking that recommending this one was a mistake.
