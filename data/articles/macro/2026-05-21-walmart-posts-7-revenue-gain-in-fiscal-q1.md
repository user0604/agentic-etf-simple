Key Takeaways
Walmart delivered strong sales growth and profit gains driven by digital and international segments.
Earnings per share rose to $0.66 as revenue increased 7.3% from the prior year.
Management kept full-year guidance unchanged, signaling a prudent outlook amid cost pressures.

Walmart (NASDAQ:WMT) delivered a notable win this quarter as continued momentum in its digital and international businesses led to robust growth. The leading global retailer and operator of over 10,900 stores worldwide released its first-quarter fiscal 2027 results on May 21, 2026, reporting for the period ended April 30, 2026. Overall, the quarter demonstrated broad business strength but also highlighted investment-driven cash outflows and rising inventory levels.

Notable developments
Global eCommerce sales advanced 26%, driving higher revenue and lifting digital's total sales contribution versus last year.
Inventory climbed 8.9%, exceeding sales growth and reflecting strong grocery volume plus the timing of merchandise receipts.
Free cash flow dropped to negative $1.9 billion, a $2.4 billion swing from last year due to increased capital investment and inventory build.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year sales, profit, and earnings targets for fiscal 2027, guiding for constant-currency sales growth of 3.5% to 4.5% and adjusted EPS between $2.75-$2.85. Investors should pay close attention to expense discipline and how Walmart manages its growing inventory, especially if demand moderates.

Quarterly highlights

The digital business was a standout in the quarter. eCommerce sales rose 26% worldwide, with strong gains in store-fulfilled delivery, digital advertising, and major international platforms such as Flipkart. Digital operations now account for a larger share of transaction growth in the U.S. and abroad.

However, Walmart’s investment in supply chain automation and expansion weighed on short-term cash flow. Capital spending increased, and negative free cash flow reflected both higher capital expenditures and a buildup of inventory. While management cited timing and strong grocery demand as drivers, this area merits ongoing scrutiny.

At the same time, profitability benefited from alternative income streams. Revenue from advertising increased 37% globally, and membership fee revenue grew 17.4%, both supporting higher margins even as core operating expenses climbed due to fuel and healthcare costs.

What Walmart does

Walmart operates retail stores and eCommerce platforms offering groceries, general merchandise, and membership services to hundreds of millions of customers worldwide.
