Key Takeaways
Best Buy posted improved profits and growth in most key categories.

Best Buy (NYSE:BBY) posted a solid business win this quarter, as growth in its digital marketplace and services helped drive higher profitability. The leading consumer electronics and appliances retailer reported its first quarter fiscal 2027 results on May 28, 2026, covering the three months ended May 2. Overall, the quarter showed progress across key business areas despite continued pressure in certain categories.

Notable Developments
Comparable sales rose 2.0%, beating the company’s prior outlook for about 1% growth and showing broad category strength.
Operating margin jumped by 1.6 percentage points to 4.1% as sales of services, digital ads, and the Best Buy Marketplace played a greater role in profits.
Appliance sales dropped by 13.6% domestically, the steepest category decline, while entertainment products climbed 38.1%.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management left its full-year guidance unchanged, calling for flattish comparable sales and adjusted operating margin between 4.3% and 4.4%. The company expects second quarter comparable sales growth to slow to around 1%, as it laps a strong prior year period driven by gaming launches. Investors should watch for sustained momentum in new digital profit streams, like ads and marketplace, and for signs of improvement in appliance sales.

Quarterly Highlights

Comparable sales, a key retail metric showing growth in stores open at least one year, increased 2.0%, with most major product categories contributing. Gains in gaming, computing, and mobile phones offset weakness in appliances, which continue to face soft demand and price competition.

The company’s push into digital and higher-value services paid off. Revenue from the Best Buy Marketplace and its advertising business grew, helping support an improved gross profit rate. Services, including tech support and in-home assistance, now make up 10% of domestic revenue and posted a 5.5% comparable sales gain.

International operations outperformed the domestic business, with overseas revenue up 7.3% and comparable sales increasing 4.7%. However, product margins internationally came under pressure, and total group inventory and accounts payable both rose year over year, reflecting ongoing investments in supply chain and product availability.
