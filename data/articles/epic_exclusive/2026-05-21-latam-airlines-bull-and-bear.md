LATAM Airlines: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 21, 2026, 9:14 AM

Comments

LATAM Airlines Group S.A. (NYSE:LTM) is the largest airline group in Latin America, operating a diversified network across South America, North America, Europe, and Oceania. The company’s growth strategy centers on leveraging its broad route map, investing in premium offerings, expanding its loyalty program, and modernizing its fleet to drive profitability through both robust passenger demand and resilient ancillary revenues.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Record Passenger Demand and Load Factors Point to Enduring Growth Momentum

LATAM’s ability to consistently grow passenger volumes while optimizing capacity directly supports a durable recovery and future expansion. In the first quarter of 2026, the company grew its capacity as measured by Available Seat Kilometers (ASK) by 10.4% year over year and transported 22.9 million passengers, reflecting a 9.1% annual increase. Importantly, its consolidated load factor reached 85.3%, an improvement of two percentage points compared to the previous year. This tight capacity management, especially during the Southern Hemisphere’s peak summer travel season, showcases sustained demand and effective network optimization. Robust occupancy and volume growth in both international and Brazil domestic markets position the airline to capture further upside as global and regional travel continues to recover.

2. Premium Revenue Mix Expansion Drives Margin Resilience and Reduces Cyclical Risk

The shift toward premium passenger revenue is transforming LATAM’s earnings quality and defensiveness. In Q1 2026, premium passenger revenue surged by 28% year over year, now making up 27% of total passenger revenues — a significant jump from pre-pandemic levels. This shift is not just cyclical recovery; it’s the result of targeted upgrades like Wi-Fi-enabled widebody aircraft, a growing elite customer base in the LATAM Pass loyalty program (2.6 million out of 55 million members), and upcoming Airbus A321XLR premium cabins. With loyalty members now accounting for nearly 60% of passenger revenue, management’s focus on this less price-sensitive segment cushions results against economic downturns and intensifying price competition, building long-term margin stability.

3. Diversified Revenue Streams from Cargo and Ancillaries Bolster Stability Amid Disruptions

Cargo operations and ancillary business streams provide a crucial buffer against macroeconomic and demand volatility. In the first quarter of 2026, cargo revenues grew 3.4%, highlighting LATAM’s successful diversification. Management regards this business mix as a “key lever” for resilience, especially during periods of economic stress or demand shocks — be it from oil price spikes or currency swings. This segment not only smooths revenue and cash flow during cycles but also helps the company maintain service to core markets when passenger demand becomes unpredictable.

4. Strong Operating Leverage and Capital Structure Enable Resilience and Strategic Flexibility

LATAM’s low net leverage and substantial liquidity support both operational continuity and opportunistic growth. For Q1 2026, the company delivered an adjusted operating margin of 19.8% — the highest in its history — and net income of $576 million, with a healthy net margin close to 14%. More importantly, it maintained $4.1 billion in liquidity and a net leverage ratio of just 1.3x (well below its 1.8x target), with $1.5 billion in unencumbered assets providing added optionality. This financial strength allows LATAM to manage through industry shocks, invest in fleet upgrades, and adapt to unpredictable fuel or currency swings better than more levered competitors still restructuring from the pandemic era.

5. Market Leadership and Network Breadth Support Superior Pricing Power and Defensible Market Share

LATAM’s dominant regional network enables adaptive capacity management and strategic fare increases, translating into sustained profitability gains. As Latin America’s largest carrier by market value, LATAM has captured or preserved market share in most of its key geographies. Unlike ultra-low-cost peers who have been forced to cut capacity faster, LATAM maintains a scalable network spanning international and domestic routes, focusing on profitable growth rather than market share for its own sake. This approach has underpinned healthy forward bookings — even amid rising fuel prices — allowing the company to pass on higher costs through fare increases with only modest effects on demand, particularly within its premium and loyalty-driven base.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Sustained Jet Fuel Price Volatility Threatens Margin Expansion and Earnings Trajectory

Persistently high jet fuel prices could significantly erode LATAM’s margins even in the face of strong demand. Management has already updated its 2026 guidance to reflect $700 million or more in additional fuel expenses for Q2 alone, with assumed jet fuel market prices of $170 per barrel for the middle of the year. Despite partial hedging and gradual cost pass-through strategies, such volatility can quickly offset operational improvements and place pressure on the company to continue raising fares or reducing capacity — both of which may ultimately hit volumes. Industry-wide shocks from geopolitical conflict further compound this unpredictable risk, creating the potential for a sharp profit reversal in subsequent quarters if cost increases outpace revenue gains.

2. Currency Depreciation (Especially of the Brazilian Real) Intensifies Cost Pressures and Forecasting Difficulty

Ongoing weakness and volatility in local currencies, particularly the Brazilian real, directly inflate LATAM’s U.S. dollar-reported cost base. In Q1 2026, cost per available seat kilometer excluding fuel (CASK ex fuel) increased, mainly due to local currency depreciation, prompting LATAM to revise its full-year cost guidance upwards ($0.045–$0.047). Given Brazil’s crucial role as a demand and capacity engine for LATAM, further swings in the real or other regional currencies can magnify input costs, disrupt planning, and undermine future margin recovery — especially if fare increases lag behind real-time movements in currency markets.

3. Fare Increases and Capacity Adjustments Risk Alienating Price-Sensitive Demand Segments

Strategic moves to offset rising costs through higher fares and targeted capacity reductions may ultimately reduce passenger volumes and erode the customer base over time. While LATAM’s premium revenue mix and loyal customer programs currently blunt these effects, the company acknowledges that fare hikes have already triggered a modest, manageable slowdown in price-sensitive segments. As persistent cost inflation compels further price adjustments, there is a real risk of a meaningful drop-off among more elastic leisure travelers or budget-conscious fliers — especially if competitors intensify promotional activity or if macro headwinds deepen in core South American economies. This risk, if realized, could dampen future top-line growth and undermine the network scale advantages that underpin LATAM’s current market leadership.
