Functional Brands faced sharply higher losses this quarter as it continued building its digital platform and expanding key brands. The company, a developer and marketer of dietary supplements with a focus on direct-to-consumer channels and regulatory compliance, reported its first-quarter 2026 results on May 18, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Notable Developments
Revenue increased by 3.5%, mainly due to stronger demand in direct-to-consumer sales.
Gross margin expanded by 3.9 percentage points, reflecting a higher mix of sales from channels with better profitability.
Net loss worsened to $6.99 million, driven largely by a one-time, non-cash equity restructuring charge of $6.3 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management stated it is prioritizing the shift from infrastructure investment toward achieving profitable growth, with a particular focus on scaling digital health offerings and core brands. Although it did not provide specific financial guidance, the company pointed to continued margin focus and capital structure improvements. Investors should closely watch the effect of spending discipline on profitability and changes to the company’s liquidity given increased financial pressures.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue rose modestly as direct-to-consumer business saw greater engagement. The increase in net revenue came mainly from digital platforms and eCommerce channels, where the company is investing for recurring sales and broader reach.

Gross margin improvement stemmed from a channel shift and operational efficiency. The company attributed higher margins to a greater portion of sales coming from its digital offerings, which generally produce better unit economics than wholesale distribution.

Net loss expanded due to a significant non-cash charge related to preferred equity restructuring, which masked operational gains from the business. Excluding this charge, results would have reflected widespread but more manageable increases in operating expenses linked to public company costs and investments in marketing and payroll.
