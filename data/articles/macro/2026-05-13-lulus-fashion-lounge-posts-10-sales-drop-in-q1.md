Lulu's Fashion Lounge (NASDAQ:LVLU) reported another quarter of declining sales and customer numbers, but it made progress on improving profit margins. The trend-focused women’s apparel retailer continued its operational reset during the first quarter of fiscal 2026, with results released on May 13, 2026. Overall, the period showed steady cost control but highlighted ongoing challenges in rebuilding top-line momentum.

Notable developments
Revenue declined 10% as active customers fell 11%, continuing a multi-quarter contraction.
Gross margin improved by 4.8 percentage points, driven by a higher mix of event-driven categories and inventory discipline.
Wholesale revenue doubled compared to a year ago, helping offset lower direct-to-consumer sales.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed a focus on margin improvement and expects positive adjusted EBITDA (a profitability metric before certain costs) for full fiscal 2026. The outlook points to stabilizing revenue trends following the current reset in lower-margin apparel categories. The most important factor to watch is whether customer and order declines can be reversed as new assortment and marketing strategies roll out.

Quarterly highlights

Profitability metrics strengthened as gross margin rose, marking a meaningful jump from the prior year. This improvement reflects tighter inventory management and a deeper shift toward higher-margin, event-driven apparel such as dresses for special occasions.

Revenue and customer acquisition remained pressured, with active customers down year over year and total orders also declining. Customer churn and reduced purchasing activity continued from previous quarters as the company reset its product mix and scaled back marketing for casual and footwear categories.

Expansion in wholesale distribution provided a bright spot, as revenue from this channel doubled from the prior year. Partnerships with large retailers like Nordstrom (NYSE:JWN) and Amazon (NASDAQ:AMZN) brought incremental sales and diversified revenue streams, even as margins in these channels can be thinner than in direct online sales.
