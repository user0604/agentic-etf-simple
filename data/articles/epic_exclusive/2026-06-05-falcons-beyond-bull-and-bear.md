Falcon's Beyond Bull and Bear

Falcon's Beyond Global, Inc. (NASDAQ:FBYD) is a themed entertainment and creative technology company specializing in designing, developing, and operating large-scale attractions and immersive experiences. With a strategy centered on winning marquee contracts in growing global entertainment markets, especially through multi-year projects and strategic partnerships, the company targets robust top-line growth while expanding its creative offerings and leveraging fresh capital to fuel its ambitions.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Capital Infusions Provide Fuel for Project Execution and Expansion

The listing of 11% Series B Cumulative Convertible Preferred Stock on Nasdaq delivers much-needed liquidity to fund growth initiatives. The company’s recent capital markets actions—including listing preferred shares with a substantial 11% coupon—demonstrate both an ability to access public markets and intent to secure resources for ongoing and future projects. This move increases the company’s cash runway and positions it to aggressively pursue capital-intensive opportunities in bespoke attractions and immersive technology, areas with escalating demand in regions like the Middle East and Asia. Ready access to capital is especially critical for themed entertainment firms, where upfront investment and timely milestone delivery directly drive revenue inflection points and competitive advantages.

2. Favorable Market Response Signals Growing Investor Confidence

Significant share price appreciation following quarterly results points to positive market perception of operational momentum. Following the May 2026 release of first quarter results, shares of the company advanced by 9.4% and 10.9%, despite limited explicit financial detail in public disclosures.

3. Project-Based Model Creates Revenue Upside from Milestone Achievements

Large, multi-year contracts offer step-function growth as significant milestones are reached. The business is inherently leveraged to high-value, long-duration project wins—for example, in entertainment mega-projects like Qiddiya or VAI Amusement Park. As project phases reach completion and become eligible for revenue recognition, the company can report outsized sequential growth compared to traditional quarterly cycles. This structure offers clear visibility into future revenue for already-won projects, while successful execution unlocks new tiers of customer demand and cross-selling opportunities in ancillary offerings, driving recurring and incremental revenues over time.

4. Increased Transparency and Active Engagement with Investors Builds Institutional Trust

Regular quarterly reporting and capital markets activity reinforce the company’s commitment to best practices in disclosure and governance. The cadence of earnings releases and transparent engagement—such as announcing major financing moves and providing timely updates—demonstrates a culture of accountability. Newly public companies that establish reliable reporting processes and investor communication are more likely to attract institutional capital, which can reduce volatility, provide additional funding avenues, and open doors for strategic mergers or partnerships. Over time, this can transform company valuation multiples and support sustained investor support during business cycles and macro uncertainty.

5. Addressable Market Tailwinds Provide Structural Growth Opportunities

Global demand for immersive entertainment, especially in emerging markets, offers enduring growth potential. The proliferation of major destination developments and experiential attractions—particularly across the Middle East, Asia, and select U.S. markets—is driving strong demand for specialized providers of themed entertainment and creative technology. The company’s early involvement in high-profile projects and demonstrated ability to access growth capital position it well to capitalize on these secular tailwinds over the next several years.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Reliance on Large, Lumpy Contracts Heightens Revenue Volatility and Customer Concentration Risk

The project-centric nature of the business means revenues and cash flows are highly dependent on a handful of major clients and contracts. The company’s performance remains closely tied to the successful execution and timely delivery of sizable entertainment projects. Any delay, cancellation, or deferral—whether due to client-side funding issues, project complexity, or external shocks—can materially impact top-line results and cash conversion. In sectors with high customer concentration, a single adverse development may have outsized repercussions, limiting predictability and increasing risk exposure relative to more diversified, recurring-revenue business models.

2. High-Cost Capital Raises Pressure on Long-Term Profitability and Increases Financial Commitments

The 11% coupon on the recently listed Series B Convertible Preferred Stock reflects investor-required risk premium and increases fixed obligations. While the capital raise boosts immediate liquidity, the high dividend obligation reduces operational flexibility and places substantial pressure on the business to deliver timely project ramp-up and robust cash flows. Should the company struggle to break even or deliver major project milestones, dividend payments could further erode resources available for reinvestment or necessitate additional, potentially dilutive, fundraising. This cycle jeopardizes long-term shareholder returns and exposes the business to funding shortfalls during downturns or execution missteps.

3. Delays in Project Wins or Execution Could Expose Liquidity Risk and Impact Strategic Plans

Capital-intensive business models require continuous pipeline conversion and timely revenue realization. The company’s need to raise significant new capital highlights the importance of flawless operational execution and ongoing contract wins. Any slowdown in securing new business, unexpected delays in project milestones, or cost overruns could rapidly tighten cash flow, especially given fixed financial obligations from preferred stock. This environment creates a scenario where even one major setback could force tough strategic choices, impact growth plans, or increase exposure to external financing on less favorable terms, posing a real threat to shareholder value and strategic continuity.
