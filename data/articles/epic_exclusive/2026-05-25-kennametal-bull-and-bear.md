Kennametal Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 3:06 PM

Comments

Kennametal Inc. (NYSE:KMT) is a global supplier of advanced tooling, materials, and wear-resistant solutions for sectors including earthworks, aerospace and defense, energy, general engineering, and transportation. The company’s growth strategy emphasizes vertical integration in critical inputs such as tungsten, aggressive market share capture in high-value industrial niches, and ongoing operational restructuring to enhance margins and efficiency. Through rapid pricing actions and innovation—including focused campaigns in emerging areas like medtech machining—Kennametal aims to solidify its competitive position and drive sustainable long-term growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Vertical Integration Amid Tungsten Supply Disruption Drives Structural Margin Advantage

Kennametal’s deep vertical integration in tungsten processing creates lasting value during industry supply volatility. In fiscal Q3 2026, the company benefited from a ninefold rise in tungsten prices (from $900 to $3,000 per metric ton year over year), a shock that left less-integrated peers scrambling for supply and extending lead times. By contrast, Kennametal leveraged its control across mining, refining, and recycling (sourcing primarily from Bolivia, East Asia, and reprocessed scrap) to maintain operational continuity. This advantage not only ensured fulfilment of existing customer orders but also enabled the company to win new business as rivals faltered, transforming supply chain resilience into both a margin buffer and a platform for share gains. As geopolitical risks heighten uncertainty around Chinese export controls, the durability of this integration becomes a strategic moat that could propel the company ahead of competitors well beyond this price cycle.

2. Market Share Gains in High-Growth Sectors Are Likely to Prove Sticky Beyond Current Cycle

Recent market share advances are anchored in strategic wins that could endure post-supply crisis. Kennametal reported double-digit organic sales growth year over year in fiscal Q3 2026, with the Americas up 27% and Asia Pacific up 25% (constant currency), and Infrastructure segment sales up 30%. While supply-driven opportunities contributed, management emphasized significant, long-term accounts secured in aerospace, defense, and energy—including sectors with secular tailwinds such as AI/data center power infrastructure. These wins, combined with digital customer engagement and channel expansion, lay the groundwork for sustained volume even as price effects moderate. In addition, the company’s entry into precision medtech machining (as highlighted by recent product rollouts) expands its exposure to niche, less cyclical applications, reducing reliance on legacy industrial cycles.

3. Aggressive and Proven Pricing Power Protects Profitability in an Inflationary Environment

Unmatched price-to-raw pass-through capabilities are safeguarding and expanding margins during cost surges. During Q4 FY26, Kennametal expects price and tariff surcharges to rise approximately 35% versus the prior-year quarter. Especially in Infrastructure, spot and indexed contracts enabled near-immediate cost recovery, while Metal Cutting needed only a 3–6 month lag. This agility delivered a $39 million price raw timing tailwind and raised adjusted EBITDA margin to 20.8% (from 17.9% a year prior). With industry input inflation and ongoing raw material volatility expected to remain themes into at least early FY27, the company’s demonstrated ability to swiftly transfer costs to customers suggests robust protection for near-term earnings power and resilience against further shocks.

4. Robust Geographic Growth Momentum Tied to North American and Asia Pacific Infrastructure Cycles

Kennametal’s exposure to recovering and infrastructure-driven regions is fueling above-trend organic expansion. In fiscal Q3 2026, sales growth was spread widely, but most pronounced in the Americas and Asia Pacific. This outperformance reflects vibrant infrastructure and defense spending in North America and incremental momentum in Asia, positioning the company to capitalize on large multi-year projects. The Infrastructure segment, especially, demonstrated resilience and pricing flexibility, capitalizing on regional industrial upswings. As the economic recovery broadens outside of Europe, Kennametal’s well-diversified customer base provides leverage to continued rebounds in capital spending and industrial activity, supporting forward revenue prospects even if some pricing tailwinds diminish.

5. Capital Structure Optimization and Consistent Dividends Enhance Resilience for Shareholders

Disciplined capital allocation, dividend continuity, and proactive debt management support long-term value creation. Despite working capital pressure from tungsten inventory, Kennametal has maintained its $0.20 per share quarterly dividend and initiated a public offering of senior notes alongside a cash tender offer for outstanding debt in May 2026. This capital structure management signals balance sheet flexibility and a commitment to shareholder returns, even during periods of earnings normalization. By locking in favorable borrowing costs and preserving liquidity, Kennametal is creating an environment that supports ongoing investment, buyback activity when prudent, and consistent dividends—a combination that could prove attractive for investors seeking both growth and stable income in cyclical industrial firms.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Margin and Earnings Outperformance May Prove Short-Lived as Tungsten Prices Normalize

A substantial portion of current profits hinges on transitory raw material price effects that are unlikely to persist. Management disclosed that approximately $2.45 of the FY26 adjusted EPS guidance ($3.75–$4.00) is attributable to “price raw timing” benefits stemming from the lag between price increases and input cost realization as tungsten surged. As supply-side pressures ease and competitors re-enter the market with normalized lead times, these transient windfalls are set to roll off, beginning as early as the back half of FY27. Without these effects, normalized earnings could drop to roughly $1.63–$1.73 per share (adjusted basis), calling into question how sustainable recent margin expansion truly is. If volumes do not rise meaningfully to offset the fade in pricing tailwinds, Kennametal’s near-term results may not represent its mid-cycle earning power.

2. Temporary Market Share Gains Could Reverse When Industry Supply Stabilizes

Supply-constrained share wins may evaporate as rivals recover, exposing volume risk post-cycle. Kennametal’s recent customer wins in Infrastructure and Metal Cutting were partly the result of competitors’ inability to deliver due to tungsten shortages and elongated lead times. While some new business is based on strategic account targeting or product innovation and may prove durable, supply-driven share gains—especially in earthworks and general engineering—are at risk of reversal once raw material constraints abate. Should the competitive landscape normalize, these customers may return to their prior suppliers, potentially eroding volumes and undercutting the perceived structural improvement in the business. Monitoring trailing 12-month volume trends will be critical to assess whether market share stickiness endures through the commodity cycle.

3. Elevated Working Capital and Weak Cash Conversion Increase Balance Sheet Risk if Prices Fall Rapidly

Sizable inventory and receivables built up for supply security could strain cash flow if input values drop swiftly. Kennametal’s primary working capital swelled to $819 million (32.4% of sales) year over year in Q3 FY26 as the company locked in expensive tungsten to support customer commitments. Free operating cash flow for the fiscal year is expected to be approximately negative 30% of adjusted net income, reflecting heavy inventory investment. If tungsten prices fall sharply, the company could face marked-to-market losses on inventory, delayed conversion of earnings to cash, and reduced flexibility for capital returns or reinvestment. This risk is particularly acute if a price correction collides with declining demand or unsuccessful pass-through of prior cost increases, leaving working capital elevated precisely as profit margins come under pressure.
