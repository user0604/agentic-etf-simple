What Simon Property Group does

Simon Property Group is a real estate investment trust (REIT) that owns, develops, and manages retail properties, including malls, premium outlets, and mixed-use centers, in the United States and abroad.

Notable developments
Rental revenue increased as total revenue rose 19.3%, and base minimum rent per square foot climbed over 5% from the prior-year period.
Net operating income from domestic properties advanced 6.7% year over year.
The board declared a second-quarter 2026 dividend of $2.25 per share, a 7.1% increase from last year's second-quarter payout.
Quarterly highlights

This period, retail leasing and occupancy reached some of their highest levels in years, with portfolio occupancy at 96.0% and average retailer sales per square foot up double digits compared to the previous year. Growth in Funds From Operations and core property earnings reflected strong retailer demand for the company's mall, outlet, and mixed-use properties.

The company expanded capital returns by raising its dividend and buying back $175 million of its own shares, supported by a balance sheet with over $8.7 billion in liquidity. Operational spending increased more than revenue growth, especially in property expenses and non-cash depreciation, which slightly pressured profit margins despite overall financial strength.

The company continued investing in redevelopment and property upgrades, emphasizing disciplined capital management. Performance from unconsolidated investments turned negative for the period, and management noted ongoing monitoring of tenant health due to several retailer bankruptcies and broader retail industry changes.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its 2026 Real Estate Funds From Operations guidance, now expecting $13.10-$13.25 per share for the year. The company signaled continued confidence by lifting the quarterly dividend and highlighting leasing momentum and retailer sales strength. Investors should watch for trends in expenses and results from non-core investment platforms, as these areas could affect profitability even as property fundamentals remain strong.
