SaaSpocalypse Update: What the Data Shows

11:15 am

When we published Meilin Quinn's "Who Survives Software’s Reckoning" on April 9, the market was in a full-blown "SaaSpocalypse." The core thesis? AI agents are dismantling traditional per-seat licensing. Software companies that fail to shift from charging per human head to charging "per outcome" (Agent-as-a-Service, or AaaS) are facing a structural crisis.

Two months later, the data shows Meilin’s framework wasn't just theoretical — the market is actively validating it.

By tracking the average performance of the stocks explicitly named in the article against the iShares Expanded Tech-Software Sector ETF (NYSEMKT: IGV) from April 9 through June 1, the power of identifying "Defensible" moats becomes undeniable:

Defensible Stocks: Averaged +61%, beating the IGV benchmark by a massive 24 percentage points.

Unclear Stocks: Averaged +31%, trailing the IGV by 6 percentage points.

Vulnerable Stocks: Averaged +38%, basically just matching the broader software market.

The Takeaway: This isn't just a general software bull market lifting all boats. Wall Street is aggressively rewarding the "Defensible" platforms that own proprietary enterprise data and critical network permissions. —  the exact infrastructure AI agents must route through to execute tasks. Meanwhile, vulnerable user-interface tools are getting left behind.

If you haven't read or revisited Meilin's piece yet, now is the time. Understanding who owns the data layer versus who just provides a human interface is proving to be the ultimate divider for tech portfolios.
