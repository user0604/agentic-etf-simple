1. HPE Fast-Forwards to 2028 Early

Hewlett Packard Enterprise (NYSE: HPE) just dropped a quarter that sent jaws to the floor, posting a 40% revenue jump to $10.68 billion and blowing past the $9.8 billion analysts had penciled in. CFO Marie Myers says enterprises are scrambling for server space to feed their shiny new agentic artificial intelligence (AI) tools -- the long-awaited adoption moment, finally clocking in. Investors approved: Shares popped 32% after hours.

Someone hit fast-forward: HPE's beefed-up outlook puts it on track to hit targets it didn't expect to reach until fiscal 2028 -- now arriving a full two years early. Full-year revenue growth guidance leaped to between 29% and 33%, up from a sleepier 17% to 22%, while adjusted earnings guidance climbed to between $3.35 and $3.45 from $2.30 to $2.50.
Networking went absolutely bananas: That segment rocketed 148% to $2.69 billion, with data center networking more than tripling and routing revenue exploding to $775 million from a rounding-error $1 million a year ago.
2. Micron's $1 Trillion Year Isn't Over Yet

Micron (NASDAQ: MU) just elbowed its way into the $1 trillion club, one of several companies to bust through in a single dizzy week. A year ago, this was a $105 billion chipmaker nobody had on their bingo card.

Thank the AI hyperscalers: Those data-center beasts are gobbling DRAM faster than Micron can ship it, and the resulting memory shortage has sent chip prices -- and Micron's revenue and earnings -- straight up the elevator shaft.
Good times may keep rolling: Management figures it can satisfy only one-half to two-thirds of demand for a while and sees its high-bandwidth memory market ballooning from $35 billion in 2025 to $100 billion by 2028. Shortage plus hunger is a tidy recipe for more upside.
3. Salesforce Rips 10% on Jensen Hype

Salesforce (NYSE: CRM), recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers, popped roughly 10% Monday after Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) chief Jensen Huang told a Computex crowd it's "an incredible time" to be a software company.

The doomers got their lunch money taken. All year, the story was "AI kills SaaS" (software-as-a-service). Huang flipped it into "AI feeds SaaS," and a sector that's been left for dead came roaring back on the spot.
Eye on the balance sheet: In March, Salesforce completed its largest-ever debt raise, issuing $25 billion in senior notes to fund an accelerated share repurchase program. "The aggressive capital allocation marks a strategic shift toward shareholder returns while the company navigates slower growth and elevated leverage concerns amid its AI transformation," wrote Team Rule Breakers analyst Jason Moser.
