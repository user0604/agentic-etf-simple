Key Takeaways
Puma Biotechnology delivered a mixed quarter, outperforming its prior guidance but reporting lower year-over-year revenue and earnings.
Non-GAAP EPS for the quarter was $(0.04), down from $0.10 a year ago.
Management raised its full-year revenue and net income forecasts as demand for NERLYNX improves and debt levels fall.

Puma Biotechnology (NASDAQ:PBYI) posted a quarter that topped its own expectations, but still showed a decline in revenue and profitability compared to last year. The cancer drug developer relies primarily on NERLYNX, its oral therapy for HER2-positive breast cancer. For the first quarter ended March 31, 2026 (results released May 7, 2026), the company lifted its full-year guidance for both revenue and profit based on improving underlying demand. Overall, results marked progress in cash flow and debt reduction but highlighted the continued pressure on growth and margins.

Notable Developments
Net product revenue for NERLYNX declined year over year, but exceeded the company’s Q1 forecast at $42.0 million versus guidance of $36–$39 million.
Research and development costs surged by 43.5%, primarily due to advancing alisertib clinical trials.
Puma Biotechnology reduced its total debt to zero after quarter-end and generated $15.4 million in operating cash flow, a substantial increase from a year ago.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised full-year revenue and net income guidance, now expecting total revenue of $222–$229 million and net income of $16–$19 million for fiscal 2026. The key focus for investors will be the progress and data readouts from the alisertib clinical trials, as these results are critical to diversifying Puma’s revenue and improving long-term sustainability.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure for the period declined from the year-ago quarter, as net product revenue from NERLYNX, Puma’s oral breast cancer therapy, slipped 2.6%. However, reported sales came in above management’s Q1 expectations, with year-over-year demand described as steady and inventory dynamics accounting for much of the difference.

Research and development spending increased sharply, tied to expanding clinical trial enrollment for alisertib, the company’s investigational oncology drug. Puma plans to provide additional data on alisertib’s effectiveness in hormone receptor-positive breast cancer and small cell lung cancer later in the year, which could shape its future product pipeline.

Operationally, while profit margins were pressured by the higher R&D spend, Puma eliminated all outstanding debt following quarter end and sharply improved its cash flow from operations. This bolstered the company’s financial flexibility heading into the rest of fiscal 2026.

What Puma Biotechnology Does

Puma Biotechnology develops and commercializes targeted cancer therapies, with its main marketed product being NERLYNX for HER2-positive breast cancer.
