Key Takeaways
Loss of a key office tenant weighed heavily on Clipper Realty's performance.
Revenue fell 3.3% year over year in the first quarter of 2026 as commercial property income declined.
Management is focused on stabilizing residential assets and resolving commercial challenges.

Clipper Realty (NYSE:CLPR) struggled this quarter as the loss of its largest office tenant significantly reduced commercial property income. The New York-focused real estate owner and operator continued to benefit from strong residential leasing and rent growth. The company released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026, showing residential strength offset by commercial setbacks and legal costs. Overall, the quarter reflected stable performance in residential buildings but raised questions about the company’s flexibility given weaker commercial results.

Notable developments
The commercial portfolio took a significant hit with the loss of the New York City lease at 250 Livingston Street, reducing commercial rental income 39% year over year to $6.2 million and resulting in the property entering receivership.
Residential rental income climbed 9.3% to $31.9 million, supported by record-high rents and nearly full occupancy, with the newly developed Prospect House reaching 100% leased by March 31, 2026.
A $3.6 million litigation settlement related to payroll practices weighed on profits, and operating cash flow halved year over year to $3.6 million.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management is focusing on maintaining strong residential occupancy and rent growth as the main driver of future stability. The biggest issue ahead is resolving the situation around 250 Livingston Street, where court-appointed receivership and a halted mortgage payment put this office asset at further risk. Investors should monitor whether cash flows recover enough to support the ongoing dividend, and if management can turn around its challenged commercial holdings.

Quarterly highlights

Commercial revenue dropped steeply due to the New York City lease ending at 250 Livingston Street, which produced only $0.4 million in quarterly revenue versus $4.6 million in the prior year and is now under a receiver’s control. This drove a loss from operations at the property and sharply reduced company-wide income from its office properties.

On the residential side, new leases and renewals continued to command above-prior rents, averaging 7% higher for new leases and over 5% higher for renewals. Prospect House, a new Brooklyn apartment building, moved from 78% to 100% leased in the quarter, pointing to robust demand even as rent growth decelerated from the previous year’s levels.

Legal and compliance costs resurfaced as a central theme: a $3.6 million charge for a likely payroll-related litigation settlement trimmed available capital. Despite declining cash flow, the company kept its quarterly dividend steady at $0.095 per share, a payout now exceeding quarterly cash generation as measured by AFFO.
