Key Takeaways
Business win: Earnings received a significant boost from investment gains, while core shipping revenues remained steady despite lower charter rates.
Performance: Adjusted EBITDA rose 5.2% year over year.

Danaos (NYSE:DAC) reported a mixed quarter, as investment gains propelled bottom-line growth even as core shipping revenues showed little improvement. The global shipping and vessel leasing specialist experienced steady top-line results, but faced persistent pressure on charter rates in its container segment. The results, announced on May 12, 2026, covered the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period showed strong cash generation and operational discipline, with growing exposure to market risks from major fleet expansion.

Notable developments
Net income rose 22.0% to $140.4 million, fueled by a one-time $23.5 million gain on investments and higher dividend income.
Operating revenues in the core container segment fell 2.8%, but were offset by a 40.9% rise in the smaller drybulk segment, which reported improved profitability.
Danaos declared a $0.90 per share dividend and repurchased 60,819 shares for $5.9 million under its ongoing buyback program.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management highlighted its strong liquidity position, with enough funding to cover both ongoing operations and a substantial orderbook for new ships. The expanding vessel commitments, mostly set for delivery between 2027 and 2029, underline the need for continued high fleet utilization and stable charter rates. Investors should monitor how effectively Danaos fills and deploys its new tonnage against an uncertain market backdrop, including changes brought by tariffs and geopolitical shifts.

Quarterly highlights

The company increased its average fleet size, with 75.0 container vessels and 10.1 drybulk vessels operated during the quarter. While container utilization ticked higher to nearly 98%, lower charter rates and non-cash accounting effects caused container segment revenue and per-day charter rates to both decline year over year.

Strong operating cash flows—up by $29.2 million—came from lower vessel expenses, higher investment and dividend income, and a $4.9 million positive change in working capital. Major investment gains, most notably from holdings in Star Bulk Carriers, accounted for the largest jump in net income this period.

On the cost side, average daily vessel operating costs dropped due to lower repairs and maintenance, signaling efficiency improvements. However, the positive voyage expense trend included a $4.9 million non-recurring gain and might not repeat, and capital spending increased as Danaos ramped up shipbuilding commitments.

What Danaos does

Danaos provides global seaborne transportation services, focusing on leasing container and drybulk vessels on long-term charter contracts to shipping operators.
