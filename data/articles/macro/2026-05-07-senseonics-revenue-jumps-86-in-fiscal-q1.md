Senseonics (NASDAQ:SENS) achieved commercial momentum for its long-duration glucose-monitoring technology, but rising costs led to a much steeper quarterly loss. The medical technology developer specializes in implantable continuous glucose monitoring (CGM) systems for diabetes management. Notable developments
Revenue nearly doubled as Senseonics took direct control of sales and launched its Eversense 365 CGM in Europe.
Operating costs surged, with selling, general, and administrative expenses more than tripling due to insourcing and new marketing campaigns.
The company raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to $60–64 million and continued expanding its product pipeline.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management is targeting $60–64 million in full-year revenue, citing increased adoption of its Eversense 365 system in the U.S. and Europe, but expects to continue investing heavily in marketing and product launches. Profitability remains out of reach in the near term due to these investments. Investors should watch for progress in controlling operating expenses and for additional updates on insurance reimbursement and uptake of new products.

Quarterly highlights

Senseonics’ revenue growth was driven by strong demand for its Eversense 365 CGM, which offers a one-year sensor life and saw its initial European rollout, with U.S. sales also increasing following the move to direct commercialization. U.S. revenue was $9.3 million for the first quarter of 2026 compared to $4.5 million for the first quarter of 2025, and DTC new patient adds were up nearly 100% year over year.

Operating losses grew sharply as the company insourced its sales force and ramped up marketing spending, increasing selling, general, and administrative expenses to $30.2 million from $7.7 million a year earlier. R&D costs also climbed as work continued on pipeline products like Gemini and Freedom, which aim to expand product features and integration.

Senseonics ended the quarter with $64.6 million in cash, down from $94.0 million at the prior year’s end, despite raising over $100 million in new capital during the period. The company plans to maintain high investment levels to drive growth but will need to carefully manage costs and cash as it seeks to reach profitability.

What Senseonics does

Senseonics develops and sells implantable continuous glucose monitoring systems for people with diabetes.
