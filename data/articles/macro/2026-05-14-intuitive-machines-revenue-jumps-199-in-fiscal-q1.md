Business overview

The company builds and operates spacecraft and space infrastructure, providing lunar and orbital transportation, data, and communications services to government and commercial clients. Its strategy focuses on transitioning from single-mission contracts to recurring infrastructure and service revenue, supported by recent acquisitions and a growing contract backlog.

Quarterly performance

Revenue nearly tripled year over year, driven by the Lanteris Space Systems acquisition and new contract awards. Product revenue, a new line item from Lanteris, contributed significantly to the top line, while service revenue declined compared to the prior year. Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, adjusted for nonrecurring items) turned positive, but the company posted a GAAP net loss of over $52 million due to integration and transaction costs.

Free cash flow was negative, reflecting cash outflows for acquisitions and increased expenses. The company ended the quarter with $231.6 million in cash, down sharply from year-end, as it paid for the Lanteris deal and absorbed higher operating costs. Backlog surged to over $1 billion, providing strong revenue visibility for the rest of fiscal 2026.

Contract wins remained a strength, with $428.9 million in new awards, including a $180.4 million NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services contract and government defense work. Management credited these new projects with supporting future growth and underpinning the company’s shift from mission-based activities to longer-term infrastructure contracts.

Looking ahead

Management reaffirmed its full-year revenue guidance of $900 million to $1 billion for fiscal 2026, with positive adjusted EBITDA targeted for the year. About two-thirds of this revenue is already supported by contracted backlog. Investors should watch how well the company converts its record backlog into profitable, recurring revenues while controlling integration costs and cash burn.
