Key Takeaways
Orla Mining delivered a strong quarter with sharply higher profitability.
Revenue rose year over year.

Gold miner Orla Mining (NYSEMKT:ORLA) posted a strong quarterly improvement as its integration of the Musselwhite Mine lifted profitability and scale. The Canada and Mexico-focused company saw gold output and sales jump following its recent acquisition, supporting a major turnaround compared to the prior-year period. The company issued its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026, indicating robust operating performance and steady progress on key expansion projects.

Overall, the quarter showed meaningful growth in revenue and profits but comes as Orla enters a period of increased capital commitments and execution risk.

Notable Developments
Revenue more than doubled year over year, reaching $378.9 million as the Musselwhite Mine drove higher gold output and sales.
Net income swung to a profit of $75.4 million compared to a loss last year, reflecting both higher gold prices and the impact of the Musselwhite integration.
Outstanding purchase orders and project commitments grew to $105.7 million, highlighting an acceleration in growth spending.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not update its full-year production or cost guidance in this quarter's report but reaffirmed ongoing investment plans. The most important focus for investors is Orla's ability to successfully deliver major projects on time and within budget amid increased capital spending. Execution at South Railroad and steady performance at existing assets will be key for the remainder of fiscal 2026.

Quarterly Highlights

The full-quarter impact of Musselwhite Mine, now Orla’s largest source of earnings, was clear as segment profit reached $229.3 million, making up the bulk of company profit. Musselwhite’s contribution transformed the company’s scale and profitability profile versus the prior year, when it was only partially owned.

Camino Rojo, the company’s open-pit gold mine in Mexico, delivered lower segment profit despite steady operating costs. While gold returns remained strong, the segment underperformed compared to the same period last year.

Development activity at South Railroad in Nevada moved into a higher gear, with outstanding commitments surging, indicating that project construction and procurement are ramping up. Exploration spending stayed disciplined, and cash reserves remained strong as of March 31, 2026, supporting upcoming capital needs.
