Earnings Preview: Cytosorbents

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 5:03 PM

Comments

Cytosorbents (NASDAQ:CTSO), a company that makes blood purification cartridges for use in critical care and cardiac surgery, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings on Wednesday, May 13, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

For the fourth quarter of 2025, Cytosorbents reported revenue of $9.2 million, which represented a 1% increase year over year but was down 8% on a constant currency basis. Gross margin improved to 74% in the quarter, reflecting better manufacturing efficiencies. The company reported an operating loss of $4.6 million and an adjusted net loss of $4.3 million, or $0.07 per share. These results missed analyst expectations for both earnings per share and sales. International sales outside Germany continued to show strength, but German sales declined due to ongoing restructuring. The company also took a $0.5 million restructuring charge related to workforce reductions and other cost-saving initiatives. Management highlighted early signs of improvement in Germany and continued progress in product innovation and regulatory efforts.

Positives
Gross margin improvement to 74%: Manufacturing efficiencies helped the company reach its highest quarterly gross margin in recent years, which supports future profitability if sales volumes increase.
International sales growth outside Germany: Direct sales outside Germany grew 13% and distributor sales increased 11.4% for the full year, showing that the company is diversifying its revenue base beyond its largest market.
Operational execution on cost controls: The company implemented a workforce and cost reduction plan, including a 10% reduction in workforce, which is expected to reduce cash burn and help achieve cash flow breakeven in the second half of 2026.
Product innovation and clinical adoption: The launch of the PuriFi hemoperfusion pump and HotSwap cartridge exchange system has received positive feedback from clinicians, which may drive future adoption and disposable usage.
Negatives
German market sales decline: Revenue from Germany fell 10% year over year, reflecting the near-term impact of restructuring and highlighting ongoing challenges in the company’s largest market.
Missed analyst estimates for revenue and EPS: Both top-line and bottom-line results for Q4 were below consensus, which may weigh on investor sentiment in the near term.
Restructuring costs impacted operating results: The $0.5 million restructuring charge in Q4, mostly for severance, increased operating expenses and contributed to the quarter’s higher loss.
Limited cash position: Cash and equivalents stood at $7.8 million at year end, which may not be sufficient to fund operations beyond the next twelve months without additional financing or improved cash flow.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Progress in German sales turnaround: Investors should look for signs that the restructuring and new commercial strategy in Germany are translating into improved sales or pipeline activity, as management has indicated early positive momentum.
Updates on DrugSorb-ATR FDA resubmission: The company is still in discussions with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) about the requirements for a new De Novo application for its DrugSorb-ATR device. Any update on timing or progress will be important for assessing future U.S. market potential.
Cash burn and liquidity management: Watch for details on operating cash burn, cost controls, and whether the company is on track for its goal of cash flow breakeven in the second half of 2026. The adequacy of the current cash position and any new financing activity will also be key.
Adoption and feedback on new product launches: Monitor for early sales or usage data for the PuriFi pump and HotSwap system, as well as any feedback from clinicians that could signal broader adoption or future revenue growth.
Performance versus analyst expectations for Q1 2026: Consensus estimates call for sales of $9.4 million and an EPS loss of $0.05. Results relative to these benchmarks will shape market reaction and set the tone for the rest of the year.
