1. Palo Alto Pops 10% as AI Panic Pays Off

Rule Breakers rec Palo Alto Networks (NASDAQ: PANW) is essentially selling umbrellas in a thunderstorm. Shares popped as much as 12% after hours (before giving back most of those gains) on a fiscal third-quarter beat -- adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $0.85 against $0.80 expected, revenue of $3.0 billion. Management raised full-year guidance.

The monster pays the monster hunter: The very artificial intelligence (AI) models keeping digital security teams up at night are exactly why customers keep signing checks. CEO Nikesh Arora is happy to credit the chaos. Palo Alto is even an early Project Glasswing tester, poking at Anthropic's Mythos model to see how scary it really is.
Shopping spree, agentic edition: As Motley Fool analyst David Meier put it, Arora "now has 20 acquisitions under his belt" and is "full speed ahead" on building a one-stop shop for cybersecurity.
2. Ulta's Quarter Needs No Filter

Ulta Beauty (NASDAQ: ULTA), a rec in Stock Advisor (Team HG), showed up flawless to its fiscal first quarter. Earnings of $7.74 a share blew past the $6.89 Wall Street was hoping for, and revenue rose 11% to $3.16 billion. CEO Kecia Steelman says the strategy is working "in an uncertain economic landscape." Shares were up 5% after hours.

The register keeps ringing: Same-store sales jumped 5.3%, blowing past the Street's 4.7% guess. Shoppers visited more often and spent more per visit. "Ulta's loyalty flywheel and consistent execution make me wonder if this is one of those categories that looks commoditized on the surface but quietly compounds for years," TMFPrestonFreeman wrote earlier this year.
Then management raised the bar: Full-year EPS guidance ticked up to $28.36 to $28.80, from $28.05 to $28.55. The sales and comp targets stayed put.
3. Solomon Says Fear Took the Day Off

Goldman Sachs (NYSE: GS) CEO David Solomon checked Wall Street's mood ring on CNBC and declared the vibe is "greed," not fear -- and he's perfectly fine with that. With OpenAI, Anthropic, and SpaceX all circling public debuts at trillion-dollar valuations, Solomon figures there's more than enough cash sloshing around to soak up the deluge of offerings headed our way.

His receipt: Alphabet's (NASDAQ: GOOG) stock barely flinched after the company floated an $80 billion equity raise -- the first real proof, Solomon says, that markets will happily swallow issuance this enormous. His memo to cash-hungry AI firms is refreshingly blunt: When the money's on the table, grab it.
The asterisk: Solomon admits that greed can flip to fear in a heartbeat. He's betting it won't anytime soon, which is exactly what you'd expect the banker booking these deals to say. His read is that we're still early in the party.
