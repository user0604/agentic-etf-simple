Nvidia (NASDAQ:NVDA), a leading supplier of chips and systems for artificial intelligence and high-performance computing, is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2027 earnings after the market closes on Wednesday, May 20, 2026.

Q4 FY 2026 Recap

The fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 was another record-setting period for Nvidia. The company reported total revenue of $68 billion, up 73% from the prior year, with data center revenue reaching $62 billion, a 75% increase year over year. This growth was primarily driven by strong demand for Nvidia’s Blackwell architecture in data centers, including hyperscalers, AI model makers, and enterprises.

Networking revenue also surged, reflecting robust adoption of Nvidia’s NVLink and Spectrum-X Ethernet products. Gross margin for the quarter was 75% on a non-GAAP basis. The company generated $35 billion in free cash flow in Q4 and returned $41 billion to shareholders over the full fiscal year through share repurchases and dividends. These results beat analyst expectations for both revenue and earnings per share.

Positives
Record data center growth: Data center revenue reached $62 billion, reflecting continued demand for AI infrastructure and accelerated computing across a broader customer base.
Networking business expansion: Networking revenue hit $11 billion, up more than 3.5 times year over year, driven by strong adoption of NVLink and Spectrum-X Ethernet products.
Strong free cash flow and capital returns: The company generated $35 billion in free cash flow for the quarter and returned $41 billion to shareholders for the year, showing strong financial discipline and shareholder focus.
Broadening customer diversity: Growth is no longer concentrated among only the largest cloud providers, with enterprises, sovereigns, and AI model makers now making up a significant share of demand.
Negatives
Gross margin decline year over year: Gross margin decreased to 71.1% for the full year, down from 75% in the prior year, mainly due to the transition to Blackwell systems and a $4.5 billion charge related to H20 inventory for China.
Ongoing supply constraints in gaming: Gaming revenue grew 47% year over year, but management noted that supply constraints are expected to be a headwind in the coming quarters.
Uncertainty in China market: U.S. government licensing requirements and tariffs have limited Nvidia’s ability to ship certain products to China, resulting in minimal revenue from H200 products in that region.
What to Watch in Q1 FY 2027's Report
Data center revenue trajectory and product mix: Watch for continued sequential growth in data center revenue, especially as Blackwell and the new Rubin platforms ramp up. Analyst consensus expects sales of about $78.9 billion for the quarter.
Gross margin stability: Management guided for gross margins in the mid-70s percentage range. Investors should monitor if margins remain stable as new products ramp and supply chain costs evolve.
Progress on Vera Rubin platform launch: The company began shipping Vera Rubin samples to customers in Q4 and expects production shipments to start in the second half of the year. Updates on customer adoption and initial revenue contributions will be important.
Gaming and supply chain constraints: Management has flagged ongoing supply constraints in gaming. Any update on supply improvements or further headwinds could affect the gaming segment’s growth outlook.
Partnerships and ecosystem investments: Nvidia announced or completed several major partnerships and investments, including a pending agreement with OpenAI and a completed $10 billion investment in Anthropic. Progress on these initiatives and their impact on the broader AI ecosystem are key areas to watch.
