Exploration-stage mining company New Pacific Metals (NYSEMKT:NEWP) delivered a stable, if unspectacular, quarter as project investments increased only gently while net losses remained almost unchanged. The company explores for silver, gold, and related metals in Bolivia, a country where regulatory and permitting hurdles can be significant for resource developers. In its fiscal third-quarter update, released May 15, 2026 (for the period ended March 31), New Pacific showed solid liquidity and inched forward on community engagement but saw no major operational or permitting breakthroughs.

Overall, the quarter highlighted disciplined expense control and cash strength, but little technical advancement or revenue progress.

Quarterly Highlights

Operating expenses rose year over year, reflecting increased work at the Silver Sand flagship project, but were offset by a notable gain in investment income. The company saw net loss remain almost unchanged from a year ago, underscoring careful cost management amidst the ongoing lack of revenue as New Pacific remains pre-production.

New Pacific bolstered its financial position, reporting $39.28 million in working capital at quarter end, enough to cover near-term exploration and corporate costs without immediate need for new funding. This strong liquidity is key in a sector notorious for swings in commodity prices and regulatory timelines.

Community relations advanced with the signing of a Framework Agreement for the Carangas project’s development, a requirement for earning social license in Bolivia. However, there was no disclosure of new permits or technical achievements, and overall progress remained incremental.

What New Pacific Metals Does
