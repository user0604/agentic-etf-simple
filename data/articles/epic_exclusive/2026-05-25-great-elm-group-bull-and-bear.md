Great Elm Group Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 11:05 AM

Comments

Great Elm Group, Inc. (NASDAQ:GEG) is a diversified alternative asset manager pursuing growth through credit, real estate, and specialty investments. The company’s strategy emphasizes active capital rotation, fee-based asset management, and disciplined risk controls, with platforms spanning private credit, business development company (BDC) sponsorship, and the expanding Monomoy real estate and construction services businesses. Management is focusing on strengthening portfolio resilience, scaling recurring fee income, and deploying capital opportunistically to build shareholder value amid market volatility.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Portfolio Quality Upgrades and Deleveraging Enhance Stability and Future Returns

Deeper allocation to senior secured credit, together with substantial deleveraging, positions the company for greater resilience and improved risk-adjusted returns. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, the company’s BDC affiliate, Great Elm Capital Corp. (GECC), increased its exposure to first lien, senior secured loans to nearly 75% of its corporate credit portfolio—the highest level in recent history. This shift, alongside a decisive reduction in corporate debt (eliminating all maturities until 2029), directly lowers risk and refinancing pressures during sector volatility. By strengthening risk management and freeing capital for strategic deployment, the company sets a firmer foundation for durable earnings growth and NAV (Net Asset Value) protection going forward.

2. Real Estate Expansion Drives Recurring Fees and Platform Scalability

Growth in the Monomoy real estate platform boosts fee income and operational diversification, supporting multi-year earnings potential. Monomoy Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) and its affiliated construction management businesses delivered robust results in the latest quarter, closing five real estate acquisitions totaling $28 million and recording over 20% year-over-year growth in management fees. Notably, Monomoy’s build-to-suit pipeline advanced with a third completed Florida development and new activity in Texas, while Monomoy Construction Services added $0.7 million of revenue in its fourth full quarter. This momentum points to scalable, recurring fee streams and lower correlations with credit markets—strengthening the company's long-term growth platform.

3. Demonstrated Capital Discipline Through Accretive Share Buybacks

Expanded and ongoing share repurchases at levels management believes are undervalued can provide tangible support for shareholder returns even during volatile periods. The Board authorized a $15 million increase in its share buyback program in Q3 2026, totaling $40 million since inception. Over 4% of shares outstanding were repurchased in the latest quarter at an average price of $2.04, with 7.8 million shares cumulatively bought back at an average price of $2.00. With more than $24 million of buyback capacity remaining and management purchasing aggressively at discounts to intrinsic value, the program has the potential to drive accretion and reinforce market confidence through disciplined capital return.

4. Successful Specialty Investments Such as CoreWeave Provide Flexible Upside

Profitable stakes in external ventures offer asymmetric upside and capital recycling benefits. The company’s minority investment in CoreWeave, a rapidly growing cloud infrastructure firm, has already generated cumulative distributions of $6.8 million on a $5 million initial investment, with management expecting further upside due to favorable valuations and capital raises. These specialty investments diversify returns away from core credit exposures and give the company the means to recycle capital opportunistically into new, higher-yielding opportunities, thereby enhancing long-term value creation potential outside their core fee businesses.

5. Robust Liquidity Enables Opportunistic Investment and Resilience in Volatile Markets

A strong cash position and flexible balance sheet empower the company to navigate market turbulence and capture future growth opportunities. As of March 31, 2026, the company reported approximately $45.5 million in cash and equivalents, representing a substantial buffer relative to operating needs and scale. This liquidity affords management the ability to support growth initiatives in alternative credit and real estate, take advantage of market dislocations, and cushion against sector downturns. Well-capitalized firms are best positioned to emerge stronger from industry cycles, underlining the strategic value of the company's current liquidity in weathering adversity and seizing attractive opportunities.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Unrealized Losses from GECC Holdings Threaten Profitability and Net Asset Value

Ongoing mark-to-market volatility in GECC continues to drive deep unrealized losses, undermining earnings and NAV stability. In fiscal Q3 2026, the company recorded $9.8 million in non-cash unrealized losses, primarily related to its holdings in GECC stock and related special purpose vehicles. This resulted in a widened GAAP net loss of $13.5 million and negative adjusted EBITDA of $1.6 million for the quarter, both deteriorating from the prior year. If volatility in the underlying BDC persists or worsens, it could impede the company’s ability to protect NAV and delay a return to sustainable profitability, undermining investor confidence and the broader bull narrative.

2. Declining Fee-Paying AUM Signals Weakness in Core Credit and Asset Management Platform

Reductions in assets under management erode the recurring fee base and earnings power of the flagship asset management business. As of March 31, 2026, fee-paying assets under management had dropped 7% year over year to $528 million, while total AUM fell 3% to $744 million. Given that recurring management fee revenue is a central pillar of the company's business model, persistent AUM outflows signal both competitive and cyclical pressures. If management is unable to stabilize and regrow the asset base—whether due to investor redemptions, market outflows, or underperformance in private credit strategies—longer-term earnings and valuation multiples could be structurally impaired.

3. Near-Term Operating Losses Highlight Structural Profitability Challenges

Negative net income and adjusted EBITDA suggest cost structures and revenue growth are not yet aligned, raising questions about the speed and viability of a turnaround. In the recently reported quarter, despite a 7% year-over-year revenue increase to $3.4 million, the company posted a net loss of $13.5 million (GAAP) and negative adjusted EBITDA of $1.6 million—both markedly weaker than the same period last year. The discrepancy is largely driven by unrealized losses but also reflects ongoing sector pressures and operating cost drag. If revenue growth from new platforms like Monomoy is insufficient to cover losses in private credit, then the business may face a prolonged period of unprofitability, limiting its ability to reinvest, return capital, or weather further headwinds.
