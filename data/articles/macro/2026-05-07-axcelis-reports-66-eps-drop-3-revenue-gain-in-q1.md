Key Takeaways
Sales grew in the memory business and aftermarket, but profits declined as costs rose.
Earnings per share fell 65.9% from the prior year while revenue increased 3.3%.
Management guided for flat annual revenue but expects sequential growth in the next quarter.

Equipment maker Axcelis Technologies (NASDAQ:ACLS) combined stronger sales in its memory and aftermarket businesses with weaker overall profit for the period. The company is a key supplier of ion implantation equipment and services to the semiconductor industry. In its earnings release dated May 7, 2026, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, management delivered results slightly ahead of internal targets, but profit margins remained under pressure due to rising expenses and costs related to its planned merger with Veeco.

Overall, the quarter saw healthy sales in strategic areas but notable declines in profitability compared with the same period a year ago.

Notable Developments
Product sales rose just under 3% from a year earlier, while aftermarket and service (Customer Solutions & Innovation) revenue increased 12%, marking a strong performance in support and upgrade offerings.
Profitability declined as gross margin fell nearly six percentage points from last year, with operating costs up 22%, driven in part by merger expenses of $10.4 million.
The company maintained a strong liquidity position with $570 million in cash and investments, and positive free cash flow of $18 million for the period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Executives restated their expectation for full-year 2026 revenue to be roughly flat, but forecast a sequential improvement in both revenue and profit for the second quarter. The company's ability to control costs and achieve margin recovery, especially as the Veeco merger moves forward, is the key area for investors to monitor next.

Quarterly Highlights

Sales in the memory segment, especially for advanced DRAM (dynamic random access memory) and HBM (high-bandwidth memory) chips, drove business gains and boosted demand for ion implantation systems. Power and general mature product segments remained weak, continuing a trend of lower capital investment in these areas.

The aftermarket business, delivered through Customer Solutions & Innovation, expanded 14% year over year in 2025 as customers invested in upgrades, services, and parts. Management emphasized this business line as a strategic source of stability amid more volatile tool sales.

Profit margins shrank across the company, reflecting merger-related integration costs and higher spending on research and development as well as sales and marketing. Management continues to cite investments in innovation as essential to maintaining long-term competitiveness.

What Axcelis Technologies Does

Axcelis provides systems and services for ion implantation, a vital process used in semiconductor chip manufacturing.
