Construction Partners Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 7:08 AM

Comments

Construction Partners, Inc. (NASDAQ:ROAD) is a major player in road construction and infrastructure services, with a growth strategy rooted in consolidating fragmented local markets in the fast-growing Sunbelt region. The company combines organic expansion—like launching new facilities and deepening vertical integration—with disciplined acquisitions of regional operators. By focusing on essential infrastructure and leveraging a resilient backlog, Construction Partners pursues ambitious financial targets, aiming to double in size as outlined in its ROAD 2030 plan.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Robust Public Infrastructure Funding Ensures Multi-Year Growth Tailwinds

Federal and state funding are set to accelerate demand for core services, providing visibility and durability to growth. The company stands to benefit directly from the bipartisan momentum behind new federal surface transportation reauthorization in the $500–$600 billion range—an act that, even when conservatively modeled, would mean multiple years of elevated public spending. As of March 31, 2026, Construction Partners’ backlog reached a record $3.14 billion, covering 80–85% of projected revenue for the next twelve months. This high level of coverage, dominated by recurring state and municipal maintenance projects, minimizes exposure to funding volatility. Ongoing underinvestment in U.S. roads, paired with the momentum from the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, creates a long runway for demand, especially as state-level investment also remains robust in Sunbelt markets. This public spending ballast helps anchor the company’s longer-term revenue outlook, giving management the confidence to raise FY26 guidance and reaffirm ROAD 2030 targets.

2. Vertical Integration Drives Margin Expansion and Shields Against Commodity Volatility

Internal sourcing of key materials and cost pass-through models protect margins and enhance predictable profitability. Construction Partners sources over 50% of its liquid asphalt internally, up significantly from prior periods, and management targets higher internalization in the years ahead. This approach, along with having over 80% of liquid asphalt costs tied to indexed or pass-through contracts, limits direct exposure to price spikes in commodities like oil and diesel. Vertical integration is a strategic lever not only for defending gross and EBITDA margins—12.9% and 12.1%, respectively, in Q2 FY26—but also for driving margin improvement as the company scales. Margin resilience was evident in the second quarter of fiscal 2026, with adjusted EBITDA up 35% year over year and steady progress highlighted toward the long-term goal of 17% EBITDA margins by 2030. Such operational discipline also supports integration of acquisitions and helps cushion potential shocks from input cost inflation.

3. Disciplined Acquisition Strategy Unlocks Value in Fragmented Sunbelt Markets

Acquiring and integrating local leaders accelerates revenue growth and establishes durable local moats. The company’s playbook combines greenfield expansion with disciplined acquisition, targeting high-quality operators in rapidly growing, fragmented Sunbelt regions. In fiscal 2026 alone, Construction Partners closed four acquisitions, including Four Star Paving, marking the seventeenth since the start of fiscal 2024. Within the second quarter, acquisitions contributed 24% to revenue growth, with organic growth at 11%. Importantly, acquired businesses are fully integrated culturally and operationally, maintaining or improving profitability in line with company targets. This pace and discipline position the company to capture generational turnover among local competitors while preserving local relationships—an essential moat given logistics constraints. The robust pipeline of deals further supports the scalability of this strategy, providing ongoing upside to both revenue and margin targets.

4. Essential Infrastructure Focus Makes Business Resilient to Cyclical Downturns and Technological Disruption

Critical infrastructure work underpins consistent demand and provides defense against economic and technological shocks. Road construction and maintenance are fundamental needs, insulated from rapid obsolescence by technological or regulatory shifts. As management notes, even as automation and artificial intelligence advance, these tools enhance internal processes but cannot replace the hands-on nature of paving and infrastructure work. This structural defensibility—combined with end-market diversification across public, commercial, and industrial customers—supports relatively stable project flow and minimization of risk tied to individual sectors. During Q2 2026, demand from both data center construction (part of the broader reindustrialization trend) and steady governmental work illustrated the company’s ability to capture cyclical upside without increasing its risk profile. This resilience is a cornerstone for long-term investors seeking predictable exposure to ‘hard asset’ infrastructure spending.

5. Strong Execution and Transparent Guidance De-Risk Long-Term Target Achievement

Consistently meeting raised guidance and transparent backlog disclosures demonstrate management credibility and support the ROAD 2030 vision. Throughout 2026, Construction Partners has repeatedly delivered on and exceeded internal guidance, underscored by a 35% increase in second-quarter revenue (to $769.2 million) and improvements in both gross profit and adjusted EBITDA. Management’s willingness to raise guidance following outperformance, paired with detailed disclosures around backlog and organic/acquisitive revenue splits, enhances investor confidence. The company’s ROAD 2030 targets—over $6 billion in revenue, $1 billion in adjusted EBITDA, and 17% EBITDA margins—are not predicated on outsized future public funding increases, but on the consistent execution of a proven growth and integration model. This reliability is evidenced by rising return on equity, robust cash generation (with 75–85% EBITDA to operating cash flow conversion targeted), and ongoing deleveraging, aligning management incentives with long-term shareholder value creation.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Elevated Valuation Heightens Risk if Growth or Margins Underwhelm Expectations

Premium earnings multiples amplify downside exposure if operational momentum stalls. Construction Partners trades at a steep valuation—recently near 50 times earnings—reflecting high investor expectations for continued double-digit revenue growth, margin expansion, and disciplined capital allocation. If organic growth slows below the projected 7–8% for fiscal 2026, acquisition integration falters, or EBITDA margins plateau short of the 17% ROAD 2030 target, the stock could experience sharp multiple contraction. Such a scenario might play out if public infrastructure funding levels disappoint, input cost inflation erodes incremental margins despite internal sourcing, or if competitor activity in the Sunbelt intensifies. Simply put, the bar for operational excellence is set high, and any sustained shortfall in results could trigger a reassessment of the appropriate premium for this business model.

2. Acquisition Integration Risks and Goodwill Accumulation Could Threaten Discipline

Active M&A introduces execution risks, especially as the pace of acquisitions accelerates. The company’s expansion plan relies heavily on adding new businesses—seventeen acquisitions since the start of fiscal 2024—including four in fiscal 2026. Even with stable integration practices, the risk of overpaying for local operators, accumulating excessive goodwill, or facing unexpected cultural and operational misalignments rises as deal activity increases. While management emphasizes focus on employee retention and local relationships, successful integration cannot be guaranteed in perpetuity. If acquired units underperform or disrupt the local culture and customer base, the company could face unforeseen costs, distraction, or eventual write-downs that detract from both growth and profitability targets. These risks become more pronounced if generational turnover accelerates and raises acquisition multiples in the busier Sunbelt regions.

3. Leverage Remains Above Target and Could Limit Strategic Flexibility in Downturns

Although improving, the company’s leverage ratio is still above its stated long-term goal, potentially restricting responses to new risks. At March 31, 2026, debt-to-EBITDA stood at 3.23 times, with a target to reduce this metric to 2.5 times. While management emphasizes that recent acquisitions will be funded from operating cash flow, an economic downturn or a period of weaker cash generation could delay deleveraging efforts. This would, in turn, limit the company’s ability to fund further acquisitions or respond nimbly to attractive opportunities, especially if credit conditions tighten. If margin expansion slows or working capital demands rise during a less favorable macro environment, higher-than-planned leverage could amplify financial risk and reduce capacity for long-term investment—potentially compromising execution on key growth initiatives and ROAD 2030 objectives.
