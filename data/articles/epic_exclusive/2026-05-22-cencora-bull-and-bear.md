Cencora Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 22, 2026, 11:08 AM

Comments

Cencora, Inc. (NYSE:COR) is one of the world’s largest pharmaceutical distribution and healthcare solutions companies, serving as the critical middle layer connecting drug manufacturers, healthcare providers, and pharmacies. Its growth strategy is centered on expanding its specialty pharmaceutical management and logistics footprint, investing in digital and physical infrastructure, and sharpening its portfolio focus through acquisitions, divestitures, and capital deployment—aiming to deliver consistent earnings growth and market leadership in a changing healthcare landscape.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Specialty and Management Services Expansion Accelerates Margin Growth

Shifting focus to high-margin management services organizations (MSOs) and specialty logistics is driving structural profitability improvements. The company’s acquisition of OneOncology in February 2026 materially increased its exposure to management services for oncology practices, which are inherently higher margin than traditional drug distribution. For the quarter ended March 2026, consolidated gross profit rose by 16% year over year to $3.4 billion, and gross margin expanded by 45 basis points to 4.31%. This margin uplift was principally attributed to the recent MSO acquisition and rebounding specialty logistics. In the long term, this transition positions Cencora for more resilient, scalable profitability as specialty therapy demand (e.g, cell and gene therapy) continues to climb, supporting management’s 7%–10% annual operating income growth target.

2. Robust Free Cash Flow Enables Aggressive Capital Returns and M&A

Sustained free cash flow generation gives Cencora meaningful flexibility to offset volatility and pursue growth. In the March 2026 quarter, the company generated $1.1 billion in free cash flow and ended the period with $2.2 billion in cash, maintaining guidance for approximately $3 billion in full-year free cash flow. This robust liquidity underpins a balanced approach to capital deployment, including $1 billion in authorized share repurchases by year-end 2026 and steady dividends (annualized yield of 0.9%). Consistent cash flow not only supports opportunistic buybacks—which directly increase adjusted earnings per share—but also enables bolt-on acquisitions in strategic segments (such as additional MSOs), strengthening long-term competitive positioning even in uneven revenue environments.

3. Digital Transformation and AI Investments Drive Operational Efficiencies

Digital and automation initiatives are improving efficiency, customer satisfaction, and working capital management. Cencora continues to invest in AI-enabled customer support tools and highly automated fulfillment systems, resulting in tangible gains in service quality and cost management. Management highlighted recent launches of AI-supported operations improving consistency and accelerating issue resolution for customers. These investments are expected to drive ongoing operational leverage, enhancing the company’s ability to deliver differentiated service to both manufacturers and providers. As supply chain complexity increases—especially for advanced specialties—these technology advantages could widen the gap versus competitors and compound the benefits of scale and process automation, supporting future earnings growth.

4. Strategic Portfolio Optimization Sharpens Focus on Core Pharmaceutical Strengths

Active divestiture of non-core businesses and targeted acquisitions are concentrating resources on profitable, scalable opportunities. Recent moves—such as the sale of U.S. hub consulting services and the merger agreement for MWI Animal Health with Covetrus—underscore a disciplined approach to portfolio management. Management’s decision to classify MWI as an asset held for sale suspended related depreciation expenses, boosting forecasted operating income. By focusing resources and management attention on core pharmaceutical and specialty solutions—including MSOs and digital infrastructure—Cencora is aligning its asset base with future industry tailwinds, potentially accelerating both margin expansion and capital returns. This disciplined strategy provides resilience against sector headwinds and enables more tactical responses to changing market dynamics.

5. Resilient Earnings Growth Framework Inspires Confidence Despite Revenue Headwinds

Ability to preserve and grow operating income through mix improvements and capital deployment underpins robust EPS projections. Although revenue guidance for full-year 2026 was trimmed to 4%–6% growth amid slower GLP-1 sales and rapid biosimilar substitution, consolidated operating income growth guidance was raised to 12%–14%. Cencora is retaining—or even growing—margin dollars as low-margin revenue rolls off and as capital deployment (including share buybacks) boosts per-share metrics. The company reaffirmed its 10%–14% long-term annual EPS growth target, combining consistent organic profit growth with incremental upside from ongoing capital returns. This consistent achievement of (and increase in) adjusted EPS targets, even in the face of industry headwinds, reinforces the credibility of management’s financial framework and its commitment to shareholder value creation.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Accelerated Customer Losses and Biosimilar Conversions Pressure Revenue Sustainability

Rapid shifts in customer relationships and generic substitution threaten top-line stability and volume scale. In fiscal 2025/2026, the company absorbed the loss of major oncology and grocery customers, while also facing an unexpectedly fast pace of brand-to-generic and biosimilar conversions for a large mail order pharmacy client. These developments produced meaningful revenue headwinds—$2 billion in lost sales due to the Inflation Reduction Act and rapid generic shift—cutting revenue growth to just 3.8% in Q2 2026. While margin impact has thus far been muted due to these being lower-margin revenues, ongoing or additional customer attrition, or faster-than-forecasted payer and product mix changes, could undermine scale advantages and challenge the long-term operating income growth model if not fully offset.

2. Industry and Policy Headwinds Risk Margin Compression and Guidance Shortfalls

Healthcare policy changes and payer dynamics are increasing earnings volatility and exposing guidance to downside surprises. The impact of the Inflation Reduction Act—specifically mandated wholesale acquisition cost (WAC) reductions—resulted in a $2 billion quarterly revenue headwind. The company narrowly missed Q2 consensus earnings per share (EPS) expectations, with reported adjusted EPS of $4.75 versus a $4.80 consensus, and net margins contracted to 0.50%. As biosimilar/generic penetration accelerates in mail order channels (notably Medicare Part D), large revenue volumes may shift away from the wholesaler, reducing scale without a proportional reduction in fixed costs. If further policy directives intensify or volume degradation outpaces margin recovery, earnings could fall short of ambitious guidance in coming periods, eroding investor confidence.

3. Legal and Headline Risks Heighten Volatility and Distract Management

Recent share price declines and pending securities law investigations introduce litigation overhang and raise execution concerns. Following the Q2 2026 earnings release, shares dropped roughly 15%, hitting a new 52-week low after the company missed EPS targets and trimmed revenue guidance. Multiple law firms swiftly launched investigations into potential securities violations, adding legal and headline risk to the investment case. While there is no evidence of direct operational consequence yet, such developments can distract management, increase costs, and unsettle employees and customers, especially as the company navigates integration and ongoing strategic realignment. Persistent legal scrutiny or shareholder litigation may also weigh on sentiment, potentially limiting future capital deployment flexibility or eroding market perceptions of stability.
