Key Takeaways
Vishay Intertechnology beat its own fiscal first-quarter 2026 revenue and margin targets as orders and backlog grew.

Vishay Intertechnology (NYSE:VSH) delivered a business win in the fiscal first quarter of 2026, as robust customer demand and a focus on higher-margin products drove results above its own guidance. The global manufacturer of discrete semiconductors and passive electronic components continued to advance its Vishay 3.0 transformation strategy. The results, released on May 13, 2026, showed broad progress, though rising costs and higher inventory levels remain areas to monitor. Overall, the quarter demonstrated notable improvements in revenue, profitability, and future sales visibility.

Notable developments

Order momentum remained strong, with the overall book-to-bill ratio rising to 1.34 and backlog expanding to 5.7 months.

Gross and operating margins each improved by about two percentage points, reflecting efficiency gains and a shift toward higher-margin products.

Free cash flow stayed negative at a $46.9 million outflow, mainly due to elevated capital spending for capacity expansion.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management projects fiscal second-quarter 2026 revenue between $875 million and $905 million, with gross margin expected to improve further to 22.0% plus or minus half a percentage point. The company aims to accelerate backlog conversion and continue margin growth. Investors should monitor capital spending, productivity from new capacity, and whether inventory growth slows as sales increase.

Quarterly highlights

The period saw significant order wins, with the book-to-bill ratio for discrete semiconductors at 1.47 and for passive components at 1.23, indicating strong demand across both product lines. The expanded backlog, up from 4.9 to 5.7 months, provides high visibility for future revenue.

Margins improved as the company prioritized high-growth, high-margin products. Operating margin rose to 2.6%, supported by investments in manufacturing capacity and greater efficiency, though higher selling and administrative expenses offset some gains.

Cash flow turned negative due to capital expenditures of $110.7 million, part of the peak phase of the company’s five-year expansion plan. Inventory levels increased, reflecting preparation for further growth but also signaling a risk if demand slows.
