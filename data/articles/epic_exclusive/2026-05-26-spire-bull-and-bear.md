Spire Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 3:06 PM

Comments

Spire Inc. (NYSE:SR) is a regulated natural gas utility serving customers across Missouri, Alabama, and more recently Tennessee. The company’s long-term growth strategy centers on disciplined investment in regulated gas infrastructure, a sharpened focus on core utility operations after major divestitures, and a commitment to predictable, growing returns through both rate base expansion and stable dividend policies.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Completing the Shift to a Pure-Play Regulated Utility Simplifies Earnings and Lowers Risk

Spire’s transformation into a focused, fully regulated gas utility business has materially reduced its exposure to volatile, non-core operations. By divesting Spire Marketing, Storage, and the Mississippi utility business throughout 2026, and completing the acquisition and integration of Spire Tennessee, the company has concentrated operations in three highly scalable, demographically attractive states. This pivot has been executed without issuing common equity, avoiding shareholder dilution and further stabilizing the company’s long-term earnings outlook. As a result, management has improved forward earnings visibility, creating a more straightforward and resilient profile that appeals to investors seeking steady, utility-like returns in a sector often challenged by regulatory and volumetric volatility.

2. Significant Rate Base Growth Drives Predictable and Compounding Earnings

Disciplined capital deployment underlies a robust long-term earnings growth outlook. Spire continues to advance its 10-year, $11.2 billion capital plan, investing nearly $800 million in fiscal 2026 alone to upgrade and expand natural gas infrastructure. The resulting 7% annual rate base growth in Missouri and Tennessee (and 6% in Alabama and Gulf) is the fundamental engine behind management’s updated guidance of $3.90–$4.10 in adjusted earnings per share (EPS, non-GAAP) for fiscal 2026 and $5.40–$5.60 for 2027. These investments link directly to constructive regulatory frameworks, which enable timely cost recovery and compound earnings power over time, supporting the company’s reaffirmed 5–7% long-term adjusted EPS compound annual growth rate (CAGR) target.

3. Stable and Growing Dividend Supported by Durable Cash Flows and Policy Discipline

Steady dividend expansion enhances total return and income visibility for investors. Spire has a consistent record of maintaining a 55–65% payout ratio and recently declared a quarterly dividend of $0.825 per share, translating to an attractive yield near 3.85%. This commitment is anchored by improved cash flow reliability from its now fully regulated operations and disciplined balance sheet management. As legacy non-regulated business risks are shed, Spire stands out as a top 10 utility dividend stock, providing income-oriented investors with confidence that dividend growth will continue to mirror earnings expansion without overleveraging or capital uncertainty.

4. Constructive Regulatory Climate Enables Timely Investment Recovery and Reduces Political Overhang

Supportive regulatory outcomes underpin both near-term and long-term value creation. Spire has demonstrated productive relationships with utility commissions across its territories, securing swift approval for the Tennessee acquisition (finalized in just six months) and a recent $16.5 million Infrastructure System Replacement Surcharge in Missouri. The company’s success in proactively filing for Accounting Authority Orders (AAOs), as seen with the mitigation of weather-driven margin shortfalls, further showcases regulatory acumen. This constructive environment allows Spire to accelerate infrastructure upgrades and customer growth initiatives, while aligning regulatory mechanisms to evolving usage trends—thus reducing headline and political risk, which is often a concern for utility investors.

5. Entry into High-Growth Tennessee Market Expands Customer Base and Geographic Diversification

The integration of Spire Tennessee unlocks access to robust demographic and demand tailwinds. By adding over 200,000 customers in the Greater Nashville area—a region outpacing national population and economic growth rates—Spire not only broadens its revenue base but also mitigates concentration risk historically centered in Missouri and Alabama. The successful transition of employees and processes, supported by an 18-month transition services agreement, sets a foundation for scale economies and improved customer service. This expansion supports the company’s 2027 and beyond growth ambitions, as Tennessee’s higher connection rates and favorable regulatory structure could generate outsized contributions to group earnings over the coming decade.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Weather and Usage Sensitivity in Missouri Could Undermine Earnings Predictability

Volatility in weather and customer demand remains a material risk to earnings visibility. Despite weather normalization mechanisms, Spire experienced an 11.5% below-normal heating season in Missouri in early 2026, with January’s usage falling a dramatic 28% below the regulatory base year. This led to a notable downward revision in Gas Utility segment earnings guidance for 2026, as the current normalization scheme did not fully offset the shortfall. While management is addressing this via future rate cases and AAOs, there is no guarantee of timely or complete regulatory relief. Continued unseasonably warm winters or shifts in customer behavior could lead to further earnings variability and undermine the “smooth, predictable” utility story Spire seeks to project.

2. Regulatory and Divestiture Timing Risks Could Delay Capital Recovery and Prolong Balance Sheet Strain

Execution risk around regulatory rulings and pending asset sales introduces potential for earnings hiccups. The company’s restoration of capital flexibility hinges on successfully closing the sales of Spire Storage and Mississippi and gaining timely regulatory approval for extraordinary cost recovery filings in Missouri. Any delays in these processes could require prolonged use of higher-cost bridge loans, increase near-term interest expense, and defer recognition of critical proceeds tied to the balance sheet and capital plan. Since the timing and accounting impact of such situations is specifically linked to regulatory order language and year-end cutoffs, there is a real risk that annual earnings and capital efficiency targets for 2026 could fall short unless execution remains flawless.

3. Rising Debt Levels Increase Financial Leverage and Expose Spire to Higher Interest Expense

The company’s avoidance of equity financing for acquisitions results in a heavier reliance on debt. While Spire’s strategy of using proceeds from asset sales and debt instruments to finance its Tennessee acquisition has minimized shareholder dilution, it has also increased total debt and interest obligations. Interest expense rose in fiscal Q2 2026 as a direct effect of higher long-term debt balances and the temporary necessity of an $800 million term loan ahead of sale closings. Even with a revised, lower funds-from-operations to debt (FFO-to-debt) target of 14–15%, persistent reliance on the debt markets—especially in a volatile interest rate environment—could threaten future dividend growth, financial flexibility, and ultimately, shareholder value if capital costs outpace regulated return adjustments.
