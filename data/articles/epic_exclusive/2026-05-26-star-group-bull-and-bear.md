Star Group Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 1:07 PM

Comments

Star Group, L.P. (NYSE:SGU) is one of the nation’s largest full-service home heating oil and propane distributors, serving primarily residential and light commercial customers across the Northeastern United States. The company grows through a combination of disciplined acquisitions and organic customer retention, aiming to build scale, improve operational efficiency, and mitigate industry decline through geographic reach and margin management.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Operational Resilience and Improved Margins Drive Robust Profitability

Strong margin management and resilient operations have led to significant earnings growth – In the second quarter of fiscal 2026, Star Group not only increased home heating oil and propane delivery volumes by 0.004% year over year but expanded per-gallon margins enough to boost product gross profit by $19 million (up 7% to $277 million). Year to date, gross profit surged 12% to $457 million. Management credits this performance to inventory controls, supply chain initiatives, and proactive pricing, demonstrating the company’s ability to manage input cost fluctuations. This improved profitability foundation—especially when delivery, branch, and G&A expenses rise—shows that scale and operational discipline can deliver bottom-line results even in volatile conditions, laying groundwork for continued earnings power regardless of external shocks.

2. Low Customer Attrition Suggests Durable Market Position Amid Industry Change

Stable customer retention strengthens long-term revenue predictability – The company reported net customer attrition of just 0.6% through the harsh winter season, an achievement credited to superior service responsiveness and operational reliability. In a market facing long-term conversion pressures (such as electrification), this low churn underscores the resilience and stickiness of Star Group’s largely residential customer base. Confidence in maintaining and even modestly growing the customer pool, especially paired with successful integration of recent acquisitions, improves visibility into future revenues, which is a critical strength in a sector susceptible to gradual secular decline and intense competition.

3. Strategic Acquisitions Supplement Organic Growth and Support Industry Consolidation

Disciplined acquisitions enable scale advantages and incremental profit – In the latest quarter, acquisitions contributed meaningfully to higher volumes and EBITDA, offsetting modest declines in organic growth. While only one small acquisition closed during Q2 2026, management’s articulation of a multideal pipeline signals ongoing industry consolidation. By targeting tuck-in acquisitions that integrate smoothly and add $3 million in incremental operating expenses (with no reported major integration issues), Star Group achieves both defensive and offensive objectives: expanding its footprint, capturing new customers, and leveraging fixed cost absorption. In a fragmented and declining heating oil market, thoughtful consolidation is a proven method for sustaining margins and offsetting competitive erosion.

4. Effective Risk Management Programs Limit Volatility in Weather-Driven Earnings

Weather hedges and margin management help stabilize historically volatile results – The company’s continued investment in weather hedges and derivatives has begun to act as a shock absorber in seasons of unusual warmth or volatility. For fiscal 2027, Star Group has already committed to a $12.5 million weather hedge, following a quarter in which the company did not recognize any benefit or expense under its weather hedge, compared to a $3.1 million expense recorded for the same quarter in the prior year. By matching its risk profile to seasonal volatility and maintaining transparency around GAAP and adjusted results, management shows that it is proactively protecting cash flows and reducing the risk of major earnings shortfalls in abnormally warm years—a key differentiator in a highly weather-dependent sector.

5. High Return on Equity Validates Capital Allocation and Underscores Value Creation

Thirty-one percent return on equity in Q2 2026 highlights effective capital deployment – For the quarter ended March 2026, Star Group posted a return on equity of 30.99%, reflecting a blend of improved margins, efficient expense control, and disciplined acquisition policy. This stellar return is especially notable for a seasonal, mature industry and demonstrates successful balancing of reinvestment in operations with acquisition and risk management initiatives. Such capital efficiency positions the company to deliver strong economic value for unitholders and provides flexibility both to absorb macro shocks and to invest further in business development or customer experience, fueling a virtuous cycle of growth and financial resilience.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Earnings Power Is Highly Dependent on Abnormal Winter Weather, Questioning Sustainability

Weather-driven outperformance raises doubts about repeatability – Much of Star Group’s recent earnings surge has been attributed to colder-than-normal temperatures (11% colder year to date vs. prior year and a three-week period 25% colder than average). Volume improvements and margin expansion were closely tied to this weather anomaly, meaning results may prove difficult to replicate in more typical or warmer winters. Management itself emphasizes that operational performance was greatly impacted by weather, and a reversion to normal-degree days would likely see both revenues and profits revert as well, exposing a fundamental risk for sustainable growth and stable earnings.

2. Rising Service and Insurance Costs Undermine Margins as Operational Complexity Grows

Escalating expenses threaten net profitability even with strong top-line gains – Delivery, branch, and G&A costs rose $5.4 million year over year in Q2, and insurance expenses jumped $4 million due to a surge in claims from severe weather. Additionally, increased demand for repair services pushed service segment losses higher ($3.4 million in Q2). These trends highlight the challenge of controlling costs tied to both organic and acquired volume as well as the risks associated with operating in harsh winter environments. If such shocks persist or if inflationary pressures continue, expense growth could outpace gains in revenue, pressuring margins and long-term cash-generation capability.

3. Long-Term Structural Decline in Heating Oil Demand Poses Unavoidable Secular Headwind

Industry shift toward electrification threatens the core business model – Despite current stability in customer attrition, Star Group operates in an industry facing inevitable contraction due to widespread electrification, renewable energy adoption, and potential future regulatory mandates to phase out home heating oil and propane. While the company has shown resilience and adaptability in the short term, management offered no commentary or plan on these long-term threats during the most recent earnings call. Over time, these secular dynamics are likely to erode the addressable market and may force more aggressive restructuring, asset write-downs, or shifts in capital allocation, especially if regulatory change accelerates or customer conversion incentives expand across primary service regions.
