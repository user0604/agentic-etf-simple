Key Takeaways

StoneCo (NASDAQ:STNE) posted modest gains in the first quarter, supported by ongoing expansion but weighed down by higher credit risks and profitability pressures. The financial technology firm, focused on payment, banking, and credit services for Brazilian merchants, released its first quarter 2026 results on May 14, 2026 (covering the three months ending March 31). The period highlighted a company in transition, balancing new opportunities against mounting operational risks.

Overall, StoneCo’s quarter showed visible growth but also signaled key areas of concern as it evolves beyond payments into credit and broader banking services.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 6.5% year over year to R$3,578.0 million, mainly due to growth in credit product lines.
Provisions for credit losses soared 389% year over year to R$166.3 million, driving up the cost of risk and impacting gross margins.
The active client base expanded 13.2% year over year to 4.7 million, but average revenue per client declined, and quarter-over-quarter client numbers fell after new measurement methods were applied.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide a quantified outlook for the coming quarters but stressed caution with a focus on quality clients and operational discipline. Rising credit risks and margin pressures remain top concerns. Investors should closely monitor credit quality indicators and the performance of newly emphasized banking and credit services.

Quarterly Highlights

StoneCo’s credit business continued to expand rapidly, with the credit portfolio more than doubling from a year earlier and credit revenue up 186%, but this boost came at the expense of credit quality. Provisions for expected losses rose sharply, and non-performing loan ratios also increased.

Growth in StoneCo’s client base remained strong year over year, driven by its reach among small and medium-sized Brazilian merchants, but lower average revenue per client and a quarter-over-quarter drop in active clients show pressure on client economics and engagement.

The company paid a one-time extraordinary dividend of US$2.53 per share, distributing R$3.08 billion from the sale of its software subsidiary Linx, and continued with share buybacks, highlighting significant capital returns this quarter.

What StoneCo Does

StoneCo provides payment processing, banking, and credit solutions for merchants across Brazil, with a focus on digital integration and financial technology platform services.
