The specialty pharmaceutical company develops therapies for gastrointestinal diseases, focusing on rare and orphan conditions. Notable developments
Licensing revenue from the Future Pak agreement was the main driver of the $20.3 million quarter revenue, with $16 million recognized plus a $3 million buy-back payment. Product revenue excluding licensing fell 45% year over year to $1.2 million.
The company posted its first positive operating income in recent history ($10.2 million), but ongoing operations remain loss-making, with recurring EBITDA at a negative $5.1 million.
Jaguar continued to invest in research and development for rare-disease programs, with research spend up to $3.9 million, and is preparing data presentations for pediatric intestinal failure at a major industry meeting in June 2026.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management emphasized that the licensing deal funds near-term operations and enables a sharper focus on developing crofelemer for rare conditions. The most important item for investors is the timing and success of regulatory milestones, including upcoming conference data presentations and future approval filings. Cash burn remains significant, so tracking future licensing or milestone revenue is key.

Quarterly highlights

The $16 million licensing payment from the Future Pak deal, plus a $3 million option buy-back, nearly completely replaced prior product revenue in the U.S. As a result, the company’s recurring business will now depend on manufacturing supply payments and new partnerships.

Prescription product revenue dropped sharply as the company exited direct U.S. sales of Mytesi and Canalevia-CA1, which are prescription therapies for gastrointestinal conditions. Net product revenue fell 45% compared to the same period last year, reflecting the strategic pivot rather than a sudden loss of product demand.

The company increased its investment in clinical research, particularly for crofelemer’s powder formulation, supporting upcoming presentations at the annual ESPGHAN pediatric gastroenterology meeting. There were no new regulatory filings or approvals, but management reiterated its plan to seek U.S. approval for crofelemer in rare pediatric disorders in 2027.

What Jaguar Health does

Jaguar Health develops and supplies prescription and non-prescription therapies for gastrointestinal diseases, focusing on rare and orphan indications and leveraging key licensing partnerships to support its research pipeline.
