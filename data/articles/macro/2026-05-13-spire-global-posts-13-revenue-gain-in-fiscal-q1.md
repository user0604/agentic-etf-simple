Spire Global Posts 13% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 4:22 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Revenue excluding the maritime segment grew despite a headline revenue drop after the maritime divestiture.
Net loss per share (Non-GAAP) improved to $(0.37), while revenue fell 34% year over year.
Management expects ex-maritime revenue to rise up to 61% for fiscal 2026.

Satellite data firm Spire Global (NYSE:SPIR) pressed forward in its business transformation after selling its maritime segment, growing its core non-maritime revenue base even as overall revenue declined for the quarter. The company, which operates a large constellation of nanosatellites that deliver weather, aviation, and radio-frequency data, announced its first quarter 2026 results on May 13, 2026. Overall, the quarter featured continued revenue gains in government and security sectors but raised concerns about cash burn and profitability.

Notable Developments
Revenue excluding the maritime segment was up 13%, offsetting a sharp drop tied to the sale of that business.
Gross margin (non-GAAP) rose 5 percentage points year over year, as lower software and depreciation costs improved profitability rates.
Cash and marketable securities fell to $49.5 million at quarter end, but a post-quarter $70 million capital raise restored liquidity.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now offers annual guidance only, estimating non-maritime 2026 revenue between $71.3 million and $81.3 million -- representing up to 61% growth. It expects losses to remain sizable but to narrow compared to the current period. Investors should watch the pace of government contract wins, as these large deals drive results but can be unpredictable in timing.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue shifted from its previous maritime focus to government and security markets, evidenced by non-maritime revenue growth and several new radio-frequency geolocation (RFGL) contracts. The company signed five new RFGL orders with U.S. customers and three with international clients in the period.

Spire continued to invest in its satellite fleet, launching 19 satellites and debuting its Hyperspectral Microwave Sounder demonstrator, which achieved “first light” and validated new technology capabilities. Technical progress included successful on-orbit demonstration of single-satellite RFGL, which allows for geolocation without multiple satellites and broadens its product applications.

Despite margin improvement, the company’s free cash flow deficit for the first quarter of 2026 widened by nearly 98% year over year, reflecting high operational and investment spending. A sharp drop in cash resources necessitated a $70 million equity raise in April to support ongoing operations and investment plans. For now, the company remains debt-free.

What Spire Global Does

Spire Global gathers, analyzes, and sells space-based data using its constellation of nanosatellites, providing insights for weather forecasting, aviation, shipping, and government security customers.
