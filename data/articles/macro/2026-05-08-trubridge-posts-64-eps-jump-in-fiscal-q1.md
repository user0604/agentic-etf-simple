Key Takeaways
Earnings reflected operational stagnation, with a 1% year-over-year revenue decline and shrinking adjusted (non-GAAP) margins.
Non-GAAP earnings per share climbed 63.9% year over year.
Management did not issue forward guidance; visibility is limited due to a pending acquisition.

TruBridge (NASDAQ:TBRG)’s quarterly results showed little momentum as topline growth stalled and profitability metrics softened compared to its previous ambitions. The leading provider of healthcare revenue cycle management and technology solutions is now navigating through a strategic transition, influenced by its pending acquisition. The report, released May 8, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, underscored muted revenue alongside modest changes in segment performance. Overall, the period was marked by steady, but lackluster, financial health and fewer new business wins.

Notable Developments
Revenue dipped slightly, declining to $86.3 million from $87.2 million.
Non-GAAP earnings per share surged to $0.59, while adjusted EBITDA and margin shrank compared to the prior year.
Total bookings edged higher at $17.7 million, but nearly all gains came from selling more services to existing clients rather than attracting new ones.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management withheld forward guidance this period, citing its ongoing acquisition by Inventurus Knowledge Solutions as the reason for pausing investor communications. Previously, leadership had targeted modest annual revenue growth and margin expansion, but did not issue forward guidance this period due to the pending acquisition and limited visibility. Investors will want to monitor updates on completion of the acquisition and any reset of strategic direction after ownership changes.

Quarterly Highlights

Performance in the Financial Health segment, which includes revenue cycle management services essential for billing and collections, weakened as revenue and adjusted EBITDA both fell year over year. The decrease was coupled with a sharp drop in new client bookings, highlighting a growing reliance on existing customers to fill the order book.

The Patient Care segment, encompassing TruBridge’s electronic health record (EHR) platform and related technology, posted moderate growth in both revenue and segment profitability. This came as non-subscription product sales grew, but subscription bookings, which provide more predictable recurring revenue, declined—signaling potentially less stability ahead.

On the expense side, general and administrative costs increased significantly, even while product development spending slowed. This expense inflation, together with almost flat overall revenue, compressed adjusted EBITDA margins and points to operational pressures that may persist unless cost structure is addressed.

What TruBridge Does

TruBridge provides software, consulting, and technology services that help healthcare organizations manage billing, patient records, and business operations more efficiently.
