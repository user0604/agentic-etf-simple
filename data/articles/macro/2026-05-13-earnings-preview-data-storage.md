Earnings Preview: Data Storage

May 13, 2026, 9:04 AM

Data Storage (NASDAQ:DTST), a provider of business voice and data connectivity solutions, is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on Friday, May 15, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Data Storage completed a major transformation. The company finalized the sale of its CloudFirst subsidiary for $40 million, resulting in net proceeds of $31.6 million and a $20.1 million gain. This transaction, along with a substantial tender offer that returned $29.3 million to shareholders and reduced the share count by about 72%, reshaped the company’s balance sheet and operating structure. The core ongoing business is now Nexxis, which provides recurring, subscription-based voice and data solutions.

For the year ended Dec. 31, 2025, revenue from continuing operations (Nexxis) reached $1.4 million, up 13.4% from the prior year. Gross margin improved to 44.4%. Net income attributable to shareholders was $19.2 million, but this figure was driven by the one-time gain from the CloudFirst sale and does not reflect ongoing earnings power. The company ended the year debt-free, with over $10 million in cash after the tender offer. These results met analyst expectations for the period, with no significant beats or misses reported.

Positives
Significant capital return to shareholders: The company completed a large tender offer, returning $29.3 million to shareholders and reducing the share count by about 72%. This is a rare move for a company of this size and demonstrates a focus on shareholder value.
Improved balance sheet and liquidity: After the CloudFirst sale and tender offer, the company remains debt-free with over $10 million in cash, providing flexibility for future initiatives and acquisitions.
Growth in core Nexxis business: Nexxis revenue increased 13.4% year over year, and gross margin expanded to 44.4%. The business is now less concentrated, with no single customer accounting for more than 10% of revenue.
Reduced operating losses from continuing operations: Loss from continuing operations narrowed significantly compared to the prior year, reflecting improved efficiency and cost control after the business transition.
Negatives
Ongoing operations remain small scale: With CloudFirst sold, the remaining Nexxis business generated only $1.4 million in annual revenue, limiting near-term growth potential until acquisitions are completed.
Elevated expenses due to nonrecurring items: Selling, general, and administrative expenses rose 9.1%, mainly from non-cash stock-based compensation tied to the CloudFirst sale. This distorts the underlying expense trend.
Profitability driven by one-time gains: Net income for the year was primarily due to the CloudFirst sale. The ongoing business remains unprofitable on a GAAP basis, with a loss from continuing operations of $866,000.
