Wix Plunges 30% on Huge EPS Miss: The Team HG View

Wix.com (NASDAQ: WIX) posted solid top-line growth in its fiscal first quarter but handed investors a jarring earnings miss, signaling that its current investment cycle is weighing heavily on profitability.

Revenue rose 14% year over year to $541 million, with the company's business solutions segment -- which includes e-commerce tools and payment processing -- growing 17% to lead the way. Its partners channel, which serves web design agencies and developers, also grew 19%. Together, those segments show that Wix is successfully pushing upmarket beyond its roots as a DIY website builder. Yet the revenue figure still came in short of analyst expectations of roughly $544 million, and the bigger shock was on the bottom line. Non-GAAP earnings per share landed at $0.68, less than half the $1.22 Wall Street had penciled in, as research and development costs jumped 40% and sales and marketing spending surged 79% compared to the prior year. Those investments are funding Wix's aggressive AI push, including its Harmony website creation platform and Base44 -- an AI-powered app-building tool it acquired in 2025 that lets users build functioning apps without writing code. Base44 hit $150 million in annualized recurring revenue by May, up from $100 million just months earlier, suggesting the product is gaining traction fast, even as it compresses margins in the near term.

Management acknowledged a weak start in its Partners business but maintained its full-year outlook for revenue and bookings growth in the mid-teens, and guided for free cash flow margins in the high teens. The company also completed a $1.6 billion share buyback in April, repurchasing roughly 30% of its outstanding shares at $92 each through a Dutch auction -- a transaction that now leaves it carrying more debt and paying higher interest costs, both of which ate into free cash flow this quarter. Investors should watch whether the spending surge on AI and marketing eventually translates into margin recovery, or whether the cost base has simply reset higher.

The market's verdict was swift. Shares were down by 30% from yesterday's close as of this writing, as investors reacted to the steep drop in earnings per share and to growing concerns that AI competition from tools like Anthropic's Claude Design threatens Wix's core business. The EPS miss was the clearest trigger -- when a stock already trading well below its 52-week high delivers profits less than half of what analysts expected, the market tends not to give the benefit of the doubt, even when the strategic rationale for the spending is sound.

On the surface, Wix's quarter wasn't great, but it also wasn't terrible enough to warrant a 30% plunge in the stock price. The company is buying back its own stock aggressively, new-user cohort bookings increased strongly, and it's worth emphasizing that Wix maintained its full-year guidance.

As a SaaS business that has serious potential to be disrupted by AI coding models in its core website-building business, there's little room for error. Software businesses that are not only embracing the agentic AI revolution but also seeing the benefits reflected in the numbers are being handsomely rewarded by investors. Wix's numbers, on the other hand, are starting to show some cracks, and that's why the stock is being punished.
