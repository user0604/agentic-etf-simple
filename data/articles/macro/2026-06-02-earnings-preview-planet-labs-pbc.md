Earnings Preview: Planet Labs PBC

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Planet Labs PBC (NYSE:PL), a provider of daily Earth imagery and analytics for government and commercial clients, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2027 on Thursday, June 4, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2026 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026, Planet Labs PBC delivered record revenue of $86.8 million, representing 41% growth year over year. The company reported its fifth consecutive quarter of adjusted EBITDA profitability, with adjusted EBITDA for the quarter at $2.3 million. Full-year revenue reached $307.7 million, up 26% from the prior year, and the company achieved positive free cash flow for the first time on an annual basis. These results beat analyst estimates for both revenue and profitability, driven mainly by strong demand from Defense and Intelligence customers and new contract wins. The company ended the year with a backlog exceeding $900 million, up 79% year over year, providing strong visibility into future revenue. Management highlighted continued momentum in signing large, multi-year satellite services agreements and ongoing investments in artificial intelligence (AI) and next-generation satellite fleets.

Positives
Record revenue growth and profitability milestones: Planet Labs PBC achieved its highest-ever quarterly and annual revenue, along with its first full year of adjusted EBITDA profitability and positive free cash flow. This demonstrates improved operational efficiency and strong demand for its core offerings.
Significant backlog growth: The backlog reached over $900 million, up 79% year over year, giving the company excellent visibility into future revenue streams and supporting management’s raised growth outlook.
Strong performance in Defense and Intelligence: Revenue from this segment grew more than 50% year over year, reflecting heightened global demand for satellite data and analytics amid geopolitical tensions.
Successful execution of large satellite services contracts: The company signed major multi-year deals, including a nine-figure agreement with the Swedish Armed Forces and continued progress on contracts funded by Germany and JSAT, validating its satellite services model.
Negatives
Flat or declining revenue in Civil Government and Commercial sectors: Civil Government revenue was flat year over year, and Commercial revenue declined modestly, mainly due to a strategic focus on larger government contracts and ongoing headwinds in the agriculture sector.
Lower gross margin in Q4: Non-GAAP gross margin for the quarter was 57%, down from 65% in the prior year period. This was due to investments in satellite services contracts and a changing mix that included more AI-enabled partner solutions.
Customer count decreased: End-of-period customer count fell to 897 from 976 a year earlier, reflecting a deliberate shift toward larger accounts and more self-serve customers, which management notes makes this metric less relevant going forward.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2027's Report
Revenue growth and guidance relative to backlog: Management guided Q1 revenue to $87 million to $91 million, or about 34% growth at the midpoint, with analysts expecting sales near $90 million. Investors should watch for how quickly Planet Labs PBC is able to convert its record backlog into recognized revenue.
Gross margin trends and investment impacts: The company expects non-GAAP gross margin to step down to 49% to 51% in Q1, reflecting a higher mix of satellite services and investment in next-generation fleets. Monitoring margin stability will be important for assessing profitability as the business scales.
Progress on AI-enabled solutions and new product launches: Management has highlighted 2026 as a key year for returns on AI investments, with ongoing collaborations with NVIDIA and Google (SunCatcher project) and the integration of Bedrock Research. Updates on generic AI solutions and their adoption in non-defense sectors could signal new growth opportunities.
Status of satellite manufacturing expansion and launches: The company is doubling manufacturing capacity in San Francisco and Berlin to meet demand for new contracts, with multiple Pelican satellite launches planned this year. Any updates on the timing and execution of these initiatives will be closely watched.
Large contract pipeline and timing of new deals: Investors should look for commentary on the pace and scale of new multi-year satellite services agreements, especially given management’s comments about an expanding pipeline and increasing average deal size since the last Investor Day.
