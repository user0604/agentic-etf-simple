Alico Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 28, 2026, 11:05 AM

Comments

Alico, Inc. (NASDAQ:ALCO) has transformed from a legacy citrus grower into a diversified Florida land company, now focused on monetizing, leasing, and developing its substantial acreage. The company’s strategy centers on maximizing the value of its 46,000 acres through land sales, long-term leases, and securing entitlements for high-value real estate projects, positioning itself to unlock returns from both recurring income and one-time development milestones over the coming years.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Accelerating Land Sales at Premium Prices Unlock Hidden Asset Value

Recent land transactions demonstrate a critical upward re-rating of Alico’s acreage value. In the first half of 2026, the company completed $34.6 million in land sales, including a standout sale in January 2026 of 2,950 acres for $26.9 million—roughly $9,100 per acre. This price is nearly double the $4,000–$5,000 per acre management uses for conservative book value estimates. As proven transactions occur well above book, this not only provides immediate cash flow and earnings but also signals that Alico may be materially undervalued on a net asset basis. Replicating this success across even a portion of its 46,000-acre portfolio could lead to further upward revisions of intrinsic value, supporting continued capital deployment and potential share price appreciation.

2. Robust Recurring Lease Income Smooths Volatile Development Cycles

High utilization of agricultural and other leases ensures steady cash flow to bridge the company’s transition. As of March 2026, 97% of Alico’s approximately 32,500 farmable acres (89% of total land) were under revenue-generating leases—with partners ranging from citrus growers to sod farmers and mining interests. Lease revenue jumped 113% year over year in the latest quarter, now establishing a growing base of predictable, recurring income. This revenue stream insulates the business from commodity swings and the “lumpy” nature of large land sales, enabling management to wait for optimal development or sale windows and to fund entitlement costs without forced asset sales, reducing downside financial risks in periods of slower capital markets or regulatory delays.

3. Progress on Corkscrew Grove Entitlement Unleashes Multi-Year Development Optionality

The recent county approval for Corkscrew Grove East Village is a game-changing development milestone. In April 2026, Alico secured unanimous Collier County approval to entitle 1,446 acres for up to 4,502 homes and 238,000 square feet of commercial space—the most significant regulatory hurdle at the local level. This paves the way for state and federal permits (targeted for 2027 and 2028, respectively) and positions the project as a multi-phase monetization engine. Management estimates the development pipeline (5,500 acres total) could yield $335–$380 million in net present value by 2031. As Alico moves closer to construction or sale of these premier development sites, the uplift in value could be substantial, transforming the company’s earnings trajectory and asset profile.

4. Prudent Capital Allocation and Buybacks Create Shareholder Leverage to Land Value Upside

Disciplined share repurchases enhance per-share exposure to potential asset revaluation. In fiscal Q2 2026, Alico repurchased $10 million in shares (245,399 shares), signaling management’s confidence that the market undervalues its land portfolio relative to realized sale prices and net asset value. By returning capital and reducing the share count at depressed levels, the company increases each remaining shareholder’s proportional claim on future land sale and development profits. This approach also evidences a balanced capital allocation mindset—investing in entitlement while opportunistically returning excess cash, which could magnify upside if current NPV estimates are ultimately realized through successful sales or partnerships.

5. Strong Balance Sheet Enables Flexibility to Optimize Sale Timing and Development Path

Ample liquidity and low net debt protect against forced asset sales and maximize strategic optionality. At the end of March 2026, Alico reported $52.9 million in cash, working capital of $52.2 million, and a net debt position of just $32.6 million—supported by a sizable credit facility with $92.5 million in availability. With expected year-end cash guidance of $40 million after buybacks, the company is positioned to weather multi-year entitlement processes and choose the most advantageous path for each asset: outright sale, developer partnership, or self-development. This financial strength means Alico can advance key milestones on its timeline, not the market’s, and avoid being a forced seller in soft markets, preserving the long-run value creation opportunity for shareholders.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Significant Regulatory and Execution Risk Could Delay or Reduce Value Realization

Regulatory timelines and permitting hurdles could materially stall or diminish value capture for major development projects. Although Corkscrew Grove East Village cleared an important local entitlement hurdle in April 2026, state approvals are not anticipated before early 2027 and federal clearance may extend into late 2028. Complexities in working with multiple agencies—especially for large-scale developments in Florida’s environmentally sensitive regions—increase the risk that delays, mitigation requirements, or adverse rulings push back disposition or project launch windows. Should the regulatory timeline lengthen or market conditions worsen over this period, discount rates rise and net present value for these assets suffers, potentially diminishing the transformative upside management currently projects.

2. Highly Concentrated Exposure to a Few Large Projects Increases Lumpiness and Vulnerability

The company’s future value is highly dependent on a handful of major entitlement and development successes. Nearly all expected upside from development is tied to Corkscrew Grove and a small number of additional projects covering just 12% of the total land portfolio. Should Alico fail to secure final approvals, attract development partners, or meet market demand for these specific projects, monetization would fall short of current projections. Heavy concentration means unforeseen setbacks—such as infrastructure cost spikes, adverse zoning changes, or weakening Southwest Florida real estate demand—can disproportionately impact earnings and asset values, leading to volatile outcomes and investor disappointment if even one major project misses expectations.

3. Transition Away from Citrus Creates Near-Term Revenue Volatility and Reduced Cash Generation

Loss of legacy citrus revenue exaggerates the “lumpiness” of results, exposing shareholders to potentially weak interim earnings. Total revenue for the three months ended March 2026 dropped from $18 million to $5.3 million year over year as citrus operations were wound down, and large profits are now driven predominantly by episodic land sales. While leasing income is growing, it has not fully replaced high operating cash flow from legacy agriculture, and short-term earnings and cash generation will be more unpredictable until development and recurring income scale further. If land sale momentum slows or leasing demand softens, investors may face extended periods of weak profitability, challenging the company’s ability to fund entitlements and return capital to shareholders at prior levels.
