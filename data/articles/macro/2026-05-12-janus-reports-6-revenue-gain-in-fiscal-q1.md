Key Takeaways
Janus International Group reported higher revenue, but profits fell due to cost pressures and acquisition expenses.
Adjusted EPS dropped to $0.01 and adjusted EBITDA margin declined 3.4 percentage points versus the prior year.
Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance, expecting revenue between $940 million and $980 million.

Janus International Group (NYSE:JBI), a leading manufacturer and provider of building solutions for the self-storage and industrial sectors, delivered mixed results for its first quarter of fiscal 2026. While the company grew its top-line revenue, profitability dropped significantly as increased costs and one-time expenses weighed on the bottom line. The earnings release, dated May 12, 2026 and covering the first quarter, shows ongoing momentum in self-storage but notes clear margin and profit dilution compared to the previous year. Overall, the quarter highlighted strong sales growth but marked headwinds in earning quality and cost management.

Notable Developments
Self-storage revenue, which made up 70% of sales, grew 8.7%, with acquisitions like Kiwi II Construction adding $18.1 million to new construction.
Adjusted EBITDA margin fell from 18.2% to 14.8%, as higher input costs and acquisition-related expenses reduced profitability despite sales growth.
Janus bought back 2.9 million shares for $15.7 million and ended the quarter with $112 million in cash, down from $194.4 million at year-end due to acquisition spend and share repurchases.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its 2026 outlook, with expected total revenue between $940 million and $980 million and adjusted EBITDA of $165 million to $185 million for the year. Achieving these targets requires margin recovery, better cost controls, and successful integration of acquisitions like Kiwi II. For investors, tracking margin improvement and the effect of input cost changes will be key in the coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

The self-storage segment drove growth, making up over 70% of company sales and growing 8.7% year over year. Recent acquisitions, including Kiwi II Construction, immediately added revenue, but these new operations contributed to lower overall profit margins and brought higher integration and restructuring expenses.

Profitability declined as higher raw material costs, notably steel, and a shift toward lower-margin business lines depressed gross profit and adjusted EBITDA. Expenses related to acquisitions and general overhead also rose, while interest expense was lower but still a factor.

The company’s free cash flow decreased 20.3% year over year, yet it remained solid. Janus used available cash to finance the Kiwi II acquisition and repurchase shares, leading to a drop in cash balance and a rise in net leverage to 2.7 times adjusted EBITDA.
