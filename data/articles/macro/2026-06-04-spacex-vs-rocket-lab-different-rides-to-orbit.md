SpaceX vs. Rocket Lab: Different Rides to Orbit

As SpaceX barrels toward the largest IPO in history, here's how Rocket Lab's launch business measures up against the gorilla of the launch pad.

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 4, 2026, 8:00 PM

Comments
10.0

For years, the cleanest way to bet on the new space economy through the rockets themselves was Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB). That's about to change: SpaceX has priced its initial public offering (IPO) and is set to trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX. You still can't own a slice of SpaceX until it lists -- which makes this the moment to ask how the two stack up on the one thing they both do: put payloads into orbit.

Where They Go Head-to-Head
Both design, build, and fly their own orbital rockets. Rocket Lab's workhorse is Electron, a small-lift vehicle carrying up to 300 kg (0.30 metric tons) to low Earth orbit (LEO) -- the band a few hundred miles up where most satellites live (Electron Payload User Guide). SpaceX flies the far bigger Falcon 9, with a payload capacity of roughly 23 metric tons to LEO.
The real overlap is coming, not here yet. Rocket Lab is developing Neutron, a reusable medium-lift rocket designed to carry about 13,000 kg (13 metric tons) to LEO -- its bid to compete for the larger missions Falcon 9 dominates today.
By the Numbers
Segment Revenue: Rocket Lab's launch services brought in $199.0 million. SpaceX's comparable Space segment -- Falcon, Dragon, and Starship -- generated $4.09 billion, roughly 20 times larger.
Launch Cadence: Rocket Lab flew 21 Electron missions. SpaceX completed 170 missions across Falcon and Starship and launched over 2,200 metric tons -- more than 80% of all the mass the world sent to orbit in 2025.
Track Record: Rocket Lab counts 75 successful launches and 200-plus spacecraft deployed to date. SpaceX reports an over 99% mission success rate across its Falcon rockets and has reflown a single Falcon 9 booster as many as 34 times.
Profit Picture: Both are investing hard. Rocket Lab posted a $198.2 million companywide net loss; SpaceX's company net loss topped $4.9 billion as artificial intelligence (AI) spending surged.
Edge and Differentiation
SpaceX's moat is reusability at scale. NASA credited the first Falcon 9 with cutting launch costs to about $2,700 per kilogram, roughly 85% below the old average -- economics no rival has matched.
Rocket Lab's edge lies in being the dedicated small-satellite specialist and an end-to-end provider. The Motley Fool's Space Race team calls Rocket Lab "the natural chocolate to SpaceX's peanut butter," arguing that space investors will want diversified exposure rather than betting on Musk alone.
Notably, Rocket Lab's launch unit already makes money per flight: $8.5 million in revenue against $4.8 million in cost per launch in 2025.
Risks and Wildcards
Neutron is the swing factor. It's behind schedule, and in the Space Race team's words, "until the Neutron flies, there will be doubts surrounding Rocket Lab's order book."
SpaceX carries its own asymmetries: Musk controls more than 85% of shareholder votes through super-voting shares, and the IPO leans heavily on an AI story that Morningstar cited in valuing the company at just $780 billion -- far below the $1.77 trillion IPO price.
Bottom Line

These aren't really the same bet. SpaceX offers exposure to the dominant launch franchise -- one that generated a $653 million segment-adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) in its Space business in 2025 -- wrapped inside a $1.77 trillion debut. Rocket Lab is the smaller, faster-growing challenger whose launch story hinges on Neutron clearing the pad. Scale and proven economics on one side; optionality and a pure-play space focus on the other.
