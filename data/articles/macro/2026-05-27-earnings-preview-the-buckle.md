Earnings Preview: The Buckle

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 9:03 AM

Comments

The Buckle (NYSE:BKE), a specialty retailer focused on casual apparel, footwear, and accessories for men, women, and youth, is expected to report earnings for its first fiscal quarter of 2026 on Friday, May 29, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, The Buckle reported net income of $80.8 million, or $1.59 per diluted share, on net sales of $399.1 million. Comparable store sales increased 3.9% year over year, while online sales rose 6.4% to $74.2 million. The company’s operating margin for the quarter was 25.2%. These results beat analyst consensus estimates for both earnings per share and revenue. The company ended the quarter with 440 stores in 42 states and continued to maintain a strong balance sheet, with $306.6 million in cash and investments after paying $225.1 million in dividends during the year.

Positives
EPS and revenue beat expectations: The Buckle exceeded analyst consensus for both earnings per share and revenue, reflecting stronger-than-expected demand in key categories.
Women’s business delivered double-digit growth: Women’s merchandise sales rose about 12% for the quarter, driven by strong performance in denim and higher average price points, which suggests effective merchandising and pricing strategies.
Online sales growth remained solid: Online sales increased 6.4% in the quarter, showing continued strength in the company’s digital channel and ongoing relevance with online shoppers.
Healthy cash position and ongoing dividend payments: The company’s robust cash and investments position enabled it to pay a significant dividend, supporting shareholder returns and financial flexibility.
Negatives
Men’s merchandise sales declined: Sales in the men’s category fell about half a percent, with men’s denim down 3.5% year over year, indicating some softness in this segment compared to the women’s business.
Inventory levels increased significantly: Inventory rose 15.5% year over year to $139.5 million, which could pose a risk if demand slows or fashion trends shift unexpectedly.
General and administrative expenses increased: G&A expenses rose to 5.1% of net sales, up from 4.8% the previous year, reflecting higher compensation and other costs that could pressure margins if not managed closely.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Comparable store and online sales trends: Investors should monitor whether the positive momentum in comparable store sales (up 5.1% for the 13-week period ended May 2, 2026) and online sales continues into the new quarter, as these are key drivers of overall growth.
Inventory management and markdown risk: With inventory up sharply at the end of Q4, watch for any signs of excess inventory or increased markdowns, which could impact gross margins and profitability.
Women’s and youth category performance: The women’s and kids’ businesses have been standout performers. Continued strength here could offset softness in men’s categories and support overall sales growth.
Store expansion and remodel progress: The company plans to open 12 to 14 new stores and complete 12 to 14 remodels in fiscal 2026, with a focus on relocations to outdoor shopping centers. Updates on the pace and early performance of these initiatives will be important for assessing future growth potential.
Expense control and margin outlook: Given recent increases in general and administrative expenses, investors should look for commentary on cost management and any impact on operating margins in the upcoming quarter.
