Earnings Preview: PEDEVCO

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 3:03 PM

Comments

PEDEVCO (NYSEMKT:PED), a small oil and gas producer focused on legacy fields in the Rocky Mountains and Permian Basin, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings after the market closes on Thursday, May 14, 2026.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

Q4 2025 marked the first full quarter since PEDEVCO completed its merger with the Juniper portfolio companies on Oct. 31, 2025, nearly doubling its proved reserves and significantly expanding its production base. The company reported $23.1 million in revenue and $15.4 million in adjusted EBITDA for the quarter, with average daily production exceeding 5,300 barrels of oil equivalent (BOE) per day. Adjusted EBITDA grew 203% year over year, even though realized crude oil prices fell 16% compared to the prior year. The quarter included only two months of contribution from the newly acquired assets, so the results reflect an initial view of the combined business. The company reported a net loss of $10.4 million for the full year, mainly due to merger-related costs and nonrecurring charges. On a non-GAAP basis, adjusted EBITDA for the year was $27.0 million, up from $22.9 million in 2024. The company’s results met analyst expectations for the quarter.

Positives
Significant production growth after merger: Daily production increased to over 5,300 BOE per day in Q4, reflecting the impact of the Juniper merger and new wells coming online. This scale-up provides a stronger base for future growth.
Adjusted EBITDA more than tripled year over year: Despite lower oil prices, the company delivered a 203% increase in adjusted EBITDA, showing improved operational efficiency and the benefits of greater scale.
Expanded reserves and inventory: Proved reserves nearly doubled to 32.1 million BOE, supporting the company’s claim of a decade-plus of drilling opportunities and giving investors more visibility into future production potential.
Cost optimization program underway: Management has begun executing projects expected to reduce lease operating expenses by up to $1 million per month, with visible impact anticipated by mid-2026.
Negatives
Net loss driven by merger costs: The company reported a net loss for the year, mainly because of $7.5 million in nonrecurring merger expenses and other one-time charges. This masks underlying operating improvements.
Higher operating expenses post-merger: Lease operating expenses and general and administrative costs rose sharply due to the integration of acquired assets and merger-related payroll and legal fees.
Working capital deficit post-merger: At year-end, current liabilities exceeded current assets by $26.7 million, reflecting assumed liabilities from the merger and increased payables.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Production levels and sustainability: Q1 is expected to be a peak production quarter due to the flush output from new wells. Investors should watch for updated guidance on whether this level can be maintained or if a decline is expected as wells mature.
Progress on cost optimization: Management has targeted $10 million to $12 million in annual lease operating expense reductions. Look for updates on the pace and impact of these initiatives, especially in the DJ Basin.
Capital spending discipline and allocation: With a 2026 capital budget of $16 million to $20 million (90% for the DJ Basin), investors should monitor whether spending remains on track and if management provides any signals about expanding activity if oil prices remain strong.
Balance sheet and liquidity position: The company ended Q4 with $98 million drawn on its credit facility and a working capital deficit. Watch for any changes in leverage, liquidity, or plans to access additional capital through the ATM equity program.
Updates on development plans and inventory: Management has indicated flexibility to accelerate drilling if market conditions support it. Any new details on permitting, partner-operated projects, or Powder River Basin plans could affect the outlook for the rest of 2026 and beyond.
