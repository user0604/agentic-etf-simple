Sangoma Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

11:06 AM

Comments

Sangoma Technologies Corporation (NASDAQ:SANG) provides software, cloud-based communications, and managed infrastructure solutions to enterprises globally. The company is pursuing a growth strategy centered on recurring services revenue, especially through integrated communication infrastructure, cloud subscriptions, and bundled managed offerings. Recent changes show a deliberate pivot away from commoditized hardware and legacy software toward higher-value, scalable infrastructure and subscription services, supported by investments in artificial intelligence and security.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Accelerated Shift to Recurring Subscription Revenue Drives Long-Term Growth and Predictability

An increasing focus on recurring services and cloud subscriptions positions Sangoma Technologies Corporation for stable, compounding growth. Service revenue made up 92% of total revenue in Q3 2026, but declined year over year while product and hardware sales continued to decline. Churn has improved to under 1% per month, demonstrating robust customer retention. In an industry where predictability and scalability are prized, this evolving revenue mix supports stronger cash flows and insulation against economic volatility, providing a clearer pathway to sustained top-line and cash flow growth.

2. Emerging Infrastructure and Networking Segments Unlock Higher Growth and Margin Expansion

Strategic emphasis on data and voice networking is enabling double-digit growth in these segments with attractive operating leverage. While application and commoditized software segments have experienced low single-digit declines, infrastructure businesses—including data networking (+9% year over year in North America) and global voice networking (+17% year over year)—are posting robust growth. These segments now support over 30% of revenue and are built on scalable, high-value usage models increasingly driven by artificial intelligence. As these offerings become a greater share of the business, management expects to realize higher margins through operating leverage and bundled contract pricing, pointing to margin expansion as deployments scale. The company’s ability to capture demand for trusted, secure, and always-on business networks supports both immediate and future competitive advantage.

3. Continued Strong Free Cash Flow and Prudent Balance Sheet Management Strengthen Financial Resilience

Strong free cash flow performance enables funding for growth while reducing leverage and supporting shareholder value. Active share repurchases and renewal of the buyback program show management’s confidence, while a conservative approach to dividends or excessive leverage provides flexibility to invest in key strategic initiatives or make opportunistic acquisitions. This financial stewardship positions the company to weather macro headwinds and capitalize on future growth opportunities.

4. High Customer Retention and Expansion with Large, Multi-Product Deployments Build Long-Term Value

Low churn, high customer satisfaction, and successful client expansions underscore the strength and stickiness of Sangoma’s solutions. The company reported active customers exceeding 100,000 and highlighted major wins—such as retail deployments scaling to 350+ locations and medical clients expanding to hundreds of sites, with monthly recurring revenue growing during phased rollouts. Customer churn improved to 0.79%, the best in company history, while substantial long-term contracts (often three to five years) are locked in with multiproduct bundles. High net promoter scores and client expansion contribute to a stable and growing revenue base, providing both defensibility against competitors and opportunities for upsell and cross-sell as more solutions are integrated over time.

5. Strategic Review Could Unlock Value Through Partnerships, M&A, or Transformational Change

The board’s formal review of strategic alternatives represents a potential catalyst for significant value realization. In response to market undervaluation and increasing inbound interest, the board initiated a structured strategic review that could include asset sales, partnerships, mergers, or even a sale of the business. With financial advisors involved and active board oversight, Sangoma’s review may result in strategic transactions that could immediately crystallize value for shareholders or set the stage for accelerated growth with new partners, especially as industry consolidation continues.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Sustained Weakness in Software and UCaaS Segments Threatens Overall Growth Trajectory

Persistent decline in core application and Unified Communications as a Service (UCaaS) revenue segments limits Sangoma’s top-line momentum and raises questions about long-term competitive positioning. Despite strong growth in infrastructure, approximately 60% of the company’s revenue is still derived from applications and software, segments that are experiencing low single-digit annual declines. Increased pricing pressure, industry commoditization, and intense competition from larger cloud-only vendors have eroded UCaaS margins, with management openly deprioritizing further investment in these challenged areas. This structural headwind weighs on consolidated revenue and could delay or blunt a meaningful return to overall top-line growth, especially if infrastructure segment scaling proves slower or less reliable than anticipated.

2. Lowered Revenue Outlook and Leadership Transition Signal Heightened Uncertainty and Execution Risk

The reduction in full-year revenue guidance and the announced Chief Financial Officer (CFO) transition introduce incremental strategic and operational risks. In May 2026, Sangoma lowered its revenue forecast for the year to $204–$205 million, citing geopolitical turmoil and macro headwinds, particularly in international markets, as well as persistent competitive and pricing results in software businesses. Simultaneously, the planned retirement of CFO Larry Stock and the appointment of an interim successor add a leadership transition at a pivotal time. Together, these developments highlight uncertainty regarding execution, capital allocation, and the pace of any forthcoming strategic changes, increasing the likelihood of operational missteps or delays in returning to profitable growth.

3. Ongoing GAAP Losses and Absence of Formal Forward Guidance Raise Concerns on Profitability and Visibility

Repeated GAAP net losses and management’s decision not to issue updated forward revenue or earnings guidance create uncertainty for investors seeking evidence of lasting profitability and rebound potential. For the quarter ended March 31, 2026, Sangoma posted a GAAP net loss of $2.3 million (widened from a $1.5 million loss a year ago), even as adjusted EBITDA was positive. This reliance on non-GAAP metrics for profitability highlights the gap between operational improvements and true bottom-line performance. Additionally, management has suspended formal financial guidance due to ongoing volatility in product sales and macroeconomic demand, further clouding short-term forecasts. When combined, these factors may reduce investor confidence and heighten skepticism toward sustained improvement absent clearer evidence of consistent GAAP profitability and visible growth drivers.
