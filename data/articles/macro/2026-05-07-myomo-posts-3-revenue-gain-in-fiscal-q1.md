Myomo Posts 3% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:34 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Myomo delivered modest progress with improved margins and revenue mix, but device volumes slipped.
Gross margin rose and net loss narrowed while revenue increased 3% year over year.
Management reaffirmed full-year guidance and expects sequentially higher revenue in the next quarter.

Myomo (NYSEMKT:MYO) reported a moderate business win as its efforts to build predictable, recurring revenue and control costs paid off but devices sold declined and overall growth remained limited. The medical robotics company, which makes wearable myoelectric orthoses to help people with neuromuscular disorders regain arm and hand function, shared first-quarter results on May 7, 2026 (for the three months ended March 31). The period featured steady gross margin improvement, increased payer access, and a higher average selling price, but device volumes and cash generation continue to challenge progress.

Overall, the quarter delivered measured gains in profitability metrics and recurring revenue but underscored the need for greater scale to reach self-sustaining growth.

Notable Developments
Recurring patient revenue made up 49% of total revenue, nearly doubling from the previous year’s 25% share.
Gross profit improved with margin rising by one percentage point compared to the prior-year period, primarily driven by a higher average selling price.
The backlog of patients waiting for devices rose to 226, up 14% from December 31, 2025, indicating stable future demand.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects higher revenue for the next quarter, guiding to $10.3–$10.8 million, and reaffirms its full-year forecast of $43–$46 million. The company expects continued margin improvements and operating leverage gains in fiscal 2026. Investors should watch for whether rising orders and patient backlog translate into increased device deliveries and faster revenue growth while monitoring cash usage.

Quarterly Highlights

Recurring patient sources, such as follow-up services and replacement units, climbed to nearly half of revenue in the period. This shift boosts revenue predictability and indicates early success in building a steadier business model.

The MyoPro device, a powered orthosis that assists arm and hand movement, remains Myomo’s core product. The average selling price for the device increased 9% year over year, helping maintain revenue stability despite a 5% drop in delivered units.

Innovation advanced with the introduction of the Myomo Mobile App, which is expected to lower material costs by over 10% and improve access for clinicians and patients. Meanwhile, enrollment continues at a University of Utah clinical trial that could improve insurance coverage under Medicare Advantage in the future.

What Myomo Does

Myomo designs, manufactures, and sells powered orthoses that help people with arm and hand paralysis or weakness regain the ability to perform daily activities.

To stay informed on Myomo’s progress, track future updates on orders, revenue mix, and new developments in its product and payer access initiatives.
