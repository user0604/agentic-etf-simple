Earnings Preview: Cavco Industries

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Cavco Industries (NASDAQ:CVCO), a leading U.S. manufacturer of factory-built homes and related financial services, is expected to report earnings for the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Thursday, May 21, 2026, after the market closes.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, Cavco Industries reported net revenue of $581 million, an increase of 11.3% from the prior year period. This growth was primarily driven by the completed acquisition of American Homestar, which contributed $42 million in revenue. However, despite higher sales, the company’s profitability declined compared to the prior year. Net income fell to $44.1 million, and diluted earnings per share (EPS) dropped to $5.58 from $6.90 in the same quarter last year. Gross margin decreased to 23.4% from 24.9% a year ago, mainly due to higher per-unit costs and some price compression in company-owned retail operations. The results missed analyst estimates for both EPS and revenue, and the stock reacted negatively following the release.

Positives
Revenue growth driven by acquisition: The addition of American Homestar boosted overall sales, offsetting some of the volume declines in Cavco’s base business. This shows the company’s ability to use acquisitions to expand its footprint.
Stable average selling prices: Average revenue per home sold increased to $106,971, reflecting a shift toward higher-value multi-section homes and more sales through company-owned stores. This helps support revenue even when unit volumes soften.
Strong insurance performance: The financial services segment, especially insurance operations, delivered higher gross margins due to improved underwriting and fewer severe weather claims. This provides a cushion against swings in the core homebuilding business.
Healthy cash position post-acquisition: After funding the American Homestar purchase and share buybacks, Cavco ended the quarter with $225 million in unrestricted cash, supporting future flexibility.
Negatives
Profitability missed analyst expectations: Both EPS and net income declined year over year and fell short of consensus estimates, reflecting margin pressures and acquisition-related costs.
Gross margin compression in core business: Factory-built housing gross margin fell to 21.7%, mainly because higher input costs outpaced price increases and retail price compression affected profitability in company-owned stores.
Increased operating expenses: Selling, general, and administrative expenses rose to 14% of net revenue, up from 12.6% last year, due to American Homestar integration costs and higher compensation.
Lower interest income: Interest income dropped sharply, mainly because of lower cash balances after the acquisition, reducing a source of non-operating earnings.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report
Synergy realization from American Homestar integration: Management expects annualized cost savings from the acquisition to exceed $10 million, with about half already achieved by the start of Q4. Investors should look for updates on how much of these savings are reflected in Q4 results and whether integration costs have declined as projected.
Gross margin trends and input cost pressures: The company has flagged ongoing headwinds from higher materials costs and tariffs, which impacted margins in Q3. Watch for any improvement or further pressure on gross margins, especially in the factory-built housing segment.
Backlog stability and production pace: Cavco reported backlogs of 4 to 6 weeks at the end of Q3 and noted stable conditions heading into spring. Investors should monitor whether backlogs have increased, stayed flat, or declined, as this will signal the strength of demand and the company’s ability to manage production rates.
Impact of weather and seasonality on shipments: Management indicated that severe weather in January could temporarily affect shipments and plant operations, but expected these delays to be made up later in the quarter. The Q4 report may clarify if weather disruptions had any lasting impact on volumes or revenue recognition.
Updates on financial services and lending operations: The insurance business continues to perform well, but the lending arm has seen fewer loan sales. Any progress in loan originations or sales, as well as trends in insurance claims, will be important for understanding the stability of Cavco’s non-manufacturing earnings.
