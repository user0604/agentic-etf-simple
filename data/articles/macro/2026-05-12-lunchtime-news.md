1. Amazon Pushes 30-Minute Delivery

★ AMZN is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) is rolling out 30-minute deliveries in dozens of U.S. cities through its Amazon Now service, marking the company's most aggressive quick-commerce push yet. The expansion brings the service to Austin, Denver, Minneapolis, Phoenix, and more parts of Seattle, Philadelphia, Dallas, and Atlanta, with plans to reach tens of millions of customers by year-end.

Competitive pressure: The push squeezes gig-economy delivery players like Instacart (NASDAQ: CART), DoorDash (NASDAQ: DASH), and Uber (NYSE: UBER) Eats, which typically drop orders in a few hours. Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT) has touted that it can deliver to 95% of U.S. households in under three hours, raising the stakes on last-mile speed.
How it works: Amazon uses 5,000- to 10,000-square-foot micro-fulfillment centers, or "dark stores," stocked closer to customers than traditional warehouses. The service relies on on-demand Flex drivers and is available 24 hours a day in most areas. Prime members pay a $3.99 fee plus $1.99 for orders under $15, while non-Prime users pay $13.99 plus a $3.99 small-order fee. CEO Andy Jassy has said faster delivery boosts conversion rates and repeat visits.
2. Tesla Slips Ahead of China Trip
★ TSLA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) shares slipped after a five-session run that pushed the stock up nearly 15%, as investors looked ahead to CEO Elon Musk's trip to China with President Trump on Thursday. The visit comes alongside meetings between U.S. and China trade delegations and a separate sit-down with President Xi Jinping that also includes Boeing (NYSE: BA) CEO Kelly Ortberg and Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) CEO Tim Cook.

FSD approval is the prize: The main point of negotiation for Tesla is regulatory approval of its full self-driving (FSD) software in mainland China. Musk previously targeted February or early spring, then pushed the timeline to the third quarter on the Q1 call. Chinese regulators have grown more cautious after Baidu (NASDAQ: BIDU) autonomous vehicles reportedly stopped mid-street, leading to a pause on autonomous vehicle licenses.
China competition heats up: Tesla sold 25,956 vehicles in China in April, down nearly 10% year over year, with its share of the new-energy vehicle market slipping to 3%. Local rivals BYD (OTC: BYDDY), Xiaomi (OTC: XIACY), and Geely (OTC: GELYF) are pressuring Tesla, though FSD approval would be a major competitive edge.
3. Hims & Hers Plunges on Weak Outlook

Hims & Hers Health (NYSE: HIMS) shares tumbled nearly 15% after the telehealth company posted a wider first-quarter loss and weak earnings guidance, signaling a transition period as it moves away from compounded GLP-1 weight loss drugs. Net loss came in at $92 million, up from $50 million a year earlier, while adjusted EBITDA fell to $44 million from $91 million.

Guidance and metrics: Revenue rose just 4% to $608 million, a sharp slowdown from the 111% growth Hims posted in the first quarter of 2025. Average monthly revenue per subscriber slipped to $80 from $85, the first decline in more than a year. The company's $3 billion full-year revenue guide implies decelerating growth through the back half.
GLP-1 pivot: Hims reached a March deal with Novo Nordisk (NYSE: NVO) to sell branded Wegovy on its platform while agreeing to stop advertising compounded copycat versions. The shift follows a contentious dispute in which Novo accused Hims of "illegal mass compounding" that posed patient safety risks. Hims' stock has historically been highly sensitive to news affecting its ability to sell weight loss drugs, a key profit driver.
