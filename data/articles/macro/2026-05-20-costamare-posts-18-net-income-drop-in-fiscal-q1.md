Costamare Posts 18% Net Income Drop in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Revenue and profits fell as core containership operations faced pressure after the dry bulk spin-off.
Earnings per share from continuing operations declined to $0.62 from $0.88 year over year.
Management did not provide updated guidance, but new vessel orders and long-term charters add future revenue visibility.

Costamare (NYSE:CMRE) faced a challenging start to 2026 as profitability slipped amid higher costs and a narrower focus on containerships. The global ship lessor, which completed its dry bulk spin-off last year, reported reduced earnings and lower revenue for the first quarter (released May 20, 2026, covering the period ending March 31). Overall, the quarter marked a transition, with new vessel investments but clear headwinds in its main shipping business.

Notable Developments
Core containership revenue declined by $15.6 million, while direct operating and voyage expenses increased by over $9 million combined.
The NML leaseback segment posted a 116% jump in profit, contributing $6.5 million, but remains a small contributor to overall results.
Costamare maintained its quarterly dividend at $0.115 per share, with newbuild orders for sixteen vessels supported by long-term charter contracts and secured financing.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not issue forward earnings guidance for fiscal 2026, but emphasized significant charter coverage with about $3.6 billion in minimum contracted time charter revenue and a high proportion of its fleet fixed through 2027. Investors should closely watch the impact of rising customer concentration and monitor how new vessel deliveries and leaseback investments contribute to results amid a softer container shipping market.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue dropped primarily due to the absence of dry bulk results after the spin-off last year, and a dip in containership voyage revenue; at the same time, the company faced rising operating costs, particularly for vessel expenses and fuel. The NML leaseback segment, which involves investing in vessels and leasing them back to operators, increased its profit contribution but not enough to offset weakness in the core business.

Costamare placed substantial newbuilding orders in and after the quarter, with sixteen large containerships set to join the fleet between 2027 and 2030, all backed by multi-year charters and financing secured in advance. Customer concentration increased, with its largest customer now accounting for 37% of total revenue, raising potential exposure to counterparty risk.

Operating cash flow declined significantly alongside profits, though the company ended the quarter with $575 million in cash and a stable balance sheet. The dividend remained unchanged, and the company continues to prioritize contracted, long-term fleet employment to underpin steady revenue.

What Costamare does

Costamare provides ship leasing services by owning and chartering large container vessels, primarily on long-term contracts to global liner operators.
