iQIYI Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 1, 2026, 7:07 AM

Comments

iQIYI, Inc. (NASDAQ:IQ) is a leading Chinese online video platform specializing in premium drama, variety, animation, and emerging short-form content. The company’s growth strategy balances blockbuster IP, robust international expansion, and pioneering use of artificial intelligence (AI) to scale content creation and lower costs. iQIYI’s roadmap focuses on deepening member engagement, driving operational efficiency, and transforming into a decentralized platform to unlock value for creators and shareholders alike.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Global Expansion Accelerates with Higher-Value International Membership Growth

International markets are becoming a major revenue engine, outpacing domestic trends and offering higher per-user monetization. In Q1 2026, iQIYI’s overseas membership revenue soared over 40% year over year, with Indonesia up more than 80% and Brazil and Mexico each delivering over 100% annual growth. Notably, average revenue per user (ARPU) from these international subscribers now surpasses that of domestic members. This performance signals that iQIYI’s localization and premium Asian drama strategy resonates with young, female, under-40 audiences in key growth markets. As the company continues to invest in local productions—from “Running Man Thailand” to original Indonesian dramas—there is substantial runway to increase both scale and profitability abroad, helping diversify revenues and offset China’s mature streaming market dynamics.

2. AI-Driven Content Production Dramatically Reduces Costs and Unlocks Margins

The company’s deep investment in AI platforms like Nadou Pro is poised to disrupt the economics of content creation. By Q1 2026, over 10,000 creators had onboarded, producing 3,000 AI-generated micro dramas and more than 14,000 titles overall. This scale is unprecedented and directly impacts production efficiency. Content costs in Q1 2026 fell 2% sequentially to RMB 3.7 billion, while total operating expenses dropped 10%. Management credits AI tools for shorter production cycles and lower variable costs, which, if sustained, present a powerful path to greater operating leverage. As these AI and platform features expand to international creators, iQIYI is positioned to continually expand its content library while flattening or lowering core expenses, potentially delivering superior margins compared to traditional media peers.

3. Premium Content Pipeline Drives Durable Membership and Advertising Uptake

A consistent supply of blockbuster series and innovative formats underpins both member growth and advertising gains. Titles such as “The Punishment 2” and “Pursuit of Jade” exceeded popularity thresholds in Q1 2026, and “Born to Be Alive” was the top-rated domestic drama that quarter on Douban. The Q2 pipeline includes over 100 new short-form dramas for 2026 and a diverse slate of serials, variety, and animation—demonstrating iQIYI’s commitment to content innovation. This diversified pipeline not only supports higher conversion and retention rates for core and premium memberships, but also attracts advertiser demand, evidenced by double-digit annual growth in targeted drama brand ads and more than 60% year-over-year growth in revenue per inventory unit for micro drama ads. Strong content is the backbone driving both sides of the platform’s monetization engine.

4. Regulatory Tailwinds Protect Intellectual Property and Monetization Potential

Crackdowns on piracy and faster regulatory approval cycles in China represent a distinct advantage for iQIYI’s premium content model. Recent directives require platforms to remove infringing content within hours, significantly mitigating the risk of value leakage from copyright breaches. Management expects these anti-piracy campaigns to further bolster the commercial viability of both premium and AI-generated content. This supportive regulatory environment, combined with a robust domestic enforcement apparatus, directly sustains iQIYI’s investments in high-quality IP and reduces industry revenue risks, giving the company greater confidence to innovate and monetize new formats in an increasingly protected market landscape.

5. Strengthened Capital Structure and Shareholder Returns Improve Investment Profile

Disciplined capital allocation is positioning the company for greater financial flexibility and shareholder engagement. As of Q1 2026, iQIYI reported cash, cash equivalents, restricted cash, short-term investments, and long-term restricted cash including prepayments and other assets totaling RMB 4 billion after repurchasing convertible notes and initiating a share repurchase program of up to USD 100 million, with 6.45 million ADSs repurchased for USD 8 million to date. The proposed listing on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange will further broaden access to capital. Early evidence of non-GAAP operating improvements (operating loss margin narrowed to -2% in Q1) and a commitment to returning capital, even as strategic investments continue, support the thesis that iQIYI is transitioning from a growth-at-all-costs model to one focused on sustainable value creation for shareholders.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Reliance on Blockbuster Content Creates Volatility in Revenue and Engagement

Dependence on high-profile drama releases continues to expose the company to quarter-to-quarter swings in both subscriber and ad revenues. iQIYI’s Q1 2026 revenue declined 8% sequentially, largely due to a thinner pipeline of distributable dramas and typical post-holiday seasonality. The company openly acknowledges that when signature titles underperform or are delayed—whether from internal scheduling, regulatory holdups, or intense competition from rival streaming platforms—membership growth and retention can falter. This volatility poses a risk to both forecasting and long-term investor confidence, especially as premium content development remains inherently unpredictable and expensive despite ongoing AI-driven cost optimizations.

2. International Expansion Magnifies Localization and Execution Risks

Rapid growth in overseas markets heightens operational, cultural, and regulatory challenges that could undermine profitability. While international membership revenue growth rates above 40% are impressive, successful long-term scaling requires tailored content strategies, local business model adaptation, and complex partnership ecosystems with telecom and e-commerce platforms. ARPU, churn, and product-market fit can vary widely across Southeast Asia, Latin America, and the Middle East. Additionally, local regulatory changes or new entrants could disrupt progress, while the costs and risks of ongoing market entry campaigns may pressure margins and dilute management attention from the core China business.

3. Advertising and Content Distribution Businesses Remain Vulnerable to Macroeconomic and Sector Volatility

Non-membership revenue streams have shown sharp sequential declines and face ongoing volatility that could weigh on overall financial performance. In Q1 2026, advertising revenue dropped 8% sequentially and content distribution revenue plummeted 54% as fewer dramas were licensed to third parties. While AI tools are improving advertising efficiency, the broader Chinese digital ad market is susceptible to cyclical slowdowns, shifting client budgets, and structural headwinds. Furthermore, content distribution’s lumpy performance underlines how these auxiliary segments can act as a drag on top-line growth and overall margin expansion when hit by weak content cycles or softer macro demand.
