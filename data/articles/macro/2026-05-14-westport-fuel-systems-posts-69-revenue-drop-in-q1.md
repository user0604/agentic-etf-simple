Key Takeaways
The underlying business continues to face headwinds as legacy revenues end and losses widen.
Revenue declined year over year.

Westport Fuel Systems (NASDAQ:WPRT)’s core business contracted sharply amid ongoing structural changes. The developer and manufacturer of alternative fuel, hydrogen, and emissions-reducing solutions for transportation saw a significant drop in consolidated sales after the planned sunset of legacy contracts. The company released first-quarter results for the three months ended March 31, 2026, on May 14, 2026, which revealed a deepening net loss. Overall, the quarter highlights persistent financial pressure despite operational progress in select areas.

Notable developments
Total revenue fell 69% as the Heavy-Duty OEM segment completed its transition and ended legacy business, aligning with prior guidance that forecasted a structural drop in reported sales.
Gross margin improved by two percentage points, even with a much lower top line, reflecting improvement in business mix and cost control in High-Pressure Controls.
Management issued a warning regarding liquidity, stating there is substantial doubt about the company’s ability to fund operations over the next 12 months.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management continues to focus on growing its Cespira joint venture with Volvo Group and expanding High-Pressure Controls while working to reduce costs. Its outlook is cautious, underscoring ongoing concerns about cash sufficiency and the need to secure additional funding. The most important issue for investors is whether the company can resolve its near-term liquidity challenges.

Quarterly highlights

The ending of transitional revenue in Heavy-Duty OEM left the business reliant on Cespira and High-Pressure Controls, both of which delivered operational improvement but are not yet profitable on a company-wide basis. Cespira, the company’s joint venture focused on natural gas systems for heavy-duty vehicles, saw a 33% increase in revenue and reduced net losses compared to the same period last year.

High-Pressure Controls segment revenue grew 21% year over year, supported by higher service revenue for testing and development services provided to an original equipment manufacturer. Gross profit in this segment remained flat, even as the customer mix shifted from product to services.

Despite these operational gains, the underlying company reported its seventh consecutive quarter of consolidated net losses. Cash and cash equivalents fell to $24.5 million, and with a cash outflow continuing, management formally reiterated a "going concern" warning in its filings.
