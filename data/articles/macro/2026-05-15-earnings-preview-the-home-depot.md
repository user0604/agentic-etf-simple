Earnings Preview: The Home Depot

The Home Depot (NYSE:HD), a leading home improvement retailer, is expected to report its first-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on Tuesday, May 19, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, The Home Depot reported net sales of $38.2 billion, reflecting a decrease of 3.8% from the prior year. Comparable sales increased 0.4%, with U.S. comparable sales up 0.3%. Adjusted diluted earnings per share (EPS) for the quarter were $2.72, which met analyst consensus. The company’s performance was influenced by storm activity in January, which provided a modest sales benefit, while the absence of significant storms earlier in the year and ongoing consumer uncertainty pressured results. Gross margin for the quarter was 32.6%, down 20 basis points year over year, in line with expectations and primarily due to the inclusion of the recently acquired GMS business. Operating margin was 10.1%, with adjusted operating margin at 10.5%. The company continued to invest in its stores, digital capabilities, and Pro customer initiatives, while maintaining a disciplined approach to capital allocation.

Positives
Stable underlying demand despite macro pressures: Comparable sales growth of 0.4% shows resilience even with ongoing uncertainty in the housing market and consumer sentiment.
Growth in Pro customer engagement: Pro sales outperformed do-it-yourself (DIY) sales, and the Pro Xtra loyalty program saw continued growth, indicating effective targeting of professional customers.
Strong online sales momentum: Online sales grew approximately 11% year over year for the quarter, reflecting successful investments in digital platforms and fulfillment capabilities.
Successful integration of acquisitions: The GMS acquisition (completed Sept. 4, 2025) contributed to incremental sales and is expected to enhance the company’s distribution capabilities and cross-selling opportunities.
Negatives
Declining earnings per share: Adjusted diluted EPS for the quarter fell 13.1% year over year, reflecting margin pressures and the absence of a 53rd week that benefited the prior year.
Operating margin compression: Operating margin declined to 10.1% from 11.3% a year ago, mainly due to the mix impact from acquisitions and higher operating expenses.
Inventory turnover slowed: Inventory turnover dropped to 4.4 times from 4.7 times, driven by higher inventory levels and the addition of GMS inventory.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Comparable sales performance in a challenging housing market: Monitor whether flat to low-single-digit comparable sales growth continues, as management expects ongoing pressure from low housing turnover and affordability concerns.
Gross margin impact from GMS acquisition: Expect gross margin to be down year over year in Q1, with the largest impact in the first half of the year due to the full-period inclusion of GMS and related integration costs.
Progress on Pro and digital initiatives: Watch for updates on the rollout of new digital planning tools, AI-powered project management features, and enhancements to order management and delivery reliability for Pro customers. Some capabilities are still being implemented in 2026.
Inventory and supply chain management: Assess how inventory levels and turnover are tracking, especially as the company prepares for the spring selling season and manages tariff-related pricing actions.
Expense management and margin outlook: Pay attention to operating expenses as a percentage of sales, which are expected to be at their highest level in Q1, and how this affects profitability in the context of ongoing investments and acquisition integration.
