1. Nvidia Rolls Out Humanoid Robot

★ NVDA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) tapped Chinese start-up Unitree to power its first humanoid robotics system, pairing Unitree's nearly 6-foot-tall H2 robot with Nvidia's Jetson Thor hardware and Blackwell graphics processing unit (GPU). The package targets university researchers and goes on sale later this year as Unitree pursues a $620 million Shanghai initial public offering (IPO). CEO Jensen Huang also unveiled the new N1X PC processor at Taiwan's Computex conference, calling it a "reinvention of the computer" on par with the smartphone's debut.

Physical AI ambitions: Huang has predicted that "physical AI" could become a market worth tens of trillions of dollars. Stanford Robotics Center, ETH Zurich, Seattle-based Ai2, and UC San Diego are among the first research institutions planning to use the H2 Plus robot when it becomes available in October. No China-based research institutions made the launch list, even though Unitree gets over 40% of its revenue from outside China.
Software stocks pile in: Nebius (NASDAQ: NBIS) surged 18%, Arm Holdings (NASDAQ: ARM) jumped 14.7%, ServiceNow (NYSE: NOW) climbed 8.4%, IBM (NYSE: IBM) rose 8.1%, and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (NYSE: HPE) added 5.8% on the N1X news.
2. Smartphone Market Heads for Worst Year Ever
★ AAPL is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

The global smartphone market is on track for its steepest annual contraction on record, with Counterpoint Research projecting shipments will fall 13.9% this year to 1.08 billion units. The squeeze comes from a worsening memory chip shortage, exacerbated by the Iran war and chipmakers shifting capacity to AI production.

Budget phones get squeezed out: Global smartphone wholesale prices rose 14% in the first quarter, while shipments fell 3.1% year over year. Some models priced below $150 are expected to disappear from the market entirely, with Transsion forecast to take a 32% shipment hit this year. Xiaomi and Honor face projected declines of 28% and 20%, respectively.
Premium tier holds up: Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) posted record Q1 revenue thanks to iPhone 17 upgrades, with shipments expected to stay flat this year before rising 5% in 2027. Samsung Electronics (OTC: SSNLF) is forecast to log just a 4% decline thanks to stable chip supply and a consistent product lineup.
3. Walmart Puts a Leash on Its AI Puppy

Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT) is limiting employee use of Code Puppy, an in-house AI agent that helps with spreadsheets and presentations, after staff demand surged past what the retailer planned. Workers previously had unlimited "tokens" -- the unit of data for AI computing -- but now get a capped allotment per person.

AI costs add up: Corporations are running up bills for letting employees use AI freely. Uber (NYSE: UBER) reportedly burned through its entire annual AI budget in just months, and Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) has pulled back on some offerings. Walmart staff still have access to other platforms, like Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT.
Out front on AI: Walmart has moved faster than most retail peers on AI integration, from supply chain management to its Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) partnership for AI-assisted shopping on Gemini. The Bentonville giant has been gaining market share with its focus on low prices, fast delivery, and product breadth.
