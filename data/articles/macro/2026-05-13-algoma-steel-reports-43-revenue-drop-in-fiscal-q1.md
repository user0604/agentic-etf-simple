Algoma Steel Reports 43% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 6:22 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Algoma Steel Group reported a loss as it transitioned fully to electric arc furnace steelmaking.
Adjusted EBITDA loss was $28.7 million, narrowing compared to the previous year.
Management expects further improvements as new production units ramp up and cost pressures ease.

The first quarter reflected a transitional loss for Algoma Steel Group (NASDAQ:ASTL) as it completed the switch from blast furnace operations to its new electric arc furnace system. The company is a leading Canadian manufacturer of plate and flat steel products, serving industries like infrastructure, construction, and defense. In its earnings release dated May 12, 2026 (for the three months ended March 31, 2026), Algoma detailed lower volumes, higher tariffs, and continued negative cash generation as it repositioned its business model. Overall, the quarter marked progress within a strategic shift, but core challenges remain as the company ramps up new technology.

Notable Developments
Algoma shifted entirely to electric arc furnace steelmaking, shutting down its blast furnace in January and achieving 24-hour EAF operations.
Steel shipments fell steeply to 223,681 tons, but plate sales reached a record 116,000 tons, reflecting a new product focus.
Direct tariff expenses rose to $27.4 million, nearly triple the previous year's cost, as U.S. sales dropped to 28% of shipments.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects sequential improvement as it finishes commissioning its second electric arc furnace and focuses production on higher-margin plate products. The most important factor for investors is whether Algoma can grow plate volumes and reduce overhead costs quickly enough to offset lost revenue from U.S. markets and legacy operations. Ongoing tariff costs and demand trends in infrastructure and defense will be pivotal through the rest of fiscal 2026.

Quarterly Highlights

This was Algoma’s first full quarter making steel solely with electric arc furnace technology, ending 125 years of coal-based operations. The company operated Unit 1 around the clock, reported strong quality, and plans to have Unit 2 running by the third quarter. Plate steel production, crucial for infrastructure and defense, reached a record high and now dominates Algoma’s shipments.

Despite the switch in technology, total steel shipments dropped over 50% compared to the same period last year. Canadian market oversupply and tariffs that raised U.S. export costs led to sharply reduced volumes, although sales prices per ton rose, reflecting the strategic shift toward plate. Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed versus the previous quarter, mainly due to improved product mix and initial gains in cost efficiency, but remained negative as legacy costs and tariffs weighed on margins.

On the cost side, direct tariff expenses nearly tripled from the prior year, and fixed costs remained high as production volumes were still ramping up. Liquidity stood at $553 million at quarter end, but operating cash flow remained negative. Management described these headwinds as temporary, anticipating further improvement as the new operations stabilize and the business pivots fully to serving domestic, higher-value customers.

What Algoma Steel Group Does

Algoma Steel Group produces plate and sheet steel products for sectors such as infrastructure, construction, automotive, and defense, with a manufacturing base in Sault Ste. Marie, Canada.
