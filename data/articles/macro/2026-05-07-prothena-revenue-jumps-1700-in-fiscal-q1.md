Key Takeaways
Prothena delivered a quarter driven by a large partnership milestone.
Earnings shifted to profit as revenue increased sharply year over year.
Management improved its full-year cash and net loss outlook, with future results reliant on milestone payments and clinical progress.

Prothena (NASDAQ:PRTA) shifted to profitability on the back of a major partnership milestone but faces questions over the long-term sustainability of its earnings. The neuroscience-focused company, which develops therapies for conditions like Parkinson’s, Alzheimer’s, and rare diseases, announced its results for the three months ended March 31, 2026, on May 7, 2026. Overall, the period saw substantial growth in revenue from milestone payments, alongside lower research and administrative spending, leading management to improve its full-year guidance.

Notable Developments
Revenue surged as the company received a $50 million milestone payment from its partnership with Novo Nordisk (NYSE:NVO), a sharp increase from the prior year.
Expenses fell considerably, with research and development spending down around 75%, helping drive the shift from a net loss to profitability.
Cash reserves increased, ending the quarter at $330.3 million, partly due to the milestone payment and lower spending.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management now expects full-year 2026 net cash use of $18–23 million, a significant improvement from its prior forecast, and projects ending the year with about $273 million in cash. The outlook points to continued reliance on milestone revenue from strategic partners as pivotal studies progress. Investors should watch for upcoming decisions on additional milestone payments and clinical trial updates, particularly those with Bristol Myers Squibb (NYSE:BMY) and Roche (SWX:ROG).

Quarterly Highlights

Milestone revenue was the main driver of Prothena’s results this quarter. Nearly all of the $51.1 million revenue came from a single $50 million payment linked to progress on coramitug, a therapy partnered with Novo Nordisk. This influx transformed both the top and bottom line for the period, offsetting historically low operating expenses.

Research and development costs, which cover expenses for advancing drug candidates such as prasinezumab for Parkinson’s disease and BMS-986446 for Alzheimer’s, dropped to $12.6 million. This decrease, the largest for any recent quarter, resulted from the timing of development activities and reduced project spending.

Prothena repurchased shares valued at $7.3 million through March and spent a total of $15 million by the end of April, as part of its approved share buyback program. Management maintained a debt-free balance sheet, providing flexibility for future drug development and partnership activities.
