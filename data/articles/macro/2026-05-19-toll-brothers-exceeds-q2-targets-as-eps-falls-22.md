Key Takeaways
Toll Brothers delivered a business win by exceeding its own revenue and contract targets.
Earnings per share fell 22.3%, while revenue declined 7.6% year over year.

Toll Brothers (NYSE:TOL) delivered better-than-expected home sales revenue and contract activity in its second fiscal quarter, but faced headwinds with lower year-over-year earnings and slimmer profit margins. The luxury homebuilder, known for its focus on high-end residential developments, reported its results for the quarter ending April 30, 2026, with the release published on May 19, 2026. Overall, the quarter displayed strong demand and expanded community count, but operating profits and gross margins softened compared to the same period last year.

Notable Developments
Sales rose above guidance, with revenue $110 million over the company’s outlook and net signed contract value of $2.81 billion.
Profitability declined, as earnings per share dropped to $2.72 from $3.50 and adjusted gross margin narrowed by 1.3 percentage points.
The company expanded its active communities by 9% and raised its quarterly dividend 4% to $0.26 per share.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased its full-year projections for home deliveries, average selling price, and adjusted gross margin after the quarter’s results outpaced earlier expectations. They now guide for up to 10,700 home deliveries and a higher community count by fiscal year end. Investors will want to watch gross margin and inventory writedown trends, as ongoing cost pressures and impairments impacted profit growth.

Quarterly Highlights

The period saw Toll Brothers exceed internal benchmarks on both the number of homes delivered and sales value, indicating ongoing demand within the luxury segment. Net signed contract value, which measures the dollar total of new home contracts, increased to $2.81 billion, reflecting greater buyer activity than one year prior.

However, lower home deliveries and higher cost items, such as inventory impairments, led to a fall in earnings from $3.50 to $2.72 per share compared to last year. Gross margin—representing the share of revenue left after paying for construction and materials—continued to shrink, and general and administrative costs rose as a percentage of sales.

On the balance sheet, the company finished the quarter with $1.11 billion in cash and launched significant share repurchases. The company’s community count reached 459, up from 421 the previous year, pointing to its ongoing investment in new developments and land acquisition to support future growth.
