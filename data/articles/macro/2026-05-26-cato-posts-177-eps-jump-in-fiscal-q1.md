Key Takeaways
Profit rose sharply, but gains were significantly boosted by a one-time tariff refund.
Earnings per share climbed to $0.47, up 176.5% year over year.
Management signaled caution as softening sales and inflation pressures weigh on the outlook.

Cato (NYSE:CATO) posted sharply higher profits as a one-time tariff refund drove a big jump in its quarterly earnings. The specialty retailer operates over a thousand stores focused on affordable, on-trend women’s fashion and accessories. The results, announced on May 26, 2026 and covering the quarter ended May 2, showed minimal sales growth even as cost measures helped boost margins. Overall, the quarter was marked by strong headline earnings but mixed underlying performance.

Notable Developments
Profitability soared mostly due to a $5.7 million one-time refund for past tariffs under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.
Revenue increased only slightly, and management noted that sales momentum worsened in the latter part of the period.
Store count shrank by 44 year over year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expressed caution about future quarters, pointing to inflation and higher fuel and food prices that are likely to weigh on shoppers’ discretionary spending. The company flagged that sales trends weakened as the period progressed, suggesting continued challenges ahead.

Quarterly Highlights

The bulk of the earnings gain resulted from an unusually large, nonrecurring tariff refund which lifted gross margin to 37.2% from 35.1% a year ago. Absent this item, profit growth would have been much less significant, as core merchandise sales faced pressure from increased promotions and markdowns.

Sales growth was minimal, with total revenue edging up just 0.5% and same-store sales (an important retail benchmark tracking sales at stores open at least a year) rising 3%. However, management cited declining trends later in the quarter and ongoing customer strain from rising prices for essentials.

Cato continued paring its store base, ending the period with 1,065 locations, a decrease of 44 stores year over year. The company also kept a tight rein on costs, reducing selling, general and administrative expense by 2.5% compared to the prior year period.

What Cato Does

Cato is a specialty retailer offering value-focused women’s apparel and accessories through its network of strip mall-based stores and its own brands.
