Key Takeaways
Revenue and profits surged on strong royalty gains despite higher expenses.
Non-GAAP earnings per share increased 44% year over year.

Drug delivery specialist Halozyme Therapeutics (NASDAQ:HALO) opened fiscal 2026 with a strong showing, as both revenue and earnings climbed sharply, driven by growth in its royalty portfolio. The company, which provides subcutaneous drug delivery technologies to leading pharmaceutical partners, posted its results for the first quarter on May 11, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed broad-based strength in both core partnerships and new initiatives, but costs also moved higher.

Notable Developments
Royalty revenue jumped 43% year over year, reaching $240.7 million, with new approvals and expanding indications for key partner drugs.
Operating expenses rose 56%, in part due to higher costs for patent litigation and investments in R&D and acquisitions.
Management announced a $1 billion share repurchase program through 2028, with at least $400 million targeted in 2026.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook for double-digit revenue, royalty, and earnings growth. It expects royalty gains to accelerate in the back half of the year as additional products launch and earn milestone payments. Investors should watch whether new product approvals and technology deals fuel continued royalty growth and help offset any persistent cost pressures, especially in litigation.

Quarterly Highlights

Royalty income formed the backbone of the period’s strong revenue, up 43% with the ENHANZE subcutaneous drug delivery platform continuing to see broad adoption. Blockbuster partner products, such as subcutaneous formulations of anti-cancer and autoimmune drugs, won new FDA approvals for more indications and at-home use.

On the partnership front, the company secured three new collaboration agreements, including with Vertex and GSK, focused on Hypercon and ENHANZE. This expands Halozyme’s pool of long-term royalty streams, with the first product approvals expected at the start of the next decade.

Operating expenses climbed at a faster rate than revenue, largely due to increased legal costs linked to ongoing patent disputes and higher spending for recent acquisitions and research. Litigation costs alone contributed $11.2 million to expenses this quarter, a potential margin pressure if they persist.

What Halozyme Therapeutics Does

Halozyme Therapeutics develops and licenses drug delivery technologies that enable pharmaceutical partners to convert intravenous medicines into faster, more convenient subcutaneous injections.
