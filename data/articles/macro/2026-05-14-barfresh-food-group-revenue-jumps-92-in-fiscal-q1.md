Barfresh Food Group Revenue Jumps 92% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 4:25 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Barfresh Food Group started fiscal 2026 with strong revenue growth, but profitability continues to lag. Revenue grew 92% year over year, driven by the Arps Dairy acquisition. Management maintained its full-year targets, with margin improvement expected later in the year.

Barfresh Food Group (NASDAQ:BRFH) reported a business win as sales rose sharply thanks to the first full quarter of operating its newly acquired dairy facility. The beverage maker, known for single-serve smoothies and innovative frozen drinks for institutional markets, benefited from a boosted product mix and expanded manufacturing. The company released results for its first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026, highlighting major operational changes over the past year. Overall, the quarter underscored rapid scale-up, but also ongoing pressure on margins as integration costs and lower-margin milk sales weighed on profitability.

Notable Developments
Revenue surged 92%, reaching $5.6 million, mainly driven by the Arps Dairy milk business acquired in 2025.
Gross margin dropped to 18% from 31%, reflecting the lower-margin profile of the dairy segment and costs from the new facility ramp-up.
Selling, marketing, and distribution expenses decreased 15% to $0.70 million, thanks to greater use of brokers and reduced product sampling costs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated full-year guidance of $28–$32 million in revenue and $3.2–$3.8 million in adjusted EBITDA, expecting margin recovery in the second half of the year as the new plant reaches full efficiency. For the next quarter, it projects revenue of $5.2–$5.6 million and a smaller adjusted EBITDA loss. Investors should monitor progress on facility upgrades and signs of improving profit margins as increased capacity and efficiency take hold.

Quarterly Highlights

The company broke past its own Q1 revenue forecast of $5.0–$5.2 million, supported by stronger-than-expected sales of Arps Dairy’s raw and processed milk. While increased scale brought in more business, much of this came from product lines that earn lower profit per dollar of sales compared to legacy frozen beverage products.

Despite nearly doubling revenue, gross margin compressed by 13 percentage points, a challenge management attributes to startup costs in the new Ohio facility and the impact of the acquired dairy operation. The company says these inefficiencies are temporary and expects productivity to improve as volumes and manufacturing optimization increase.

Selling, marketing, and distribution expenses dropped year over year due to a shift toward broker-led sales, fewer sampling events, and a product mix that requires less equipment support. This signals early success in cost containment, even as Barfresh ramps investment in capacity and integration.

What Barfresh Food Group Does

Barfresh Food Group develops and supplies ready-to-blend and ready-to-drink beverages and dairy products to institutional customers such as schools and foodservice distributors.
