Top of the Morning

4:15 am

By Morning Show host Jim Mueller, CFA
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I write for a living.

Well, I do other things like analyze companies, follow earnings, design option strategies, etc. But it eventually comes down to writing. I put my ideas down on (electronic) paper and use that process for two things.

First, it helps me think. It helps me organize and present my thoughts in a clear and cogent manner. It helps me make sure that my arguments are sensible and presented clearly. Sometimes it even helps me realize that what I’m writing about is absolutely bat-**** weird and I better go back to the drawing board for a while. (Thankfully, not often.)

Second, it helps the reader, the user of the content I produce. It lets them grow to know me because I have a distinct writing style. All writers develop a voice, a way of expressing themselves that can be as unique as a finger print. (Several years ago, one person at the Fool told me that if he read a piece of mine with the author’s name covered, he could tell it was written by me pretty early on. Similarly, I can often identify a Jim Gillies piece within the first 50 words.)

Further, it lets the reader grow to trust me, to trust what I have to say and to at least think about it. For someone making investment recommendations, that’s important. I mean, why should you put money into something from someone you don’t know?

Do you know who (or what) cannot write? AI.

Sure, it can produce electronically-displayed words that make sense. Especially if there’s a human to check it out first before the words are released into the wild. (Recognizing sarcasm – or satire! – can be tough for a predictive algorithm, which is what all AI is, after all. Better not to ask it to give a summary of Gulliver’s Travels or something similar.)

But there’s no soul. No heart. No voice.

Worse, there’s no thought behind it. There’s no crafting in order to pick the exact right words to write. (That kind wordplay is a part of my voice, for example.) It’s simply answering its own eternal question: What word most likely follows the last word used given what I’ve been fed?

Thankfully, school teachers are beginning to pick up on this. More are requiring writing assignments to be done in the classroom, not at home, where smart (lazy) students get some AI to write out what the student is assigned to do. Even better, the assignments are beginning to require interpretation and thought, not just regurgitation. AI is good at the latter, not so good with the former. That still requires human input.

When you read investment analysis or recommendations, how much is being crafted by a human and how much is being spawned by AI? (More of that voice for you. “Spawned” conjures up such wonderful images!) Unfortunately, the fraction that’s spawned is likely large and growing.

When you make your investing decisions, take a moment to wonder if both the source of the idea and the write up was from an actual person who put some work into it, or the product of some predictive computer algorithm. Thankfully, we can still usually tell the difference, at least for the writing part.
