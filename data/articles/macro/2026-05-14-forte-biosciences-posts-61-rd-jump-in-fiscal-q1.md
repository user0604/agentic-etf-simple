Key Takeaways

Forte Biosciences (NASDAQ:FBRX) disappointed some with increased losses but delivered a regulatory milestone for its experimental therapy. The clinical-stage biotech focuses exclusively on advancing FB102, a treatment for autoimmune disorders like celiac disease, vitiligo, and alopecia areata. The company reported its first quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026, highlighting growing research investment but no revenue as it remains pre-commercial. Overall, the quarter saw regulatory progress, heavier spending, and strengthened funding due to a post-quarter capital raise.

Notable Developments
FB102 received Fast Track designation from the FDA for celiac disease, a major regulatory milestone for the pipeline.
Research and development expenses surged 61% from a year ago as overlapping clinical trials drove higher costs.
The company completed a $172.5 million capital raise in April, issuing 6.6 million additional shares of common stock, which substantially increased the total share count.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its timeline: key clinical readouts for FB102 in celiac disease are expected in 2026, with vitiligo data expected shortly and alopecia areata readouts due next year. The company expects research spending to remain high as multiple trials progress in parallel. Investors should watch for the upcoming clinical trial results as the critical determinant of future prospects and funding needs.

Quarterly Highlights

Progress for FB102 was highlighted by the FDA Fast Track designation, which signals regulatory support for accelerated development in celiac disease but does not guarantee approval. This development aligns with management’s prior guidance and supports plans to advance FB102 through later-stage trials.

Operating expenses expanded sharply, mainly due to higher clinical trial and personnel costs as the company advanced Phase 2 and Phase 1b studies. While general and administrative expenses declined year over year following a one-off legal settlement in the prior year, research costs far outpaced these savings.

A public equity offering completed in April provided $172.5 million in fresh capital and increased the number of outstanding shares by 6.6 million. This move strengthens the company's funding position into 2026, but also raises dilution risk, making clinical progress all the more critical for value creation.
