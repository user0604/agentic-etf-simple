Costamare Bulkers Reports 97% Utilization in Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 7:39 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Costamare Bulkers reported steady operational momentum in its first post-spin-off quarter.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) reached $0.51 as liquidity stayed high and utilization stood at 97.4%.
Management expects the transition out of legacy trading positions to complete by year end.

Costamare Bulkers (NYSE:CMDB) delivered a solid debut quarter, showing early resilience after separating from its parent. The dry bulk shipping specialist reported its first results as a standalone company for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (released May 13, 2026). Overall, the period indicated robust fleet performance but highlighted the impact of short-term transition costs as the firm settles into its independent structure.

Notable Developments
The company became net cash positive, holding $353.3 million in liquidity at quarter-end and keeping total cash ahead of debt by $127.2 million.
It completed a vessel sale resulting in a capital gain of $7.0 million and purchased a newer vessel as part of its fleet renewal efforts.
Legacy trading portfolio exposure was largely transferred to Cargill, with management expecting complete de-risking by year end.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Costamare Bulkers' management signaled confidence in completing the exit from legacy trading positions by year end. It continues to focus on counter-cyclical growth and fleet modernization, leveraging its net cash position to remain flexible. The top issue to monitor is the wind-down of residual legacy trades and the associated impact on financial clarity and recurring earnings.

Quarterly Highlights

Liquidity remained a highlight, with the company reporting $353.3 million in available cash and credit. This net cash position strengthens its ability to pursue potential vessel acquisitions and supports flexibility during industry cycles.

Fleet renewal advanced as the firm sold its older Capesize vessel, Miracle, securing a $7.0 million gain, and acquired a modern Ultramax-class vessel. Utilization of owned dry bulk vessels reached 97.4%, signaling strong operational efficiency.

Operationally, the transition of most legacy trading activity to Cargill reduced risk but brought $5.1 million in transition-related charges for the period. With much of the trading portfolio exited, the company is repositioning its operating platform around Kamsarmax-type vessels and index-linked charters, which allow it to capture market upturns but also increase earnings variability.

What Costamare Bulkers Does

Costamare Bulkers operates a fleet of dry bulk cargo ships, transporting raw materials such as iron ore, coal, and grain worldwide with a focus on period charters and asset management.
