Applied Materials Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 28, 2026, 7:09 AM

Comments

Applied Materials, Inc. (NASDAQ:AMAT) is the world’s leading supplier of semiconductor manufacturing equipment, services, and software used in chip fabrication and packaging. The company’s growth strategy centers on capturing market share in leading-edge chip nodes and packaging, capitalizing on structural tailwinds from artificial intelligence (AI)-driven semiconductor demand, advancing its innovation ecosystem through collaborative R&D, and expanding a high-margin services business that leverages its vast installed base of equipment worldwide.

Potential Bull Cases
1. AI Infrastructure Boom Propels Multiyear Growth and Visibility

The secular surge in AI-related semiconductor demand is fueling historic growth rates and long-term revenue visibility for Applied Materials. The company’s management projects its semiconductor equipment business will expand by more than 30% year over year in calendar 2026, with further robust growth possible in 2027 and 2028. This momentum is underpinned by cloud and hyperscale data center operators, who are increasing capital spending to support generative AI, agentic AI, and high-bandwidth memory. Customers such as TSMC, Samsung, and Micron are providing the company with 8-quarter rolling forecasts to secure capacity, a level of demand visibility rarely seen in the industry. This clarity allows Applied Materials to plan and invest confidently, reduce order volatility, and optimize manufacturing, all of which position the company for sustained, above-market growth as AI infrastructure buildouts accelerate into the decade.

2. Leadership in Next-Generation Chipmaking Technologies Drives Share Gains

Applied Materials’ leadership in key technology inflections such as gate-all-around (GAA) transistors, advanced DRAM, and 3D packaging enables it to capture outsize growth and market share. The company is recognized as the top provider for advanced process equipment in leading-edge logic, DRAM, and high-bandwidth memory, and recently launched industry-first products like the Trillium ALD for advanced gate stacks. Its role as a process innovator was further strengthened by the acquisition of NEXX, greatly expanding its advanced packaging capabilities at a time when chiplets and heterogeneous integration are critical for AI workloads. Management expects packaging revenue alone to rise by over 50% in calendar 2026, with these technology inflections unlocking higher value per tool and deeper customer relationships at the most advanced nodes.

3. Expanding Services Business Offers Stable, High-Margin Recurring Revenue

The company’s Applied Global Services (AGS) segment is rapidly growing, providing a recurring, defensive revenue stream and margin resilience. In Q2 2026, AGS delivered record revenue of $1.67 billion, up 17% year over year, as more than 35,000 chambers worldwide are now connected to Applied’s AI-driven diagnostic platforms. This services business benefits from an expanding installed base, elevated fab utilization rates, and higher value-added service attach rates. Management has upgraded its medium-term AGS growth target to “mid-teens” annually and expects even faster growth near term. The recurring nature and high margins associated with AGS help offset hardware cycle volatility, support consistent cash flow, and add balance to the overall financial profile of the company.

4. Robust Financial Execution Enables Capital Returns and Strategic Investment

Applied Materials’ record financial performance supports aggressive reinvestment in the business and substantial shareholder returns. In Q2 2026, the company reported non-GAAP revenue of $7.91 billion (up 11% year over year), non-GAAP gross margin topping 50% (the highest in 25 years), and record non-GAAP earnings per share of $2.86 (up 20% year over year). The company allocated $765 million in the quarter to dividends and buybacks, including a 15% dividend hike that achieved its goal of doubling the dividend per share. However, free cash flow declined sharply year over year in Q2 2026. With continued operating leverage, prudent cost discipline, and ample liquidity, Applied Materials is positioned to both invest for future growth (such as expanding the EPIC Center and manufacturing capacity) and reward shareholders consistently.

5. Strategic R&D and Partnerships Cement Technology Pipeline and Ecosystem Influence

Sustained investment in R&D, co-development partnerships, and industry alliances consolidates Applied Materials’ critical role in the chipmaking ecosystem. The $5 billion EPIC Center in Silicon Valley, set to open in Fall 2026, is already drawing major foundry, memory, and design partners for joint development of next-generation manufacturing technologies. Recent announcements include co-innovation deals with TSMC, Samsung, and Broadcom, as well as collaborations with research universities such as Stanford, ASU, and RPI. These relationships provide deeper, early access to customer roadmaps, accelerate technology commercialization, and create a high barrier to entry for competitors, further securing Applied’s leadership position as process complexity increases across chip generations.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Geopolitical Tensions and Regulatory Risks Threaten Revenue Stability

Ongoing U.S.-China restrictions and export controls present a material risk to Applied Materials’ near-term and long-term revenue. In the second quarter of 2026, China accounted for approximately 24% of the company’s Semiconductor Systems and AGS revenue. Recent actions by the U.S. Commerce Department, which restricted tool shipments to Chinese chipmakers like Hua Hong, underscore the unpredictability of global policy shifts. Further tightening of export controls or new restrictions on technology transfer could disrupt revenue streams from China, create demand volatility, and force the company to redeploy capacity at potentially lower margins in unconstrained regions. Management states these risks are already reflected in guidance, but the frequency and scale of regulatory changes could still impact top-line growth and strategic planning.

2. Persistent Supply Chain Constraints May Hinder Order Fulfillment and Margin Progress

Despite extensive investments in manufacturing and logistics, the company’s ability to meet surging demand remains vulnerable to supplier bottlenecks and industry-wide shortages. Applied Materials has nearly doubled its manufacturing capacity and is actively increasing inventory to respond to customer needs. However, the ramp in demand for advanced semiconductor tools involves working with around 2,000 suppliers and orchestrating highly complex parts flows. The company explicitly cites supply chain ramp—not internal capacity—as the key limiting factor in its near-term guidance. Any escalation in component shortages, logistics disruptions, or delays in supplier readiness could slow shipment releases, create order backlog pressure, and constrain gross margin expansion at a time of strong market opportunity.

3. Customer and End-Market Concentration Increases Exposure to Cyclical Swings

Revenue remains heavily concentrated among a small number of leading semiconductor companies, increasing sensitivity to order shifts and technology transitions. Even as the company’s exposure diversifies with growth in packaging and services, the bulk of systems revenue is still tied to major foundries, integrated device manufacturers, and memory makers who provide long-term forecasts but remain susceptible to capital spending pauses or technology changes. The pace of over 100 global greenfield fab projects, while offering visibility, could introduce volatility if any large customer slows investment due to macro weakness, overcapacity, or alternative sourcing. This concentration risk could magnify cyclical downturns and challenge efforts to maintain consistent earnings during inevitable industry slowdowns.
