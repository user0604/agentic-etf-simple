Earnings Preview: Ambarella

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 11:03 AM

Comments

Ambarella (NASDAQ:AMBA), a provider of low-power chips and software for artificial intelligence (AI) in cameras and edge devices, is expected to report its next quarterly earnings on Thursday, May 28, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2026 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2026, Ambarella reported revenue of $100.9 million, which was up 20.1% from the same period last year and slightly above the midpoint of its guidance. The company’s non-GAAP gross margin was 59.8%, also at the midpoint of guidance. Non-GAAP net profit for the quarter was $5.5 million, or $0.13 per diluted share. Ambarella’s performance met analyst expectations for both earnings per share and sales. The company highlighted strong growth in its edge AI business, which now accounts for about 80% of total revenue. Full-year revenue reached a record $390.7 million, up 37.2% year over year, outpacing the broader semiconductor industry.

Positives
Record annual revenue and strong growth in edge AI: Ambarella’s full-year revenue rose 37.2%, driven by high demand for its AI-enabled chips, especially in the Internet of Things (IoT) and automotive markets. This growth outpaced most of its semiconductor peers.
Return to non-GAAP profitability and positive free cash flow: The company achieved non-GAAP profitability for the full year and generated over $58 million in free cash flow, showing improved operational efficiency and financial health.
Successful new product launches and customer wins: The CV75 and CV72 chips, based on advanced five-nanometer technology, contributed to revenue and saw early adoption in applications like robotics, video conferencing, and automotive cameras. Ambarella also entered production with a major warehouse automation customer in the U.S.
Healthy balance sheet and cash position: Cash and marketable securities increased to $312.6 million at year-end, giving the company flexibility to invest in growth and weather market fluctuations.
Negatives
Sequential revenue decline in Q4: Revenue fell 7% from the previous quarter, following typical seasonality, but still may be seen as a slowdown after a strong year.
Gross margin pressure: The non-GAAP gross margin for the year dipped to 60.7% from 62.7% in the prior year, mainly due to higher manufacturing costs associated with advanced chip technologies.
Operating expenses continue to rise: Non-GAAP operating expenses increased 12.9% year over year, reflecting higher personnel and development costs as Ambarella invests in new products and markets.
Concentration of revenue among a few customers: One logistics partner, WT Microelectronics, accounted for over 70% of quarterly revenue, indicating ongoing customer concentration risk.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2027's Report
Progress of new product ramps: Investors should look for updates on the adoption and revenue contribution from the latest chips, including the CV75, CV72, and the newly announced CV7, which is Ambarella’s first four-nanometer product. Management expects CV7 to begin generating revenue in the fourth quarter of the current year.
Trends in automotive and IoT revenue mix: The company guided for both auto and IoT segments to grow in fiscal 2027. Watch for any shifts in the balance between these segments, as well as commentary on new design wins or customer launches.
Gross margin outlook and cost pressures: Ambarella expects gross margin to remain within its long-term model of 59% to 62%. Any mention of component cost inflation, especially for memory chips, or changes in product mix could affect profitability.
Updates on indirect sales channel and semi-custom ASIC business: The company has announced initiatives to expand its go-to-market strategy through indirect channels and a new semi-custom ASIC (application-specific integrated circuit) business. Investors should look for milestones, such as new partnerships or design wins, and any early signs of revenue impact, though management does not expect significant contributions this year.
Cash flow and capital allocation: Monitor free cash flow generation and any changes to the company’s share repurchase program, as well as updates on capital expenditures for product development and infrastructure.
