Cerebras Systems IPO: Your Cheat Sheet

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 8:00 AM

Cerebras Systems (NASDAQ: CBRS) went public on May 14, 2026, after one of the most heavily oversubscribed IPOs of the year. Shares were priced at $185, above the marketed range of $150 to $160, valuing the company at roughly $56.4 billion on a fully diluted basis. The IPO raised $5.55 billion, making it the largest stock market debut of the year so far.

This briefing is based on the company's S-1/A filed May 4, 2026.

Key Takeaways
Cerebras builds the only working "wafer-scale" AI chip in the world, a processor 58 times bigger than Nvidia's (NASDAQ: NVDA) flagship B200. Its systems run AI models up to 15 times faster than Nvidia-based systems.
Revenue grew 76% in 2025 to $510 million, but two UAE customers (G42 and MBZUAI) accounted for 86% of that revenue.
Two new contracts signed in early 2026 -- a massive multiyear OpenAI deal and an AWS partnership to deploy Cerebras chips in Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) data centers -- lessen UAE customer concentration. But they also create new dependency on two giant customers with strict delivery terms.
The Business

Cerebras makes specialized AI chips and the full computers that hold them. Its core invention is to take an entire silicon wafer (the round disc chipmakers normally cut into dozens of small chips) and use the whole thing as one giant chip. Nvidia and everyone else chops their wafers up and wires the pieces back together with expensive networking gear, which slows things down. Cerebras keeps it whole, so data doesn't have to leave the chip. That's why its systems run AI faster and use less power. Customers buy hardware to install in their own data centers (70% of 2025 revenue), rent capacity through the Cerebras cloud, or access it through marketplaces from AWS, Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT), and IBM (NYSE: IBM) (30%).

Cerebras is the only company that has ever commercialized a wafer-scale processor. It took a decade of solving yield, cooling, and power-delivery problems no one else has cracked. But Cerebras faces a huge competitive disadvantage in scale. Nvidia generated nearly $194 billion in data center revenue last fiscal year, which was roughly 380 times what Cerebras did. And Nvidia ships faster chips every year.

Green Flags
Revenue is compounding fast. Revenue is large and still growing. Cerebras grew revenue 76% in 2025 to $510 million, up from $25 million in 2022. Its 76% growth rate is strong for a chip company even if it's stabilizing from earlier years.
Existing customers keep spending more. The top 10 2025 customers increased their spending with Cerebras by 80% within 12 months of first purchase.
$25 billion in contracted backlog. Cerebras expects to recognize 15% of that in 2026-2027 and 43% in 2028-2029.
OpenAI and AWS picked Cerebras over Nvidia alternatives. OpenAI signed a $20-billion-plus, multiyear deal to deploy 750 megawatts of Cerebras compute and co-design future models. AWS will become the first hyperscaler to deploy Cerebras chips in its own data centers.
Red Flags
Customer concentration is severe. G42 and MBZUAI (UAE-based and related parties to each other) accounted for 86% of 2025 revenue. Cerebras' S-1 names OpenAI, G42, MBZUAI, and AWS as customers whose departure "would harm our business."
The OpenAI deal can be cancelled. If Cerebras misses data center capacity delivery tranches, OpenAI can terminate part or all of its $20 billion contract. OpenAI also lent Cerebras $1 billion that can be called back if certain triggers occur.
Material weaknesses in internal financial controls. Cerebras disclosed that it lacks staff with the expertise to review reconciliations and apply standard accounting rules in several areas, including revenue. Investors should expect possible restatements in early public quarters.
Its 2025 profit isn't from operations. Cerebras reported $238 million in net income, but $363 million of that came from a one-time accounting gain on a preferred stock contract. The underlying business lost $146 million.
Public shareholders have no voting power. Class B insider shares get 20 votes each; Class A public shares get 1. Insiders control 99.2% of votes post-IPO with no sunset clause.
Bottom Line

But a $48.8 billion fully diluted valuation works out to roughly 95 times trailing 2025 revenue, while Nvidia trades closer to 25 times and is profitable. For investors who believe Cerebras' wafer-scale advantage can defend itself as inference demand explodes, the technology case justifies a closer look despite concentration risk. For everyone else, watching it for a few quarters first might be the better call.
