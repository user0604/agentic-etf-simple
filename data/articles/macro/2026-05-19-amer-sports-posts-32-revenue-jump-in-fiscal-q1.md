Key Takeaways
Amer Sports delivered a strong business win, surpassing its own revenue and earnings guidance. Adjusted EPS climbed 41% year over year. Management raised its full-year 2026 outlook across all key metrics.

Performance apparel leader Amer Sports (NYSE:AS) posted a robust start to fiscal 2026, propelled by double-digit sales growth across all product segments and geographies. The company, known for brands like Arc'teryx, Salomon, and Wilson, released its first-quarter results on May 19, 2026, reporting broad-based momentum well above the upper end of its previous guidance. The quarter showed rising profitability and strong sales, although ongoing inventory build and sports segment margin pressure remain issues to watch.

Notable Developments
Revenue outpaced management's prior guidance, with total sales up 32%, driven by strong gains in Technical Apparel and Outdoor Performance segments.
Gross margin improved by over 2 percentage points, supported by a favorable product and channel mix, while adjusted operating profit rose 46% versus last year.
Inventory grew in line with sales, ending the quarter up 33%, with management stressing the importance of monitoring inventory quality and levels going forward.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 guidance following the strong quarter, now targeting revenue growth of 20% to 22%, higher profit margins, and increased adjusted earnings. For the rest of the year, investors should closely monitor the pace of inventory growth and margin trends in Ball & Racquet Sports, as further margin compression or slow-selling stock could challenge results even as demand remains high in core categories.

Quarterly Highlights

Technical Apparel, including brands like Arc'teryx, drove substantial growth with revenue up 33% year over year and an adjusted segment margin improvement of 2.5 percentage points. Outdoor Performance, which covers outdoor equipment and softgoods, saw a 42% revenue jump and nearly five points of margin expansion, largely fueled by continued momentum in Salomon Softgoods. Ball & Racquet Sports posted revenue gains, but experienced a significant drop in segment margin, falling to 3.6%.

Direct-to-consumer channels stood out, with DTC revenue surpassing $1 billion and growing 44.6% year over year, outpacing wholesale channel growth. Store expansion supported this multi-channel strategy, with 722 locations open at quarter’s end, a 39% increase from the year before. Management called out this retail growth as critical to its broader omnichannel ambitions.

Regional growth remained broad, with Greater China and Asia-Pacific both delivering over 40% revenue gains, and all major areas recording double-digit increases. The company finished the quarter in a net cash position, marking a notable improvement from last year, although the 33% rise in inventory remains an area to watch if future sales growth moderates.
