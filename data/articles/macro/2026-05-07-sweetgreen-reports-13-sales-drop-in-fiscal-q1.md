Key Takeaways
Sweetgreen reported a sharp business setback as same-store sales and restaurant-level profit declined substantially compared to the prior year period.
Same-store sales declined 12.8% compared to the prior year period.
Outlook for fiscal 2026 calls for modest improvement, but risks remain given recent performance.

Sweetgreen (NYSE:SG) reported a difficult start to fiscal 2026 as consumer demand weakened and key operating metrics fell. The fast-casual salad and healthy foods chain released its first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, showing disappointing sales and margin figures, with net income boosted by a one-time business sale. Overall, the quarter reflected setbacks across most major financial and operating measures.

Notable developments
Same-store sales dropped by 12.8%, driven mostly by an 11.2% fall in customer traffic and a 2.3% weaker product mix.
Margins compressed sharply, with restaurant-level profit margin at 10.0%, well below management’s stated full-year target range of 14.2%–14.7%.
Net income turned sharply positive, but this reflects a one-time $160.6 million gain from the sale of Spyce, not ongoing business improvement.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintained guidance for modest improvement in same-store sales and margins for the full year, but lowered its outlook for new restaurant openings from about 15 to around 13. Investors should watch whether the recently launched Wraps menu item and continued digital engagement efforts can help reverse the steep declines in existing-store performance.

Quarterly highlights

Same-store sales declined steeply year over year, reflecting lower guest traffic and some softness in new product offerings. Promotional activity and weather impacts contributed to weaker sales, and a shift from the Sweetpass+ subscription towards the SG Rewards loyalty program was partly blamed for the weaker results.

Restaurant-level profit margin dropped nearly eight percentage points as higher ingredient usage, larger protein portions, and more discounts increased costs. While Sweetgreen did achieve some supply chain savings and cut general and administrative expenses, these were not enough to offset operational volume pressures or support margin recovery.

Total revenue fell compared to the prior year despite a higher digital mix, highlighting that growth in digital ordering did not compensate for the loss in store traffic. Only four net new restaurants opened in the quarter, prompting management to cut its expected number of new unit launches for fiscal 2026.

What Sweetgreen does

Sweetgreen operates a chain of fast-casual restaurants focused on salads and health-oriented meals, with an emphasis on digital ordering and menu customization.
