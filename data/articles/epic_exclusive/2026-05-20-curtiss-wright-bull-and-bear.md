Curtiss-Wright Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 20, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments

Curtiss-Wright Corporation (NYSE:CW) is a diversified manufacturer with leadership positions in mission-critical markets, including defense, commercial nuclear power, and aerospace/industrial technologies. The company’s growth strategy focuses on leveraging its entrenched, high-barrier portfolio to capture secular tailwinds in global defense modernization, clean energy (especially nuclear and small modular reactors), and operational excellence initiatives, while using disciplined capital deployment for both organic investment and targeted acquisitions.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Entrenched Mission-Critical Portfolio Enables Durable Growth and High Margins

Competitive moats in defense and nuclear power reinforce sustainability – Curtiss-Wright Corporation’s portfolio is deeply embedded in vital, high-barrier markets such as U.S. naval nuclear propulsion, advanced military electronics, and commercial nuclear reactor systems. As of Q1 2026, its technologies are used on over 400 platforms and 3,000 programs globally, making the company an irreplaceable partner for the U.S. Navy, Westinghouse (AP1000/SMR), and other core customers. These relationships drive high switching costs and regulatory/certification hurdles, supporting sustained top-quartile operating margins. The broad integration across platforms and programs contributes to a resilient revenue base and limits competitive threats, setting a strong foundation for durable growth through the rest of the decade.

2. Record Orders and Backlog Provide Visibility for Continued Revenue Expansion

Robust order momentum underpins future growth – In Q1 2026, Curtiss-Wright delivered 15% year-over-year order growth, achieving a 1.3x companywide book-to-bill ratio and reaching a record backlog of nearly $4.3 billion. The Naval & Power segment, in particular, posted a 1.5x book-to-bill, and all three operating segments reported at least mid-teens order growth. This backlog, spanning both long-duration defense/nuclear contracts and fast-growing commercial programs, increases visibility and confidence in meeting—and potentially exceeding—full-year 2026 guidance (sales up 7–8%, EPS up 13–16%). Sustained demand in global defense and commercial nuclear markets, supported by strong booked business, limits near-term downside risk and supports a multi-year growth runway.

3. Expanding Margins and Record Free Cash Flow Reflect Operational Excellence

Margin expansion and cash conversion drive shareholder value – The company’s Q1 2026 adjusted earnings per share (EPS) grew 23% year over year, with operating margin rising 100 basis points to 17.6% (non-GAAP). Management expects full-year margins to reach 19–19.2%, with free cash flow guidance set at a record $580–600 million (up 5–8% year over year), implying a best-in-class conversion rate of approximately 105%. These gains stem from sustained operational leverage, restructuring, pricing optimization, and supply chain improvements, which help offset inflation and high capital spending. Strong cash flow supports ongoing investments, strategic mergers and acquisitions, and continued growth in dividends, as evidenced by the recent 8% dividend increase in May 2026.

4. Participation in Nuclear Renaissance and Next-Gen Energy Projects Fuels Long-Term Upside

Exposure to global nuclear and SMR buildout positions company as a structural winner – Curtiss-Wright has exclusive content on every operating reactor in North America and South Korea, and is transitioning from design to prototype manufacturing on advanced small modular reactor (SMR) programs like X-energy’s Xe-100. The company expects its commercial nuclear revenues to grow at mid- to high-teens rates in 2026, with SMRs comprising an increasing share as programs move beyond the development phase. Aftermarket and life-extension work further bolster the revenue base. Success in nuclear—an area with strong ESG (environmental, social, governance) and global decarbonization support—provides secular growth opportunities well into the late 2020s and beyond, particularly as new AP1000 reactor orders remain an upside catalyst not yet factored into current guidance.

5. Healthy Balance Sheet and Disciplined M&A Support Both Growth and Shareholder Returns

Financial flexibility enables investment and rewards for shareholders – Curtiss-Wright maintains record-low leverage and a fully untapped $3 billion revolving credit facility, giving it ample firepower for both organic expansion and disciplined acquisitions. With $2.5 billion deployed in mergers and acquisitions since pivoting to a growth strategy, the company continues to target strategic bolt-on deals in defense electronics and nuclear adjacencies, while maintaining strict valuation discipline. Concurrently, rising free cash flow and operational improvements have enabled ten consecutive years of dividend increases and ongoing share repurchases. This balanced approach allows the company to swiftly capitalize on market opportunities while providing meaningful, growing returns to long-term shareholders.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Elevated Valuation Increases Sensitivity to Any Slowdown in Execution

High share price may amplify downside if growth momentum stalls – In May 2026, Curtiss-Wright’s shares surpassed the average analyst 12-month price target, illustrating strong recent outperformance. However, elevated valuation heightens vulnerability to any earnings disappointment, order slowdown, or sector rotation away from defense industrials. If book-to-bill falls below 1.0x, or if delays in major defense or nuclear contracts materialize, the stock could face multiple compression even without absolute declines in revenue or profit. Investor expectations for sustained double-digit EPS growth and top-quartile margins leave little room for missteps without risking sharp negative sentiment and share price volatility.

2. Dependence on Government Budgets and Large Contracts Creates Policy Exposure

Reliance on defense and nuclear policy cycles exposes company to spending volatility – A significant portion of Curtiss-Wright’s revenue and orders are generated from long-term U.S. defense programs, the U.S. Navy, and major nuclear projects (such as AP1000 and SMR partnerships with Westinghouse and X-energy). Shifts in government budget priorities, delays from continuing resolutions or government shutdowns, and slower-than-expected new build commitments in either defense or commercial nuclear could all impede growth. While a record backlog provides some buffer, program deferrals or funding shortfalls could lead to lower bookings, downward earnings revisions, or missed guidance, particularly for out-years beyond 2026 where current pipeline visibility diminishes.

3. Ambitious Capacity Expansion and M&A Strategy Carries Execution and Integration Risks

Rapid scaling and acquisitions may create operational strain or disappoint on returns – With nearly 30% year-over-year capital expenditure increases planned in 2026 and a stated focus on bolt-on deals for technology and market expansion, Curtiss-Wright faces the dual challenge of maintaining operational discipline and delivering accretive integration. Not all recent acquisitions have been immediately accretive to margins or cash flow, as evidenced in the post-merger smoothing period in Defense Electronics. Execution missteps—such as delayed synergies, cost overruns, supply chain bottlenecks, or overpaying in a competitive M&A environment—could erode the margin and cash flow gains underpinning the bull case. Additionally, failure to realize expected benefits from innovation investments or to successfully transition SMR and nuclear projects from prototype to scale may disappoint investors seeking predictable, compounding earnings.
