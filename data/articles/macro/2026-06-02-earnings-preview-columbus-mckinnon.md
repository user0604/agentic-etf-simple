Earnings Preview: Columbus McKinnon

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Columbus McKinnon (NASDAQ:CMCO), a company specializing in industrial material handling equipment such as hoists, cranes, and motion control systems, is expected to report its fourth-quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on Thursday, June 4, 2026, after the market closes.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, Columbus McKinnon delivered results that beat analyst expectations for both earnings and revenue. The company reported net sales of $258.7 million, representing a 10.5% increase year over year, and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $0.62, up 11% from the prior year. Adjusted EBITDA reached $39.8 million with a margin of 15.4%. The company also closed the significant Kito Crosby acquisition during the quarter and announced the pending divestiture of its U.S. power chain hoist and chain operations, expected to close by the end of Q4. Orders increased 11% to $247 million, with notable strength in the U.S. market. The backlog rose 15% to $342 million, indicating healthy demand going forward. However, gross margin contracted due to product mix and tariff impacts, and management withdrew prior guidance because of the recent acquisition and pending divestiture.

Positives
Strong year-over-year sales and order growth: Net sales and orders both grew by double digits, reflecting robust demand in key end markets, especially in North America.
Successful closing of the Kito Crosby acquisition: The acquisition was completed as planned and is expected to double the company’s revenue base and enhance its global presence.
Significant backlog growth: Backlog increased 15% year over year, positioning the company for continued revenue strength in upcoming quarters.
Progress on tariff mitigation and operational initiatives: The company made headway on cost-saving actions, with management expecting to achieve tariff cost neutrality by the end of fiscal 2026.
Negatives
Gross margin contraction: Adjusted gross margin declined 170 basis points year over year, mainly due to less favorable product mix and ongoing tariff impacts.
Increased general and administrative expenses: Costs rose due to acquisition-related expenses and higher employee-related costs, which weighed on operating margins.
Continued softness in Europe: Economic conditions in the EMEA region led to slower order conversion and weaker demand compared to the U.S.
Withdrawal of full-year guidance: The company pulled its prior outlook due to the timing of the acquisition and divestiture, creating some uncertainty for investors.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report
Integration progress and synergy realization from Kito Crosby acquisition: Investors should look for updates on the pace of integration and the realization of cost synergies, with management targeting $70 million in annualized cost savings over three years.
Timing and financial impact of the U.S. power chain hoist and chain operations divestiture: The divestiture is expected to close by the end of Q4, and the proceeds are earmarked for debt reduction. The timing and net proceeds will affect the company's leverage and interest expense outlook.
Margin recovery and tariff cost neutrality: Watch for signs that the company is achieving its goal of tariff cost neutrality by year-end and making progress toward margin neutrality in fiscal 2027, especially as product mix and pricing actions work through the backlog.
Order trends and backlog conversion, especially in North America versus EMEA: Continued strength in U.S. demand and any improvement or further weakness in Europe will be important for the growth outlook. Backlog conversion rates and new order activity will also be key metrics to monitor.
Updated capital allocation plans and leverage targets post-acquisition and divestiture: Management has stated that debt repayment is the top priority. Updates on free cash flow generation, leverage ratios, and any changes to capital spending or dividend policy will be important for investors tracking the company’s financial flexibility.
