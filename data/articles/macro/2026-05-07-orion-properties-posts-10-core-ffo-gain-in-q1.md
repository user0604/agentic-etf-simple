Key Takeaways
Orion Properties’ strategic transition continued, but it remained unprofitable and faced heightened uncertainty.
Core FFO per share rose to $0.21, with occupancy and lease durations both improving from last year.
Management reaffirmed full-year guidance but cited ongoing strategic review and continuing pressure on cash flow.

Orion Properties (NYSE:ONL) navigated another quarter of transition, improving leasing and asset composition, but widening its net loss as financial headwinds persisted. The office-focused real estate investment trust is shifting its portfolio toward specialized, dedicated-use properties and signed several large, long-term leases. Reporting results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (released May 7, 2026), the company saw core earnings rise, yet struggled to cover its dividend and maintain profitability. Overall, the period reflected cautious progress but underscored the ongoing challenges of the repositioning process.

Notable Developments
Core FFO per share climbed 10.5% as leasing of 355,000 square feet helped stabilize income streams, even as revenue declined.
Occupancy rate rose to 83.1%, up from 78.7% at year-end 2025, reflecting progress in reducing vacancies across Orion’s properties.
The company declared a quarterly dividend of $0.02 per share, though ongoing net cash outflows and negative funds available for distribution raise questions about coverage.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed full-year non-GAAP core FFO per share guidance of $0.69 to $0.76, highlighting ongoing progress in shifting toward dedicated-use properties and maintaining balance sheet flexibility. The key item for investors to watch in coming quarters is the outcome of Orion’s portfolio repositioning, specifically asset dispositions and the ongoing strategic review, given the implications for dividend stability, leverage, and long-term direction.

Quarterly Highlights

During the quarter, Orion Properties increased its exposure to specialized dedicated-use assets, which now make up 37.1% of annualized base rent. This reflects the acquisition of a fully-leased property in Illinois and the company’s aim to insulate income from market cycles affecting traditional offices.

Leasing activity was robust, totaling 355,000 square feet. Major agreements included a new 12-year lease in Irving, Texas, and a three-year extension in Buffalo, New York. These transactions increased the portfolio's weighted average lease term to 5.9 years, contributing to portfolio stability.

The company continued to execute asset sales, closing on nine property dispositions (two during the quarter for $13.1 million and seven after quarter-end for $35.6 million). Proceeds support debt reduction and capital investment, but net losses widened and funds available for distribution remained negative, underscoring the cost of repositioning and ongoing financial challenges.

What Orion Properties Does

Orion Properties owns and operates a portfolio of office and specialized commercial real estate with a focus on long-term single-tenant leases, primarily in suburban U.S. markets.
