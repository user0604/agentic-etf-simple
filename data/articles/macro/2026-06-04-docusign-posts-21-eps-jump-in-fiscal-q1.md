Key Takeaways
DocuSign recorded a solid business period with steady revenue and profit growth.
Non-GAAP earnings per share climbed 21.1%, and revenue increased 8.7% versus last year.
Management guided for continued single-digit growth, with platform adoption and margins as key areas to watch.

DocuSign’s platform expansion paid off this quarter, producing reliable revenue and cash flow as its transition beyond eSignature gathered momentum. The leading agreement management software provider delivered another steady quarter, showing progress in adoption of its new Intelligent Agreement Management (IAM) platform. For the quarter ended April 30, 2026, and reported on June 4, 2026, the company nudged past its own guidance for revenue and reported gains in profitability. Overall, DocuSign’s period showed stable growth and efficiency, but with a few important issues to keep monitoring.

Notable Developments
Revenue totaled $830.2 million, a year-over-year increase of 9% including approximately 1.6% positive impact from foreign exchange rates, just above management's prior outlook range.
Profitability improved, with non-GAAP earnings per share at $1.09, and operating margin (non-GAAP) rising to 32.0%.
The IAM platform accounted for 12.6% of annual recurring revenue, though still short of the 18% target for the end of the year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management addressed the slower pace of IAM adoption but reaffirmed its focus on accelerating platform transition and expanding international reach. For the upcoming quarter, it expects revenue growth around 8%, and continued efficiency in cost control. The most important issue for investors remains the adoption speed of the IAM platform, which is seen as the key engine for future growth.

Quarterly Highlights

DocuSign’s revenue for the quarter ended April 30, 2026, was $830.2 million, above the top end of its $822–$826 million guidance. Free cash flow and profits improved, supported by higher collections and ongoing efforts to contain operating expenses.

The IAM platform, which aims to manage the full agreement lifecycle through artificial intelligence, grew to 12.6% of annual recurring revenue. This is an improvement but remains below the company’s goal for fiscal 2027, so management’s focus will likely stay on driving customer conversions and net new deals.

The company continued integrating its IAM platform with major business and AI tools—such as OpenAI, Google Gemini, and Salesforce—and announced expanded capabilities for HR and sales workflows. A new Chief Product Officer with experience in automation was also hired to further boost innovation in platform features.

What DocuSign Does

DocuSign offers cloud-based software that helps organizations manage electronic signature workflows and the full agreement process, increasingly enhanced with artificial intelligence and automation.
