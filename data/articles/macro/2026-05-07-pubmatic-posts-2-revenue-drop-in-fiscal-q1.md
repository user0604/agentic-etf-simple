Key Takeaways
PubMatic posted a business loss as headline revenue and profitability both declined year over year. Non-GAAP EPS came in at a loss of $0.11, as revenue dropped 1.9%. Management anticipates a return to double-digit revenue growth and margin expansion in the second half of 2026.

PubMatic (NASDAQ:PUBM) reported a business loss as both revenue and profitability declined compared to the prior-year period. The technology platform enables digital publishers and app developers to automate the sale of advertising across devices and formats. The company released its first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, covering the period ending March 31, 2026. Overall, the quarter highlighted resilient cash flows and rapid growth in new business segments, though margin pressure and legacy platform attrition weighed on results.

Notable developments
Revenue declined 1.9% year over year to $62.6 million while adjusted EBITDA margin compressed to 4% from 13% last year.
Emerging revenues, including contributions from artificial intelligence (AI), grew 80% year over year and accounted for 14% of total revenue.
The company repurchased one million shares in the quarter, with $85.1 million still available in its authorization.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management projects revenue of $68 million–$70 million and adjusted EBITDA between $8 million and $10 million for the second quarter of 2026, signalling expected margin recovery. Looking ahead, leadership indicated that double-digit revenue growth and margin expansion may return in the second half of the year. Investors should monitor ongoing customer transition away from legacy demand-side platforms and progress in higher growth segments like AI and connected television (CTV).

Quarterly highlights

The quarter featured rapid AI adoption, with over 1,000 AI-powered deals and more than 20 live AI agents deployed, helping drive faster campaign setup and strong advertiser engagement. Revenue from mobile app and CTV grew at double-digit rates—Americas CTV rose 13% and mobile app revenue improved 25% year over year, supported by new product offerings and integrations.

Despite operational improvements, profitability declined: adjusted EBITDA margin fell by nine percentage points versus the prior year, reflecting increased sales and marketing investment and persistent pressure from a loss of business with a major legacy demand-side platform. However, infrastructure efficiency improved, with the cost per million impressions processed falling 20% on a trailing twelve-month basis and total impressions handled climbing 26% to over 94 trillion in the period.

Cash generation remained solid, as free cash flow jumped 47% versus last year and net cash from operations grew 11%. The company also emphasized its commitment to shareholder returns by repurchasing one million shares in the quarter.
