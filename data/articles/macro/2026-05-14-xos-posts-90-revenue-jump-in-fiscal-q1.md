Xos Posts 90% Revenue Jump in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 4:26 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Xos narrowed losses and improved margins as product mix shifted to higher-value deliveries.
Revenue nearly doubled as gross margin reached a record high.
Management reaffirmed its annual revenue target but noted execution risk for the remainder of the year.

Xos (NASDAQ:XOS) made substantial gains on the path toward scalable profitability, driven by higher-margin sales and ongoing cost discipline. The maker of battery-electric commercial vehicles, charging systems, and fleet transition services reported further progress in expanding units shipped, broadening its product mix, and boosting operational efficiency. In its first-quarter update for the period ended March 31, 2026, released May 14, 2026, the company showed signs of momentum, although the need for stronger revenue growth remains as it targets its annual outlook. Overall, the quarter highlighted structural gains in profitability accompanied by ongoing cash burn that will need monitoring in the coming months.

Notable developments
Unit deliveries jumped to 95, more than triple the prior year, led by 63 powertrain systems shipped for Blue Bird school buses.
Gross margin reached a record 38.6% as higher-margin powertrain and Xos Hub sales made up a larger share of revenue.
Cash and equivalents ended at $9.8 million; free cash flow was negative, highlighting continued capital needs even with cost improvements.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management kept its full-year targets, planning for $40 million–$50 million in revenue and 350–500 units delivered in fiscal 2026. The company stressed that margin improvements are the result of lasting changes, not temporary factors. Investors should watch for sustained growth in unit shipments and close progress toward the annual revenue goal over the next several quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Unit shipments and revenue both nearly doubled compared to the same period last year, with the business benefiting from a shift to higher-value products. The bulk of deliveries included 63 battery-electric powertrain systems for school buses, a move aligning with Xos's plan to focus on higher-margin segments within commercial vehicles.

Profitability metrics improved sharply: gross margin expanded to 38.6%, and both operating loss and adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to multi-year lows. The shift in product mix and ongoing cost control strategies were the main drivers behind these gains.

Despite these operational improvements, free cash flow turned negative at $(1.6) million, and cash on hand fell to $9.8 million, up from one year ago but down from the previous quarter. Liquidity management and execution on top-line growth will remain critical as the company continues scaling.

What Xos does

Xos designs and manufactures battery-electric commercial vehicles, charging infrastructure, and fleet services for medium- and heavy-duty operators.
