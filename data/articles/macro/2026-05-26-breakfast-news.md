1. Stock Reverses as Ferrari Debuts EV Luce in Rome

Ferrari (NYSE: RACE) unveiled its first fully electric vehicle – the Luce, priced at $640,000 – in a move that contrasts with direct competitors that are steering away from the EV space as it aims to boost revenue. The stock fell around 3% in pre-market trading.

Launch comes despite scaled-back electric plans: The Stock Advisor recommendation by Team Rule Breakers has halved its 2030 EV lineup target from 40% to 20% to better reflect customer demand. The Luce is expected to account for 5% of first-year sales from Ferrari’s new “e-building” in Maranello.
Design collaboration led by former iPhone creative Jony Ive: The Luce was co-designed by LoveFrom, run by former Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) exec Ive, with a focus on physical controls over touchscreens.

2. Earnings Ahead From SA Recs by Team Hidden Gems

4. Uber Plots Delivery Hero Takeover

Delivery Hero (OTC: DLVHF) surged almost 10% yesterday after confirming Uber (NYSE: UBER) has expressed takeover interest, raising the potential for a bidding war with DoorDash (NASDAQ: DASH). 

Uber deal makes strategic sense for geographic expansion and potential synergies: A full takeover would allow Uber to tap into the existing Delivery Hero presence in Asia and Africa. It already holds 19.5% of Delivery Hero’s issued share capital. Uber is beating the S&P 500 by 139% since the July 2022 Rule Breakers rec.
“If I was going to [give] gold, silver, bronze [to Uber, DoorDash, and Instacart]… from a faraway view, I think Uber would be the gold”: In March, Fool contributing analyst Rick Munarriz praised Uber, saying the business has “always been this free cash flow machine, even when they weren't necessarily profitable all the time, but they were generating money.”

5. S&P 500 Nails 8th Straight Weekly Gain

U.S. stocks posted another positive week, with the S&P 500 rising 0.89% and the Nasdaq gaining 0.5%, as progress on peace talks in the Middle East helped to reduce geopolitical risk. Both S&P 500 and Nasdaq futures rose over 0.5% ahead of the opening bell.

Caution ahead due to U.S. “self-defense strikes”: Sentiment could be under pressure due to early Tuesday attacks from the U.S. in Southern Iran to protect troops. 
Key inflation data set to show further price pressure: Investors will digest April’s Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) inflation data due out on Thursday. The core reading – the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge – is expected to rise from 3.2% last month to 3.3%.
