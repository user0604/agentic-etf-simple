Earnings Preview: Capital Southwest

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 11:04 AM

Comments

Capital Southwest (NASDAQ:CSWC), a business development company focused on lending to smaller U.S. companies, is expected to report its fourth-quarter fiscal year 2026 earnings after the market closes on Wednesday, May 13, 2026.

Q3 FY2026 Recap

In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, Capital Southwest delivered net investment income before taxes of $34.6 million, or $0.60 per share. Total investment income reached $61.4 million, representing an 18% increase year over year. The company’s net asset value (NAV) per share rose to $16.75 from $16.62 in the prior quarter. The board declared regular monthly dividends totaling $0.58 per share for January through March 2026, plus a supplemental dividend of $0.06 per share for March. Portfolio growth remained strong, with the investment portfolio at fair value reaching $2.01 billion, up from $1.79 billion at the end of March 2025. Non-accruals remained low at 1.5% of the portfolio at fair value. The company also completed a refinancing, redeeming $222 million in notes due 2026 and 2028, and issued $350 million in new notes due 2030. These results met analyst expectations for the quarter.

Positives
Solid portfolio growth and diversification: The investment portfolio grew to $2.01 billion, with 132 portfolio companies and continued emphasis on first lien senior secured debt. This helps spread risk and supports stable income.
Strong dividend coverage and supplemental payout: Undistributed taxable income (UTI) rose to $1.02 per share, supporting both regular and supplemental dividends. This signals confidence in earnings quality and sustainability.
Effective capital management: The company refinanced debt at attractive rates and raised $53 million in equity at a premium to NAV, improving its balance sheet flexibility and lowering funding costs.
Low non-accruals and conservative underwriting: Only 1.5% of the portfolio was on non-accrual status, and new loans maintained conservative leverage and strong covenants, limiting credit risk.
Negatives
Operating expenses increased: Employee compensation and general administrative costs rose, partly due to higher bonus accruals. This pressured operating leverage, though it remains better than the industry average.
Yield compression on new investments: The weighted average yield on debt investments declined to 11.3% from 12.1% year over year, reflecting tighter spreads in a competitive market.
Unrealized depreciation in portfolio: The quarter saw $2.2 million in net unrealized depreciation, mostly from debt investments, which partially offset realized gains.
What to Watch in Q4 FY2026's Report
Initial impact of the new joint venture: The off-balance sheet joint venture with a private credit asset manager, announced and already ramping, is expected to help CSWC access larger, higher-quality deals. Investors should look for early contributions to income and details on the pace of ramp-up.
Dividend policy and supplemental payouts: With UTI and realized gains supporting recent supplemental dividends, watch for any changes in the regular or supplemental dividend declarations for the new quarter.
Portfolio credit quality and non-accrual trends: Monitor whether non-accruals remain low and if the company maintains its conservative underwriting as economic conditions evolve.
Net asset value (NAV) per share movement: Changes in NAV per share will reflect both realized gains and unrealized portfolio marks, which are important for assessing shareholder value creation.
Operating expense trends and efficiency: Pay attention to the trajectory of operating expenses and whether the company continues to leverage its internally managed structure to maintain below-industry-average expense ratios.
