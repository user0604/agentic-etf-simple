Key Takeaways

Indoor farming operator Edible Garden AG (NASDAQ:EDBL) grew revenue but continued to face losses as costs outpaced sales gains. The company, a national supplier of herbs and nutrition products to supermarkets across the US and internationally, reported first-quarter financials on May 15, 2026, covering the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter featured strong top-line growth but deepened operating losses.

Notable Developments
Edible Garden's revenue climbed 22.9% year over year, fueled by broader distribution and sales growth in cut herbs, vitamins, and condiments.
Operating expenses jumped 77.5%, mainly due to higher cost of goods sold and increased depreciation from the ready-to-drink (RTD) manufacturing pivot.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management pointed to ongoing expansion in store partnerships and branded products, with further progress on its RTD beverage manufacturing investment. However, it did not provide financial targets for the rest of fiscal 2026. Investors should closely monitor whether the company can stabilize costs and move gross margins toward breakeven as scale increases.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue growth came from new and existing retail partners, with distribution now spanning over 6,000 store locations and strong gains in every product segment. The cut herbs business led all categories, rising 46% year over year, with significant new placements at Kroger and Weis Markets.

Operating expenses jumped, largely due to almost $2.5 million in extra depreciation and amortization tied to the company's manufacturing shift into RTD beverages. Selling, general, and administrative costs rose but at a slower pace than total expenses.

Despite these revenue gains, negative gross margins and higher costs kept the net loss elevated at $3.7 million. The quarter included a $3.4 million income tax benefit from a state program, but the main profitability challenge remains gross margin recovery.

What Edible Garden Does

Edible Garden operates as a controlled environment agriculture company, supplying herbs, produce, and nutrition products to major retailers and food outlets across North America.
