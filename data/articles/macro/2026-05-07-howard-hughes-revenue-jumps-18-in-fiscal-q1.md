Howard Hughes Revenue Jumps 18% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:27 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Howard Hughes Holdings reported higher revenue but a year-over-year decline in earnings.
Revenue rose 18.4% from the prior year period.
Management’s future outlook centers on integrating a new insurance acquisition and shifting to longer-term performance objectives.

Howard Hughes (NYSE:HHH) posted stronger land sales but saw profits fall compared to a year ago. The leading developer and operator of master planned communities and mixed-use properties saw solid growth in its core business lines. The results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, released on May 7, 2026, reflect a company growing its revenue base ahead of a strategic transition. Overall, the quarter showed that underlying real estate activity was resilient, though headline earnings were impacted by higher costs and fewer asset sales.

Notable developments
Master Planned Communities (MPC) revenue jumped 33% to $112.3 million, driven by higher residential and commercial land sales volume.
Total Operating Assets NOI rose 2% to $73.1 million, supported by gains across office, retail, and multifamily properties.
Howard Hughes ended the quarter with $1.8 billion in cash and is preparing to finalize the acquisition of Vantage, a specialty insurance business, in the coming quarter.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management highlighted a plan to move away from annual earnings guidance and emphasized long-term objectives for each business platform as it completes the Vantage insurance acquisition. There was no new specific financial guidance for 2026, but commentary suggested stability in recurring income streams and high liquidity levels. Investors should watch for updates on the incoming insurance operation and progress toward stated long-term goals in recurring revenues and asset base expansion.

Quarterly highlights

Master Planned Communities continued to be a growth engine. Residential land sales rose 24% in the quarter, and commercial land sales contributed for the first time in a year, indicating healthy demand in focus markets like Las Vegas and Houston. Net new home sales increased across all major communities, with especially sharp gains at The Woodlands Hills.

The Operating Assets segment, which includes office buildings, retail centers, and multifamily properties, provided a stable recurring income base. Net operating income for this segment increased modestly, reflecting consistent leasing activity and small year-over-year gains across all property types.

On the balance sheet, strong liquidity and new financing deals strengthened the company’s ability to support upcoming strategic changes. Management proceeded with refinancing several debt tranches and positioned the company for the expected closing of the Vantage insurance acquisition in the next quarter, which will diversify operations beyond real estate.

What Howard Hughes does

The company develops, owns, and operates large-scale master planned communities and income-producing real estate in key U.S. growth markets.
