South Bow (NYSE:SOBO) kept revenue stable despite sharp weakness in its Marketing business and higher operating costs. The diversified midstream operator, with pipeline and energy infrastructure assets across the U.S. and Canada, released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter saw a mix of solid pipeline performance and ongoing regulatory and control challenges.

Notable Developments
Pipeline segment revenue rose 10% to $417 million, driven by higher rates and strong mainline demand, while throughput remained steady at 616 thousand barrels per day.
Marketing segment performance fell sharply; its revenue dropped 40%, and profitability declined due to realized losses on risk management contracts.
Net debt decreased by $172 million year over year, ending the quarter at $4.7 billion as cash reserves increased and no debt matured.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 guidance, with about 90% of expected earnings under contract and moderate capital spending plans. The company expects normalized EBITDA to approach $1.03 billion for the year and is focused on remedying control weaknesses identified during an internal system update. Investors should monitor progress on regulatory compliance and the performance rebound in the Marketing segment.

Quarterly highlights

The Keystone Pipeline System, South Bow’s core crude oil transport network, saw strong demand and a 10% year-over-year rise in revenue, helped by higher variable rates and a small gain from a resolved toll dispute. Operating costs for this segment increased modestly due to regulatory-mandated repairs and maintenance activity following earlier pipeline incidents.

In stark contrast, the Marketing segment struggled. Revenue fell by $7 million compared to the prior year quarter. Realized losses on risk management contracts led to a negative income before taxes of $22 million. Management cited tighter profit margins and market volatility as contributors to this segment’s weak outcome.

On project execution, the Blackrod Connection Project entered service on March 1, 2026, with no safety incidents and cash flow contribution anticipated in the second half of the year. The company’s capex dropped by more than 15% year over year as growth spending slowed following Blackrod’s completion.

What South Bow does

South Bow operates major crude oil pipelines and related energy infrastructure across Canada and the United States, moving oil and liquids from production centers to end markets.
