Kenon Revenue Jumps 73% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 20, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Kenon extended its dividend suspension and faced operational challenges at key plants.
Revenue surged 73.2% from the prior-year period, with consolidated EBITDA after proportionate consolidation up 10%.
Management expects continued growth in energy segment projects, but visibility remains low for non-energy segments.

Kenon (NYSE:KEN) extended its dividend suspension and encountered operational setbacks at several power plants. The diversified holding company operates mainly through its energy business and subsidiaries in Israel and the U.S., while shipping and automotive results were not addressed in this period’s release. The first-quarter 2026 results, released on May 20, 2026, cover the period ending March 31 and highlight a sharp rise in revenue and moderate earnings gains, offset by lower net income and non-recurring cash outflows. Overall, the quarter delivered substantial revenue growth, but operational and transparency challenges remain areas of concern.

Notable Developments
Revenue jumped 73.2%, driven by consolidation of new power plants in the U.S. and higher Israel segment sales.
Net income declined 44%, primarily due to a $70 million non-recurring incentive payment.
Dividend suspension extended through at least March 2028, with management prioritizing capital retention and project development.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its commitment to energy segment expansion and major project delivery, especially in solar and storage. The company’s outlook prioritizes project execution, further U.S. growth, and prudent leverage management, without numerical guidance for upcoming quarters. Investors should closely monitor progress restoring availability at key facilities and watch for updates on Kenon's non-energy businesses, which received little detail in this report.

Quarterly Highlights

Energy operations delivered a significant boost, with the U.S. business reporting gains in both traditional and renewable segments — helped in part by the acquisition and full consolidation of key assets like the Shore and Basin Ranch power plants. In Israel, performance improved for most plants except Zomet, which operated at only 7.1% of potential due to ongoing repairs — expected to conclude by the end of 2026.

Company-wide, consolidated EBITDA after proportionate adjustments rose 10%, supported by robust spark spreads (the gap between electricity prices and fuel costs) in critical U.S. facilities. However, group net income dropped due to a one-off, $70 million long-term incentive payment linked to the CPV Group, and a reclassification loss at the Shore plant entry.

No dividend was declared, and management announced the suspension would last until at least March 2028. Balance sheet strength improved, with cash and equivalents rising to $1.16 billion as of March 31, and ongoing leverage improvement. Shipping and automotive divisions were not covered in the current report, leaving a transparency gap for investors tracking those segments.
