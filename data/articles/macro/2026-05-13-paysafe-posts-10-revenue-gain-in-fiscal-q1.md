Paysafe Posts 10% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

Key Takeaways
Paysafe delivered higher sales and cash flow, but continued to face margin and GAAP profitability challenges.
Adjusted earnings per share increased 21% year over year, while reported revenue climbed 10%.
Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook and remains focused on cost control and margin recovery.

Paysafe (NYSE:PSFE) reported another period of revenue growth, driven by strong demand for its digital wallet products and expansion in Latin America. The global payments company provides digital wallets, merchant solutions, and alternative payment methods such as Skrill, paysafecard, and PaysafeWallet. Paysafe released its first-quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 13, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter showed solid top-line gains, but rising costs led to a wider GAAP net loss.

Notable developments
Digital Wallets revenue rose 15% from the prior year, with strong performance in Europe and Latin America.
Merchant Solutions revenue increased, but adjusted EBITDA for the segment declined 5%, reflecting continued margin pressure.
Operating cash flow grew 22% year over year, helping the company reduce its net leverage ratio.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 guidance, targeting revenue and adjusted EBITDA growth between 5% and 8%, and double-digit growth in adjusted earnings per share. The company remains focused on lowering its net leverage ratio below five by year-end. Investors should monitor whether the Merchant Solutions segment can achieve margin improvements as planned.

Quarterly highlights

The Digital Wallets division led growth, supported by increased user activity in Latin America and steady adoption of PaysafeWallet in Europe. Promotional campaigns around major events and new product launches contributed to this momentum.

Merchant Solutions delivered higher revenue, especially from North American e-commerce, but segment profitability slipped as adjusted EBITDA declined compared to the prior year. Management cited higher costs and a less favorable business mix as key factors behind the margin pressure.

Paysafe generated more operating and unlevered free cash flow, using these funds to pay down net debt and move closer to its leverage target. This financial discipline continued even as expenses such as share-based compensation and credit losses increased from the prior period.
