Voya Financial Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 21, 2026, 3:10 PM

Comments

Voya Financial, Inc. (NYSE:VOYA) is a leading provider of workplace retirement solutions, investment management, and employee benefits across the United States. The company's growth strategy centers on disciplined expansion of its core retirement and benefits businesses, ongoing margin and efficiency gains (notably through acquisitions like OneAmerica), and robust capital returns to shareholders via dividends and buybacks. Supported by secular trends and strong policy tailwinds, Voya aims to leverage its scale, recurring fee-based revenues, and technology investments for sustained long-term growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Margin Expansion and Fee-Based Revenues Sustain Durable Earnings Growth

Margin growth backed by recurring fee income can drive sustained profitability for years to come. Over the trailing twelve months ending Q1 2026, Voya grew adjusted operating earnings per share by 20%, hitting $9.11 per share, and returned on equity exceeded 18%. Fee-based revenues now account for nearly 60% of net revenue in the Retirement segment—up significantly over recent years—offering resilience against economic swings and reducing earnings volatility. Improved margins, especially a 39% margin in Retirement and 28.6% in Investment Management, outpace many peers. These developments position the company to consistently generate excess capital and support future buybacks and dividends, reinforcing predictable shareholder returns well into the future.

2. Successful Integration of Acquisitions Enhances Scale and Competitive Positioning

Seamless integration of OneAmerica has boosted scale and lifted earnings power in core markets. The 2025 acquisition of OneAmerica has been instrumental in propelling Voya’s Retirement business to top-five national status, now serving close to 10 million participants. Management cited the integration as a strategic and operational success, resulting in an immediate positive impact on both scale and segment margins. The increased scale helps drive cost efficiencies and operational leverage—already evident in the reported $209 million of Retirement adjusted operating earnings in Q1 2026, a 14% year-over-year gain. As the organization transitions from integration to “steady-state” operation, further run-rate expense reductions are likely to drive even greater profitability in the coming quarters.

3. Disciplined Capital Allocation and Robust Shareholder Returns Support Long-Term Value Creation

Voya’s consistent generation and return of excess capital underpins its long-term investment case. In Q1 2026, the company generated approximately $200 million in excess capital, returning all of it to shareholders through dividends and share buybacks. Voya has reduced its diluted share count by 14% since 2022. Ongoing buybacks of $150 million are planned for Q2 2026, and dividends remain steady at $0.47 per share with a sustainable 20% payout ratio. This disciplined approach to capital deployment, with a high bar for M&A and a preference toward shareholder returns, ensures that intrinsic value is consistently delivered back to investors and supports compounding growth in per-share metrics over time.

4. Structural Regulatory Tailwinds Expand the Addressable Market for Core Franchises

Public policy changes are unlocking new growth opportunities in workplace retirement and benefits. The introduction of new federal mandates and favorable legislative action—such as broader auto-enrollment and improved access to workplace retirement plans for underserved demographics—directly benefit Voya’s core segments. As a scale provider with significant administrative and technology capabilities, Voya is well-positioned to win incremental business from employers seeking to comply with new mandates. Management sees these policy shifts as a multi-year secular tailwind that will drive new plan formation, lift recurring deposits (over $50 billion in annual recurring deposits), and further solidify its leadership across the retirement and employee benefits landscape.

5. Strategic Focus on Margin Restoration in Employee Benefits Enables Upside Optionality

Purposeful improvements in Stop Loss underwriting and reserving are unlocking new earnings potential. After years of elevated claims volatility and industry-wide cost pressures, Voya has prioritized margin restoration over simple topline growth in Employee Benefits. Management implemented 24% average price increases for Stop Loss insurance, adopted more conservative reserving (an 87% ‘loss pick’ for 2026), and maintained discipline in risk selection. These measures culminated in $63 million of adjusted operating earnings in Employee Benefits for Q1 2026 and a $25 million reserve release, suggesting that favorable claim development is emerging. As these trends persist, Voya could see further upside to segment profitability, translating into higher quality and more predictable earnings—an advantage that may narrow the valuation gap with top-performing peers.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Claims Volatility and Reserve Uncertainty in Stop Loss Could Disrupt Margin Progress

Unpredictable claim trends in Stop Loss insurance may threaten near-term earnings momentum. Despite recent improvements and a conservative approach to setting reserves, the Employee Benefits segment remains exposed to medical inflation, new therapy costs, and potential claim spikes that are hard to fully forecast. Voya’s ‘high-end’ reserving at an 87% loss pick for 2026 offers a safeguard, but any adverse development—such as a sudden surge in high-cost claims or misestimation of underlying risks—could erode recently restored margins and prompt negative reserve adjustments. As Stop Loss has been a volatile segment industry-wide post-pandemic, future claim shocks remain a real risk to earnings stability.

2. Activist Pressure for Breakup or Divestiture Risks Strategic Distraction and Capital Allocation Shifts

Investor activism could lead to destabilizing strategic shifts that create value disruption. Both Toms Capital Investment Management and TOMS Capital have publicly pressured Voya to consider a breakup or sale of its health insurer business, arguing persistent undervaluation. While management is intent on organic margin improvement and measured capital returns, ongoing activist agitation risks distracting leadership, prompting rushed divestures, or shifting priorities away from the current strategy. Such actions could undermine the benefits of the company’s integrated model, jeopardize existing scale and cost advantages, and inject uncertainty into the capital structure—creating a potential overhang for long-term shareholders.

3. Continued Macroeconomic or Market Headwinds Could Weaken Capital Generation and Flows

External shocks may challenge organic growth and capital generation across core franchises. Voya’s strong results have increasingly relied on stable asset markets, favorable underwriting trends, and positive net flows in both Retirement and Investment Management. A turn in macroeconomic conditions—such as market corrections, falling interest rates, or weakening employment trends—could slow recurring deposit growth, reduce fee-based income, and increase pressure on investment management flows. In such a scenario, the company’s ability to continue generating excess capital for buybacks and dividends could be tested, potentially dampening per-share growth and challenging its premium market valuation.
