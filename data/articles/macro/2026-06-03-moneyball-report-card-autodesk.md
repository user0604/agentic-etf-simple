What the Hidden Gems Primary database scores can tell us about Autodesk.

Autodesk (NASDAQ: ADSK) is the kind of company investors stop thinking about precisely because it works so well. Its software is the default tool for architects, engineers, and designers, and the business has quietly become one of the cleaner subscription models in tech. The Moneyball database rewards that durability, but it also surfaces a governance question serious enough that the quality of the business can't be the whole story.

The financial engine is the headline. Financial 1Y of 75 and Financial 5Y of 76 reflect a company that executed one of the better business-model transitions in software, shifting almost entirely to recurring subscription revenue. Over five years, the top line roughly doubled -- from about $4 billion to more than $7 billion -- while gross margins held above 90% and operating margins nearly doubled. In fiscal 2026, revenue grew 17.5%, operating margin pushed toward 25%, and the company threw off more than $2.4 billion in free cash flow. A ROUNTA reading of 130% underscores how efficiently this model converts into returns. This is a high-margin, cash-generative compounder.

The product and technology story holds up alongside it. A Tech score of 86 reflects genuine cloud and AI investment, anchored by Autodesk Forma's unification of workflows across the built environment, while the MaintainX acquisition signals a deliberate push into the operational phases of the building life cycle, extending the moat beyond design into how structures are actually run. The AI score of 75 is solid, capturing real integration of automation into core engineering tools rather than bolt-on features.

It's not frictionless. The Product score commentary flags meaningful user-community discontent -- concerns over interoperability, licensing terms, and the disruption of aggressive corporate restructuring. Sentiment of 61 reflects that tension: customers stay because switching is painful, not always because they're delighted. For a business whose moat rests on stickiness, persistent user frustration is worth watching, even if it isn't yet showing up in the numbers.

The sharper flag is Governance, which lands at just 36 -- the lowest mark in this batch. The database points to active investigations into financial reporting practices and the regulatory and legal scrutiny that comes with them, plus questions about management ownership alignment. For a company this financially sound, that's a genuine overhang: accounting and disclosure concerns strike at the reliability of the very numbers that make the bull case.

Leadership earns a moderate 62 -- CEO Andrew Anagnost and CFO Janesh Moorjani communicate transparently, but the database wants more concrete milestones and granular disclosure, which reads as faint praise given the reporting questions in the background.

So the central question for Autodesk is unusual: The business quality is high, the cash generation is real, and the valuation -- at roughly a 1.0x 200-week average multiple -- is far less stretched than something like Arm Holdings (NASDAQ: ARM). What investors are really weighing is governance. If the reporting and oversight concerns resolve cleanly, this looks like a durable compounder trading at a reasonable price. If they don't, the discount is there for a reason. For once, the risk isn't the business model. It's the people running the books.
