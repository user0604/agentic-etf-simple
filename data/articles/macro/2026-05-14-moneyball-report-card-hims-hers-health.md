Moneyball Report Card: Hims & Hers Health

What the Hidden Gems Primary database scores can tell us about Hims & Hers Health.

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 2:57 PM

Comments

Hims & Hers Health (NYSE: HIMS) has spent years being misunderstood. To casual observers, it's the company that sells hair-loss pills and ED medication in pastel packaging. According to the Moneyball Hidden Gems Primary database, it's something more interesting: a subscription-driven consumer health platform with a 77.5% four-year revenue CAGR, a genuine profitability inflection, and a Superscore of 80.

The financial story is the headline. Hims earns an 87 on Financial 1Y and an 84 on Financial 5Y -- scores that reflect a business that didn't just grow fast but grew into itself. Revenue reached approximately $1.5 billion in 2024 and $2.3 billion in 2025. Net income and operating cash flow have both turned positive after years in the red. Return on Assets swung from -25.6% in 2021 to 17.8% in 2024. ROUNTA, currently sitting at 33%, jumped dramatically year over year, a signal that the company is now generating real returns on the assets it has accumulated, not just spending to acquire scale.

What's driving that efficiency? Product, primarily. Hims earns an 88 on Product 1Y and an 86 on Product 5Y -- near the top of the database. The core insight behind the business is simple but powerful: Primary care in the U.S. is broken, and consumers will pay for a frictionless, stigma-free alternative. Hims & Hers built a multispecialty telehealth platform -- sexual health, dermatology, mental health, weight loss -- and kept adding. The weight loss push, built around GLP-1 compounding, accelerated revenue dramatically and brought new subscribers into the funnel at scale.

Technology is central to how the company actually delivers this. With a Tech score of 74, Hims operates a consumer-first platform, a distributed provider network, and digital prescription fulfillment capabilities that together give it real cost advantages and subscriber retention levers. The platform increasingly personalizes treatment protocols -- not yet a deep AI story (its AI score is 40, modest relative to the rest of the database), but a real operational moat.

Leadership is a mixed picture. The 77 score reflects capable execution at the management level -- CFO Oluyemi Okupe draws particularly strong marks -- but CEO Andrew Dudum's roughly 90% voting control creates governance risk that the database flags, reflected in a Governance score of just 41. For investors who care about minority shareholder protections, that's a legitimate concern.

The risk picture doesn't stop there. The FDA's stance on compounded GLP-1 medications is the most material near-term overhang. If regulators restrict the compounding pathway that powered much of Hims' recent growth, the revenue trajectory changes. The stock's valuation -- currently trading at around $25.95, a significant premium to tangible book -- already prices in considerable optimism, which limits the margin of safety.

Still, the score profile here is notable: strong financial momentum, a product engine that keeps expanding, and an addressable market that is both enormous and chronically underserved. Hims & Hers is not a quiet infrastructure play; it's a direct-to-consumer brand operating at the intersection of healthcare and technology, betting that it can become the default front door for millions of Americans who'd rather not deal with the traditional medical system.

Given the execution so far, that bet is looking less speculative than it once did.
