CPKC Activates Plans As Signal Workers Strike

11:58AM

Canadian Pacific Kansas City (NYSE: CP) has activated contingency plans to maintain rail operations across Canada after a union representing roughly 300 signal and communications employees launched a strike Sunday morning.

The International Brotherhood of Electrical Workers (IBEW) Council No. 11 initiated the work stoppage at 8 a.m. MDT after rejecting CPKC’s latest contract offer, which included wage and benefit increases. CPKC reported that rail services continue to run safely and efficiently.

While the striking union represents a small portion of CPKC's Canadian workforce, a prolonged dispute could pose operational challenges for the major North American supply chain network. CPKC is urging the union to end the strike and accept binding arbitration. The railway has not disclosed details of the rejected proposal or how long contingency operations will last.

Tesla Tops EV Market as BYD Sales Drop

11:08AM

★ TSLA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB), in Hidden Gems, and in Rule Breakers

Tesla (NASDAQ: TSLA) recaptured the global battery electric vehicle (BEV) sales lead in the first quarter of 2026, overtaking Chinese rival BYD (OTC: BYDDY). Data from Counterpoint Research and TrendForce reveals Tesla secured nearly 13% of the global market by delivering approximately 358,000 vehicles—a 6% year-over-year increase. While BYD suffered eight consecutive months of domestic declines through April, international markets provided a vital lifeline, accounting for over one-third of its Q1 sales. Analysts warn the global EV market is growing increasingly competitive as government incentives diminish.

Sparkline Capital: Avoiding AI Era Value Traps

8:40AM

Faced with AI disruption, a number of software stocks are trading at steep discounts relative to their historic price/earnings. However, which of these are "value traps?" Is there a way to tell the potential winners apart? And have the rules of successful value investing fundamentally changed?

In its report, AI Disruption: Moats and Value Traps, Sparkline Capital aims to tackle these questions. It warns that traditional valuation metrics are blind to technological disruption, actively steering investors into potential "value traps".

The report outlines how to navigate the market's current AI upheaval, including:

Value Investing Disrupted: Traditional value factor performance has been dragged down since 2010 because cheap stocks in tech-exposed sectors are increasingly structural losers rather than bargains.

The Replicated Code Trap: Generative AI has commoditized code generation. Cheap software companies relying solely on code as their barrier to entry are value traps in structural decline.

Complementary Assets Dominate: Long-term winners capture AI profits by controlling the surrounding ecosystem infrastructure that AI models cannot easily replicate.

Irreplaceable Intangibles: True defensibility relies on deep workflow integration that creates high switching costs, proprietary historical datasets, and brand trust.

Where the Value Lies: Real upside sits in selective stock-picking, targeting deeply discounted incumbents that possess the exact intangible assets required to successfully monetize AI.

Ships Risk "Dark" Hormuz Transit As Costs Soar, WSJ

11:55AM

A small number of commercial vessels are "sailing dark" - without lights and tracking systems - to transit the Strait of Hormuz, The Wall Street Journal reports. These daring voyages may offer a minor relief valve for stranded cargo, as shipowners face idle expenses of $10,000 to $15,000 daily and double crew pay.

In order to move, they must also navigate an insurance market where war premiums have climbed to 2.5% to 4% of vessel value, up from 0.25% in peacetime. Safe arrivals claw back 50% of that fee. Underwriters remain split: some discount rates for U.S. naval escorts, while others charge more, viewing escorts as targets. A $40 billion U.S. reinsurance backstop led by Chubb (NYSE: CB) remains completely unused due to its strict escort mandate. Meanwhile, Washington has sanctioned Iran's new toll-collecting authority.

Can the World Function With 9% Less Oil?

7:15AM

How have oil prices remained relatively stable, amid the Strait of Hormuz's 3-month closure? According to JPMorgan (NYSE: JPM) strategists, a major factor cushioning the market is unexpected demand destruction—most notably in China, where oil demand abruptly dropped by 9% (1.5 million barrels per day) with minimal daily disruption.

Chinese consumers are making quiet economic choices, shifting to cheaper alternatives like subways and electric buses. Similar signs of demand destruction are emerging globally: Southeast Asian nations have shortened workweeks, New Delhi is urging conservation throughout India, and Europe's Lufthansa (OTC: DLAKY) has reduced regional flights.

While low Middle Eastern oil dependence keeps the U.S. insulated, domestic pump prices remain above $4 entering summer. Strategists now wonder if this shifting demand will permanently alter global consumption after the war ends.
