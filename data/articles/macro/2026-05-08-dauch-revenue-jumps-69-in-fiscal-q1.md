Dauch Revenue Jumps 69% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 8:15 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Dauch delivered strong revenue growth following its acquisition of Dowlais.
Adjusted earnings per share rose year over year.
Management raised its full-year 2026 sales and profit guidance.

Dauch (NYSE:DCH) marked its first full quarter since acquiring Dowlais with a substantial increase in revenue, driven by the larger combined company. The leading supplier of driveline, metal forming, and powder metallurgy systems to global automakers reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 8, 2026. The period saw improved adjusted profitability but also sizable upfront expenses, with performance in key adjusted figures partly offset by integration and restructuring costs.

Overall, the quarter highlighted early progress toward synergy goals, yet added short-term financial pressures from the major acquisition.

Notable Developments
The Dowlais acquisition drove a 68.8% revenue increase as the company integrated major new operations.
Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 13.0% from 12.6%, showing early progress on synergy savings.
GAAP net income turned negative, hit by $98.9 million in restructuring and acquisition-related costs and higher interest expense.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its full-year targets for both sales and adjusted EBITDA, reflecting confidence in early integration gains and synergy benefits. Cash flow guidance was reiterated, but leadership stressed that major restructuring and implementation costs will continue in the near term. Investors should watch the pace and success of integration and synergy delivery, as this will be critical to future profitability and cash flow improvements.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s revenue boost was fueled by consolidated operations after the Dowlais deal closed, bringing both GKN Automotive’s electric driveline technology and GKN Powder Metallurgy products into the portfolio. Driveline segment sales nearly doubled from $987.0 million to $1.77 billion, while Metal Forming sales rose to $726.2 million.

Profit trends were mixed: adjusted EBITDA, a measure of core operating profit, surged to $308.5 million, with margin improvement showing that synergy capture is underway. However, significant restructuring and acquisition expenses as well as nearly doubled interest costs led to a net loss of $100.3 million.

Cash flow came under pressure, mainly from upfront costs tied to integration and seasonality. The use of adjusted free cash flow widened to a use of $(40.8) million from a use of $(3.9) million in the prior-year period, though management kept full-year guidance unchanged.
