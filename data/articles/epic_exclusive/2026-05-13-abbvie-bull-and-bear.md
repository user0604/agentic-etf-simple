AbbVie Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 7:11 AM

Comments

AbbVie Inc. (NYSE:ABBV) is a major global biopharmaceutical company specializing in immunology, neuroscience, oncology, aesthetics, and metabolic disease. Following the U.S. patent expiration of its blockbuster drug Humira, the company is executing an ambitious strategy to shift growth drivers toward new therapies like Skyrizi, Rinvoq, and an expanding neuroscience portfolio. This portfolio transformation is supported by strong R&D investment, aggressive business development, and a commitment to maintaining robust cash flows and dividends even as former growth engines mature.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Skyrizi and Rinvoq’s Explosive Growth Replaces Humira and Drives New Record Revenues

Robust performance from next-generation immunology therapies already outpaces legacy loss – In the first quarter of 2026, Skyrizi sales rose 29.2% operationally to $4.5 billion, while Rinvoq climbed 20.2% operationally to $2.1 billion, with the combined immunology duo generating more than $6.6 billion—far eclipsing Humira’s $688 million as it declined 40.3% operationally due to biosimilar competition. These new blockbusters, supported by best-in-class efficacy in indications like psoriasis and inflammatory bowel disease, have regulatory exclusivity in the U.S. extending well into the next decade. With further label expansions and new combination regimens (such as Skyrizi + anti-alpha-4 beta-7), the company’s immunology franchise is poised to more than replace Humira revenue and support record-setting top-line growth through at least 2031.

2. Neuroscience Portfolio Emerges as a Multi-Billion-Dollar Pillar with Blockbuster Potential

Strategic investments and pipeline launches rapidly diversify revenue streams – AbbVie’s neuroscience segment grew 24.3% operationally in the first quarter of 2026, reaching nearly $2.9 billion in revenue. Vraylar sales rose 18.4%, and new launches like Vyalev in Parkinson’s disease are on track for blockbuster revenue this year. Management expects its franchises in psychiatry, migraine, and Parkinson’s to each peak above $5 billion in sales over time, supported by multiple pivotal trials, business development, and a late-stage pipeline. This diversified growth reduces reliance on any single product and supports the company’s position as a neuroscience industry leader.

3. Expansive Pipeline and Aggressive Business Development Create Durable Long-Term Growth Engines

Broad late-stage pipeline and active M&A drive forward-looking growth visibility – The company has executed significant deals for innovative assets over the past two years, including oral peptides (Nimble), in vivo CAR-T therapies (Capstan), KRAS inhibitors (Kestrel), and non-opioid pain programs (Haisco). Multiple late-stage pipeline readouts in immunology, oncology, and obesity/metabolic disorders are slated for 2026 and beyond. With Skyrizi’s exclusivity protected through 2031 and pipeline assets like ABBV-295 showing strong promise (nearly 10% weight loss in just 12 weeks in Phase 1), the company is well positioned to address future market shifts. The financial flexibility and willingness to pursue both early- and late-stage innovation increase the ability to mitigate patent cliffs and drive sustainable growth.

4. Strong Free Cash Flow and Rising Dividend Underpin Shareholder Returns

Financial discipline enables robust capital allocation for both growth and income – In Q1 2026, the company reported $15 billion in revenue (up 12.4% year over year) and adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings per share of $2.65, slightly ahead of guidance despite $0.41 headwind from acquired R&D. Full-year adjusted earnings guidance was raised, reflecting management’s confidence in ongoing growth. Strategic capital spending, such as a new $1.4 billion manufacturing campus in North Carolina, further underscores commitment to both innovation and long-term shareholder value.

5. Market Leadership in Commercial Execution Maintains Competitive Moats Amid Threats

Superior market share, physician adoption, and patient retention signal durable franchise strength – Skyrizi now commands more than four times the “in-play” share versus its nearest psoriasis competitor, a position reinforced by high new-to-brand prescription (NBRx) rates and acceleration in key indications. Strong physician and patient loyalty—bolstered by industry-leading efficacy data and extensive direct-to-consumer marketing—outpace competitive threats, including emerging oral agents. Even as new entrants like Oruka report compelling Phase 2 results, the company’s proven ability to defend market share, swiftly expand indications, and secure formulary access fortifies its competitive moat, supporting both near- and medium-term growth resilience.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Rapid Humira Decline and Intensifying Competition May Outpace New Growth Offsets

The erosion of legacy revenue streams could challenge replacement efforts – Humira revenue fell more than 40% year over year in the first quarter of 2026, and although Skyrizi and Rinvoq are showing robust growth, the speed and magnitude of Humira’s fall heighten risk if new launches stumble or face unexpected bottlenecks. At the same time, competitors such as Oruka Therapeutics are producing clinical data that not only meet but, in some cases, surpass Skyrizi’s efficacy in psoriasis, raising the risk of future market share erosion if late-stage results hold. Should pricing pressure, biosimilar threats, or failed pipeline milestones arise, there is potential for the company’s much-touted growth bridge to fall short.

2. Regulatory and Executional Setbacks Threaten Aesthetics Franchise and Product Expansion

Manufacturing and approval delays undermine diversification and competitive positioning – The aesthetics division, anchored by Botox Cosmetic, is vulnerable to regulatory and operational risks. In 2026, the U.S. Food & Drug Administration issued a complete response letter regarding a potential new anti-wrinkle therapy due to manufacturing concerns, delaying competitive defense against peers and potential market expansion. The consumer discretionary nature of aesthetics, combined with macroeconomic headwinds and slower recovery in filler markets, could further pressure revenue if innovation or execution falters. Such delays may undermine the diversification strategy and dampen overall revenue growth.

3. High R&D Spend and Acquisition Charges Pressure GAAP Profitability and Could Limit Financial Flexibility

Persistent earnings pressure from pipeline investment may constrain future optionality – While the company’s adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings per share reached $2.65 in Q1 2026, its GAAP EPS dropped by 45.8% to $0.39, heavily impacted by in-process R&D and amortization charges. High spending on pipeline development and business development is critical for future growth but poses near-term risk to profitability and leverage, especially if anticipated launches underperform or cost overruns emerge. With multiple tranches of senior notes maturing in the next few years, a narrowing balance between reinvestment, debt servicing, and dividend maintenance could create future constraints during times of unforeseen market stress.
