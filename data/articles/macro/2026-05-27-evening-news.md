1. A $6 Billion Investment in Amazon Chips

Agentic artificial intelligence (AI) is very hungry for processing power. Snowflake (NYSE: SNOW), recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers, is handing Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) $6 billion over five years for access to its Graviton central processing unit (CPU) chips inside Amazon Web Services (AWS) data centers -- joining Meta (NASDAQ: META) and Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) as one of AWS's biggest CPU customers. Shares are up 30% after hours.

CPUs are having a moment: AI agents need armies of processors to orchestrate tasks, and that's sending demand -- and share prices -- soaring for chip players Intel (NASDAQ: INTC), Advanced Micro Devices (NASDAQ: AMD), and Arm Holdings (NASDAQ: ARM).
This romance started early: In 2015, Snowflake was literally born on the back of AWS, and with nearly 14,000 customers now on its platform, it's clearly not going anywhere.

2. Salesforce Beats Q1, but Guidance Disappoints

Salesforce (NYSE: CRM) CEO Marc Benioff has spent the better part of this year telling anyone who'll listen that AI won't kill SaaS (software-as-a-service), and this quarter's numbers gave him some ammunition. The company crushed first-quarter estimates on both ends, dropping $11.13 billion in revenue (up 13%) and adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $3.88 -- well clear of the $3.13 Wall Street expected. Salesforce is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers.

$3.4 billion in combined ARR: The company's Agentforce and Data Cloud platform hit nearly $3.4 billion in combined annual recurring revenue (ARR), with Agentforce alone accounting for $1.2 billion.
Growing fast, with a catch: More than half of Agentforce bookings came from existing customers -- a great loyalty signal. Less great is that Salesforce slashed its cash flow growth outlook to 4% to 5% from 9% to 10%, blaming a $25 million debt issuance tied to a share buyback.
3. Meta Joins the AI Paywall Club

Meta (NASDAQ: META) is charging for AI features for the first time, $7.99/month for One Plus and $19.99/month for Premium. Testing kicks off next month in Singapore, Guatemala, and Bolivia. (A free tier survives, for now.) Shares were up 3.74% today.

Your move, OpenAI: With its massive user base, even modest conversion rates could mean serious subscription revenue for Meta. It's all part of what Motley Fool analyst Jason Moser recently called "Meta's aggressive push to compete with OpenAI and Alphabet's (NASDAQ: GOOG) Google in frontier AI models."
Why the price gap matters: The pricier tier buys more computing horsepower for bigger, more complex tasks -- Meta's pitch to creators and businesses that it's more than just a social media company with an AI chatbot stapled on.
4. Maybe Your Retirement Plan Is Too Safe

Most retirement planning assumes you'll live to 95, but the odds say you probably won't, and that changes everything. Robert Brokamp, CFP, the Fool's senior retirement advisor and financial planning expert, has more.

By Robert Brokamp, CFP®
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Determining when you can retire and how much you can spend in retirement requires a tool that can do the math, factoring in several important variables and assumptions. One key assumption is how long you'll live, since that will dictate how long your money will need to last.

Most retirement experts recommend that you plan to live into your 90s, with 95 being the common age. The classic 4% rule (the amount retirees can safely withdraw in their first year of retirement) assumes a 30-year retirement – i.e., someone who retires at 65 will live to 95.

It's a prudent assumption. There's just one problem: You probably won't live that long.
