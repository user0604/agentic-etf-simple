Key Takeaways
Revenue outperformed guidance but losses widened as costs and investments grew.
Revenue rose 3.4% year over year.

Medical device maker Inogen (NASDAQ:INGN) delivered revenue growth above its internal target, but deeper losses marked a setback in profitability progress. The global leader in portable oxygen concentrators highlighted strong gains in international sales and product innovation, with financial results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 released on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed mixed results, with innovation and international expansion offset by rising costs and persistent challenges in the U.S. market.

Notable Developments
International revenue climbed 17.8%, driven by strong demand for portable oxygen concentrators outside the U.S, while domestic sales and rentals declined.
The company launched new products, including the Aurora CPAP mask and Rove 6 concentrator, and increased research and development spending by 26.4% to $5.1 million.
Inogen repurchased $1.9 million worth of shares as part of a $30 million buyback program, maintaining over $110 million in cash and no debt at quarter end.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $366–373 million, reflecting confidence in future growth. The company expects profitability to improve in coming quarters as investments in research and product expansion begin to bear fruit. Investors should watch for improvement in U.S. market performance and continued execution on new product launches.

Quarterly Highlights

International markets provided the biggest boost, as sales climbed to $37.7 million, up nearly 18% from last year. This performance offset declines in both U.S. sales, which dropped 4.8%, and U.S. rentals, which fell 8.0% as the U.S. rental patient base shrank by over 3,000 patients during the period. Management emphasized the impact of product mix shifts and the company's success in expanding its reach overseas.

Investments in innovation stayed front and center, with notable product launches such as the Aurora CPAP mask for sleep apnea (a type of face mask used with machines that help users breathe during sleep) and the Rove 6 portable oxygen concentrator introduced in new international markets. Research and development spending rose 26.4% year over year as the company continued to broaden its respiratory care portfolio, including work on the Simeox airway clearance device.

Profitability was pressured by these higher expenses, as total operating costs rose faster than revenue. Adjusted EBITDA (a measure of profitability that removes certain non-cash items and interest, taxes, and depreciation) swung from slightly positive to negative. Despite deeper net losses, Inogen returned capital to shareholders through a share repurchase and ended the quarter with a strong cash position and no debt.

What Inogen Does

Inogen designs, manufactures, and sells respiratory medical devices including portable oxygen concentrators and other products for patients with chronic breathing conditions.
