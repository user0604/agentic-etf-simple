Sprott Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 3:05 PM

Comments

Sprott Inc. (NYSE:SII) is a global asset manager specializing in precious metals and critical materials, leveraging a suite of exchange-traded funds, physical trusts, and bespoke strategies. Its growth strategy centers on recurring management fees from scalable products, episodic performance fees during commodity bull cycles, and consistent product innovation, with a focus on capitalizing on secular demand for real assets and the energy transition.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Explosive Assets Under Management Growth Accelerates Recurring Revenue Base

Surging assets under management fuel sustainable fee growth – The company’s assets under management (AUM) nearly doubled year over year, climbing 89% from $31.5 billion at year-end 2024 to $59.6 billion at the end of 2025, then reaching $70.1 billion by mid-February 2026. This trajectory was sustained with AUM hitting $65.1 billion at March 31, 2026—up 43% year over year and 9% sequentially. These AUM gains, driven by both substantial net inflows and robust market appreciation in metals and energy transition materials, underpin a rapidly expanding base of recurring management fees. As AUM compounds, particularly from scalable funds like ETFs and physical trusts, the company benefits from greater operating leverage, more predictable revenues, and increased institutional credibility, all reinforcing long-term earnings visibility.

2. Product Innovation and First-Mover Advantage in Critical Materials Fuel Differentiated Growth

Expanding beyond legacy gold and silver into new materials broadens growth pathways – Sprott’s rapid product development—in areas like uranium, rare earths, and copper—demonstrates an ability to capture emerging demand as global supply chains shift and the clean energy transition intensifies. The launch of the Sprott Rare Earths Ex-China ETF and the milestone cross-listing of the Physical Copper Trust in the U.S. are recent evidence of its agility and market insight. The Sprott Silver Miners ETF reached $1 billion in first-year assets, a company record. By targeting unaddressed investor needs (ex-China exposures, physical commodity access, decarbonization mandates), Sprott creates sticky, high-value mandates less prone to passive fee compression and positions itself as the leader in thematics that could dominate capital flows for years.

3. Operating Leverage and Cost Discipline Drive Margin Expansion Despite Rapid Growth

Improved efficiency boosts profitability even as the platform scales – The company’s compensation ratio—a key cost control metric—fell from 45% in 2024 to 40% in 2025 and dropped further in Q4, signaling effective cost discipline while rapidly growing headcount and product footprint. Adjusted EBITDA (which excludes episodic and non-cash compensation swings) surged by 88% year over year in Q4 2025 and 43% for the full year, reaching $121 million. With most ETFs now above breakeven scale and increased automation in back office functions, operating leverage is enhancing profitability, allowing incremental AUM to fall more directly to the bottom line. This margin expansion underpins future dividend raises and bolsters resilience against market volatility.

4. Capital Returns Strategy Aligns Management and Shareholder Interests

Meaningful share repurchases and dividend hikes demonstrate confidence and discipline – During late 2025 and early 2026, the company executed a substantial $337 million share buyback and raised its dividend by 33%. These actions signal both conviction in the company’s intrinsic value and management’s intent to return excess capital, especially given insider alignment (the CEO is the second-largest shareholder). Regular, sustainable dividend growth anchored by recurring earnings, coupled with opportunistic buybacks, provides a clear capital return framework that contrasts favorably with more levered or unloved peers. The company’s approach—prioritizing strong liquidity, operating leverage, and prudent buybacks—mitigates dilution from stock-based compensation and reassures long-term holders about ongoing stewardship.

5. Secular Macro Tailwinds for Precious Metals and Critical Materials Support Durable Demand

Global energy transition and deglobalization trends expand Sprott’s addressable market – Policy momentum around supply chain security, decarbonization, and geopolitical risk mitigation continues to elevate demand for physical metals, uranium, copper, and rare earths. Central bank gold buying, rapidly rising spot prices, and inflows into energy transition themes have been direct growth catalysts for the company’s strategies. Management expects government actions in 2026 and beyond to further increase both institutional and retail allocations to these themes. By concentrating on these macro-driven secular trends—and delivering specialized vehicles to access them—the company’s platform is well positioned to capture inflows and episodic fee upside for multiple cycles irrespective of the direction of broader equity markets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Earnings Volatility and Dependence on Episodic Performance Fees Create Unpredictable Profitability

Lumpiness in non-recurring revenues challenges smooth earnings growth – The company’s bottom line is frequently boosted by episodic performance and carried interest fees, which are inherently tied to commodity bull markets and cannot be relied on each year. In 2025, over $54 million in such fees came from specific strong fund performance and some legacy products that will not recur. Management has explicitly stated that visibility into future realized performance is limited, as these fees are highly market-dependent and comprise a volatile, non-core layer atop recurring revenues. If commodity prices correct or performance fees normalize, earnings could drop sharply, potentially unsettling investors seeking predictability and leading to valuation contraction.

2. Intensified Commodity Price Volatility Could Reverse AUM Growth and Pressure Margins

Sharp downturns in metals can drive outflows and hurt profitability – While volatility in metals prices creates upside during rallies, it also poses significant downside risk. The violent sell-off in gold and silver at the start of 2026 underscores how quickly flows can reverse amid speculative or sentiment-driven corrections. As the company’s AUM, inflows, and performance fees are all sensitive to these price swings, a prolonged slump could cause outflows, lower fee collections, and shrink profit margins. Since the business is increasingly leveraged to commodity cycles, especially in newer critical materials funds with less established client bases, this concentration risk could undermine growth plans if global risk appetite or macro dynamics abruptly shift.

3. Rising Stock-Based Compensation and Dilution Risk Erode Per-Share Value

Elevated equity-based pay may offset buyback gains and dilute shareholder returns – The shift to a cash-settled restricted stock unit (RSU) plan under IFRS 2, coupled with the company’s soaring share price (up 132% in 2025), has caused stock-based compensation expense to spike. While adjusted metrics strip out this volatility, persistent high equity-based payouts partially offset the benefits of share repurchases and can limit the accretive effect of buyback programs. If the share price continues rising or awards grow further in response to expansion, net dilution could increase, pressuring per-share earnings and potentially capping total return for outside shareholders despite headline profit growth.
