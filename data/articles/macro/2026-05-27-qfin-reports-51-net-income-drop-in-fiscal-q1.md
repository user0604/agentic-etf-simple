Qfin Reports 51% Net Income Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 6:15 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Qfin reported a sharp contraction in earnings as loan volumes and revenue declined. Management guided for further net income declines amid ongoing regulatory uncertainty.

Digital lender Qfin (NASDAQ:QFIN) reported a challenging start to the year as loan origination and profitability continued to shrink. The leading China-based fintech platform saw core business volumes pressured by regulatory tightening and cautious risk management. For the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (reported on May 26, 2026), both revenue and earnings fell sharply amid a shift to smaller, higher-quality loans and persistent market headwinds. Overall, the quarter reflected steady risk controls but significant financial contraction.

Notable Developments
Loan origination volume fell 26.8% year over year, reaching RMB 65,034 million, reflecting tighter credit and a focus on quality.
90+ day delinquency rate was 3.50%—above historical norms, but day-1 delinquency and 30-day collection rates improved versus the prior quarter.
The share of repeat borrowers remained strong at 86.8%, with cumulative registered users increasing 11% to 297.5 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects net income to decline by up to 51% again in the next quarter, with guidance ranging from RMB 830 million to RMB 910 million. The company is prioritizing defensive credit standards and expects regulatory headwinds to persist.

Quarterly Highlights

Total loan origination for the period was RMB 65.0 billion, down more than one-quarter from a year earlier as Qfin reduced exposure to higher-risk borrower segments and tightened risk standards. The company’s capital-light and technology solution loans comprised just under half of this amount and saw similar declines, suggesting regulatory and market challenges affected all business lines.

Asset quality was mixed: the 90+ day delinquency rate rose to 3.50%, reflecting the legacy loan portfolio’s impact. However, early delinquency measures improved over the prior quarter, and the provision for loan losses rose 50% year over year to RMB 1,234.7 million, showing ongoing defensive risk management.

Operating costs increased by nearly 8%, mainly due to higher loan loss provisions and servicing costs, while sales and marketing expenses fell by 23%. Net profit margin compressed, and both core lending and platform service revenue declined, even as total registered user numbers and repeat borrowing rates increased.

What Qfin Does

Qfin provides digital lending and credit facilitation services in China, connecting consumers and small businesses with financial institutions to access credit through technology-driven risk assessment and loan processing solutions.
