1. Amazon Reboots Its Shopping AI

★ AMZN is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) is discontinuing its Rufus chatbot and consolidating its AI shopping strategy around Alexa, launching a new tool called Alexa for Shopping that can answer queries, compare products, and take actions on a user's behalf. The move comes as AI shopping bots from OpenAI, Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG), and Perplexity threaten to disrupt how consumers shop online.

What's new: Alexa for Shopping combines features from Rufus and Alexa+, pulling from users' shopping history and Amazon's product catalog. The tool will appear in search results, lets users schedule purchases at target prices, and doesn't require a Prime membership. Amazon's top Alexa executive Daniel Rausch argued the offering beats rivals because it taps real-time inventory and delivery data rather than scraping web results.
Ad implications: Inserting Alexa into search results gives Amazon valuable promotional real estate, which could disrupt the millions of third-party sellers who pay for sponsored product listings. Those ads account for the bulk of Amazon's advertising revenue. The company also continues to block many external bots from accessing its site, even as CEO Andy Jassy has said Amazon expects to eventually partner with third-party agents.
2. Walmart Cuts 1,000 Roles

Walmart (NASDAQ: WMT) is eliminating roughly 1,000 jobs as the retailer simplifies its operating structure under new CEO John Furner, per Reuters. The cuts come as Walmart doubles down on a tech-focused strategy to court higher-income shoppers and expand its marketplace and delivery businesses.

The pivot: In a memo to employees, Walmart's heads of global technology and AI acceleration said the company is reorganizing to clarify ownership and align roles with the skills it needs going forward. Over the past year, Walmart shifted from running Walmart U.S., Sam's Club, and international markets separately to building on a single shared platform. Many affected staff have been asked to relocate to Bentonville or Northern California.
Bigger picture: Walmart became the first retailer to hit $1 trillion in market value in February. The company has been ramping up its digital transformation to compete with Amazon, Costco (NASDAQ: COST), and Aldi. Walmart employs about 2.1 million people globally, making it the largest U.S. private employer with 1.6 million U.S. workers. Quarterly results arrive on May 21.
3. Huang Joins Trump's China Trip
★ NVDA is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang is joining President Trump's trip to China this week after the U.S. president personally called him to invite him along, following media coverage of his initial absence from the delegation. Huang flew to Alaska to board Air Force One, with Trump set to meet Chinese President Xi Jinping Thursday and Friday.

What's at stake: Nvidia's most advanced AI chips have faced tightening U.S. export restrictions on China sales over the past four years. The company said in February that even U.S.-approved versions of its chips hadn't been allowed into China. Opening China to U.S. businesses is Trump's "first request" to Xi, the president said in a social media post.
China's response so far: Beijing has been pushing domestic chip development and homegrown AI models like DeepSeek to reduce reliance on Nvidia. A recent article in the Chinese Communist Party's official journal acknowledged that local companies have had to slow development because of U.S. chip restrictions, while highlighting Nvidia's continued dominance in global GPUs.
