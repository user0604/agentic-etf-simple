SABESP: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 1, 2026, 9:28 AM

Comments

Companhia de Saneamento Básico do Estado de São Paulo - SABESP (NYSE:SBS) is the main water supply and sewage provider in São Paulo, responsible for serving nearly 28 million people, and since 2026 has also operated in energy generation via its acquisition of EMAE. The company’s recent privatization kicked off an ambitious transformation, focusing on operating efficiency, infrastructure modernization, and the universalization of water and sanitation while seeking to unlock private-sector governance and growth levers in a rapidly evolving regulatory and environmental landscape.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Structural Efficiency Gains Drive Sustainable Margin Expansion

Material operating efficiency improvements post-privatization are underpinning lasting profit growth. In the first quarter of 2026, the company’s adjusted EBITDA climbed 26% year over year to $3.8 billion Brazilian real, with adjusted net income up 32% to R$1.6 billion, compared to the same period in 2025. These gains are backed by a rigorous cost transformation program that includes a 13% headcount reduction, migration of 86% of energy sourcing to the free market, R$170 million in personnel savings, and a R$56 million year-over-year reduction in power costs in the quarter. With digital upgrades (like SAP S/4HANA), these are not one-off results but structural changes, positioning the company to deliver durable margin gains even as industry peers struggle with inflation and regulatory headwinds. As a result, adjusted EBITDA margin reached 60% for the quarter, compared to 54% in the same period last year. This demonstrates that operational discipline is becoming embedded, allowing for high profitability alongside growth investments and sector-leading service delivery.

2. Regulatory Certainty Provides Long-Term Revenue Visibility

Extended and unified concession agreements secure cash flows for decades ahead. The 2024 URAE-1 agreement unified 371 municipal contracts under a single regulatory framework through 2060, sharply reducing legal and political uncertainty. This alignment grants significant contractual and legal security to recover capital investments and lock in revenue streams for the long term. With regulator-approved tariff increases phased in through 2025, net sanitation revenue rose 10.9% year over year in Q1 2026, outpacing historical inflation and providing cash flow predictability even as the customer base expands. This structure, rare in Latin American infrastructure, enhances the company’s credit profile and underpins ongoing access to capital, which is essential for the multibillion-real investment cycle required to meet universalization mandates and modernize assets.

3. Diversification into Energy Accelerates Growth and Resilience

The strategic acquisition of EMAE transforms the company into a vertically integrated utility, opening new growth vectors. By finalizing the purchase of Empresa Metropolitana de Águas e Energia (EMAE) in January 2026, the company has immediately elevated its growth platform, expanding from core water and sanitation into renewable energy generation and commercialization. This not only diversifies revenues but unlocks operational synergies: joint reservoir management strengthens water security, co-optimized asset bases reduce climate risk, and integrated power solutions lower long-term operating costs. Such a model mirrors best-in-class European utilities, positioning the company at the forefront of Brazil’s energy transition and ESG evolution, and potentially enabling bundled service offerings that enhance customer stickiness and regulatory goodwill.

4. Accelerated Investment Expands Service Coverage and Social Impact

Rapidly scaling investment is driving a step change in service coverage and ESG outcomes. Capital expenditures surged 31% year over year in Q1 2026 to R$3.7 billion, enabling further progress toward universalization: water supply coverage hit 87% and sewage collection 77% of the addressable market, ahead of regulatory benchmarks. The number of new low-income/special-rate connections is climbing, directly improving social inclusion and resilience in underserved communities. These investments not only fulfill core regulatory obligations and future-proof the business against tightening environmental standards, but also strengthen the brand’s social license to operate — a growing advantage as investors globally demand more from essential infrastructure providers in both financial and ESG performance.

5. Robust Liquidity and Active Capital Markets Access Underpin Growth

The company’s diversified funding structure and capital market credibility enable strategic flexibility. In 2026, the company raised over R$6.3 billion from new debentures and US$1.5 billion from “A/B” Blue Bonds, boosting gross debt to R$51.6 billion while maintaining ample covenant cushion. Liquidity is strong, with R$3.54 billion in cash and R$15.6 billion in ‘AAA’ Brazilian bank deposits. Despite rising net debt (now 42% of equity), all major foreign currency obligations are hedged, and debt maturities are staggered well into the 2030s and 2040s. This balance sheet resilience enables the company to sustain elevated CapEx and seize strategic opportunities (such as the EMAE acquisition) without undue pressure on operations or share dilution, setting the stage for compounded growth above peer averages.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Rapidly Rising Leverage May Threaten Financial Flexibility

Accelerated debt accumulation is introducing new balance sheet risks. The company’s aggressive capex program — R$3.7 billion in Q1 2026 alone — has outpaced cash flow growth, resulting in net debt rising by R$4.7 billion in the first quarter and taking the leverage ratio from 40% to 42%. With R$51.6 billion in gross debt and free cash flow turning negative from investing activities, future upward moves in interest rates or loss of capital access could strain financial flexibility. While covenant headroom currently exists and hedging is in place, persistence of this capex-to-cash flow gap could eventually constrain dividend capacity or even force asset sales or equity issuance — especially if financial markets sour on Brazilian infrastructure or the company faces macro shocks.

2. Growing Subsidized Customer Base Risks Diluting Per Capita Revenue

Expanding social tariffs and subsidized rates may erode average yield. While regulatory mandates and social goals are core to the company’s mission, the share of customers receiving subsidized rates (expanded under the amended frameworks from July 2025) is rising sharply. In Q1 2026, mix dilution from these segments reduced overall yield by 3.4%, even as headline tariffs rose. This structural shift means that, even with more connections and improved collections, top-line growth becomes increasingly reliant on regulatory tariff adjustments and efficiency gains rather than volume or customer mix. Over time, this could cap profit growth if inflation outpaces allowed tariff hikes, or if political pressure makes future increases harder to secure.

3. High Short Interest and Share Price Volatility Signal Market Skepticism

Recent spikes in short interest and share price lows reflect persistent investor concerns. In May 2026, short interest in the company’s shares rose over 400% from late April and the stock set new 12-month lows, despite reporting strong earnings growth and completing a major reverse stock split to meet exchange requirements. Such activity may indicate worries about intensified capex, regulatory risk, or potential equity dilution—especially in the context of broader macro volatility in Brazil. It also suggests that investor confidence in near-term returns and efficiency improvements remains fragile, heightening the risk of a negative market reaction if execution falters or external shocks materialize.
