Netflix Ads: The New Revenue Powerhouse?

10:20AM

★ NFLX is recommended in Stock Advisor

Netflix’s (NASDAQ: NFLX) decision to hike its standard ad-free plan to $19.99 signals a definitive shift in streaming economics, reports CNBC. As the gap between subscription fees and advertising revenue narrows, the industry is reaching a "tipping point" where highly engaged viewers on cheaper tiers may actually be more valuable than premium subscribers.

With over 325 million global subscribers, Netflix viewers consumed over 95 billion hours of content in the first half of 2025 alone. This massive scale allows Netflix to narrow the revenue gap between tiers; analysis shows a subscriber watching 41 hours monthly on the $9 ad-supported plan can generate nearly $25 in total revenue—notably outperforming the $19.99 premium fee. Consequently, Netflix is on track for its advertising revenue to reach $3 billion in 2026, up 2X year-over-year. 

While rivals like Disney’s (NYSE: DIS) Hulu, Paramount (NASDAQ: PSKY), and Warner Bros. Discovery (NASDAQ: WBD) have long used ad-supported models, Netflix’s advantage lies in its larger subscriber base and viewing hours. As 71% of new industry growth shifts to ad-supported tiers, Netflix is leveraging its scale to capture high-margin revenue. For investors, the strategy is clear: the most valuable customers are no longer just those who pay the most, but those who watch the most.

Goldman Pushes Rate Cut Forecast Back One Quarter

3:35PM

Goldman Sachs (NYSE: GS) has pushed back its projected timeline for U.S. interest rate cuts by one quarter, citing "stickier-than-expected" inflation. The bank now forecasts that the Federal Reserve will implement its next two rate cuts in December 2026 and March 2027.

The adjustment follows reports that U.S. PCE inflation will likely remain near 3% through 2026, significantly above the Fed’s 2% target. Economists attribute this persistence to a recent oil shock and high energy costs. According to Goldman’s research, the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) will likely require lower monthly inflation prints and further labor market softening before a policy reset occurs.

While the timeline has shifted, Goldman’s terminal Fed rate forecast remains unchanged at 3%–3.25%. The current policy rate stands at 3.50%–3.75% following the FOMC’s April decision to hold steady amid global geopolitical tensions.

Nike Faces Class Action Over Tariff Price Hikes

12:11PM

★ NKE is recommended in Stock Advisor

Consumers have filed a proposed class action lawsuit against Nike (NYSE: NKE) in a Portland, Oregon federal court, alleging the company failed to refund tariff costs passed on to shoppers. The legal action follows a February 2026 U.S. Supreme Court ruling that struck down sweeping Trump-era tariffs imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act.

According to the filing, Nike paid approximately $1 billion in import levies, leading to price increases of $2 to $10 on footwear and apparel. Plaintiffs argue that Nike is not entitled to keep the "significant" government refunds resulting from the court's decision while also retaining the higher prices paid by consumers. The suit aims to prevent Nike from recovering the same payments twice. Other major firms, including Costco (NASDAQ: COST) and FedEx (NYSE: FDX), are reportedly facing similar legal challenges over the now-invalidated tariffs.

Copper Prices Rally on Tight Supply, AI Demand

7:12AM

Copper futures climbed above $6.24 per pound in early May 2026, nearing the all-time record of $6.58 as the market grapples with a tightening supply squeeze and surging demand from the artificial intelligence sector.

Supply conditions have deteriorated significantly, particularly in Chile, where production hit a nine-year low in February. Regional output dropped 9% in March, hampered by operational challenges and a critical shortage of sulfuric acid. This chemical scarcity is being driven by a Chinese export ban and shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, which have blocked over half of Middle Eastern sulfur shipments.

Simultaneously, the rapid expansion of AI infrastructure is fueling massive demand. Analysts note that a single large AI data center can require up to 50,000 tonnes of copper. With total data center demand expected to reach 475,000 tonnes annually by 2026, Citigroup (NYSE: C) forecasts prices will average $13,000 per metric ton through the end of the year.

While a major new mining project is under discussion in the Democratic Republic of Congo, it lacks a firm timeline, leaving the market in a structural deficit for the foreseeable future.
