Key Takeaways
Mixed results as top-line growth and core income were offset by wider net losses and weaker joint ventures.

BRT Apartments (NYSE:BRT) faced a quarter of mixed outcomes, with modest gains in operating income and Funds from Operations but continued net losses and disappointing results from joint venture properties. The multi-family property owner and operator, which concentrates on Class B or better assets in the Southeast United States and Texas, reported its first-quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Overall, the period reflects progress in some financial measures but ongoing challenges in profitability, leverage, and certain operating regions.

Notable Developments
Funds from Operations per share rose to $0.33, up 10%, but Adjusted Funds from Operations was unchanged at $0.39 per share.
Net loss attributable to common stockholders widened year over year to $2.7 million, reflecting higher operating expenses and weaker profitability in joint ventures.
Total portfolio units grew from 7,947 to 8,311, but occupancy in Texas dropped from 92.2% to 88.6% and same-store Net Operating Income in Texas decreased 8.4%.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management chose not to provide explicit new financial guidance for fiscal 2026, citing uncertainty. The most important factor for investors to track in upcoming quarters is the company’s elevated level of leverage—currently at 75% of enterprise value—and how this impacts financial flexibility and property returns if revenue growth fails to accelerate.

Quarterly Highlights

Operating income and funds from operations showed improvement due to additions to the property portfolio and stable performance in most target regions. The company owned or co-owned 31 properties, up from 29 a year ago, resulting in a 4% rise in Net Operating Income at the combined portfolio level. However, same-store results were mixed, as occupancy in Texas—a key market—declined and rent growth in most locations was limited.

Joint venture properties were a weak spot, contributing a loss of $308,000 compared to a modest gain previously. This reflects higher property operating costs and a stagnation in cash flow from these unconsolidated properties, which weighed on overall profitability. Performance gaps in joint ventures offset some gains achieved in the core wholly owned portfolio.

The company maintained its quarterly dividend at $0.25 per share and repurchased 318,593 shares at an average price of $14.14 early in the year. While this demonstrates a commitment to shareholder returns, high levels of outstanding debt and a market capitalization decline to $251.9 million mean careful capital management will be critical going forward.

What BRT Apartments Does

BRT Apartments owns, operates, and invests in multi-family rental properties—mainly Class B or better—focused on the Southeast United States and Texas.
