Jack in the Box Reports 4.3% Revenue Drop in Q2

Key Takeaways
Jack in the Box reported a challenging quarter as traffic and same-store sales continued to decline.
Revenue fell 4.3% year over year as non-GAAP restaurant-level margins dropped to 16.4%.
Management now expects a low single digit same-store sales decline for the full year.

Jack in the Box (NASDAQ:JACK) faced another tough period as negative traffic trends and shrinking margins weighed on its business. The quick-service burger chain, which mainly serves the West Coast and Southwest, struggled to gain ground in the second quarter of fiscal 2026 (reported on May 13, 2026). Overall, the quarter signaled continued headwinds, with little relief on core growth or profitability measures.

Business overview

Jack in the Box operates and franchises quick-service restaurants, offering burgers, sandwiches, and other fast-food favorites across the United States.

Quarterly performance
Same-store sales declined 3.8% across the system, an improvement from the prior quarter but still negative, with all weakness driven by fewer customer visits.
There was zero net restaurant growth in the period, as nine new locations opened but nine closed.
Margins compressed further, with restaurant-level margin falling 3.2 percentage points, mainly because of cost inflation and lower sales volumes.
Looking ahead

Management now projects a low single digit decline in same-store sales for fiscal 2026, dialing back earlier hopes for improvement. It expects company-owned restaurant margins will remain under pressure, forecasting about 17% margin for the year.

Additional highlights

System same-store sales fell 3.8% in the quarter, compared to a 4.4% decline one year earlier and a 6.7% drop in the previous quarter. The company cited declining customer transactions as the primary issue, with price increases providing only partial relief.

Restaurant and franchise-level margins both fell sharply year over year. Higher food costs led the restaurant-level margin to decline to 16.4% from 19.6%. Franchise-level margin fell to 37.9% from 40.0% due to lower sales, a decrease in the number of restaurants, and lower lease termination fees.

Store count held flat, recording no net openings for the quarter. Management’s closure program, combined with slow new unit growth, implies little expansion ahead and suggests continued pressure on scale-driven revenues and royalties.
