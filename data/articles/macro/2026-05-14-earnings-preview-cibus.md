Q4 FY2025 Recap

The fourth quarter of 2025 marked a period of operational streamlining and strategic progress for Cibus. The company continued to focus on developing and commercializing gene-edited traits, especially herbicide-tolerant rice, while also advancing its sustainable ingredients program. Revenue for the quarter was $3.6 million, which missed analyst estimates, mainly due to timing of collaboration revenue recognition. The net loss for the quarter was $31.9 million, reflecting ongoing investment in research and development, as well as restructuring costs. The company ended the year with $9.9 million in cash and subsequently raised $22.3 million in January 2026, extending its cash runway into late 2026. Management emphasized cost-reduction efforts, including facility consolidation and workforce reductions, aiming to lower annual net cash usage to $30 million or less in 2026.

Positives

Progress in commercialization partnerships: The company now has seven rice customers, with potential annual royalties exceeding $200 million, and has executed a letter of intent with Interoc for Latin American commercialization, targeting initial market entry in 2027.
Advances in regulatory environment: Positive regulatory developments in the European Union, United Kingdom, and several Latin American countries have improved the outlook for gene-edited crop commercialization. This regulatory clarity supports future expansion opportunities.
Technical milestones achieved: The company reported significant improvements in gene-editing efficiency and scalability, which should support future growth in both licensing and service models.
Negatives
Revenue missed analyst estimates: Collaboration and research revenue was lower than expected, mainly due to timing issues, which resulted in a miss compared to consensus forecasts.
Continued net losses: The company reported a net loss of $31.9 million for the quarter and $132.2 million for the year, reflecting ongoing investment needs and limited near-term commercial revenue.
Impairment charges and restructuring costs: The quarter included a $9.1 million impairment related to facility wind-downs and ongoing restructuring expenses, which weighed on the bottom line.
Liquidity remains a concern: While the January 2026 capital raise extended the cash runway, management noted that additional funding will be needed beyond late 2026 to sustain operations and support commercialization plans.
