CBAK Energy Technology (NASDAQ:CBAT) achieved strong sales growth but struggled with profitability as cost pressures mounted. The advanced battery solutions provider, which supplies lithium-ion batteries and battery materials for electric vehicles and energy storage, reported its first quarter results on May 18, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Notable Developments
Revenues nearly doubled, reaching $69.62 million due to expanded production capacity and strong international demand, particularly in Light Electric Vehicles (LEV), which grew revenue by over 440% year over year.
Gross margin dropped sharply to 1.5%, down more than twelve percentage points, as higher costs during production ramp-up offset top-line gains.
Hitrans, the battery materials segment, turned profitable with $1.57 million in net income, reversing a $1.75 million segment loss in the prior year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled optimism about profitability improvement in the second half of the year, citing the expected ramp-up of new production lines and gradual pricing adjustments for raw materials. The key focus for the coming quarters is whether margins in the battery segment will rebound as promised, as ongoing cost pressures have significantly reduced earnings. Investors should closely monitor progress on production efficiency and cost pass-through in upcoming periods.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue growth was broad-based and especially notable in the LEV category, which posted a 441% increase over the prior-year quarter, reflecting gains in export markets like India, Vietnam, and Africa. Hitrans, CBAK’s battery materials business, continued its turnaround by delivering double-digit revenue growth and posting its first segment profit, as raw material pricing and new customer wins boosted this line.

Despite strong sales, gross margin fell sharply as new battery production lines in Dalian and Nanjing remained in the ramp-up stage, and higher input costs were only partially passed on to customers. Management pointed to both delayed cost recovery in finished battery pricing and elevated production costs during the early stage of capacity expansion as drivers for the lower profitability.

Inventory and capital expenditures grew as CBAK prepared for future sales growth, with cash and cash equivalents and restricted cash rising to $98.60 million. While operating cash flow was strong, rising leverage and ongoing operating losses highlight the risk if profitability does not stabilize soon.

What CBAK Energy Technology Does
