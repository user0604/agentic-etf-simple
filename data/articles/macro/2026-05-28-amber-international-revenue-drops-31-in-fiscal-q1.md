Key Takeaways
Amber International reported a challenging quarter as revenue and client activity fell sharply.
Adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP) swung to a loss, and revenue dropped nearly 31% year over year.
Management expects a flat or lower revenue outlook for the next quarter as new product launches ramp up.

Amber International (NASDAQ:AMBR) struggled this quarter, with core business metrics declining as market activity slowed further. The digital wealth management and technology platform operator faced a pronounced deterioration in revenue, profitability, and client engagement. The earnings results released on May 28, 2026, cover the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period showed significant pressure, with management focusing on strategic pivots and cost control as financial performance weakened.

Notable Developments
Total revenue fell by 30.9% from the prior year, with the Amber Premium business showing the steepest drop within the core business lines.
The new A-MM (Agentic Market Making) product launched, with management expecting it to start generating revenue in the next quarter.
Management completed a strategic shift towards higher-value clients, which led to fewer active clients and lower assets on the platform.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects Amber Premium segment revenue to remain flat or decrease slightly in the next quarter, with guidance between $9.0 million and $10.0 million. Key areas to watch are revenue contributions from the new A-MM product and whether client activity stabilizes as the platform shifts focus to institutional clients. The effectiveness of technology initiatives and regulatory licensing progress will be important for future growth.

Quarterly Highlights

The Amber Premium segment, focused on digital asset management, saw revenue declines of more than 57% year over year. Wealth Management Solutions, the largest contributor, now makes up nearly 75% of core segment revenue, highlighting reliance on recurring income streams as trading activity falls.

Gross margin compressed by 7.8 percentage points compared to the prior year, standing at 67.7%. Management pointed to a greater share of lower-margin products and a "dual product mix" as reasons for this decline.

On the client side, platform assets dropped almost 24% to $1.0 billion, while active client numbers and new verified users (those who completed "Know Your Customer" requirements) both declined sharply. The company attributes these drops to a deliberate focus on fewer but higher-value clients, as it prepares for new regulatory licenses in key markets.

What Amber International Does

Amber International provides digital wealth management, asset trading, and technology services for institutional and high-net-worth clients across global markets.
