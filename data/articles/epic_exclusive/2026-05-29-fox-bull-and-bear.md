Fox Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 29, 2026, 7:06 AM

Comments

Fox Corporation (NASDAQ:FOX) is a leading American media company known for its dominance in live sports, news, and ad-supported streaming. Its growth strategy centers on marrying traditional cable and broadcast strengths with rapid digital expansion, particularly through its streaming platforms Tubi and Fox One. The firm is leveraging premium live content, robust advertising demand, and innovative digital platforms to sustain revenue amid major shifts in media consumption, even as it faces rising costs and industry disruption.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Digital Platform Momentum Is Offsetting Legacy Declines and Enabling Sustainable Growth

Tubi’s rapid expansion and rising profitability are helping future-proof Fox’s business model. In the most recent fiscal third quarter of 2026, Tubi’s revenue rose by 23% year over year, driven by growing engagement—total view time increased by 19%—and nearly 100 million monthly active users. Notably, Tubi achieved breakeven or slightly positive EBITDA for the third consecutive quarter, signifying a pivotal shift toward digital profitability. As cord-cutting accelerates industry-wide, Fox’s digital initiatives are meaningfully offsetting declines in traditional pay-TV, allowing the company to recapture audiences and advertisers migrating to streaming. With investments in Tubi now moderating, Fox demonstrates operational discipline and scalability, positioning the company to capitalize on the advertising shift from linear to digital, and creating a recurring digital revenue base that supports long-term shareholder value.

2. Market Leadership in Live News and Sports Drives Pricing Power and Revenue Stability

Fox’s dominance in premium, must-have content underpins resilient pricing and robust advertiser demand. Fox News continues to achieve record advertising revenues, with national pricing up over 45% year over year and more than 200 new premium clients added in fiscal year 2026. Similarly, live sports—including the NFL, FIFA World Cup, and Major League Baseball—anchor Fox’s brand and attract massive audiences, such as over 170 million regular-season NFL viewers in the 2025-2026 season. Marquee events propel high CPMs (cost per mille) and attract top-tier advertisers, resulting in consistently strong scatter pricing and low cancellation rates even as the broader TV landscape fragments. This market leadership allows Fox to command premium rates from both advertisers and distributors, stabilizing revenue streams and helping the company outperform key competitors in a volatile media environment.

3. Strategic Integration of Streaming, Sports, and News Unlocks Multi-Platform Monetization

Multi-platform distribution maximizes asset value and expands Fox’s total addressable market. Fox is deliberately integrating premium sports events like the FIFA Men’s World Cup across linear TV, Tubi, and Fox One, amplifying reach and strengthening cross-platform engagement. For the 2026 World Cup, Fox will deliver 104 matches and use Tubi as a simulcast and on-demand hub, exposing nearly 100 million Tubi users to marquee content. This strategy deepens audience loyalty, attracts diverse demographic cohorts, and increases advertising opportunities across every platform. By leveraging rights management and vertical content integration—especially for live sports and news—Fox diversifies its revenue, drives incremental engagement, and protects its competitive moat as audiences and advertisers seek out real-time, must-watch content in both traditional and digital environments.

4. Robust Free Cash Flow and Disciplined Capital Allocation Create Upside Optionality

Strong financial health and share buybacks enhance shareholder returns and strategic flexibility. In Q3 2026, Fox generated $1.77 billion in free cash flow and repurchased $1.95 billion of its own shares, totaling about 36% of all shares outstanding since 2019. The company maintains a solid balance sheet with $3.6 billion in cash against $6.6 billion in debt, and continues to invest judiciously in high-growth digital assets while delivering returns to shareholders. Flexible capital allocation enables Fox to weather event-driven earnings volatility, pursue new digital initiatives, and opportunistically invest in areas such as sports betting via strategic stakes in FanDuel and Flutter. These financial levers provide a buffer against macroeconomic cycles and allow the company to seize new market opportunities as they arise, supporting long-term valuation growth.

5. Political and Event-Driven Advertising Cycles Offer Near-Term Revenue Tailwinds

Upcoming marquee events and record political ad spending will drive outsized incremental revenue. The 2026 U.S. election cycle is projected to produce a midterm record $11 billion in political advertising market spend, with Fox’s station footprint—especially in critical battleground states—well-positioned to capture a disproportionate share. Further, the impending FIFA Men’s World Cup will bring 104 live matches to U.S. audiences, driving both advertising and distribution revenue, with EBITDA projected to be accretive. Fox’s unique blend of live sports, news, and ad-supported digital reach means it can capitalize on event-driven surges in advertiser demand, supporting upside in revenue and profitability beyond baseline trends over the next 12 to 18 months.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Escalating Sports Rights Costs Could Outpace Revenue Growth and Squeeze Margins

Rising expenses for marquee sports rights may erode profitability if revenue fails to keep pace. In the latest quarter, Fox’s Cable segment expenses rose 13% year over year, mainly due to sports rights amortization. The absence of the Super Bowl in Q3 2026 exposed volatility in margin and net income, with profit declining compared to prior-year metrics. With future cost inflation for must-have sports content—such as upcoming NFL and FIFA rights renewals—ahead, there is risk that Fox’s discipline in bid negotiations could falter or that escalating costs could exceed gains in advertising and distribution, compressing margins. If event-driven revenue proves episodic while long-term rights costs become structural, Fox may face sustained pressure on earnings and free cash flow.

2. Ongoing Subscriber Attrition and Industry-Wide Cord-Cutting Remain Fundamental Headwinds

Structural declines in legacy pay-TV could offset gains from digital transition if churn accelerates. While Fox’s reported net subscriber declines remain below 6.5% among third-party distributors and are being partly offset by Fox One additions, industry cord-cutting and rising competition from streaming pose persistent risks to its traditional revenue base. If subscriber attrition accelerates or the success seen to date with digital platforms like Fox One slows, distribution revenues could stagnate or turn negative, especially as TV distribution is guided to be flat through fiscal 2026 with growth forecasted only in fiscal 2027. Prolonged erosion in affiliate fees or an unexpected spike in churn could undercut Fox’s ability to maintain its multi-platform revenue mix.

3. Softer Than Expected Digital Monetization Could Undermine the Transition Narrative

Failure of Tubi and Fox One to sustain profitable growth could challenge Fox’s digital pivot. Despite Tubi posting three consecutive quarters near breakeven and rapid user growth, digital platforms operate in a highly competitive ad-supported streaming landscape. If monetization per user disappoints, content costs rise, or advertiser demand slows, Fox could face difficulty scaling Tubi’s profits as fast as its traditional businesses decline. While management is moderating investments and points to improving operational leverage, industry competition, potential dilution from additional digital spend, or shifts in digital advertising economics could threaten the margin narrative underlying Fox’s bullish digital pivot—potentially weighing on overall returns if digital is unable to more fully offset legacy contraction over the medium term.
