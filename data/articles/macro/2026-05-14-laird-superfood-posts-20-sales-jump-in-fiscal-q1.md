Key Takeaways
Laird Superfood delivered strong sales growth but saw a sharp decline in profitability due to margin contraction and integration costs.
Revenue rose 20% year over year.

Laird Superfood (NYSEMKT:LSF) posted robust sales growth, but its quarterly profit picture darkened as costs and margins deteriorated. The natural and functional foods platform operator is building out its business through new acquisitions and expanding sales channels, with recent deals adding fresh brands to its portfolio. Results for the first quarter (released May 14, 2026, for the quarter ending March 31) show encouraging top-line momentum, but underline the difficult path to profitable growth as integration and cost pressures persist.

Overall, the period was marked by strong net sales growth, a meaningful channel and product mix shift, and the first signs of revenue benefit from its recent acquisitions, but also significant gross margin compression and increased operating costs.

Notable Developments
Net sales rose 20% to $13.9 million, including a $1.6 million revenue contribution from the newly acquired Navitas Organics brand.
Gross margin contracted by 8.6 percentage points year over year, with management citing inflation, tariffs, and channel mix as the biggest drivers.
Operating expenses jumped nearly 50% to $7.7 million, reflecting both integration costs and stepped-up spending on selling and marketing activities.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects full-year net sales of $138 to $148 million, which includes planned contributions from three brands. The outlook for adjusted EBITDA is $8 to $12 million, but this excludes transaction and integration costs that are holding down near-term profitability. Investors should closely monitor gross margin recovery and see if the company can realize cost synergies from its recent acquisitions as integration continues.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter saw the first revenue from Navitas Organics, which contributed to both wholesale and e-commerce channels, helping shift wholesale to become the majority of total sales for the first time. This marks early progress in building a multi-brand platform in the fast-growing superfood sector.

Cost pressure was severe, with gross margin declining and adjusted EBITDA switching to a loss, primarily due to higher input costs, inflation, greater reliance on lower-margin wholesale, and increased marketing spend. Nearly all of the reported net income for the period is the result of a one-off tax benefit connected to the Navitas deal and does not reflect ongoing earnings strength.

Recent acquisitions, including Terrasoul Superfoods (which closed after quarter end), are set to broaden the company’s range in superfoods, snacks, and wellness products. However, the ongoing integration of multiple new brands has increased overall expenses, and management acknowledges the complexity and costs involved in combining operations at this pace.

What Laird Superfood Does

Laird Superfood develops, acquires, and markets plant-based and functional foods like coffee creamers, hydration products, snacks, and wellness staples, with an expanding portfolio built around natural ingredients and nutrient density.
