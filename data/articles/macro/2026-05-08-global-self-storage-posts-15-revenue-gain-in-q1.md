Global Self Storage (NASDAQ:SELF) posted solid tenant demand and operational stability, but rising costs cut into its profits. The self-storage real estate investment trust specializes in operating and acquiring self-storage facilities across the Midwest, Northeast, and Mid-Atlantic regions. Notable Developments
Revenue increased 1.5% due to higher occupancy and better rates, with same-store occupancy climbing to 93.1% by period end.
Net income, Adjusted FFO, and operating income all declined from last year as operating expenses jumped 8.3% and property costs rose 10%.
No new property acquisitions occurred, but a property expansion in Lima, Ohio, was completed and cash resources remain strong at $24.5 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed intentions to pursue acquisitions and expansion opportunities, citing ample liquidity and a strategic focus on underserved markets. However, no new deals closed during the quarter, and rising costs remain a key concern. Investors will be watching for tangible progress on acquisitions and signs of expense control next quarter.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure increased slightly even as profitability metrics moved in the opposite direction, with higher employment costs and rising real estate taxes pushing total expenses up much faster than revenue. This meant that both funds from operations (a core REIT profitability measure) and net income fell versus the prior year.

Operationally, the company’s properties led the self-storage sector in occupancy, reaching 93.1% on average, and the typical tenant duration hit a record 3.6 years. These results reflect strong customer retention, supported by proprietary pricing tools and digital marketing.

Despite management’s focus on growth via acquisition, no new acquisitions were made in the period. Only a modest property expansion occurred at the Lima, Ohio facility, where previously unused space was converted into extra climate-controlled units, nudging occupancy higher at that site.
