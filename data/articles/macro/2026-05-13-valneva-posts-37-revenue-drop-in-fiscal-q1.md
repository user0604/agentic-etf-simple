Valneva Posts 37% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 4:59 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
The company reported a significant drop in revenues as it nears completion of its transition from third-party product distribution.
Earnings fell on widened losses as revenue decreased sharply compared to the same period last year.
Management revised its revenue guidance downward as travel vaccine demand weakened, while cash burn improved substantially.

Valneva (NASDAQ:VALN) faced another challenging period as it pressed ahead with its strategy to exit third-party product distribution and focus on its proprietary vaccine portfolio. The specialty vaccine maker completed the planned wind-down of outside product sales, resulting in sharply lower revenues and a much steeper loss for the quarter reported on May 13, 2026, for the three months ended March 31. Overall, the quarter was marked by weak commercial performance but notable progress in operational cost savings and advancement of its clinical pipeline.

Notable developments
Total revenue fell 37.2% as legacy third-party product sales were almost fully eliminated, and proprietary products also saw lower demand.
Profitability deteriorated sharply with a net loss more than tripling year over year, mainly due to one-time manufacturing and restructuring costs coupled with lower gross margins.
Management cut its full-year revenue guidance amid a drop in travel vaccine uptake, but cost reductions helped slash operating cash burn to nearly break-even.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reduced its full-year 2026 product sales outlook to between €135 million and €150 million due to lower-than-expected travel vaccine demand, especially for DUKORAL and IXIARO. The sharp focus remains on reducing operating expenses, with a newly announced workforce reduction targeting a substantial cost drop for the year. The most important area for investors to monitor is progress in the vaccine pipeline, especially clinical milestones and regulatory submissions for the Lyme disease vaccine in partnership with Pfizer.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue decreased mainly because third-party distribution sales—previously a meaningful share of the business—almost vanished after completing a wind-down plan. Proprietary travel vaccines IXIARO (Japanese encephalitis), DUKORAL (cholera/ETEC), and the new chikungunya shot IXCHIQ each saw lower sales compared to last year, driven by shipment timing issues, distributor transitions, and the absence of prior-year outbreak-related demand. However, the company highlighted a major positive data readout for its investigational Lyme disease vaccine, showing over 70% efficacy in a large Phase 3 clinical trial, and reaffirmed plans for partner Pfizer to seek regulatory approval this year.

Margins came under pressure as manufacturing expenses rose and gross profit for core marketed products fell, with IXIARO and DUKORAL both negatively affected by batch write-offs and underutilized production capacity at the company's new Scottish facility. IXCHIQ, which had negative gross margins, also recorded cancellation fees linked to slow early sales. In response, Valneva announced a 10%-15% global workforce reduction expected to lower operating expenses by up to 35% for fiscal 2026, and operating cash burn improved sharply to just €0.3 million.

The company closed the period with €105.3 million in cash, not including an additional €37 million raised in April after quarter-end, and expects this to fund operations through key regulatory milestones in its pipeline. Full-year revenue and product sales forecasts were both revised downward, reflecting the negative impact of weaker global travel and geopolitical factors on vaccine demand.

What Valneva Does

Valneva is a biotech company that develops, produces, and sells vaccines for infectious diseases, focusing on both marketed products and next-generation candidates in its pipeline.
