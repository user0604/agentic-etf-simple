Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 25%, driven by a 32% increase in Journal Technologies sales, as licensing, consulting, and public service fees all improved.
The company moved from a $44.7 million profit last year to a net loss of $34.6 million, mainly due to $51.2 million in pre-tax investment losses.
Net cash used in operating activities was $2.2 million, a reversal from positive operating cash flow in the prior-year quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management said its reported earnings are heavily affected by movements in its investment portfolio, rather than underlying business performance. It did not offer a specific financial outlook for upcoming periods. Investors should watch how the Journal Technologies segment performs and whether the negative operating cash flow turns around in the coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Journal Technologies, the company’s software arm, continues to deliver robust growth, serving justice agencies with browser-based case management solutions. The segment drove the bulk of revenue growth, with consulting and licensing fees both climbing markedly and operating income more than tripling year over year.

The traditional newspaper and publishing business, focused on specialized legal and public notice content, remained steady. Segment revenue showed a modest year-over-year gain, resisting wider print industry declines, though growth was limited and not a major profit engine.

Large unrealized losses in the company’s investments portfolio, which swung to a $51.2 million pre-tax loss from a year-ago gain, were the main cause for the swing to a net loss and negative earnings per share. This marks a risk for reported results, given that these investments are subject to market price swings unrelated to operational progress.
