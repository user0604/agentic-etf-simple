Key Takeaways
Modest business win as revenue and net loss both improved slightly.

Bragg Gaming Group (NASDAQ:BRAG) made modest progress as its core business stabilized and net losses were cut in half, though cash generation weakened. The business-to-business online gaming platform provider continued to operate a single, integrated platform and highlighted the strength of its content partnerships. The first quarter results, released on May 14, 2026 for the three months ended March 31, show operational steadiness but also raise concerns about cash flow and near-term liquidity. Overall, the quarter reflected flat revenue with some improvement at the bottom line, even as cash reserves decreased and working capital weakened.

Notable developments
Revenue was stable at €25.65 million, nearly flat year over year as geographic growth was offset by declines in markets like the Netherlands and United States.
The company announced the planned acquisition of Drayton International, which adds five game studios and three technology platforms, to be funded by issuing new shares.
Cash and cash equivalents dropped to €3.41 million, with the current ratio falling below 1, highlighting ongoing liquidity concerns.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management focused on diversification through acquisition. Management’s most recent published quantitative financial guidance for 2026 anticipates full year revenue between €97.0 million and €104.5 million, and Adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization) of €16.0 million to €19.0 million. Investors should monitor the company’s ability to improve cash flow and successfully integrate new acquisitions.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue increased slightly, driven by higher sales in Malta, Brazil, and Belgium, but was held back by drops in the Netherlands and United States. No single region accounted for more than 23 percent of total revenue, which helps spread risk.

The Drayton International deal, signed after the quarter, aims to broaden the company’s proprietary game library and technology portfolio. The transaction is structured with new shares rather than cash, which could help preserve existing resources but adds complexity regarding integration.

Operating cash flow fell sharply to €1.65 million, mainly due to a negative swing in working capital. Cash reserves dropped by over 50 percent versus the prior year, and the current ratio now sits below 1, raising short-term funding questions.
