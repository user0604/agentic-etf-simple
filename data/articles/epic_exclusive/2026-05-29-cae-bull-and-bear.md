CAE Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 29, 2026, 3:05 PM

Comments

CAE Inc. (NYSE:CAE) is a global leader in simulation-based training for the civil aviation and defense sectors, providing pilot training, flight simulators, and mission rehearsal solutions. The company’s growth strategy relies on leveraging its civil aviation platform and capitalizing on secular pilot demand while undergoing a transformation that emphasizes operational efficiency, cost optimization, and a pivot toward the fast-growing defense and security market. Amid industry headwinds, CAE aims to structurally improve its profitability by 2030 through a combination of internal efficiencies and focused defense expansion.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Structural Defense Spending Growth Supports Multi-Year Expansion

Rising global defense budgets provide a powerful tailwind for CAE’s defense segment. The company’s Defense and Security business achieved 9% year-over-year revenue growth in fiscal 2026, reaching $2.2 billion, underpinned by robust demand from NATO and allied countries. With major governments, like Canada, committing to significant, multi-year increases in defense budgets—Canada alone plans $82 billion in new spending over five years, with about 10% allocated to training and mission rehearsal—CAE faces a generational market opportunity. The segment’s book-to-sales ratio of 1.11 in Q4 and a growing pipeline that is several times its current backlog indicate long-term revenue visibility. As defense budgets worldwide rise toward 5% of GDP, sustained order momentum and new program wins could accelerate segment growth and profitability for years to come.

2. Transformation Plan Targets Significant Cost Reduction and Margin Expansion

Strategic restructuring is set to unlock substantial recurring savings and drive margin improvement. CAE’s transformation program, launched in fiscal 2027, targets $125–$150 million in annual cost savings by 2030 through network rationalization, ERP system consolidation, divesting non-core assets, and organizational streamlining. Already, full-year 2026 free cash flow conversion climbed to 123% from below 50% on average in the prior four years, reflecting early success in tightening capital allocation and cutting civil capital expenditures by roughly 30%. Management projects that over half of the anticipated rise in operating profit by 2030 will come from these internal initiatives, setting a foundation for sustainable earnings growth and higher returns on invested capital.

3. Secular Civil Pilot Shortage and Regulatory Requirements Ensure Long-Term Demand

Global pilot shortages and mandated recurrent training provide a resilient secular growth engine. Despite near-term headwinds in civil aviation, the company’s core platform is underpinned by a multi-year backlog of aircraft deliveries and worldwide regulatory training requirements. Commercial aircraft and business jet manufacturers have multi-year delivery pipelines, and every new plane added to fleets generates recurring training demand. Additionally, as older pilots retire and fleets expand, airlines and operators depend on stable, high-quality training providers like CAE. This dynamic ensures that once geopolitical disruptions and regional volatility ease, the underlying demand drivers for simulation and recurrent pilot training are likely to reassert themselves, offering a base for recovery and renewed growth.

4. Portfolio Optimization and Asset Sales Could Unlock Value and Strengthen the Core

Active divestment of non-core assets may create incremental capital for reinvestment and sharpen strategic focus. As part of its transformation plan, CAE is reviewing and pursuing alternatives for several non-core businesses, notably its Flightscape aviation software subsidiary. Such actions demonstrate a willingness to refocus on the most profitable and scalable segments—core pilot training and defense systems—while freeing up capital and management attention. This approach not only reinforces the balance sheet during a challenging period but positions the company to reinvest in areas with the strongest competitive advantage and return potential, supporting long-term shareholder value creation.

5. Recent Defense Order Momentum Signals Sustainable Backlog and Revenue Growth

Robust new contract wins with leading defense partners point to healthy future revenues. Recent agreements with Airbus and other industry majors in the Canadian and international defense sector highlight CAE’s increasing relevance and capacity to secure major multi-year contracts. These partnerships are in keeping with management’s articulated strategy to drive growth by embedding advanced simulation and training expertise earlier in the platform life cycle. With a pipeline containing several $1 billion-plus opportunities and strong recent order intake outpacing current sales, the company is well positioned to benefit from both large-scale global defense programs and enhanced recurring revenue streams, which could lead to higher predictability and resilience across future cycles.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Civil Aviation Underperformance Could Delay Recovery and Depress Returns

Weaker civil utilization and disrupted regional demand threaten near-term profitability and margins. The Civil Aviation segment faces significant challenges, with utilization rates and network performance falling short of expectations in fiscal 2026 due to geopolitical disruptions, particularly the Iran-related conflict in the Middle East. This has already led to lower segment operating income (down year over year to $152.4 million in Q4) and pushed management to announce the removal of approximately 10% of simulators and the closure of up to six training centers. A soft book-to-sales ratio of 0.96 for fiscal 2026 and continued volatility put further pressure on the prospect of a quick recovery, undermining near-term earnings and exposing the company to sustained margin risk if travel patterns or geopolitical tensions do not normalize soon.

2. Execution Risks in Ambitious Transformation Could Lead to Cost Overruns or Disruption

The complexity of the transformation plan brings significant operational and financial execution risk. CAE’s success through 2030 is heavily reliant on delivering a series of interlocking initiatives—network rationalization, major system upgrades, and organizational redesign. Management has named fiscal 2027 a “reset year,” emphasizing that these efforts are “full on execution” and require cultural change across the business. Any delays, cost overruns, missteps in asset closures, or negative impacts on employee or customer relationships could lower the expected cost savings and even result in lost market share. The company also faces the risk of temporary earnings dilution and cash flow pressure, as substantial up-front costs and organizational complexity must be navigated before the intended benefits materialize.

3. Dependence on Sustained Defense Budget Growth May Not Be Durable

Defense outperformance could falter if global budget cycles or program timing reverse. While defense backlog and order intake are currently strong, management admits that some recent strength may reflect “lumpy” contract wins and favorable program timing. The segment’s future growth is closely linked to continued government spending and political will, both of which are subject to macroeconomic, fiscal, or electoral reversals. Should allied nations reduce or delay their budgetary commitments, or should mix and contract performance in the Defense and Security division fall short, CAE could struggle to achieve its targeted margin expansion and operating profit growth, putting downward pressure on consolidated performance mid-decade, especially if Civil has not yet fully rebounded.
