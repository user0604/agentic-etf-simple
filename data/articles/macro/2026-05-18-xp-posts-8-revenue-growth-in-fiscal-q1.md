Key Takeaways
XP delivered moderate growth in revenue and profit, but client asset inflows were in line with prior periods.
Revenue grew 8% year over year in the quarter.

XP (NASDAQ:XP) posted steady growth in revenue and profit, with client asset inflows in line with previous periods. The leading Brazilian digital investment and wealth management platform saw its business continue to expand, while profitability reflected investment in technology and network growth. The results, covering the quarter ended March 31 and released on May 18, 2026, show the company at a crossroads between operational growth and softer client activity. Overall, the quarter presented mixed progress, with some business lines posting solid gains and others facing headwinds.

Notable Developments
Total client assets grew 15% to R$1.53 trillion, but net inflow dropped sharply to R$14 billion, down 39% year over year.
Retail revenue rose 10%, led by equities and funds platform growth, while fixed income revenue declined 25%.
Operating expenses climbed 14%, exceeding single-digit revenue growth, and the efficiency ratio worsened from 33.6% to 34.6%.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its target of 17% or higher revenue growth for fiscal 2026, signaling confidence even as the first quarter fell below the required run rate. The company plans to keep growing its client base, prioritizing digital engagement and capital returns. Investors should monitor whether net inflows and revenue growth accelerate in the coming quarters, as this will be critical for achieving the year’s targets.

Quarterly Highlights

Total client assets reached R$1.5 trillion, up 15% from a year ago, with year-over-year growth driven by R$85 billion in net inflow and R$116 billion of market appreciation. Net inflow was R$14 billion in the quarter, while retail net inflow was R$19 billion, in line with both the year-on-year and quarter-on-quarter levels.

Retail operations delivered 10% revenue growth, supported by a 22% jump in equities and a 22% rise in funds platform revenue. However, fixed income—a traditionally strong segment for XP—saw revenue drop by 25%, reflecting shifting client demand amid changing interest rate conditions.

Gross profit climbed 7%, with gross margin slipping slightly to 67.2%. Operating costs, particularly in selling, general, and administrative expenses, grew faster than revenue. The growing headcount and technology investments contributed to a higher efficiency ratio in the quarter.

What XP Does

XP operates a digital platform for wealth management, securities brokerage, and broader investment services focused on individuals and institutional clients in Brazil.
