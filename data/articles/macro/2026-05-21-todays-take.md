1. The Silent Portfolio Killer: Is Boredom Your Biggest Enemy?

By Rachel Warren
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Most people think fear or greed are the hardest emotions to master, but for many investors, it is actually boredom. Fear and greed are intense but usually short-lived reactions to price swings. Boredom is different. It is the restless urge to do something when the right move is actually to do nothing. It leads to over-trading, chasing mediocre ideas, and tinkering with a strategy that was working just fine on its own.

Investing at its best is often quite dull, like watching grass grow while your wealth slowly compounds. When the market is flat and nothing exciting is happening, the temptation to jump into a trendy investment just for the sake of action can be overwhelming. Successful investing requires the discipline to embrace that quiet time.

2. The Ultimate Wealth Killer: Fear

By Neha Chamaria
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

I’ve dealt with all three emotions: fear, greed, and boredom. Because I’m a hardcore long-term investor, I don’t get bored when I can do nothing to my stocks for months at a stretch. I’ve been a victim of greed, but by far, fear has cost me many an opportunity.

Fear is destructive. When markets tank and portfolio values plummet, the human brain often processes that as a real financial loss, even a threat, and triggers a “run” response. The tragedy is that fear makes an investor do the wrong thing at the wrong time: sitting on the sidelines when they should be putting cash to work and laying the foundation for wealth. I’ve faced this multiple times — but have also learned a lesson each time.

3. Loss Aversion Is a Powerful Force

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I think fear is probably the toughest to manage for most people. Often people will say they refuse to invest in the market because it’s too risky. The idea that their stocks will go down and they’ll lose money is a scary proposition. Of course, you haven’t lost anything until you actually sell.

But this fear is tied to loss aversion, which is a powerful cognitive bias. It says our losses feel twice as painful as our gains, making the idea of investing a scary one for many. Being able to see the forest for the trees is a superpower, and we must be able to do that in order to be successful. But it takes work, and no matter what, that fear never fully goes away.

4. Nothing Kills a Portfolio Like Greed

By Tim Beyers
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Bored investors tend to hang on to their holdings. Fearful investors tend to buy too late and sell too early. But greedy investors often lack patience and discipline.

I’ve experienced this. You may remember the story I told about selling Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) too soon. Why did I buy in the first place? Greed. Jeff Bezos appeared on the cover of Time, and I just had to have that stock. I paid the price.

Greed will get you if you let it. Don’t. Instead: Read, pause, consider your options, and then buy when you know well what you’re buying and what might cause you to sell.
