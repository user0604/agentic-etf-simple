Key Takeaways
AutoZone posted strong revenue and earnings growth, supported by solid domestic sales and new store openings.
Earnings per share rose 7.7% year over year, with same store sales up 5.5%.
Management maintained its store expansion guidance but faces pressure from rising inventory and margin headwinds.

Auto parts retailer AutoZone (NYSE:AZO) delivered another quarter of growth, as underlying demand in its core U.S. business and continued expansion kept sales rising. The company, which operates thousands of stores across the U.S. Mexico, and Brazil, released its fiscal third-quarter results on May 26, 2026, covering the 12 weeks ended May 9. Overall, results showed higher sales and earnings but also highlighted margin pressure and growing inventory levels.

Notable Developments
Sales climbed 8.4% year over year, with increased same store sales of 5.5%.
Inventory rose 10.8%, reaching $7.56 billion, which was well ahead of store count growth and could pose a risk if sales growth slows.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management kept its store opening and investment pace in line with prior guidance, while reiterating its long-term growth strategy and focus on cost discipline. No new financial targets were introduced for the upcoming quarter. Investors should watch for inventory management and margin trends, as inventory builds and expansion spending may create challenges if revenue growth moderates.

Quarterly Highlights

The company opened 82 new stores globally during the quarter, boosting its total store count to 7,856 locations as part of its ongoing expansion. Domestic commercial (wholesale) sales jumped over 10%, showing continued strength with repair shop customers and fleet programs. Same store sales rose both in the U.S. and internationally, though most international gains were due to exchange rate benefits rather than actual local business growth.

Profit margins came under pressure mainly from the impact of LIFO (Last-In, First-Out) inventory accounting. This non-cash charge, which adjusts inventory values for inflation, reduced gross margin by about 0.8 percentage points. Operating expenses as a share of sales improved slightly, reflecting management’s focus on controlling costs even as investment grew.

Inventory levels rose faster than both sales and store growth. Although this growth supports new store openings and higher sales, it also led to a decline in the company’s inventory turnover ratio (a measure of how quickly inventory is sold), which management called out for close monitoring as expansion continues.

What AutoZone Does

AutoZone runs retail stores that sell auto parts, accessories, and related products for both do-it-yourself customers and professional repair shops across North America, Mexico, and Brazil.
