Key Takeaways
VersaBank overcame significant one-time reorganization costs with strong loan and deposit growth.
Adjusted earnings per share climbed 50.0%, while net income slipped due to non-core charges.
Management expects improved efficiency and profitability once temporary costs abate.

VersaBank (NASDAQ:VBNK) saw strong underlying business growth, even as temporary charges related to its U.S. reorganization and a branch write-down weighed on the quarter. The specialized digital lender, known for its focus on point-of-sale financing and commercial real estate loans, reported results for its fiscal second quarter on June 3, 2026 (covering the three months ended April 30, 2026). Overall, the quarter highlighted ongoing customer and asset growth but was affected by notable but transitory costs.

Notable Developments
Total loans, called credit assets, rose 25% to $5.68 billion, driven by growth in both U.S. and Canadian operations.
Net interest margin on credit assets improved 0.12 percentage points to 2.71%, offering healthy profitability relative to peers.
Non-core charges of $6.7 million led to a drop in headline net income, but core profits (excluding these items) rose significantly.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated that temporary non-core expenses—mainly from the U.S. reorganization and branch asset sale—should fade over the rest of fiscal 2026, allowing core efficiency and profitability to improve. The bank’s top priority remains scaling its U.S. structured receivables lending, aiming for $1 billion in new fundings this year. Investors should monitor the pace of this U.S. loan growth and the normalization of operating costs as key signals in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Lending activity surged, especially in VersaBank’s Structured Receivable Program, where U.S. assets more than doubled from last year. This growth helped boost overall loans to a new high and supported a 27% increase in total revenue for the quarter.

Profitability at the core business level showed improvement, with adjusted earnings per share up 50.0%. The net interest margin—representing the difference between interest income and the cost of funds, divided by average assets—also edged higher, reflecting both lower deposit costs and improved asset mix.

Short-term reorganization and asset sale costs totaled $6.7 million, leading to a dip in reported net income and efficiency metrics. Management emphasized these are one-time events, with the expectation that the bank will realize lasting cost savings once its U.S. transformation is complete and non-core segments, like the cybersecurity unit, are exited.

What VersaBank Does

VersaBank is a digital bank that specializes in point-of-sale financing and commercial real estate loans, funded primarily by deposits from brokers and specialized client groups.
