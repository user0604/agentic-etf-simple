Key Takeaways
G-III Apparel Group delivered headline profit growth in the quarter, primarily due to a one-time tariff benefit.
Non-GAAP earnings fell to a loss and revenue declined 8% from the prior year period.
Management raised full-year earnings outlook (including tariff recovery), but expects underlying profit to decline and transition risks to remain.

G-III Apparel Group (NASDAQ:GIII) reported a headline profit boost from a significant one-time tariff refund, while underlying earnings and revenue slipped compared to the prior year. The global apparel company, known for brands like DKNY and Donna Karan, released its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 on June 5, 2026. Overall, the period reflected transition challenges amid licensing changes and ongoing cost pressures, even as cash flow and inventory management improved.

Notable developments
The quarterly profit included a $77.9 million one-time gain from expected tariff refunds, which pushed GAAP earnings per share significantly higher.
Non-GAAP earnings turned to a loss, and revenue declined by $47.6 million as Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger licenses were phased out.
Inventory dropped by 8% while cash and cash equivalents rose $136.4 million, reflecting tighter working capital management.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its fiscal 2027 earnings outlook, including the impact of one-time tariff recoveries, but expects core profit and sales to remain below last year as portfolio transitions continue. The company plans to accelerate growth in its owned brands, manage costs, and integrate the pending Marc Jacobs acquisition. Investors should watch how the transition away from major licenses affects underlying profits through the rest of the year.

Quarterly highlights

GAAP profits for the quarter were driven by an extraordinary tariff recovery, without which profit performance would have shown a loss on a non-GAAP basis. Management did not report individual brand sales but noted momentum in its core owned brands and highlighted the planned acquisition of Marc Jacobs as a future growth lever.

Revenue declined compared to the prior year period, mainly because sales of Calvin Klein and Tommy Hilfiger products, previously significant contributors, are winding down as expected. Non-GAAP profit flipped to a loss as selling, general, and administrative costs grew and the company invested in marketing and digital capabilities.

Management emphasized improved inventory discipline, with inventories lower year over year and a substantial increase in cash. The company also paid $4.2 million in dividends during the quarter, continuing a capital return policy started last year.

What G-III Apparel Group does

G-III Apparel Group designs, sources, and markets apparel and accessories globally, with a portfolio of owned and licensed brands sold through wholesale and direct-to-consumer channels.
