Analog Devices Posts 37% Revenue Jump in Fiscal Q2

Key Takeaways
Second-quarter results set new records, driven by Industrial and Communications markets.
Non-GAAP earnings per share rose 67% from the prior-year quarter.
Management predicts continued revenue growth but expects some normalization as one-time pricing benefits fade.

Record sales in Industrial and Communications markets powered a strong quarter for Analog Devices (NASDAQ:ADI). The semiconductor leader, known for its analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits used across industries including manufacturing, automotive, and technology, posted results that exceeded the upper end of its own guidance. The company released its fiscal second quarter earnings on May 20, 2026, covering the period ended May 2. Overall, the quarter reflected robust demand and successful execution, but included a meaningful contribution from non-recurring pricing actions.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 37%, with the Industrial segment now accounting for half of total sales after 56% growth versus the prior-year quarter.
Gross margin improved by 6.3 percentage points, reaching 67.3%, attributed to favorable pricing actions and product mix.
The company raised its quarterly dividend to $1.10 per share and returned $1.3 billion to shareholders through dividends and buybacks in the quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects further sales growth in the upcoming quarter, with revenue guidance at $3.9 billion and continued margin strength, although it expects lower benefit from one-time channel pricing changes. The key issue for investors to monitor will be whether growth in core end markets can offset the wind-down of non-recurring factors that boosted this quarter’s results.

Quarterly Highlights

The Industrial segment, a broad market that includes factory automation and aerospace, delivered 56% growth year over year and now comprises 50% of company revenue. Management credited both cyclical demand recovery and strategic price increases, but noted that some pricing actions were one-time events unlikely to repeat next quarter.

The Communications segment posted a 79% increase from a year ago, driven by demand for products enabling artificial intelligence infrastructure, such as power management chips and optical networking solutions. Automotive revenue, at 24% of the total, remained steady with only 2% growth, as softness in China and ongoing inventory corrections persisted.

R&D spending rose to $509 million, reflecting the company's continued focus on product innovation. While total spending grew, it declined as a share of revenue, suggesting efficiency gains as sales volumes climbed. Management emphasized continuing investment in engineering talent and customer-focused solutions as critical to sustaining future growth.

What Analog Devices Does

Analog Devices designs and manufactures high-performance analog, mixed-signal, and digital signal processing integrated circuits, supplying components for sectors including industrial, automotive, communications, healthcare, and consumer electronics.
