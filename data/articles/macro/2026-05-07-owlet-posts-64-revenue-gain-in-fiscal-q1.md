Owlet (NYSE:OWLT) shifted its focus to digital services and recurring subscription revenue, but saw profitability fall compared to the prior year. The company, a provider of smart baby monitoring devices and pediatric health software, reported first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period featured modest sales growth, improved gross margin, and a swing from net profit to net loss.

Business Overview

During the quarter, subscription revenue grew sharply, reaching $2.7 million, nearly seven times higher than the prior year. The number of paid Owlet360 subscribers surpassed 115,000, reflecting continued momentum in digital services. In contrast, hardware revenue declined by 4.3%, and overall sales growth relied on the uptake of recurring digital offerings.

Operating expenses increased by 26%, mainly due to higher headcount and compensation, which contributed to a wider operating loss of $5.5 million. The company also launched its OnCall pediatric telehealth service within its app, expanding its digital health platform and aiming to deepen subscriber engagement.

Gross margin improved to 54.5%, up from 53.7% a year ago, driven by a greater mix of high-margin subscription revenue and lower direct costs, despite ongoing tariff impacts. The cash balance remained steady at $35.5 million, supported by new financing and careful cash management.

Quarterly Performance

Revenue for the quarter grew modestly compared to the prior year, with digital services driving most of the increase. Subscription revenue rose from $0.4 million to $2.7 million, highlighting strong adoption of the Owlet360 platform. Hardware sales declined, and the company’s profitability metrics turned negative, with a net loss and negative adjusted EBITDA, mainly due to higher operating expenses.

The launch of the OnCall telehealth service marked a significant step into virtual pediatric care, broadening the company’s digital health offerings. The company’s subscriber base continued to expand, with over 115,000 paid users at quarter-end.

Operating expenses, especially payroll and stock-based compensation, rose sharply, contributing to the wider operating loss. Despite these challenges, the company maintained a stable cash position through disciplined financial management.

Looking Ahead

Management lowered its full-year 2026 revenue guidance to a range of $118 million to $122 million, down from the previous $126 million to $130 million. However, the company raised its margin outlook and more than doubled its adjusted EBITDA guidance, reflecting a focus on profitable growth. Investors should watch the pace of subscription and telehealth adoption, as future results depend on the success of these services.
