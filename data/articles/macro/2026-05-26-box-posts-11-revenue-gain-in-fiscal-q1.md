Key Takeaways
Box posted a business win as revenue and margins improved, outpacing its own guidance in the first quarter.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) increased 23.3%, and revenue rose year over year.
Management expects revenue and profitability growth to continue, but flagged ongoing foreign currency headwinds.

Cloud content management provider Box (NYSE:BOX) got off to a strong start in fiscal 2027 as it delivered higher sales and profitability, outdoing its own forecasts. The quarter, announced on May 26, 2026 for the period ended April 30, saw the company benefit from broad enterprise demand and rapid product innovation across artificial intelligence and security. Overall, the period showed continued operational improvement and positive customer momentum, offset by currency-related drag and greater investment spending.

Notable Developments
Box’s revenue grew 11% year over year, surpassing its own outlook.
Operating margin (Non-GAAP) improved to 27.7%, up 2.4 percentage points from the prior year quarter, driven by higher-value platform upgrades and efficiency gains.
Non-GAAP free cash flow reached $127.7 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management guided for continued revenue and earnings growth in the coming quarters but acknowledged persistent currency headwinds and a heavy commitment to research and development. The company is prioritizing ongoing product innovation, especially in artificial intelligence capabilities, while maintaining industry-leading security standards. Investors should monitor billings growth and the uptake of new AI-powered offerings as signals for sustained demand.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s core content management service, known as Enterprise Advanced, accelerated adoption among large customers, supporting growth in both recurring revenue and average contract value. Box launched several new artificial intelligence tools, including Box Agent, a search and analysis engine, and expanded its workflow automation features, aiming to position itself as a key AI and security partner for enterprises.

Box maintained its "Leader" ranking in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Document Management, validating progress in security and compliance -- critical elements for attracting businesses in regulated sectors. Strategic partnerships continued to expand, with deeper integrations into the platforms of companies like Google, NVIDIA, and OpenAI, which broadened the company’s reach and use cases.

Remaining performance obligations (the total value of contracts yet to be recognized as revenue) increased 12% to $1.6 billion, providing management greater revenue visibility for future quarters. The company also returned $114 million to shareholders via stock repurchases during the quarter.

What Box Does
