Earnings Preview: CBAK Energy Technology

The Motley Fool Team

May 15, 2026, 9:03 AM

Comments

CBAK Energy Technology (NASDAQ:CBAT), a manufacturer of advanced battery cells and materials for electric vehicles, light electric vehicles and energy storage, is expected to report its Q1 FY2026 earnings on Monday, May 18, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of FY2025, CBAK Energy Technology reported consolidated net revenue of $58.80 million, which represented a 131.8% increase compared to the same period in 2024. The company’s battery business saw revenue growth driven by demand for new large-format cylindrical cells, especially the 40135 and 32140 models, while the Hitrans raw materials segment experienced a significant turnaround due to favorable pricing and new customer wins. Despite this strong top-line growth, the company posted a net loss attributable to shareholders of $7.38 million for the quarter, as gross margin fell to 7.3% from 13.1% a year earlier. The Q4 results beat analyst sales estimates but missed consensus expectations for profitability, reflecting the impact of ongoing capacity ramp-ups and margin pressure.

Positives
Revenue growth driven by new product launches: The company’s transition to next-generation 40135 and 32140 battery cells led to a surge in sales, showing strong demand for these products in international markets.
Hitrans raw materials segment turnaround: Hitrans revenue grew by 944% year over year in Q4, benefiting from higher raw material prices and new customer acquisitions, which helped offset margin pressures in the battery segment.
Strong cash generation from operations: Net cash provided by operating activities reached $48.55 million for the year, reflecting disciplined working capital management despite reported losses.
Strategic partnerships and customer wins: New collaborations with international clients, including SPIRO in Africa and blue-chip companies in India and Vietnam, validated the company’s product reliability and expanded its global reach.
Negatives
Margin compression during ramp-up: Gross margin dropped significantly due to higher costs associated with scaling up new production lines and suboptimal yields in the early phase of capacity expansion.
Continued operating and net losses: The company reported an operating loss of $8.01 million and a net loss attributable to shareholders of $7.38 million in Q4, missing analyst expectations for profitability.
Decline in legacy product sales: Revenue from older battery models used in residential energy supply fell sharply, reflecting the transition away from legacy products and creating a temporary drag on overall margins.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Progress on gross margin recovery: Investors should look for signs that gross margin is stabilizing or improving as new production lines for 40135 and 32140 cells move past the initial ramp-up phase. Management has indicated that margin recovery is expected in the second half of 2026, but any earlier improvement would be a positive signal.
Capacity utilization and production ramp milestones: Updates on the Dalian and Nanjing facility expansions, including whether the new lines are approaching full capacity, will be key. The Dalian 40135 line is targeted for full ramp by mid-2026, while Nanjing Phase II is expected to reach full capacity by early 2027.
Impact of export tax rebate phase-out: The reduction and planned elimination of Chinese export tax rebates for lithium-ion batteries could affect international margins. Watch for commentary on how the new Malaysian subsidiary and supply chain localization efforts are mitigating this headwind.
Hitrans segment performance and raw material pricing: The Hitrans business delivered strong results in 2025. Investors should monitor whether this momentum continues, as raw material price trends and the ramp-up of new cathode and precursor facilities will influence results in 2026.
Updates on new partnerships and customer contracts: Management has signaled the potential for additional major international contracts and deeper collaborations, especially in Africa and Southeast Asia. Any announcements or guidance on new deals could serve as catalysts for the stock.
