Key Takeaways
Macy’s delivered a business win, posting its strongest first quarter in four years with broad sales gains.
Earnings per share (Non-GAAP) rose 18.2% year over year.
Management raised full-year guidance, but warned inventory and margin pressures warrant close attention.

Macy’s (NYSE:M) posted a solid business win as it topped its own outlook on most key measures and grew sales across all three major retail brands. The leading omnichannel department store group, spanning Macy’s, Bloomingdale’s, and Bluemercury, reported its results for the quarter ended May 2, 2026 (released June 3, 2026). Overall, the quarter showed ongoing progress, especially at Bloomingdale’s, while elevated inventories and margin pressures call for vigilance as the year progresses.

What Macy’s does

Macy’s operates a national portfolio of department stores and online platforms under the Macy’s, Bloomingdale’s, and Bluemercury brands, offering apparel, beauty, home goods, and accessories to a wide range of consumers.

Notable developments
Comparable sales across all nameplates rose, led by Bloomingdale’s with a 10.2% increase and Macy’s brand up 1.6%.
Gross margin slipped by 0.3 percentage points to 38.9%, due to tariff impacts; store cost discipline helped offset investment spending.
Inventory climbed 3.6% year over year, putting a spotlight on stock management as the summer season arrives.
Quarterly highlights

Bloomingdale’s, Macy’s luxury-focused banner, posted an especially strong showing with record first-quarter sales and a 10.2% jump in comparable sales. Macy’s brand stores also grew, with stores under the “Reimagine 200” initiative outperforming the rest of the fleet, showing that recent investments in format and in-store experience continue to resonate with shoppers.

Gross margin, the amount left after paying for products sold, slipped modestly to 38.9%. Management attributed the entire decline to tariffs, noting that excluding these, margins would have matched last year’s level. At the same time, selling, general, and administrative expense as a percent of revenue held steadfast at 39.9%, highlighting disciplined cost controls despite more spending in growth areas like store upgrades and technology.

Elevated merchandise inventory—$4.83 billion versus $4.66 billion last year—reflects both higher in-stock positions and expectations for continued demand. Management described these levels as “well-positioned” for upcoming summer sales. Still, with inventory growth outpacing sales, the risk of excess stock and more markdowns later in the year bears close scrutiny.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its outlook for fiscal 2026, now expecting net sales between $21.5 billion and $21.75 billion and adjusted earnings per share of $2.00 to $2.20. The most important factor to monitor next will be how Macy’s manages both elevated inventory levels and potential margin risk from tariffs as it continues its “Bold New Chapter” strategy.
