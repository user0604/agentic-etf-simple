FGI Industries Posts 8% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

May 14, 2026, 4:41 PM

Key Takeaways
FGI Industries posted another quarterly loss as core sanitaryware demand weakened further.
Non-GAAP EPS loss narrowed to ($0.39), with gross margin holding steady year over year.
Management reaffirmed full-year guidance, anticipating improved demand and greater product mix gains ahead.

FGI Industries (NASDAQ:FGI) continued to face demand headwinds in its core product markets, with falling sanitaryware sales driving another quarterly loss. The global supplier of kitchen and bathroom fixtures and furniture is working to offset declines by shifting into higher-growth and higher-margin segments. For the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (earnings released May 14, 2026), the company reported some improvement in profitability metrics and cash flow, but revenue and net income both remained lower than the previous year. Overall, the quarter showed stabilization in margins but ongoing top-line and net loss pressure.

Notable Developments
Sanitaryware revenue fell 20% to $16.1 million, reflecting order weakness among key homebuilder and retail customers, while bath furniture and shower systems grew double digits.
Gross margin held at 26.8%, even as revenue dropped 8.2% compared to the prior-year period, indicating successful cost containment and better product mix.
Cash on hand more than doubled year over year to $2.7 million, helped by lower inventory, improved working capital, and reduced operating expenses.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its fiscal 2026 full-year targets despite the weak start, maintaining guidance for $134–141 million in annual revenue and a potential swing to operating profitability. The company is counting on a demand rebound and further momentum in diversified product categories in the remainder of the year. Investors should monitor segment performance, particularly for a turnaround in sanitaryware and continued gains in bath furniture and shower systems.

Quarterly Highlights

The sharpest contraction in the period came from sanitaryware, which is a core product group featuring toilets and sinks. Segment revenue dropped by 20%, driven by less business from U.S. homebuilders and softer Canadian retail demand. This was only partly offset by stable performance in other categories such as kitchen cabinets.

Bath furniture, which includes vanities and cabinets, increased sales by nearly 11%. At the same time, shower systems revenue grew 14% as new dealer relationships and digital initiatives took effect. This success supports management’s push for diversification beyond legacy product lines.

Working capital improved, with cash flow from operations showing a $1.3 million year over year improvement. Inventory declined by $1.1 million from last quarter, indicating disciplined cost controls as the company adapts supply levels to shifting order patterns.
