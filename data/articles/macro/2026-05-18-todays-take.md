1. I’m Always Investing

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I wrote in March how I added to two positions that I’ve owned for a little while in Waste Management (NYSE: WM) and UnitedHealth Group (NYSE: UNH). Since then, I haven’t made any additional purchases — but I also never stop investing.

Every time I get my paycheck, I contribute a portion into my retirement account where it immediately gets invested into the Vanguard Total Stock Market Index (NASDAQMUTFUND: VITSX). It’s the one fund I own, and given its diverse nature with so many holdings — along with the fact that it remains fully invested and has a minimal expense ratio — it’s a nice way for me to protect myself from myself, so to speak. So even when I’m not buying individual stocks, I’m still always investing.

2. In Defense of Not Buying

By Tim Beyers
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Motley Fool employees have what we call a Motley: a motto that describes who we are, what we think about, and how we operate.

Mine is “slow and steady wins the race.”

I live this out by buying stocks rarely. I can go without for months and then add five names within a few weeks. Here’s why this matters: Markets are frenzied. More money than ever sloshes through the system today, usually with unprecedented speed.

When everyone else moves fast, there is power (I think) is moving slowly. I can be more deliberate and measured, and limit the damage caused by my inevitable mistakes.

So while I will be buying again, I won’t be rushing the process.

3. Adding to a Core Fund

By Matt Frankel, CFP®
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

The last stock I bought was actually an ETF. I added shares of the Vanguard Real Estate ETF (NYSEMKT: VNQ) to my portfolio. To be clear, this has been a core holding for some time, but I’ve been adding to my position somewhat aggressively. If you aren’t familiar, this ETF tracks an index of real estate investment trusts, or REITs, and top holdings include some of the companies I’m most bullish on — specifically Prologis (NYSE: PLD), Equinix (NASDAQ: EQIX), and Digital Realty Trust (NYSE: DLR). Not only do I think real estate as a sector will benefit as interest rates likely trend lower over the next couple of years, but the three I just mentioned could be major beneficiaries of the AI investment surge.

4. A High-Risk Bet on a Pilotless Future

By Lou Whiteman
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

I like to carve out a portion of my portfolio for “other bets,” to borrow a term from Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG). For me, these are businesses with very uncertain futures (and possible future zeros) that could pay off big if things go to script. It is high-risk, high (potential) reward investing and isn’t the way I invest the funds I need for retirement. But it is an interesting part of my portfolio.

My latest in this category is Merlin (NASDAQ: MRLN), which recently went public via a SPAC. Merlin is developing autonomous systems for planes, including a contract with the Air Force to reduce the crew required to fly C-130 cargo planes. You can learn more about the company from my interview with its CFO, Ryan Carrithers.

Merlin is trying to do something that is very hard. Perhaps too hard. It could fail. But if it works, it fills a huge need. And its success in attracting the Air Force’s attention is a positive step in the right direction. Even if Merlin’s systems only work well enough to reduce the need for co-pilots, they would help address a massive staffing shortage in the commercial and military aviation markets.

Again, nothing is guaranteed here. This stock is likely to be more turbulent than a C-130 flight through bad weather. Acknowledging that risk, I decided to fasten my seatbelt and go along for the ride.
