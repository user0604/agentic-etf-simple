Arvinas Revenue Drops 92% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 7:31 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
VEPPANU received historic FDA approval, marking a scientific milestone but did not bring immediate revenue.
Revenue fell sharply year over year due to one-time collaboration revenue in the prior year.
Management reaffirmed financial runway into late 2028 and highlighted major upcoming clinical milestones.

Arvinas (NASDAQ:ARVN) achieved a historic breakthrough with FDA approval of its first therapy, but near-term revenues fell significantly as major collaboration payments did not repeat. The clinical-stage biotech specializes in PROTAC protein degraders for diseases including cancer and neurodegeneration. In results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, released on May 12, 2026, Arvinas announced pivotal late-stage progress alongside reduced spending and persistent operating losses. Overall, the period brought a mix of scientific validation and near-term financial pressure as Arvinas completed its transition to a pipeline-driven business model.

Notable developments
VEPPANU became the first FDA-approved PROTAC therapy, securing a new regulatory milestone and global outlicensing to Rigel Pharmaceuticals.
Total revenue dropped 91.7% compared to the prior year, as last year included significant one-time collaboration proceeds that did not recur.
Research and development and general and administrative expenses fell by 33.6% and 28.2%, respectively, as cost controls took effect after a 2025 restructuring.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its expectation that existing cash and securities will fund operations through the second half of 2028. Leaders are focused on advancing several clinical-stage programs and launching new trials this year. Investors should watch for pipeline data from key assets including ARV-102 for neurodegeneration and ARV-806 for cancer, as well as updates on the commercial partnership for VEPPANU.

Quarterly highlights

VEPPANU, Arvinas’s first-in-class PROTAC therapy for advanced breast cancer, received FDA approval and was quickly outlicensed to Rigel Pharmaceuticals for global rights. While this provides external validation for the PROTAC technology, the license structure means Arvinas will not see immediate product sales revenue and future financial impact depends on milestone and royalty payments yet to be disclosed.

Company revenue declined meaningfully from the prior year period, as the comparison reflects a substantial prior collaboration payment tied to the development of VEPPANU with Pfizer. The drop was expected, and spending cuts in research and administrative costs narrowed Arvinas’s operating loss relative to earlier periods.

Cash and marketable securities totaled $614.9 million at quarter-end, down from December but projected by management to cover the portfolio through the second half of 2028. The company continues to advance a range of new clinical and preclinical candidates in both oncology and neurology, with several trial initiations and readouts planned for 2026.

What Arvinas does

Arvinas develops targeted protein degradation therapies, aiming to treat cancer and neurodegenerative diseases with an innovative approach that removes disease-causing proteins from cells.
