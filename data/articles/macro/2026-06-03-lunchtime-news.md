1. Meta Pushes Beyond Ads With AI Agents

★ META is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META) is jumping into the AI agent market with a new Meta Business Agent feature that runs on WhatsApp, Messenger, and Instagram to handle customer inquiries, recommend products, and book appointments. The launch marks the latest attempt by CEO Mark Zuckerberg to diversify revenue beyond ads, which still account for about 98% of Meta's top line.

How it works: The agent will be bundled into a business-focused tier of Meta One, the subscription package introduced last week. Big businesses already using the WhatsApp Business Platform will be charged on a consumption basis, and customers can connect third-party data sources like Shopify (NASDAQ: SHOP) and Zendesk through a separate Meta Business Agent Platform. Zuckerberg said agents will eventually "help you run your whole business."
A crowded field: Meta is wading into a competitive market where Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) and Microsoft (NASDAQ: MSFT) have already launched agent tools and the freely available OpenClaw platform has gone viral. Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) CEO Jensen Huang called OpenClaw "the most popular open-source project in the history of humanity," reflecting how quickly the agent space is becoming a battleground.
2. Big Tech's $5 Trillion AI Spending Bonanza
★ GOOG is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Alphabet (NASDAQ: GOOG) upsized its equity raise to $84.75 billion from $80 billion just two days after announcing it, in what's set to be the largest equity offering of all time. The Google parent is racing to fund AI infrastructure as Goldman Sachs (NYSE: GS) now expects the four largest hyperscalers to spend $5.3 trillion combined on capital expenditures from 2025 through 2030, up from a $4.5 trillion estimate before earnings season.

The offering details: Alphabet's package includes a $40 billion at-the-market program, a $10 billion deal with Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE: BRKB), $18 billion in Class A and Class C shares, and $16.75 billion in depositary shares. Google is betting on growing demand for its tensor processing units (TPUs), a key alternative to Nvidia's market-leading chips.
Hyperscalers go bigger: Amazon, Alphabet, Meta, and Microsoft plan to spend $725 billion combined on capex in 2026, up 77% from last year's already-record $410 billion. Amazon leads at $200 billion, Microsoft is tracking $190 billion, Alphabet $175 billion to $185 billion, and Meta $115 billion to $135 billion. The five top hyperscalers including Oracle (NYSE: ORCL) plan to add roughly $2 trillion in AI-related assets to balance sheets by 2030.
3. Big Pharma Goes Shopping

Eli Lilly (NYSE: LLY) is barely halfway through 2026 and has already committed more than $10 billion upfront, with potential value up to $25 billion, across eight acquisitions. That tops the roughly $4 billion the drugmaker spent on 40 deals all of last year, with new dealmaking lead Jacob Van Naarden saying "nothing is off the table."

GLP-1 cash fuels the spree: Lilly is using windfalls from Mounjaro and Zepbound to chase later-stage experimental drugs that carry larger price tags but higher odds of working. The drugmaker became the first health-care company to join the trillion-dollar club, with its market cap up from $190 billion in 2021. A new $1.9 billion partnership with RNA-editing firm Ascidian Therapeutics for kidney disease medicines was announced Wednesday.
New territory ahead: Some deals fall within Lilly's specialties of oncology, neuroscience, cardiometabolic health, and immunology, but recent vaccine acquisitions push into new areas.
