Sonida Posts 58% EBITDA Jump in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 7:50 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Business win: The CHP merger more than doubled Sonida's portfolio, driving scale and operational gains.
Performance: “Adjusted EBITDA (a non-GAAP measure) rose 58.1% year over year in the quarter.”
Outlook: Management is focused on integration, asset pruning, and refinancing in the coming year.

Senior living provider Sonida Senior Living (NYSE:SNDA) transformed its business in the latest quarter, finalizing a large-scale merger and rapidly expanding its footprint. The company, which owns and operates senior housing communities across the U.S, closed its acquisition of CNL Healthcare Properties (CHP) during the quarter reported on May 11, 2026, for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the quarter signaled strong operating improvement, but also highlighted the near-term financial impact of integration costs.

Notable Developments
The acquisition of CHP grew Sonida's portfolio to over 16,000 units and 165 properties, making it one of the largest owner-operators in U.S. senior housing.
Same-store occupancy reached 87.2%, a 2.2 percentage point increase from the prior year, and resident revenue increased by $29.1 million from last year.
Non-recurring merger, transition, and restructuring expenses surged to $26.1 million, leading to a larger net loss and negative cash from operations for the period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management said its priority is to integrate the expanded portfolio, realize merger synergies, and efficiently refinance over $1 billion in new debt. Attention over the next year is likely to focus on asset sales for balance sheet improvement and on timely refinancing of a $170 million bridge facility due by March 2027.

Quarterly Highlights

The closing of the CHP merger was the defining event of the quarter, driving Sonida's property count and revenue substantially higher. Management allocated about two-thirds of the merger consideration in new stock and about one-third in cash, immediately boosting both size and financial flexibility.

Operational metrics improved as core occupancy, average rents, and net operating income all rose versus last year, a result of growth from both newly acquired CHP communities and internal execution. The company reported its same-store net operating income margin, on a non-GAAP basis, steady above 28%, with additional cost and labor model improvements ongoing.

Short-term results were pressured by $26.1 million in one-off merger and integration costs, sharply raising the quarterly net loss and resulting in cash flow from operations turning negative. Higher debt and interest expense from financing the merger also weighed on profitability, making debt management and cost control a critical priority from here.
