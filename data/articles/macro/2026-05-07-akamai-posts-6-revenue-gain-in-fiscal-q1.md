Key Takeaways
Akamai Technologies reported strong cloud and security revenue, but saw margin pressure and declining earnings.
Revenue increased 6% compared to the prior year.
Management maintained full-year guidance, expecting flat or slightly lower margins.

Akamai Technologies (NASDAQ:AKAM) delivered solid revenue growth in cloud infrastructure and security but faced rising costs and lower margins this quarter. The provider of cybersecurity, web performance, and cloud infrastructure saw a major long-term contract win but recorded a decrease in profitability compared to last year (earnings release: May 7, 2026, for first quarter 2026). Overall, results showed ongoing momentum in newer businesses while legacy segments dragged performance.

Notable Developments
Cloud Infrastructure Services revenue rose nearly 40% year over year, boosted by a new seven-year, $1.8 billion contract with a large artificial intelligence customer.
Security solutions remained the largest segment, now 55% of all revenue, recording an 11% increase while legacy delivery revenue declined 7%.
Operating profitability fell, with non-GAAP operating margin down from 30% to 26% as higher stock-based compensation and capital investment increased expenses.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its guidance for the full year, expecting 5–8% revenue growth and a 26% non-GAAP operating margin. For the next quarter, management forecasted revenue between $1.08 billion and $1.10 billion and non-GAAP EPS in a range of $1.45 to $1.65. Investors should watch whether margin pressure persists as Akamai scales its AI-focused cloud business and secures more large contracts.

Quarterly Highlights

Cloud Infrastructure Services achieved record growth, driven by demand for workload hosting for artificial intelligence and edge computing. The new $1.8 billion customer contract represents a major step in expanding Akamai’s cloud platform.

The security division continued as the company’s cornerstone, now representing more than half of total revenue. Growth in security came from increased demand for products that protect web applications, application programming interfaces (APIs), and enterprise networks.

Profitability came under strain, with margins compressed due to higher spending on talent, cloud buildout, and costs associated with stock-based compensation. These investments support growth in new business lines but weighed down earnings compared to last year.

What Akamai Technologies Does

Akamai Technologies delivers security, web and mobile performance, and cloud infrastructure services to help businesses protect and accelerate their online applications and content.
