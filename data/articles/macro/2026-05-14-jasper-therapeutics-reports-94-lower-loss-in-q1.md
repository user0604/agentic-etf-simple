Biopharmaceutical company Jasper Therapeutics (NASDAQ:JSPR) delivered mixed results, with clinical trial advances offset by pressing cash constraints. The company, which develops antibody therapies primarily for chronic spontaneous urticaria and related diseases, released its first quarter results on May 14, 2026, covering the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter highlighted encouraging clinical data but underscored whether the pipeline can advance without near-term additional capital.

Notable Developments
Briquilimab, its lead antibody therapy, showed a complete response in 67% of BEACON study participants and a complete response or well controlled disease in 75% of open-label extension patients at 12 weeks.
Research and development costs dropped 64.2% year over year, signaling reduced spend as cash reserves fell to $14.1 million.
The company named Jeet Mahal as Chief Executive Officer, marking a key leadership change as it refocused efforts on its main drug program.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated plans to begin a phase 2b study for briquilimab in chronic spontaneous urticaria in the second half of the year, but emphasized this is contingent on securing new funding. No specific spending or operational guidance was offered for the rest of fiscal 2026. The biggest factor for investors remains whether it can raise additional capital to keep pipeline programs on track.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter's clinical highlight came from briquilimab, an antibody therapy targeting c-Kit (CD117) for mast cell-driven diseases. In the BEACON study, 67% of a small dosing cohort reached a complete response at 12 weeks, with a notable reduction in disease activity scores. In an open-label extension, 75% of participants achieved well-controlled disease or complete response, and median follow-up exceeded 200 days with mostly mild adverse events that resolved during the study.

The company's net loss narrowed sharply compared to last year, supported by a reduction in research and development expense to $5.8 million as it concentrated resources on briquilimab and trimmed broader pipeline initiatives. Cash on hand ended at $14.1 million, giving less than two quarters of operational runway at current spending levels.

No new partnership agreements or other revenue sources were announced, and the update provided no development for programs beyond the CSU franchise. The focus on briquilimab, leadership changes, and deliberate cost-cutting signal a critical juncture as commercialization and broader pipeline activities remain on hold.

What Jasper Therapeutics Does

The company develops antibody therapies for diseases driven by abnormal mast cell activity, with a principal focus on chronic spontaneous urticaria and related conditions.
