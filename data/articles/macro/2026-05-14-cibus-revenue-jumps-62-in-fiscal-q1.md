Cibus Revenue Jumps 62% in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 4:25 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Cibus recorded a modest business win with improved cost controls and new program milestones.
Net loss per share improved markedly while revenue climbed 62.5% year over year.
Management reaffirmed key commercialization milestones but pushed back the U.S. rice launch by one year.

Cibus (NASDAQ:CBUS) managed to tighten its cost structure and advance its commercial pipeline, but the path to major revenues remains slow. The agricultural gene-editing firm focuses on crop trait development, especially in rice and sustainable ingredients. In its results released May 14, 2026, for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, Cibus reported significant progress in Latin America, expanded research partnerships, and a stronger cash position, despite continued losses. Overall, the period reflected operational progress offset by a minor delay in one critical commercial launch.

Notable Developments
Revenue moved up 62.5% year over year in the first quarter of 2026.
Cost-cutting continued, with research and development expense down 26.3% and administrative costs nearly halved year over year.
Net loss improved sharply, helped by lower costs and the absence of the prior year’s goodwill write-offs, though losses remain significant.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management confirmed its 2027 goal for the Latin America rice trait launch and plans to ramp up sustainable ingredients programs, but the United States rice product debut is now expected in 2029, one year later than previously targeted. The company states that its current cash will last into the first quarter of 2027. Investors should track progress on commercial agreements in Latin America and further customer payments in sustainable ingredients as key inflection points for future revenue.

Quarterly highlights

The company kept its commercial rice launch milestone in Latin America on track by securing new import permits and transferring gene-edited traits to seed partner Interoc. At the same time, it documented ongoing discussions for expansion into large Asian markets, particularly India. Meanwhile, the U.S. rice commercialization slipped one year to 2029 because of external delays in herbicide registration by a key partner.

Revenue received a boost from expansion in the Sustainable Ingredients segment, following completion of a core customer contract amendment and first cash receipts. Management noted increased contract activity and expects additional biofragrance scale-up orders later in 2026. However, overall revenue remains modest compared to the cost structure.

The net loss per share narrowed primarily due to the absence of a prior year non-cash goodwill impairment, with strict cost controls also contributing. Research and development and selling, general, and administrative expenses each fell by more than a quarter year over year, reflecting ongoing facility consolidation and headcount reduction. A pair of early 2026 equity offerings raised $37.3 million to bolster cash reserves, extending the company’s solvency runway into the next year.

What Cibus does

Cibus uses gene editing (a method to precisely alter plant DNA) to develop crop traits, partnering with global seed companies to license and commercialize these traits.
