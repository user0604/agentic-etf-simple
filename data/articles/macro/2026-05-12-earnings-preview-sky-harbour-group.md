Earnings Preview: Sky Harbour Group

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Sky Harbour Group (NYSE:SKYH), which develops and leases private aircraft hangars at major U.S. airports, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Thursday, May 14, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Sky Harbour Group reported strong year-over-year growth in both revenue and operational scale, as the company continued to expand its network of home base operator campuses for business aircraft. Total revenue for the quarter reached $8.06 million, which missed analyst consensus estimates by 13%. However, the company delivered earnings per share (EPS) of $0.25, exceeding consensus expectations for a loss. The company highlighted that it achieved positive operating cash flow and reached breakeven on an adjusted EBITDA run-rate basis by year-end, driven in part by a significant upfront lease extension payment from a major tenant.

Operationally, Sky Harbour Group benefited from new campus openings and higher occupancy at recently delivered sites, though operating expenses also increased due to expansion and the onboarding of new ground leases. Management emphasized ongoing efforts to improve efficiency, particularly in selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) costs, and noted that ground lease expenses and development investments would continue to rise as the company grows its footprint.

Positives
Achieved breakeven on adjusted EBITDA run-rate: The company met its guidance to reach breakeven on an adjusted EBITDA basis by year-end 2025, which signals improving operational efficiency and cash flow stability as new campuses mature.
Significant year-over-year revenue growth: Total revenue for 2025 increased by 87% to $27.5 million, reflecting successful campus launches and strong leasing activity in high-demand markets.
Positive operating cash flow for the first time: The Obligated Group reported positive net cash from operating activities, supported by a $5.9 million upfront lease extension, demonstrating progress toward sustainable cash generation.
Expansion of campus network and development pipeline: The company broke ground on multiple new projects and secured additional ground leases, positioning itself for continued growth in 2026 and beyond.
Negatives
Q4 revenue missed analyst estimates: Quarterly revenue came in below consensus, indicating that the pace of lease-up or campus ramp may have been slower than anticipated by the market.
Operating expenses rose sharply with expansion: Campus operating, ground lease, and compensation expenses all increased significantly year over year, reflecting the costs of scaling but also pressuring margins until new sites stabilize.
Reliance on one-time items for cash flow: Positive operating cash flow was aided by a large, non-recurring upfront lease payment, which may not be repeated in future quarters and could mask underlying cash flow trends.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Pace of new campus lease-up and revenue ramp: Investors should monitor how quickly newly opened campuses are filling up and contributing to recurring revenue, as well as any updates on preleasing activity for projects under construction.
Progress on operational efficiency initiatives: Management has targeted SG&A and operating cost reductions for 2026. Watch for evidence of margin improvement or updates on cost-saving measures as the company scales.
Utilization of new financing and liquidity position: The company recently completed a $150 million bond issuance and expanded its term loan facility. Updates on how these funds are being deployed for development and the current cash position will be important for assessing growth capacity and balance sheet health.
Status of development pipeline and campus delivery schedule: Key milestones include the opening of Miami phase two and Bradley, Connecticut campuses. Investors should look for any changes in expected delivery dates or updates on permitting, construction, and preleasing for upcoming projects.
Guidance methodology and key performance metrics: Management has indicated that future guidance will focus on net operating income (NOI) capture rather than number of airports. Any new disclosures on how the company will measure and communicate growth targets, as well as updates on re-leasing rates and rent escalations, will be important for tracking long-term value creation.
