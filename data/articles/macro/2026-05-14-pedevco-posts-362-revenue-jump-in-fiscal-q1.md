Key Takeaways

Pedevco (NYSEMKT:PED) delivered robust production growth as it integrated new assets following its recent merger, but still posted a net loss due to non-cash derivative impacts. The independent oil and gas producer, which operates mainly in the Rockies region, continued to build on its strategy of unlocking value through modern drilling techniques and targeted acquisitions. The results, released on May 14, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, show the company making significant progress toward its operational goals, though headline results were weighed down by market-driven accounting charges. Overall, the quarter featured strong operating performance offset by commodity price hedging losses.

Notable Developments
Production soared to 8,091 barrels of oil equivalent per day, supported by 31 new D-J Basin wells brought online in the prior quarter.
Revenue reached $40.2 million, driven by significantly higher production volumes.
A non-cash loss on derivatives, totaling $31.3 million, led to a company-wide net loss despite improved operational metrics.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook, targeting average daily production of 6,500 to 7,000 barrels of oil equivalent per day and adjusted EBITDA between $60 million and $70 million. It expects production to normalize following the surge in new well activity during the latest quarter. Investors should keep an eye on the company's ability to sustain operational gains while managing ongoing exposure to commodity price swings and derivative impacts.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter marked Pedevco’s first full period as a merged entity, with results benefiting from newly acquired operations and demonstrating successful integration. Most of the production boost came from D-J Basin wells that began producing late last year, driving record operational volumes and substantial revenue increases.

Despite these gains, the company recorded a significant net loss, mainly because of mark-to-market losses on oil and gas price hedging contracts, which do not reflect the underlying operational health. Adjusted EBITDA, which excludes such non-cash charges, climbed more than fourfold from the prior year, highlighting strong cash generation from core operations.

Operating costs, including lease operating expense per unit, were kept steady even as overall activity ramped up. Management pointed out ongoing efficiency efforts, lift conversions, and new service contracts as factors supporting stable cost metrics during the transition to a larger asset base.

What Pedevco Does

Pedevco acquires, develops, and produces oil and natural gas assets in the U.S. Rockies, focusing on modern drilling and completion methods to enhance underdeveloped properties.
