MakeMyTrip Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 7:08 AM

Comments

MakeMyTrip Limited (NASDAQ:MMYT) is India’s largest online travel platform, offering air ticketing, hotel bookings, holiday packages, and ground transport services. Leveraging India’s rapidly expanding middle class, digital payment adoption, and strong travel sector tailwinds, the company’s growth strategy centers on capturing rising demand across both urban and non-metro markets, while driving operational efficiency through artificial intelligence (AI) and aggressive supply expansion in underpenetrated segments.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership Drives Pricing Power and Resilient Growth

Dominant market share in core travel segments provides a defensive moat and growth platform. MakeMyTrip’s commanding 30.8% share in the domestic air ticketing market, as reported in fiscal year 2026, demonstrates its ability to gain ground even as the overall market declined year over year. Through strategic expansion in hotels and ground transport, the company’s gross bookings more than tripled in four years—from $3.2 billion in FY22 to a record $10.4 billion in FY26, a compounded annual growth rate of roughly 34%. This scale delivers pricing leverage with suppliers and enhances cross-selling opportunities, supporting continued outperformance versus peers and improving margins as travel penetration in India grows.

2. Technology and AI Adoption Unlock Structural Efficiency and User Engagement

AI-driven platform innovations are producing measurable gains in conversion and operational leverage. The rollout of MakeMyTrip’s proprietary Myra conversational AI, now handling over 80,000 daily interactions, has led to a 10% higher booking conversion rate for engaged users compared to traditional search methods. With 60–70% of new code now generated by AI tools and over half of customer support queries resolved without human intervention, the company reports meaningful efficiency gains and productivity improvement, but has not explicitly stated that operating costs have been sharply reduced or user satisfaction improved. These efficiency gains are supported by the fact that 45% of Myra usage originates from Tier 2 and smaller cities.

3. Strategic Diversification Shields Revenue Amid External Shocks

A diversified product mix across hotels, bus ticketing, and alternative segments insulates the business from sector cyclicality. Despite headwinds such as geopolitical conflicts and rising fuel costs impacting air travel, MakeMyTrip’s revenue model has shown resilience by rapidly substituting demand between international and domestic offerings. In FY26, bus ticketing volumes grew by 27.6% in the fourth quarter, while hotel and package accommodation volumes increased 15.2% year over year, far outpacing a flat or negative hospitality industry. This strategic breadth enables MakeMyTrip to maintain gross booking growth even during periods of volatility, securing a high proportion of the travel wallet and reducing dependence on any single vertical.

4. Robust Cash Generation and Prudent Capital Management Enable Strategic Flexibility

Strong operating cash flow and disciplined buybacks support both growth investments and shareholder returns. At the close of FY26, MakeMyTrip held over $782 million in cash and cash equivalents, having converted 97% of adjusted operating profit into operating cash flow that year. The company executed nearly $100 million in share buybacks, signaling management’s confidence in the business and commitment to returning capital to shareholders. This liquidity cushion provides ample runway to invest in acquisitions—such as its majority stake in Flamingo Transworld for holiday packages—as well as in technology and new supply ventures, while maintaining resilience against macroeconomic shocks.

5. India’s Secular Travel Boom Presents a Long Runway for Multi-Year Expansion

The rapid expansion of India’s middle class, infrastructure, and digital ecosystem underpins sustained demand growth for online travel services. According to management and industry studies, middle-income households in India—projected to grow over 50% in a decade—are fueling higher travel frequency and broader destination preferences. India added more than 70 million passport holders in five years, and unified payments interface (UPI) transactions exceeded 640 million daily in 2025, erasing barriers to online adoption. With Tier 2 and Tier 3 cities becoming major contributors and infrastructure investment accelerating, MakeMyTrip is strategically positioned to harness decades of secular growth as digital penetration in travel continues to rise.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Geopolitical Tensions and Commodity Price Volatility Pressure Margins and International Growth

Exposure to regional conflicts, fuel price spikes, and currency swings can meaningfully disrupt performance. The West Asia conflict in early 2026 led to a sharp drop in international travel bookings and increased airfares due to higher fuel (aviation turbine fuel) prices. MakeMyTrip recorded a $17.7 million foreign exchange loss in one quarter alone from rupee depreciation, and profit in Q4 declined year over year as higher finance costs outpaced revenue gains. Prolonged instability could curtail high-margin outbound tourism, compress blended margins (since domestic and ground transport are lower yield), and inject further volatility into quarterly results.

2. Aggressive Technology Investment Has Yet to Fully Offset Cost Inflation and Margin Risks

Technology initiatives, while promising, involve up-front costs and may not immediately deliver expected profit improvements. Despite material advances in AI and digital automation, MakeMyTrip’s profit in the fourth quarter of FY26 declined from the prior year, with management citing increased finance costs as a primary driver. Marketing and promotional expenses, though lower as a percentage of bookings compared to earlier periods, remain substantial. While AI-driven efficiencies are projected to lift margins over time, immediate gains have been muted by ongoing investment needs and macro-driven inflation, creating potential risks to short-term earnings growth and operating leverage if savings materialize slower than anticipated.

3. Regulatory and Structural Uncertainties Around Potential India Listing Pose Strategic Ambiguity

Unresolved regulatory hurdles regarding India’s dual-listing restrictions introduce medium-term uncertainty for capital market access and shareholder value realization. MakeMyTrip has restructured its Indian operations to facilitate a future India market listing, but current rules prohibit a fully fungible dual listing with its U.S. entity. Management has not provided a definitive timeline for this transition, and preparatory work streams in legal, tax, governance, and disclosure are ongoing.
