Vtex Posts 14% Subscription Gain in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:41 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Subscription revenue grew, but services revenue sharply declined.
Subscription revenue increased 14.0% year over year.
Management maintained a cautious growth outlook for the full year.

Subscription software specialist Vtex (NYSE:VTEX) delivered modest progress in its latest quarter, with core subscription revenue climbing even as services income declined significantly. The company provides a cloud-based commerce platform for large retailers and consumer brands worldwide. The results, released on May 7, 2026, and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, highlighted ongoing focus on SaaS growth and cost discipline in a cautious macroeconomic climate. Overall, the period showed steady execution but mixed revenue trends.

Notable Developments
Subscription revenue grew 14.0% year over year, reaching $59.96 million, as the company continued to prioritize SaaS offerings.
Services revenue dropped by 53.7%, falling to $0.73 million — a result of VTEX's plan to focus on higher-margin business lines.
Cash and equivalents increased to $16.79 million, while current liabilities decreased 4.6% from the previous quarter, reflecting stable balance sheet management.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its target for mid-to-high single-digit growth in subscription revenue, adjusted for currency effects, for the full year. It did not provide new guidance in this release, instead citing a still-cautious macroeconomic backdrop as justification for maintaining its original outlook. Investors should closely monitor the pace of new customer additions and the performance of deferred revenue, which showed little sequential growth.

Quarterly Highlights

The headline result was continued subscription revenue growth, which outpaced management's guidance range and reflected robust demand for the company's cloud commerce platform. Despite this, the sharp year-over-year decline in services revenue underscored an ongoing shift away from lower-margin service work toward software subscription sales.

The balance sheet remained solid with nearly flat total current assets and a small increase in cash and marketable securities. Accumulated losses also narrowed, helping to signal improving operational discipline.

While the company's geographic reach remains broad, including operations across Latin America, Europe, and North America, management highlighted persistent macroeconomic headwinds in core markets. This environment contributed to lengthier sales cycles, particularly among large enterprise clients.

What Vtex Does

Vtex provides a cloud-based digital commerce platform that enables retailers and brands to build online stores, manage multi-channel sales, and launch third-party marketplaces.
