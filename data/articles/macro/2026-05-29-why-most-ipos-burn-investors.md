Why Most IPOs Burn Investors

Jim Mueller, CFA

May 29, 2026, 5:25 AM

Comments
9.5

What’s the biggest driver for how you invest? Cash flows? Or hope?

The former is how we should invest, the latter is how many actually do invest.

Take the upcoming IPO of SpaceX. Are you going to invest in it? Why? Positive and growing cash flows is likely not the reason, because the company doesn’t have that (and may never have that).

One analysis I read said that it’s targeting a valuation higher than Meta (NASDAQ:META), Broadcom (NASDAQ:AVGO), and Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE:BRKB) combined, while having less revenue than Macy’s (NYSE:M). And the way it is organized, it sets money on fire.

Why would anyone want to own that?

One reason is that one wants to sell it at a higher price to someone even more enamored with it. A greater fool. A bunch of people will likely make money doing that. But it’s not a reliable way to get rich. It depends on luck and emotion and not being the greatest fool, something you cannot know until it’s too late.

IPOs are designed to get people excited and eager to buy. But the harsh reality is that the vast majority of them end up losing money for investors over the following three to five years. I’d rather remember that and go with the odds. Especially for a company that burns cash and has some really crazy assumptions built into its valuation.

I may not make the quick buck. But I’m also less likely to find myself being the greatest fool, buying at the top. I can get rich investing in companies that consistently generate cash rather than incinerate it. Slow and steady wins the race.
