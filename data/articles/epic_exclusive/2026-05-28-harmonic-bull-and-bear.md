Harmonic: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 28, 2026, 7:43 PM

Comments

Harmonic (NASDAQ:HLIT) provides software-based broadband solutions that enable operators to upgrade and modernize their networks for high-speed data, video, and next-generation communications. The company is undergoing a strategic transformation by divesting its legacy Video business to become a pure-play broadband provider, sharpening its focus on recurring-revenue software, fiber, and virtualization platforms. This evolution aims to capture expanding market opportunities as demand for multi-gigabit connectivity, fiber upgrades, and AI-driven network automation accelerates into the late 2020s.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Pure-Play Broadband Focus Unlocks Margin and Growth Expansion

The full divestiture of the Video segment positions Harmonic as a focused broadband technology leader. The company is on track to complete its Video business sale by Q2 2026, unlocking approximately $145 million in cash proceeds for capital redeployment, as detailed across recent earnings calls and SEC filings. This shift allows management to concentrate all efforts and resources on high-growth broadband initiatives, particularly software-driven and virtualized solutions such as DOCSIS 4.0 and fiber. The more focused operating model streamlines costs, increases strategic clarity, and is expected to drive margin expansion, as the business transitions to a higher proportion of recurring, services, and software revenue, improving both earnings quality and financial predictability over the coming years.

2. Surging Broadband Revenue and Record Backlog Signal Durable Growth

Explosive broadband revenue growth provides strong future visibility. In Q1 2026, broadband revenue rose 43% year over year to $121.7 million (non-GAAP), driven by broad-based adoption of the company’s converged DOCSIS and fiber platforms. Particularly notable is the 87% surge in backlog and deferred revenue to a record $582.1 million, with 60% expected to convert within 12 months. This robust backlog, along with raised full-year broadband revenue guidance to $475 million–$495 million for 2026, reflects strong underlying demand and gives investors high confidence in revenue growth for the foreseeable future, setting a strong foundation for continued operating leverage.

3. Technological Leadership in DOCSIS 4.0 and Fiber Drives Share Gains

Best-in-class technology supports operator migration and market share growth. Harmonic’s unified DOCSIS 4.0 and converged cOS (cloud-native operating system) architecture provides operators with the unique flexibility to modernize using both fiber and DOCSIS solutions. Winning projects such as Optimum’s network overhaul in West Virginia highlight these platforms’ ability to enable cost-effective, multi-technology upgrades, consolidating infrastructure while supporting advanced services. New product launches (Pearl-1XL, Jetty-3), combined with an installed base of 150 operators managing over 45 million connected devices as of Q1 2026, reinforce the company’s competitive edge and set the stage for continued footprint expansion across both domestic and international markets.

4. Rest-of-Market Growth and International Wins Lower Customer Concentration Risk

Diversification beyond top customers underpins multi-year growth. The rest-of-market (ROM) segment posted 78% revenue growth year over year in Q1 2026, now comprising 42% of total revenue, up sharply from prior periods. Harmonic’s ability to secure new deployments, such as KBRO in Taiwan and multiple European and U.S. wins, demonstrates success in expanding the customer base and mitigating historical overreliance on its two largest clients, who now account for 58% of revenue versus higher levels previously. Ongoing international traction and a long-term target for 30%-plus annual ROM growth suggest further reduction in concentration risk and more resilient top-line performance through varying market cycles.

5. Recurring AI-Driven Software and Services Accelerate Margin Upside

Emerging AI-powered platforms create sticky, high-margin recurring revenue streams. New software offerings such as Beacon, Pathfinder, and Amply leverage artificial intelligence to enable network operators to optimize performance, reduce operating costs, and improve customer experience. Early deployments have reduced customer support calls by more than 30% and driven customer Net Promoter Scores to a record 85 as of Q1 2026. These solutions are central to Harmonic’s strategy to increase recurring revenue, deepen customer relationships, and strengthen its competitive moat. As adoption scales, software and services are positioned to boost overall gross margin, operating leverage, and earnings durability.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Supply Chain Bottlenecks and Component Cost Inflation Threaten Margins

Supply chain risks could cap near-term margin expansion. The company faces elevated memory prices, projected to negatively impact gross margin by approximately $6 million in the second half of 2026, with ongoing volatility in servers, CPUs, and PCB components also flagged. Management has proactively secured most needed inventory, but certain server and third-party pass-through sales, while not material (less than 5% of revenue), could delay customer deployments or create revenue recognition lags. With full-year 2026 gross margin guidance at 50%–51.5%, down from 54.1% in Q1, and explicit caution regarding future component costs, any prolonged supply constraints or further cost inflation could erode profitability and stall the anticipated near-term margin uplift.

2. Top Two Customers Still Represent a Significant Share of Revenue, Sustaining Concentration Risk

Customer concentration remains a strategic vulnerability. Despite notable diversification successes, the two largest customers accounted for 58% of revenue in Q1 2026 (each individually above 10%). While this figure has improved from historical levels, dependence on a small number of large accounts leaves Harmonic exposed to revenue volatility if these customers alter purchasing patterns, delay network upgrades, or switch to competitors. Any disruption or competitive loss in these relationships could have a disproportionate impact on both near-term results and investor confidence, especially as the company transitions its overall business mix.

3. Execution Risk Around Stranded Costs and Margin Realization Post-Video Sale

Cost reduction execution may lag, pressuring profits after divestiture. The pending Video segment sale will generate up to $10 million in “stranded costs” in 2026, with management targeting removal of roughly 30% within 12 months post-closing. While these cost initiatives are underway, delays or underachievement in eliminating stranded overhead could weigh on gross and operating margins, undermining some of the anticipated benefits of the business model transformation. If growth in high-margin software and services does not ramp as quickly as planned, or if cost rationalization proves slower than expected, there may be a material shortfall in forecasted earnings improvement.
