Booz Allen Hamilton (NYSE:BAH) delivered resilient earnings as cost management drove profit growth, even as sales declined. The global consulting firm, best known for its work with U.S. government and defense clients, reported quarterly results for the three months ended March 31, 2026, on May 22, 2026. Overall, the quarter highlighted margin strength but persistent revenue headwinds and ongoing challenges in the Civil segment.

Notable Developments
Revenue decreased 6.4% from the prior year quarter as Civil segment activity remained weak, while Defense and Intelligence segments stabilized.
Adjusted earnings per share climbed over 10%, supported by cost reductions and a lower share count, with net income up 6.2% year over year.
Backlog reached a record high of $38.2 billion, and quarterly free cash flow grew 9.3% to $212 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects fiscal 2027 revenue between $11.2 billion and $11.7 billion, implying stability after two years of contraction. Adjusted earnings per share are expected to be lower at the midpoint, reflecting fewer one-time tax benefits, while free cash flow is forecast to decrease as capital spending rises, particularly for the new headquarters. The most important area to watch is whether pipeline conversion in Civil and continued strength in government contracts can drive a return to sales growth.

Quarterly Highlights

Profitability improved, with adjusted earnings per share increasing thanks to both disciplined cost control and fewer shares outstanding. Net income also rose even as sales dropped, highlighting efficiency gains.

Sales in the Civil segment, which includes work for non-defense government agencies, continued to decline sharply. In contrast, Defense and Intelligence segment revenues were stable or slightly higher, helping offset Civil-sector weakness.

Booz Allen’s backlog hit an all-time high, offering a visible path for future revenue largely from new and existing government contracts. Quarterly bookings rebounded, and the book-to-bill ratio improved, suggesting some stabilization in awards activity.
