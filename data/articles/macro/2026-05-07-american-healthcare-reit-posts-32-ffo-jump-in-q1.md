Key Takeaways
Double-digit growth in core senior housing segments lifted American Healthcare REIT’s latest quarter.
Normalized funds from operations per share climbed 31.6% year over year.
Management raised its full-year cash flow outlook but trimmed reported earnings guidance.

Steady growth in its core senior living properties delivered a solid quarter for American Healthcare REIT (NYSE:AHR). The owner and operator of healthcare real estate posted strong gains in cash flow measures and expanded its property portfolio. In its results released on May 7, 2026 for the first quarter, management announced both improved guidance for key cash generation metrics and a lower estimate for reported net income, reflecting higher non-cash costs. Overall, the period reflected operational strength, increased financial flexibility, and continued investment in growth properties.

Notable Developments
The company’s Normalized Funds From Operations per share rose 31.6%. This exceeded earlier targets and helped prompt an increase in management’s full-year cash flow outlook.
Portfolio same-store net operating income grew 12.1% with the senior housing operating properties segment (known as SHOP) up 19.7%, making it the leading growth area in the quarter.
Management raised its guidance for full-year cash flow measures and portfolio growth, but trimmed the net income guidance due to higher depreciation and share issuance costs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased its forecast for Normalized Funds From Operations, now guiding to $2.03–$2.09 per share for fiscal 2026. It also raised its projection for property income growth across the full year, reflecting healthy occupancy and pricing power, especially in senior housing segments. Investors should track whether the company can maintain this segment growth, especially as new equity issuance increases share count and shapes future results.

Quarterly Highlights

Cash flow and operating income rose sharply, driven by outperformance in the company’s core integrated senior health campuses and the SHOP segment. The SHOP segment reported a 19.7% increase in net operating income, as higher occupancy and effective pricing drove results above the company’s full-year targets.

Balance sheet flexibility improved with net debt to annualized adjusted EBITDA falling to 3.0 times, down from 3.4 times only three months earlier. This was achieved through both earnings growth and significant new equity sales, as the company secured $357.4 million in gross proceeds and had a further $527.4 million pending from unsettled agreements.

Guidance for total portfolio net operating income growth was raised to a range of 9% to 12%. Management trimmed the expected net income per share guidance for the year, reflecting higher non-cash costs from depreciation and share issuance. The company maintained its quarterly dividend at $0.25 per share.

What American Healthcare REIT Does

American Healthcare REIT acquires, owns, and operates a diversified portfolio of healthcare real estate properties, with a focus on senior housing, integrated health campuses, and outpatient medical buildings.
