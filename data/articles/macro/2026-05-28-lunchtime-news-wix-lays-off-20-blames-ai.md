1. Wix Joins the AI Layoff Wave

WIX is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

Wix (NASDAQ: WIX) is cutting roughly 20% of its workforce -- about 1,000 employees -- as CEO Avishai Abrahami pointed to "the fast evolution of AI capabilities" and currency pressures. Shares traded flat after the news, with the company joining a growing list of tech firms citing artificial intelligence (AI) as a driver of headcount cuts.

Flatter and faster: Abrahami said the company will move toward fewer layers of leadership to speed up decisions, calling AI "the most significant shift in how companies are built since the invention of modern programming languages in the 1970s." A strengthening Israeli shekel against the U.S. dollar is adding "structural pressure" on Wix's ability to operate at its current scale.
Same script, more companies: Wix follows Meta Platforms (NASDAQ: META), which cut about 8,000 jobs last week; Cisco (NASDAQ: CSCO), trimming 5% of its workforce; and Block (NYSE: XYZ), slashing nearly half its workforce in February. Each leadership team cited AI's role in automating work -- though economists have flagged that the "AI washing" trend may be masking other cost pressures.
2. Starbucks' Afternoon Comeback

SBUX is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team HG)

Starbucks (NASDAQ: SBUX) said afternoon traffic is becoming a bigger growth engine, a sign CEO Brian Niccol's turnaround playbook is starting to land. The coffee chain is seeing its strongest U.S. traffic growth between 3 p.m. and 5 p.m., according to internal data from Feb. 15 to May 16.

Beyond the morning rush: Hours after 11 a.m. generated $11 billion in U.S. sales in fiscal 2025, and the Refreshers platform is now Starbucks' second-best-selling beverage category behind espresso. Traffic grew for the second straight quarter, and shares are up 21% year to date through Wednesday's close.
The competition is awake, too: Dutch Bros (NYSE: BROS) has highlighted momentum in afternoon energy drinks and cold beverages, while Dunkin' is pushing beverage innovation and snacks for the post-lunch crowd.
3. Ferrari Defends $640,000 EV

RACE is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Ferrari (NYSE: RACE) CEO Benedetto Vigna defended the 550,000-euro (roughly $640,000) price tag for the automaker's first fully electric vehicle (EV), the Luce, after a rocky reception sent shares down 8% on Tuesday. The Milan-listed stock was up 1.3% on Thursday as Vigna pushed back on critics.

"Nothing to do with Chinese EVs": Vigna said the price reflects what it costs to build the car and that the Luce can't be compared to other electric vehicles. Ferrari designed and manufactured every component in-house in Maranello, and the exterior was done by LoveFrom, the design agency founded by former Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) design chief Jony Ive. The car hits 60 mph in 2.5 seconds and tops out around 192 mph.
The brand backlash: Former Ferrari chairman Luca di Montezemolo called the Luce a "disgrace" and said Ferrari should remove the prancing horse logo from it. Italy's Transport Minister, Matteo Salvini, also publicly slammed the design and price.
