Creative Media & Community Trust (NASDAQ:CMCT) experienced another tough quarter as it shifted its focus toward multifamily properties and navigated ongoing losses and weak operating income. The diversified real estate company concentrates on office, multifamily, and hotel assets in major California cities. In results released on May 7, 2026, for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, the company returned to its strategic plan but continued to post substantial net losses. Overall, the period showed balance sheet progress but persistent weakness in office and hotel profitability.

Notable Developments
CMCT redeemed $242.8 million in preferred stock and completed the sale of its lending business, producing $31.2 million in cash proceeds.
Office and hotel segment net operating incomes both declined from the prior year, with office portfolio occupancy decreasing to 73.1% and hotel segment net operating income falling to $4.0 million.
Multifamily occupancy rates climbed to nearly 90%, but net rent per unit declined compared to the prior year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects the earnings benefit from reduced preferred dividends to begin noticeably in the next quarter, projecting annualized FFO relief of about $16 million. The company gave no quantified outlook for earnings or operating income, but stressed that continued focus will be on lease-up at the Echo Park multifamily project, asset sales, and ongoing debt reduction. Investors should closely watch occupancy and rent levels in the multifamily and office segments, as well as management's progress on refinancing and cost controls.

Quarterly Highlights

The company made significant progress repositioning its balance sheet by redeeming $396.2 million of preferred stock since September 2024 and selling its lending division, steps meant to reduce future dividend expense and improve financial flexibility.

Operations continued to face pressure: total segment net operating income dropped 17.0%, as the office segment struggled with lower occupancy (especially in Oakland), and hotel performance suffered from renovation-related disruptions and temporary room closures.

The multifamily portfolio had a material increase in occupancy, rising to 89.6% companywide, but the average net rent collected per unit fell, as efforts to fill vacant apartments required greater tenant concessions, especially at the new Echo Park property.
