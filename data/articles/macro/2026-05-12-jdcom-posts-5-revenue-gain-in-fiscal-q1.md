JD.com (NASDAQ:JD) saw its business growth in services and logistics overshadowed by a sharp dip in overall profitability as costs and regulatory penalties increased. Notable developments
Revenue reached RMB 315.7 billion, with services revenue rising 20.6% while product sales growth slowed to 1.0%.
Group profits dropped sharply, pressured by a RMB 635 million regulatory fine and increases in operating costs of 18% to 49% across categories.
Free cash flow remained negative but improved compared to last year, indicating better cash management even with ongoing business investment.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management stated it has confidence in the company’s progress for the full year, pointing to improvements in service, logistics, and retail margins. However, no detailed financial targets were provided. The most important thing for investors to monitor is JD.com’s ability to control costs and limit regulatory risk while sustaining growth in new business areas.

Quarterly highlights

The quarter saw service revenues grow strongly, up 20.6%, with general merchandise categories such as supermarket and healthcare outperforming electronics, which fell 8.4%. This shift echoes commentary from previous quarters about changing consumer buying patterns.

Profitability declined at the group level as fulfillment, marketing, and research spending all rose rapidly, outpacing revenue growth. A major government fine related to marketplace shop compliance drove a nearly 49% jump in general and administrative expenses and weighed on margins.

JD Retail improved its operating margin to record levels, reaching 5.6%, while JD Logistics achieved a profit with a 1.7% operating margin. Losses in new businesses, including international expansion and food delivery, remained high but narrowed compared to the previous quarter.

What JD.com does

JD.com is a technology-driven provider of e-commerce, logistics, and supply chain services offering both goods and marketplace services to consumers and businesses.
