Earnings Preview: HP

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

HP (NYSE:HPQ), a leading provider of computers, printers, and related services, is expected to report earnings for the second quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Wednesday, May 27, 2026, after the market closes.

Q1 FY2026 Recap

In the first quarter of fiscal 2026, HP reported revenue of $14.4 billion, representing a 6.9% increase year over year. The company delivered non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) of $0.81, which was at the top of its guidance range and above analyst consensus. Growth was driven by strong performance in the Personal Systems segment, especially in consumer PCs, which benefited from the Windows 11 refresh cycle and rising demand for AI-enabled computers. The Printing segment saw results in line with expectations, with ongoing momentum in consumer subscriptions and industrial print, despite a modest decline in overall print revenue.

Positives
Personal Systems revenue growth outperformed expectations: The segment grew 11% year over year, with unit volume up 12% due to the Windows 11 refresh and increased adoption of AI PCs. This helped HP gain market share in both commercial and consumer categories.
Consumer PC demand was notably strong: Consumer revenue rose 16% and unit volume increased 14%, partly due to customers accelerating purchases ahead of anticipated memory price increases. This demand pull-in supported above-seasonal performance.
Non-GAAP EPS beat analyst estimates: The $0.81 EPS result exceeded the consensus of $0.77, reflecting effective expense management and successful cost-saving initiatives.
Shareholder returns remained robust: HP returned $600 million to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases, demonstrating ongoing commitment to capital returns.
Negatives
Gross margin declined due to higher commodity costs: Gross margin fell to 19.6% from 21.0% a year earlier, mainly because of increased memory and storage costs that were only partially offset by pricing actions and cost reductions.
Personal Systems operating margin compressed: Operating margin in this segment dropped to 5.0% from 5.5% last year, reflecting the impact of higher input costs and a stronger mix of lower-margin consumer and education sales.
Printing segment revenue declined: Print revenue fell 2.2% year over year, with both consumer and commercial printing units down due to ongoing market softness and competitive pressures. Supplies revenue also decreased slightly.
Ongoing memory price volatility adds uncertainty: Management highlighted that memory and storage costs have roughly doubled sequentially, and this pressure is expected to persist, impacting margins in future quarters.
What to Watch in Q2 FY2026's Report
Impact of rising memory and storage costs: Investors should monitor how HP manages the ongoing surge in memory prices, which are now estimated to be about 35% of the PC bill of materials for the year. The company’s ability to offset these costs through pricing, supply chain actions, and product adjustments will be key for margins.
Personal Systems segment performance and demand trends: Watch for updates on PC unit volumes and average selling prices, especially as the Windows 11 refresh cycle matures and AI PC adoption continues. Management expects above-seasonal revenue in Q2, but also notes that total PC industry shipments are forecast to decline double digits for the calendar year.
Print segment profitability and recurring revenue growth: HP expects print operating margins to be near the top end of its long-term range for Q2 and the full year. Look for progress in subscription-based print services, Big Tank share gains, and industrial print growth as offsetting factors to hardware declines.
Status of AI and digital transformation initiatives: The company’s transformation plan, announced in Nov. 2025 and centered on artificial intelligence, remains on track. Investors should look for updates on AI integration into products and operations, as well as progress toward the $1 billion in annualized cost savings targeted by fiscal 2028.
CEO transition and leadership updates: The search for a permanent CEO is ongoing, with the board seeking a leader experienced in managing large, complex businesses. Any announcements or progress on this front may influence investor sentiment and strategic direction.
