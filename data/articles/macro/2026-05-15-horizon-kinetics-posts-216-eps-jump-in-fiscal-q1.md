Key Takeaways

Earnings per share (GAAP) rose sharply year over year in Q1 2026, reaching $3.89 versus $1.23 in Q1 2025.

Asset manager Horizon Kinetics (OTC:HKHC) delivered standout first quarter results, benefiting from a sharp increase in both assets under management and net income. The firm is known for its contrarian and highly concentrated investment philosophy, focusing on undervalued and off-benchmark holdings. On May 15, 2026, it released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, which showed that a one-off incentive fee and appreciation in a single major investment drove much of the performance. Overall, the quarter featured significant top and bottom line growth but also underscored the firm’s high earnings volatility and concentration risk.

Notable Developments
Net income leapt to $72.5 million due to a large, non-recurring incentive fee and asset appreciation, especially a 65% gain in Texas Pacific Land Corporation.
Advisory fee revenue, excluding incentive events, declined by 3.7% as mutual fund fee income slipped, partly offset by increased revenue from the Inflation Beneficiaries ETF.
Assets under management rose 19% quarter over quarter to $11.4 billion, primarily reflecting gains from a single investment rather than broad client inflows.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide formal forward guidance and instead pointed to ongoing earnings volatility tied to concentrated holdings and one-time incentive fees. Investors should focus on trends in core advisory fee revenue and underlying client flows, as results remain highly sensitive to movements in a few major investments.

Quarterly Highlights

The firm’s quarter was defined by a windfall $18.1 million incentive fee related to the expiration of trading restrictions on private fund investments in Miami International Holdings, as well as sharp appreciation in Texas Pacific Land Corporation, which accounted for most of the period’s asset growth and investment returns.

Excluding the incentive fee, core management and advisory fees saw a mild decline, reflecting lower mutual fund revenues that were only partly offset by gains in specialty offerings like the Inflation Beneficiaries ETF, an exchange-traded fund focused on inflation-sensitive assets.

Operating expenses rose, largely due to commissions and costs tied to the incentive event. The board maintained the quarterly dividend at $0.127 per share.

What Horizon Kinetics Does

Horizon Kinetics is an investment management company specializing in actively managed strategies, with a contrarian approach focused on unique, often concentrated investments not found in traditional indexes.
