Notable Developments
Six new contracts were signed during the period, with a total value over $5 million, and more than $4 million came from U.S. government agencies.
Cash and equivalents plus certificates of deposit grew to $37.8 million as of March 31, 2026, up from $15.7 million a year earlier, due mainly to a recent public offering.
Adjusted EBITDA loss widened year over year to $0.9 million, mostly due to higher salaries, stock-based compensation, and expanding operating expenses.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management revised its full-year revenue guidance down to a range of $4.2 million to $4.6 million, citing execution delays that slowed the ramp-up of new contracts. The company emphasized its strong cash balance and growing list of development engagements, but also acknowledged that conversion of R&D contracts to commercial revenue remains a challenge. Investors should watch for evidence that these contract wins can transition to recurring, higher-margin product sales in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Contract momentum was a high point for the quarter: Aeluma secured six new development deals, surpassing its annual onboarding goal. The vast majority of this contract value came from government agencies, further validating the company’s core technology areas.

Financially, operating losses deepened as Aeluma continued to scale its workforce and invest in business and production infrastructure. Cash burn remained contained due to capital raised from a recent offering, pushing cash and equivalents to $37.8 million at quarter end.

The company expanded its manufacturing partnerships, forged new relationships with Tower Semiconductor and Sumitomo Chemical Advanced Technologies, and reported a growing intellectual property portfolio with 36 issued and pending patents. Despite these milestones, commercial revenue outside of R&D contracts has yet to materialize in the reported results.

What Aeluma Does

Aeluma develops advanced semiconductor materials that enable high-performance photodetectors and photonic sensors for applications in AI, defense, aerospace, mobile, and quantum technology.
