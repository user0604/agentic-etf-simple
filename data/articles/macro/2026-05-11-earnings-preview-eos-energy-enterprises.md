Earnings Preview: Eos Energy Enterprises

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 3:03 PM

Comments

Eos Energy Enterprises (NASDAQ:EOSE), which makes grid-scale zinc-based battery energy storage systems for utilities and commercial customers, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on Wednesday, May 13, 2026, before the market opens. Investors will be watching closely after a volatile quarter and a year of rapid growth paired with operational challenges.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Eos Energy Enterprises reported revenue of $58 million, which nearly doubled from the previous quarter but missed analyst expectations. The company posted a full-year revenue of $114.2 million, up 632% year over year, but also reported a net loss of $969.6 million, with an adjusted EPS of -$0.84, which was below consensus estimates. While Eos set records for revenue and cash position, it failed to meet its own guidance for the quarter, citing supplier nonperformance, lower-than-expected automation yields, and higher equipment downtime. Despite these setbacks, management highlighted progress in manufacturing automation, product launches, and a strengthened balance sheet. The company ended the year with a $701 million backlog and $624.6 million in cash, removing the going concern language from its filings for the first time.

Positives
Record revenue growth and backlog expansion: Annual revenue grew over sevenfold year over year, and backlog reached $701 million, demonstrating strong demand and commercial traction for Eos’s battery systems.
Improved cash position and financial flexibility: The company ended 2025 with $624.6 million in cash, supported by multiple equity and debt financings, which provides runway for ongoing operations and expansion.
Manufacturing automation milestones achieved: Eos completed its first fully automated battery line and reached 2 gigawatt hours of capacity at its Turtle Creek facility, laying groundwork for future scaling.
Launch of new product and software platform: The Indensity battery module and DawnOS software were introduced, aiming to improve serviceability, energy density, and system control for customers.
Negatives
Missed revenue and earnings guidance: Q4 results fell short of both company guidance and analyst estimates, raising concerns about forecasting reliability and execution risk.
Continued large net losses and negative cash flow: Despite revenue growth, Eos reported a net loss of $969.6 million for the year and used $211.2 million in operating cash, highlighting ongoing profitability challenges.
Operational setbacks in automation and supply chain: Supplier nonperformance, automation quality issues, and high equipment downtime led to lost production and delayed shipments, impacting financial results.
Delayed timeline for gross margin positivity: Management pushed back its target for achieving positive gross margins to the second half of 2026, reflecting slower-than-expected cost improvements and production ramp-up.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Revenue and margin trajectory versus guidance: Investors should monitor whether Eos’s Q1 revenue and margin trends align with its full-year 2026 guidance of $300 million to $400 million in revenue and the path toward gross margin positivity in the second half.
Operational improvements in automation and yields: Watch for updates on manufacturing line uptime, yield rates, and the impact of process changes or supplier remediation on production reliability and cost structure.
Backlog conversion and new order activity: Progress in converting backlog to revenue, as well as any new large project wins or updates on pending deals (such as NYSERDA or international contracts), will be key signals for future growth.
Status of Indensity product rollout and Thornhill expansion: Look for details on initial shipments and customer feedback for the Indensity module, as well as milestones for the second manufacturing line and the Thornhill facility redesign.
Cash burn rate and funding outlook: Given ongoing losses, pay attention to quarterly cash usage and management’s comments on liquidity, capital needs, and compliance with debt covenants as Eos continues scaling.
