Key Takeaways
Ralph Lauren posted a decisive business win with robust sales and expanding margins.
Earnings per share rose 20.7% year over year in the quarter.
Management guided for continued growth, but at a more moderate pace for fiscal 2027.

Ralph Lauren (NYSE:RL) closed fiscal 2026 with a strong finish, as demand remained healthy across all regions and direct-to-consumer channels. The global leader in premium apparel and lifestyle brands expanded its customer base and increased average selling prices, while navigating higher tariffs and increased investments. In its earnings release dated May 21, 2026, for the fourth quarter and full year, the company reported improved top and bottom line results and announced a dividend increase. Overall, the quarter capped a successful year, with a continued focus on profitability and brand strength amid selective cost pressures.

Notable developments
Revenue increased 17% over the prior year, with Asia segment sales up 31% for the period.
Profitability improved as gross margin reached 69.7%, supported by strong full-price sales and lower cotton costs.
The board raised the quarterly dividend by 10%, bringing the annual payout to $4.00 per share.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects revenue to grow 4% to 5% in constant currency for fiscal 2027, with operating margin projected to expand by 0.4 to 0.6 percentage points. The strongest margin gains are anticipated in the first half of the year as tariff impacts lessen. Investors should monitor the company’s ability to maintain price discipline and margin improvements as growth moderates.

Quarterly highlights

Direct-to-consumer comparable sales rose 17% in the quarter, and Asia segment revenue climbed 31%. These gains were driven by new customer additions, expanded digital engagement, and product launches such as the Olympic Team USA line and limited-edition MLB collections.

The company prioritized full-price selling and managed supply chain costs, resulting in year-over-year expansion of both gross and operating margins. Efforts to improve product mix and customer experience also contributed to profitability.

Ralph Lauren returned over $700 million to shareholders through dividends and buybacks, highlighted by a 10% dividend increase. Inventory growth remained in line with sales, and capital expenditures nearly doubled as the company invested in technology and omni-channel retail upgrades.

What Ralph Lauren does

Ralph Lauren designs, markets, and sells premium apparel, footwear, accessories, fragrances, and home products under a portfolio of well-known luxury brands.
