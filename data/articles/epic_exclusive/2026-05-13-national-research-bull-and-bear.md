National Research Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 7:06 AM

Comments

National Research Corporation (NASDAQ:NRC) provides analytics and technology solutions to healthcare organizations, focusing on experience measurement, enablement tools, and strategic governance. The company's growth strategy centers on scaling recurring software and insights subscriptions, leveraging product innovation—including artificial intelligence (AI)—and deepening strategic relationships with large U.S. health systems. With nearly all revenue recurring, NRC emphasizes predictability, operational leverage, and long-term partnership with healthcare clients seeking data-driven improvement.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Sustained Recurring Contract Growth Reignites Top-Line Expansion

Momentum in total recurring contract value (TRCV) has shifted NRC back to revenue growth, offering visibility and compounding potential. After five consecutive quarters of sequential TRCV increases, the company reached a record $144 million TRCV in Q4 2025—an 8% year-over-year rise—culminating in NRC’s first quarter of year-over-year revenue growth since 2023, as seen in Q1 2026. With 99% of revenue from recurring contracts, this performance underpins reliable future revenue and reduces volatility. Management expects this TRCV run-rate to drive consistent revenue growth and margin expansion through 2026, mapping directly to investor confidence in forward financial performance. The combination of durable demand, high gross dollar retention, and strong new sales signals a foundation for multi-year growth.

2. Sales Reorganization Drives Explosive New Logo and Expansion Sales

Transformational change in the sales approach is rapidly expanding NRC’s customer base and deepening wallet share with existing clients. In full-year 2025, new sales surged by 86% versus the prior year, a direct result of realigning the sales organization to reflect the buying patterns of complex healthcare systems. By equipping the team to engage across decision-maker levels and target larger, more strategic deals, NRC is securing record customer wins and expanding cross-sell opportunities. This structure is designed to remain a growth tailwind through 2026, supporting both new logo acquisition and greater product penetration. As growth compounds within a market already including 74 of the top 100 health systems, NRC is positioned to sustain industry leadership while expanding its addressable market.

3. Enablement Product Innovation and AI Integration Unlock New Revenue Streams

Ongoing investment in platform expansion and AI-driven tools is broadening NRC’s value proposition, leading to new contract wins and more diversified growth. The integration and scaling of enablement solutions, especially the Rounding platform, fueled a near doubling of Rounding’s TRCV in 2025 alone. Management has identified deepening adoption of these workflow tools—now infused with AI-driven sentiment analysis and listening capabilities—as a top catalyst for 2026 and beyond. Healthcare providers show strong demand for data-driven operational improvement, and NRC’s innovation positions it as a differentiated partner. As AI features become essential to health system performance and ROI measurement, NRC is likely to further cement its relevance, unlock new use cases, and cross-sell more effectively across its client base.

4. Operational Discipline Delivers Margin Resilience and Cash Flow Strength

Consistent margin management in the face of revenue volatility underlines NRC’s financial quality and ability to fund both growth and capital returns. In full-year 2025, the company delivered an adjusted EBITDA margin of 29% and generated $40.2 million in adjusted EBITDA, despite a 4% drop in annual revenue. Even with higher Q4 expenses from the timing of NRC’s annual customer conference, management held margins steady and continued investing in talent and technology. The emphasis on converting earnings to free cash flow—supported by high recurring revenue—enables ongoing dividend payments, opportunistic buybacks, and future innovation investments. As revenue growth returns, management projects margin expansion, pointing to operational leverage and disciplined cost structure as competitive advantages for the coming years.

5. Deep Customer Partnerships and Thought Leadership Create High Switching Costs

An entrenched position among the largest U.S. health systems, combined with industry-leading customer satisfaction, forms a formidable competitive moat. NRC now serves 74% of the top 100 health systems in the country, a position achieved through decades of specialized healthcare analytics expertise and reinforced by high-trust certifications for data security and compliance. With a Net Promoter Score of 68 and a differentiated customer success model, NRC’s solutions have become foundational to these clients’ operations. Strategic offerings such as those through The Governance Institute are not just tools, but embedded drivers of organizational decision-making and improvement. These deep relationships, multi-product integrations, and demonstrated ROI create substantial barriers to exit for customers, supporting high renewal rates and helping NRC weather even challenging healthcare cycles.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Lumpy Revenue Recognition Lags Underlying Contract Growth and Delays Profit Upside

Revenue recovery may trail TRCV growth due to lag effects from prior contract losses and the timing of new deal onboarding, potentially disappointing near-term expectations. Although NRC posted record TRCV and returned to year-over-year revenue growth in Q1 2026, revenue actually declined 4% over full-year 2025 and was still recovering from elevated contract attrition in 2024. Because recognized revenue lags changes in TRCV, especially during periods of large customer churn or multi-quarter onboarding, investors could see uneven quarterly results. If the current expansion in contract value fails to translate into sustained, robust revenue and margin growth as quickly as projected, sentiment and valuation may suffer—even in the face of seemingly strong sales pipeline data.

2. Competitive Pressures and Healthcare Sector Headwinds Could Erode NRC’s Moat

Rapid innovation in healthcare IT, emerging competitors, and persistent uncertainty facing U.S. health systems may challenge NRC’s market dominance and pricing power. While NRC highlights its relationships with major health systems and high recurring revenue, the healthcare analytics and enablement landscape is evolving fast, with rivals targeting similar customer bases using advanced AI and integrated workflow products. Meanwhile, hospital and system clients are navigating budgetary strain, regulatory changes, and shifting care delivery models—all of which can lead to compressed vendor spending. If competitors outpace NRC’s rate of innovation or if clients re-evaluate spending to trim costs, NRC may face slower new sales, increased churn, or pricing concessions, narrowing its moat and dampening long-term growth prospects.

3. Overreliance on Non-GAAP Metrics Obscures True Earnings Power and Financial Risks

Heavy use of adjusted (non-GAAP) profitability measures may obscure underlying volatility or one-time impacts, potentially misleading investors about true operating performance. NRC customarily reports results using adjusted EBITDA and adjusted net income, excluding certain non-cash and non-recurring items. While this offers a clear view of normalized operations, it can also mask swings in actual GAAP profitability—especially during times of expense spikes or revenue headwinds. For instance, Q4 2025 saw elevated costs due to conference timing, but the absence of unadjusted results in filings may hinder a full understanding of cost structure and free cash flow conversion. A sustained gap between adjusted and GAAP results, or unexpected adjustments related to investments or customer attrition, could prompt investor scrutiny and impact valuation multiples.
