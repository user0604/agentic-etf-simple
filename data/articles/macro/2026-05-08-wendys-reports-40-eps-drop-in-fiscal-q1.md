Wendy's Reports 40% EPS Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 7:16 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Overall results reflect an early-stage turnaround, with ongoing U.S. sales pressure offset by growth internationally.
Adjusted earnings per share fell 40.0% compared to the prior year period.
Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook, emphasizing expected sequential improvement and continued strategic initiatives.

Wendy’s (NASDAQ:WEN) reported continued challenges in its core U.S. business as sales and profits declined despite steps to revitalize its brand and operations. The leading U.S. quick-service restaurant chain, known for its made-to-order hamburgers and Frosty desserts, saw its margin and cash flow pressured by falling traffic, rising costs, and a wave of domestic restaurant closures. The company released first-quarter results for the period ending March 29, 2026, on May 8, 2026, showing that early progress from its strategic initiatives has not yet translated into improved financial performance. Overall, the quarter highlighted mixed progress with international strength offset by weak U.S. operations and profitability declines.

Notable Developments
U.S. systemwide sales fell 7.3%, reflecting a 7.8% decline in same-restaurant sales and the closure of 164 domestic restaurants.
International operations remained resilient, growing systemwide sales by 6.0% and adding 18 new restaurants abroad during the period.
Quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.14 per share, but the company paused share repurchases as free cash flow nearly halved year over year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its fiscal 2026 guidance, calling for flat global sales, $460–480 million in adjusted EBITDA, and $0.56–0.60 in adjusted earnings per share. The company emphasized that its turnaround plan, Project Fresh, is on track, but acknowledged improvement in U.S. traffic and margins is critical. Investors should watch the pace of U.S. sales stabilization and progress on store optimization as signals for a broader recovery in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

System optimization continued rapidly, as Wendy’s closed 164 U.S. restaurants, reflecting efforts to shutter underperforming locations and improve franchisee economics. While this may benefit long-term profitability, it weighed on both sales and royalty revenue for the quarter. The U.S. restaurant count declined to 5,805, down from 5,958 in the prior year period.

International expansion provided a rare bright spot, as systemwide sales climbed to $518 million and 18 additional restaurants opened outside the U.S. International same-restaurant sales dipped slightly, but the region’s overall growth counterbalanced U.S. weakness. A new franchise agreement to expand further in China signals a long-term growth pipeline.

The period saw margin pressure driven by commodity inflation, higher wages, and continued sales declines. The company-operated restaurant margin in the U.S. dropped by 3.4 percentage points to 11.4%, while free cash flow fell 46.3% as cash from operations shrank and capital spending remained substantial.

What Wendy's Does

Wendy’s operates and franchises quick-service restaurants globally, specializing in hamburgers, chicken sandwiches, salads, and signature frozen desserts.
