StandardAero Posts 13% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
StandardAero posted solid revenue and earnings growth but faced downward pressure on margins and cash flow.
Adjusted earnings per share rose year over year.
Management lifted full-year 2026 guidance for revenue, profits, and adjusted EPS.

StandardAero (NYSE:SARO) posted strong top-line and earnings growth as demand remained firm across its major markets, though profit margins and cash flow came under pressure. The global aerospace service provider grew revenue in its Engine Services and Component Repair Services businesses, with momentum in commercial, business aviation, and military end markets. Reporting for the first quarter ended March 31, 2026 (release date: May 7, 2026), the company raised its full-year guidance even as margin softness and working capital outflows stood out as key areas to monitor. Overall, the quarter showed solid demand trends but highlighted the need for improvement in profitability and cash conversion.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 13.3% from the prior year, led by growth in both Engine Services and Component Repair Services, with business aviation revenue up nearly 20%.
Despite higher profits, overall profit margin fell to 12.5% from 13.8% as Engine Services investment and production mix weighed on results in the period.
StandardAero announced the acquisition of Unified Turbines and repurchased two million shares for $60.1 million, reflecting continued strategic and capital allocation activity.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management increased its full-year 2026 financial targets, now seeing revenue of $6,325 to $6,450 million and adjusted EPS between $1.40 and $1.50. Leadership cited "robust demand" behind the raised outlook but highlighted ongoing margin and cash flow headwinds. Investors should watch for improvement in profit margins and cash flow, as successful absorption of working capital and ramping Engine Services production will be critical in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Engine Services revenue rose 14.1% to $1,447.1 million as commercial, military, and business jet customers drove growth, but segment Adjusted EBITDA margin dropped to 12.3% from 13.7% as investments in LEAP and CFM56 engine platforms pulled down profitability. Management previously flagged these engine program ramps as causes for temporary margin dilution, expecting margins to improve in the second half of 2026.

Component Repair Services revenue increased 7.4% to $179.7 million, and the segment achieved a margin lift to 29.2%, benefiting from stronger pricing and operational improvements. The company noted the acquisition of Unified Turbines should further enhance repair capabilities and segment profitability.

Free cash flow, the cash left after operating and investing activities, showed a significant outflow ($133.7 million) as accounts receivable and contract assets rose, reflecting the seasonal pattern of cash use in the first quarter. Although the company described these working capital movements as normal for the period, the magnitude suggests further monitoring is needed through the rest of the year.

What StandardAero Does

StandardAero is a global provider of maintenance, repair, and overhaul services for aircraft engines and components, serving commercial, business aviation, and defense sectors.
