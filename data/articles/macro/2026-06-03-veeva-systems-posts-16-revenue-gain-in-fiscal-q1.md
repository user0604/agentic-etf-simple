Key Takeaways
Veeva Systems reported broad-based growth and exceeded its own outlook on all major metrics.
Non-GAAP earnings per share increased 13% from the previous year.
Management forecasts continued double-digit growth but at a slower rate for the rest of fiscal 2027.

Cloud software company Veeva Systems (NYSE:VEEV) delivered a strong quarter, surpassing its own forecast for revenue and earnings as adoption of its AI-powered tools and Vault CRM platform accelerated. The leading provider of software solutions for the life sciences industry saw balanced growth in both its Commercial and R&D business areas during the three months ended April 30, 2026 (first quarter of fiscal 2027, as announced on June 3, 2026). Overall, the quarter showed robust execution, though with signs of slowing growth and some margin compression.

Notable Developments
Subscription revenue grew 15%, helped by strong demand for R&D and Quality Solutions, which rose 19% year over year.
Non-GAAP operating margin declined 1.3 percentage points from the prior year, reflecting heavier investment in new products and services.
Veeva added 27 new Vault CRM customers in the quarter, and now supports more than 150 live CRM clients across the life sciences sector.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year revenue and profit guidance for fiscal 2027, expecting revenue between $3.635 billion and $3.645 billion and non-GAAP EPS of approximately $9.05. The outlook calls for continued double-digit growth but at a slower pace than in the recent quarter. Investors should monitor progress in AI product rollouts and the migration of clients to Vault CRM, as both are crucial to sustaining momentum.

Quarterly Highlights

Growth in R&D and Quality Solutions stood out in the period, as subscription revenue from this segment rose nearly twice as fast as Commercial Solutions. This reflects ongoing customer demand for digital tools that help pharmaceutical companies manage research and regulatory tasks more efficiently.

Professional services revenue climbed 23% from a year ago, as Veeva supported more migrations and system integrations for clients. While this expansion boosted overall revenue, it contributed to lower gross margin, as services carry higher costs than pure software subscriptions.

The company continued to invest heavily in artificial intelligence, integrating its March acquisition Ostro into the core product lineup and preparing new offerings like Vault AI and Veeva Falcon for launch later in the year. These efforts aim to deepen automation and analytics across life sciences operations.

What Veeva Systems Does

Veeva Systems provides cloud-based software and data solutions designed specifically for companies in the life sciences industry, helping them streamline clinical research, regulatory compliance, and commercial operations.
