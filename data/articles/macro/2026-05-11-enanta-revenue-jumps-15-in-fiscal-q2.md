Enanta Revenue Jumps 15% in Fiscal Q2

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 4:17 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Enanta Pharmaceuticals reported a narrower quarterly loss as operating expenses sharply declined.
Revenue increased 15.0%, driven by higher royalties compared to the prior-year quarter.
Management reaffirmed a cash runway into fiscal 2029 and remains focused on early-stage pipeline development.

Enanta Pharmaceuticals (NASDAQ:ENTA) saw modest progress this quarter, reducing its net loss as it trimmed costs while pushing forward its research priorities. The biopharmaceutical company specializes in developing antiviral and immunology medicines, and remains heavily reliant on royalty income from hepatitis C virus (HCV) therapies licensed to AbbVie (NYSE:ABBV). In its latest earnings announcement, released on May 11, 2026 (covering its fiscal second quarter), results reflected ongoing operational discipline and incremental pipeline milestones. Overall, the quarter delivered improving financials even as near-term growth drivers remain limited by pipeline maturity.

Notable developments
Revenue grew 15.0% year over year thanks to higher royalty payments on MAVYRET/MAVIRET sales, a product marketed by AbbVie for hepatitis C.
Operating expenses dropped 26%, with research and development spending declining 30.9% following less activity and cost in certain clinical trials.
Enanta initiated dosing in its first-in-human trial for EDP-978, a KIT inhibitor for chronic urticaria, and continues to move multiple drug programs forward.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide new financial or milestone guidance for the year but restated its expectation that existing cash and incoming royalties should fund operations into fiscal 2029. The main factor for investors to monitor is clinical pipeline advancement, especially updates on pivotal RSV (respiratory syncytial virus) studies and the initial clinical data for its immunology candidates.

Quarterly highlights

The period’s revenue was entirely derived from royalties on AbbVie’s hepatitis C therapies, and the increase resulted from improved product sales volumes. Management noted these royalties remain the company’s main source of income, exposing Enanta to product lifecycle risks until its own drug candidates reach market.

On the cost side, both research and general administrative expenses fell sharply. The biggest savings came from reduced clinical trial spending in Enanta’s RSV program, which offset growing investment in earlier-stage immunology assets.

Pipeline progress centered on clinical development of zelicapavir (targeting RSV) and first-in-human dosing for EDP-978, a molecular inhibitor aiming to treat chronic hives. Important updates on RSV registration strategy and immunology readouts are expected by late 2026.

What Enanta Pharmaceuticals does

Enanta Pharmaceuticals develops small molecule drugs to treat viral and immune-mediated diseases, with a portfolio spanning clinical programs for respiratory infections and early-stage therapies for inflammation.

Consider reviewing upcoming milestones and clinical trial updates if you are tracking Enanta Pharmaceuticals for developments in antivirals or immunology innovation.
