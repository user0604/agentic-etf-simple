Enbridge Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 3:09 PM

Comments

Enbridge Inc. (NYSE:ENB) is a leading North American energy infrastructure company that operates a diversified portfolio spanning liquids pipelines, natural gas transmission, gas distribution & storage, and renewables. The company’s strategic focus is on leveraging regulated and long-term contracted assets to deliver predictable cash flows, while steadily expanding its footprint in both traditional hydrocarbons and high-growth areas like liquefied natural gas (LNG), data center power, and renewable energy. With a record $40 billion growth backlog and a 31-year streak of dividend increases, Enbridge aims to balance reliable income with multi-decade capital deployment opportunities.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Predictable, Regulated Cash Flows Anchor Consistent Dividend Growth

Stability from regulated and long-term contracted assets underpins income visibility and payouts. Enbridge’s revenue base derives primarily from regulated or long-duration contracted infrastructure across Canada and the U.S, which insulates it from commodity price swings and demand shocks. As of Q1 2026, the company maintained a payout ratio within its 60–70% distributable cash flow (DCF) target, and reaffirmed over $40 billion in projected dividends for the next five years. This consistent free cash flow, validated by 31 consecutive years of dividend hikes, offers rare resilience among energy majors and allows investors to benefit from sector-leading yield and long-term portfolio stability, even as the energy landscape shifts.

2. Multi-Billion-Dollar Growth Backlog Extends High-Confidence Earnings Trajectory

A $40 billion secured capital projects pipeline is set to drive earnings and dividend growth through the next decade. Enbridge reported its largest-ever secured project backlog in Q1 2026, up nearly 35% since the previous year and now extending through 2033. This visibility comes from projects in all core segments, including $4 billion in liquids pipelines, $4 billion in gas transmission, and around $3 billion annually in gas utilities and renewables. Management forecasts 5% compound annual growth in adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) and DCF through 2030, supported by $8 billion of new projects entering service in 2026. This has already led to increases in annual investment capacity—projected at $10–11 billion per year—suggesting robust “self-funded” organic growth and expanding future returns.

3. Natural Gas and Utility Segments Offset Volatile Liquids Earnings and Capture Secular Demand

Rising gas transmission and gas utility profits stabilize results and capitalize on structural energy trends. In Q1 2026, Enbridge posted strong operational results as gas transmission and utilities offset softer liquid pipeline earnings, evidencing the benefit of business line diversification. Key systems like Texas Eastern hit record throughput (15 billion cubic feet per day in January 2026), with 100% contract renewal rates on major pipelines. New project approvals, such as the CAD 4 billion ($2.9 billion) Sunrise Expansion on the Westcoast system, fortify long-term regulated income. Enbridge’s U.S. gas utility rate base is now growing near 10% annually, capturing both organic customer growth and rapid power sector demand—especially relevant given surging data center and LNG expansions.

4. Strategic Positioning to Power AI and Data Center Growth Through Energy Transition

Emerging energy demand from artificial intelligence (AI) and data centers is expanding Enbridge’s opportunity set. With large pipeline and utility networks in key North American markets, Enbridge is directly benefitting from the explosive demand for natural gas and electricity driven by hyperscale technology clients. The company has secured $3 billion in renewables capital allocations for data center projects—highlighting partnerships with firms like Meta Inc.—and is advancing over 50 gas and power projects to serve data center loads. This strategic alignment ensures relevance as energy transition accelerates, as both traditional gas and renewables infrastructure are essential for next-generation computing and AI, making Enbridge critical to the modern digital economy.

5. Robust Regulatory Relationships and Project Permitting De-risk Future Expansion

Track record of securing regulatory approvals mitigates execution risk and supports long-term returns. Recent successes—such as federal approval for the Sunrise Expansion and favorable U.S. rate case settlements—demonstrate Enbridge’s ability to navigate complex regulatory environments. The company’s diversified North American footprint and ongoing engagement with regional governments help sustain constructive relationships, which are critical for large-scale, multi-year infrastructure deployment. Progress on contentious issues, like the Line 5 court victories and established long-term storage contracts, further enhances cash flow certainty. As policy and environmental scrutiny intensifies across energy infrastructure, established permitting and stakeholder trust provide Enbridge with a competitive edge to execute its project pipeline.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Regulatory and Legal Headwinds Pose Material Asset Disruption Risk

Persistent litigation and regulatory uncertainty threaten operations, especially key pipeline assets. Ongoing lawsuits in Michigan and Wisconsin over the Line 5 pipeline highlight how regional political risks, environmental activism, and evolving legal frameworks can jeopardize existing asset continuity. An adverse court ruling or political intervention could force costly rerouting or even asset shutdowns, impacting throughput and cash flows. While the company recently won a major federal case, the legal fight is far from settled—and similar risks exist on other controversial expansion projects. Material asset disruption would not only undermine income but could force delays or expensive mitigations on strategically-important pipelines.

2. Elevated Leverage and Rising Interest Rates Weigh on Financial Flexibility

Running at the high end of the debt-to-EBITDA range constrains options if interest costs rise or cash flow growth underperforms. Enbridge operates near its maximum targeted leverage, with a debt-to-adjusted EBITDA ratio of 4.8x at the end of 2025, hovering between 4.5-5.0x. In a higher interest rate environment, escalating debt service costs could erode coverage ratios and limit capital allocation flexibility. If cash flows flatten due to regulatory or utilization setbacks, the company may be forced to choose between dividend growth and funding its large capex backlog, or may become more reliant on asset sales and external financing, dampening shareholder returns.

3. Competition and Energy Transition Could Diminish Long-Term Growth Potential

Secular shifts toward renewables and aggressive moves by peer midstream operators threaten growth in core businesses. While Enbridge has made significant investments in renewables, the bulk of its earnings are still tied to hydrocarbons. Rising competition from independent power producers and other pipeline operators—alongside policy preferences for green projects—could squeeze project returns and reduce pipeline utilization rates over time. Any successful revival of alternative oil routing projects, or an accelerated push for decarbonization, could leave portions of Enbridge’s asset base underutilized or stranded. This could ultimately weaken the long-term earnings outlook, especially if the pace of energy transition outstrips the company's ability to adapt its asset mix and secure new growth avenues.
