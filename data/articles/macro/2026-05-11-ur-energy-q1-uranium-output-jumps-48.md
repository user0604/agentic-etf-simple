Ur-Energy Q1 Uranium Output Jumps 48%

The Motley Fool Team

Comments
Key Takeaways
The company made progress on ramping up uranium production and officially started up its Shirley Basin project.
Captured uranium volumes jumped 48% year over year in the quarter.
Most sales and revenue are expected to occur in the second half of the year as contract deliveries ramp up.

Uranium miner Ur-Energy (NYSEMKT:URG) advanced its production expansion plans with improved plant performance and the first operational steps at Shirley Basin. The company specializes in in-situ recovery (ISR) uranium production, using chemical solutions to extract uranium from underground rock formations with lower costs and less surface disruption than conventional mining. In its earnings released May 11, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, the company showed continued operational progress and set the stage for a busy remainder of the year. Overall, the quarter highlighted stronger uranium output and cost improvement, while deferring most revenue until scheduled deliveries later in 2026.

Notable Developments
Pounds of uranium captured climbed to 110,314, a 48% increase from a year earlier, as plant upgrades improved flow rates and efficiency.
The Shirley Basin project began operations in April, preparing its first shipments of uranium-loaded resin subject to regulatory approval.
Unrestricted cash levels reached $122.8 million, supporting project ramp-up and acting as a financial buffer as most deliveries are planned for later in the year.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management maintained its full-year goal of delivering 1.3 million pounds of uranium to customers with sales concentrated in later quarters. They highlighted continued optimization at Lost Creek and the ramp-up of Shirley Basin, with the main risk being timely regulatory approval and successful scaling of production. The single most important factor for investors to watch is the company’s ability to increase output and complete contract deliveries on schedule in the second half of fiscal 2026.

Quarterly Highlights

The reported quarter featured a sharp jump in uranium captured, with the Lost Creek facility capturing its highest amounts since the 2023 production ramp decision, following improvements and repairs made late last year. Average realized sales prices rose compared to the previous quarter, mostly due to uranium sales contracts that include more favorable pricing—from both fixed and market-linked agreements negotiated during stronger uranium markets.

Cash costs per pound sold fell to $37.51, down from the prior quarter, reflecting operational efficiencies. Gross product margin per pound increased, signaling better profitability on future contract deliveries, though only 55,000 pounds were sold in the first quarter due to the back-loaded nature of the sales schedule.

The Shirley Basin project crossed a major milestone by commencing initial operations in April. Construction and wellfield development are largely complete, and first commercial resin shipments to Lost Creek are expected this summer—contingent on regulatory sign-off.

What Ur-Energy Does

Ur-Energy mines uranium using low-cost in-situ recovery (ISR) techniques in Wyoming, supplying fuel for nuclear power utilities through multi-year sales agreements and other negotiated contracts.
