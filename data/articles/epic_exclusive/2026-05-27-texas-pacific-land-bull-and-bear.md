Texas Pacific Land Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 11:27 AM

Comments

Texas Pacific Land (NYSE:TPL) is one of the largest landowners in Texas, monetizing a vast portfolio of surface, mineral, and water rights in the heart of the Permian Basin. The company’s strategy leverages recurring royalty income from oil and gas, expanding water management services, and surface development linked to energy transition and digital infrastructure. TPL is increasingly focused on growth from new verticals—including data center and power campus development—while maintaining financial discipline and a resilient business model.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Permian Royalty Growth Enables High-Margin, Scalable Cash Flow Expansion

Strong volume growth and stable operator activity provide a foundation for robust future royalty income. In the first quarter of 2026, Texas Pacific Land Corporation grew oil and gas royalty production to 37,001 barrels of oil equivalent per day, achieving a 19% year-over-year increase. This growth highlights the company's leverage to ongoing drilling and completion activity in the Permian Basin, where large operators such as Occidental (NYSE:OXY), BP (NYSE:BP), Devon (NYSE:DVN), and Exxon (NYSE:XOM) continue investing in high-productivity acreage. Unlike exploration and production companies, TPL’s royalty model scales with operator investment while maintaining high operating margins. This sets the stage for compounding cash flows—especially as incremental wells come online and commodity prices remain elevated.

2. Full Commodity Price Exposure Delivers Powerful Upside in Elevated Price Environments

The company’s unhedged model allows direct participation in commodity price rallies, rapidly boosting revenue and cash flow in supportive markets. For fiscal year 2025, every $10 increase in realized oil prices adds roughly $50 million to revenue, illustrating Texas Pacific Land’s high sensitivity to favorable macro trends. With the company realizing $65 per barrel on oil in 2025 and currently benefiting from a global supply shock and surging crude prices, there is meaningful short-term upside potential. This pure play on Permian output distinguishes TPL from peers who often hedge, allowing investors to capitalize fully on oil and natural gas price swings during cyclical upturns.

3. Water Management and Desalination Verticals Offer Major New Value Streams

Expansion into water sales, produced water handling, and innovative desalination creates additional engines for growth and long-term competitive differentiation. In Q1 2026, TPL reported its second-best quarter ever for water sales and produced water royalties, underscoring the potential for sustained, high-volume activity. With large ongoing completion activity and an R&D-scale desalination plant soon operational, Texas Pacific Land is positioned to capture growing demand for both conventional and recycled water in the Permian—a critical, water-stressed region. If the pilot 10,000 barrels-per-day desalination project demonstrates economic viability, scalable commercial facilities could multiply revenue opportunities, especially as data centers and power campuses seek sustainable water and cooling solutions.

4. Data Center and Power Campus Partnerships Unlock Multiyear, Annuity-Like Revenues

Strategic deals with data center operators and power generators use TPL’s diverse asset base to secure steady, long-term returns beyond oil and gas. Texas Pacific Land recently closed its first land and water deal linked to a gas power campus, with a $43 million land component paid annually over 20 years. The company is now fielding strong interest from hyperscalers and artificial intelligence labs, which need large, strategically located sites for next-generation infrastructure. These projects—often involving tens of billions in capital—benefit from TPL’s ability to offer land, water, and aggregates under a single umbrella. As more data centers and power projects attain final investment decisions, these partnerships could provide durable, recurring revenue for decades, reducing cyclicality and increasing valuation multiples.

5. Fortress Balance Sheet Supports Both Growth Investment and Capital Returns

Prudent financial management and a strong net cash position give the company flexibility to fund next-generation projects and return capital to shareholders. Despite recent commodity price declines, Texas Pacific Land has maintained a robust balance sheet and did not require hedging or emergency capital measures. In Q1 2026, consolidated revenue hit an all-time quarterly high of $237 million (up 21% year over year, GAAP), with $181 million adjusted EBITDA and $136 million free cash flow. This enviable cash-generation profile allows the company to invest in R&D such as desalination and embedded technology partnerships while preserving optionality to increase dividends, repurchase shares, or pursue accretive acquisitions if attractive opportunities arise. The entry of major shareholders like Horizon Kinetics to the board further strengthens governance and strategic decision-making.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Overreliance on Commodity Prices Increases Downside Risk During Volatility

Unhedged commodity exposure leaves revenues and earnings vulnerable to oil and gas price downturns. While TPL’s direct exposure to commodity price rallies is positive in a bull market, it also means that any sharp reversal in crude or natural gas prices would quickly erode both top-line revenue and cash flow. The company does not hedge production, and management has emphasized this strategy. If global supply shocks prove transient or if demand weakens, earnings could rapidly drop—especially given that every $10 per barrel decline in realized oil price translates to a $50 million revenue hit (using recent royalty volumes). Such cyclicality could affect capital spending plans or investor returns in weaker periods.

2. Growth Initiatives in Water and Data Centers Carry Execution and Capital Risk

The path to value from desalination and data center partnerships is unproven, with significant operational, commercial, and financial uncertainties. TPL’s push into produced water desalination, power generation, and data center colocation requires not only technical success but also competitive scale-up and efficient capital deployment. The nearly complete 10,000-barrel-per-day desalination pilot must prove 24/7 reliability and economic viability before any full-scale commercial rollout—with larger facilities potentially requiring $100 million or more in capex, possibly through third-party partnerships. Data center and power campus negotiations involve long timelines, bespoke structures, and risk of delay or cancellation. Failure to execute or realize meaningful returns on these ambitious projects could dampen future growth—and tie up capital that might otherwise be deployed for shareholder returns.

3. Lumpy and Nonrecurring Surface Revenues May Obscure Underlying Trends

Dependence on episodic, nonrecurring SLEM (Surface, Lease, Easement & Material) transactions can create misleadingly strong quarters that may not persist. In Q1 2026, TPL entered into an agreement to sell a land parcel for $43 million, structured as annual payments over 20 years, together with a separate commercial agreement to supply water for the same development. Management acknowledges that SLEM results are highly volatile due to the timing of infrastructure projects like pipelines, often showing big swings quarter to quarter. Overemphasis on outsized SLEM contributions can mask multiperiod fundamentals and exaggerate sustainable earnings power in analyst models or investor expectations. If big deals dry up or shift out in timing, sequential comparisons could disappoint and lead to stock volatility, particularly if recurring royalty or water revenues do not offset these gaps.
