Notable Developments
Revenue fell 22%, driven largely by order delays in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) tied to ongoing geopolitical conflict.
Gross margin expanded 6 percentage points year over year, reflecting a higher share of software and services in the total revenue.
The board began a formal strategic alternatives review after concluding that current market value may not reflect the platform’s strength.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not outline formal financial guidance, citing uncertainty in both government funding and international markets. The most important thing for investors to monitor is the outcome of the company’s strategic review, which may include options such as partnerships, business combinations, or a possible sale.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue contracted compared to the prior year, as some orders in key international regions were either delayed or lost due to external political turmoil. Although sequential revenue rose 8% from the previous quarter’s low, management attributed this to a rebound after the U.S. government shutdown disrupted the prior period.

Profitability improved internally, with gross margin rising to 53% as software and services made up a larger part of total sales. This strategic shift away from hardware also coincided with a reduction in operating expenses, which dropped by nearly a third year over year and helped the adjusted EBITDA loss narrow.

Customer retention remained a challenge. The annualized contract value (ACV) of renewable software fell 13% year over year, while key customer renewal metrics weakened further. While the backlog of deals and recent bookings growth suggest potential for a modest recovery, the business continues to depend heavily on government budgets and faces ongoing volatility.
