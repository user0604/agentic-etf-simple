TJX Companies (NYSE:TJX) delivered a strong quarter as customer demand and efficient inventory management drove sales beyond its initial outlook. The leading off-price retailer operates banners like T.J. Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, and international stores, offering brand-name merchandise at steep discounts. The company announced first-quarter results for fiscal 2027 on May 20, 2026, showing robust top-line and profit growth with improved performance across all operating segments. Overall, the quarter saw broad-based gains and an optimistic revised outlook for the full fiscal year.

Notable Developments
Sales rose 9% to $14.3 billion, with every business segment showing positive comparable sales growth.
Pretax profit margin improved by 1.7 percentage points to 12.0%, helped by higher merchandise margins and efficient expense control.
The quarterly dividend was increased to $0.48 per share, and management raised its full-year guidance for sales, profit margin, and share repurchases.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year outlook after the quarter, now targeting 3% to 4% comparable sales growth and pretax profit margin of 11.9% to 12.0% for fiscal 2027. Despite the positive start, it flagged higher fuel costs and wage pressures as challenges that could impact future quarters. Investors should watch for changes in inventory levels and merchandise margins, as these will be key to sustaining growth and profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Sales growth exceeded internal targets across all banners, with HomeGoods and TJX Canada both posting double-digit net sales gains and strong customer traffic. The company attributed margin improvement to higher merchandise margins, fuel hedge benefits, and disciplined control of selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) expenses.

Inventory per store increased 7% year over year, reflecting the company’s ability to secure ample branded goods during a period of competitive vendor supply. Management noted this strategy as a positive for product freshness but acknowledged the need to manage inventory carefully if sales growth slows.

Cash flow from operations surged to $1.1 billion, supporting increased returns to shareholders through dividends and stock buybacks. Store expansion remained on track with a net addition of 48 stores during the quarter, bringing the total count to 5,262 locations worldwide.
