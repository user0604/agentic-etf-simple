Key Takeaways
Net sales and adjusted earnings per share beat internal guidance, but underlying performance declined year over year.

Bath & Body Works (NYSE:BBWI) delivered results that surpassed its own guidance but showed continued softness in core sales compared to previous years. The leading specialty retailer of body care, home fragrance, and personal care products released its first-quarter earnings for fiscal 2026 on May 27, 2026, covering the quarter ended May 2. Notable Developments
Adjusted earnings and revenue both came in above company predictions for the quarter, yet both declined compared to last year.
One-time gains from litigation settlements and tax benefits contributed to higher reported net income, masking ongoing operational margin pressures.
International sales grew 9%, while US and Canada in-store sales dropped 4.3%, and digital/direct channel sales slipped 1.5%.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its full-year 2026 guidance, forecasting sales to decline between 2.5% and 4.5%, with earnings per share expected to be slightly down from fiscal 2025. Executives believe benefits from product innovation and new digital launches will build through the remainder of the year and into 2027. Investors should watch for improvements in core category sales, early traction from upgraded product launches, and the impact of leadership changes in the finance team as key signals for progress.

Quarterly Highlights

Sales in the company’s main US and Canada store channel fell, indicating softer in-store demand. Revenue from international channels rose 9%, giving the company a partial buffer as domestic sales struggled.

The launch of Bath & Body Works products on Amazon and continued investment in digital initiatives did not immediately offset lower overall sales, but management cited these as growth priorities for the year ahead. The large difference between adjusted and reported earnings per share comes from non-recurring items: an $88 million pre-tax benefit from payment card litigation and a $62 million tax settlement. Adjusted operating income fell to $151 million from $209 million in the prior year.

Inventory levels at the end of the quarter were $782 million, down from $869 million in the prior year. However, store counts in the US and Canada also contracted slightly as the company continued to optimize its footprint. Free cash flow remained positive, supporting continued investments in its transformation plans.
