1. Down 40%, and No Zen in Sight

"Fingers crossed for a good earnings report this coming week, but I'm not hopeful," Epic member buttersdad wrote of Lululemon (NASDAQ: LULU) last Friday. The crossed fingers didn't help. Lulu just folded into a humbler pose, trimming its full-year outlook to sales down 1% to flat after promising 2% to 4% growth. Shares slipped 10.7% after hours, and with the stock already down 41% in 2026, it's a rough year on the mat. LULU is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers.

Don't blame the quarter: Revenue was up 4% to $2.47 billion, beating estimates, with same-store sales up 1%. The market didn't bail on the rearview mirror; they bailed on the road ahead.
New coach laces up in fall: Ex-Nike (NYSE: NKE) exec Heidi O'Neill takes over as CEO in September, right after Lululemon made peace with cranky founder Chip Wilson.
2. DocuSign's AI Earns Its Keep

DocuSign (NASDAQ: DOCU), recommended in Rule Breakers and on the Team Rule Breakers side of the Stock Advisor scorecard, put its name on a solid fiscal first quarter, beating Wall Street on both the top and bottom lines. Revenue climbed 9% to $830.2 million, and adjusted earnings of $1.09 a share scribbled right past the $0.99 analysts expected.

The robot reading the fine print: In April, Motley Fool analyst Jason Moser pointed out "growing enterprise traction for DocuSign's AI-native strategy." The company's artificial intelligence (AI)-native Intelligent Agreement Management platform is doing real work, now driving nearly 13% of annual recurring revenue (ARR), up from 11% last quarter.
A raise you'd need a microscope for: Management bumped its full-year revenue target to $3.49 billion to $3.5 billion, a whopping $6 million more than before. Shares fell 4% after hours.
3. Samsara's Dashcams Print Cash

Samsara (NYSE: IOT) rolled up to nearly $2 billion in ARR (up 30%) and -- plot twist -- turned a GAAP profit for the third straight quarter. Samsara is a Rule Breakers recommendation. Since joining the scorecard in 2023, shares are up 41%, 38 percentage points behind the S&P 500.

Customers can't stop adding to cart: A whopping 96% of Samsara's $100K+ accounts now run two-plus products, and 70% run three-plus. That stickiness goosed ARR from $1 million-plus customers higher by 62% year over year -- the fourth straight quarter of acceleration. Turns out, once the dashcams move in, they don't leave.
Potholes are a business model now: Samsara's new Operational AI gizmos sniff out overflowing dumpsters, map potholes, and catch transit riders left at the curb -- all from cameras already bolted to the fleet. Recent converts include Hertz (NASDAQ: HTZ) and the entire State of Connecticut.
