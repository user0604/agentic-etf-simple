Canadian National Railway Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 7:07 AM

Comments

Canadian National Railway Company (NYSE:CNI) is a leading North American freight railroad operating a 19,500-mile rail network that connects Canada’s Atlantic and Pacific coasts with the U.S. Midwest and Gulf ports. The company’s core strategy blends disciplined cost management, targeted network investments, and a commercial focus on high-value, diversified commodity and intermodal freight. Its long-term growth approach hinges on leveraging operational efficiency, expanding capacity, and deepening relationships with key resources, agricultural, and industrial customers.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Operational Excellence Enables Sustainable Margin Expansion

Rising productivity and disciplined cost controls are driving better cash flow. In Q1 2026, the company delivered record gains in operational metrics, with car velocity up 6%, train speed up 6%, dwell down 4%, and terminal and enginemen productivity up 12% year over year. These improvements, boosted by technology investments and the ongoing “fast track” terminal initiative that already delivered $40 million in annualized run-rate savings, contributed to improved operational efficiency in Q1 2026. Such efficiency gains directly support higher operating leverage and help insulate profits against soft top-line growth, positioning the company to capitalize on future volume recoveries while preserving industry-leading margins.

2. Robust Free Cash Flow Fuels Shareholder Returns and Future Investment

Strong free cash flow generation supports both capital returns and growth investments. The company posted C$900 million in free cash flow in Q1 2026, up 44% from the previous year, primarily due to lower capital expenditures, improved operating earnings, and asset divestitures. Management signaled confidence in the underlying business by raising the quarterly dividend 3% year over year and repurchasing 6 million shares for $870 million between February and March 2026, all while maintaining a healthy balance sheet with net leverage at 2.7 times. This financial strength allows for continued investment in infrastructure, technology, and network upgrades without compromising the ability to reward shareholders, even amid uncertain macroeconomic backdrops.

3. Diversified Commodity Exposure Offsets Cyclical Weakness and Drives Resilience

A balanced freight profile allows the company to weather sector downturns and capture opportunity. The railroad’s exposure spans grain, potash, natural gas liquids, intermodal, and bulk industrial products, which provides diversification against cyclical downturns in any single segment. In Q1 2026, grain movements set a first-quarter record after Chinese tariffs on canola were reduced and car cycle times improved 15%. Potash and natural gas liquids also grew robustly, more than offsetting ongoing softness in forest products and metals. This mix not only mitigates risk from regional or commodity-specific headwinds, but ensures the company is well-positioned to benefit from shifting trade patterns and resource demand.

4. Strategic Network Investments Secure Long-Term Growth Opportunities

Ongoing capital projects are unlocking future capacity in key corridors. Recent and planned infrastructure upgrades, such as the Edson Sub double-tracking (now two-thirds complete), and initiatives at Prince Rupert and Vancouver ports, expand train throughput and reduce operational bottlenecks. Management plans further investments in Western Canada and new acquisitions (like the 90% stake in Alberta’s Berland Resource Road), positioning the network to capture growth from expanding export and intermodal markets. These projects are expected to drive long-term volume and margin gains, particularly as global trade and North American energy flows evolve over the next several years.

5. Customer-Centric Commercial Initiatives Accelerate Volume Growth

A revamped sales approach is improving both pipeline momentum and realized revenues. Empowering regional sales teams and tightly aligning commercial incentives with operational capabilities drove $100 million in new business wins in Q1 2026, with another $100 million in “line of sight” conversions for Q2. This commercial agility, supported by nearly 200 active growth initiatives and fast turnaround on new opportunities, is expanding the customer base in both core and adjacent markets. Such efforts not only fill incremental network capacity but offer the potential for faster-than-expected volume acceleration, underpinning earnings leverage if macro conditions stabilize or improve.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Revenue Mix and Pricing Pressures Erode Top-Line Growth

Sustained revenue headwinds signal risk of negative operating leverage if trends persist. Despite record volume growth (revenue ton miles up 3% in Q1 2026), overall revenues declined 1% year over year. This shortfall reflected a heavier mix of lower-yield commodities (notably grain) and subdued pricing power in some segments, potentially as forest products and metals remained under pressure due to tariffs and market softness. If the company continues struggling to translate strong volume into revenue or margin gains, it may face negative operating leverage, especially if macro headwinds deepen or pricing remains under pressure through the rest of 2026.

2. External Risks from Regulation, Tariffs, and Macroeconomic Uncertainty Intensify

Trade, regulatory, and economic volatility threaten volume, costs, and strategic positioning. The company remains highly exposed to shifting global trade dynamics, including risks from new or extended tariffs (U.S. or Chinese), evolving North American trade agreements, and ongoing Surface Transportation Board industry reviews (e.g, the Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger). Management acknowledges visibility remains limited and is maintaining conservative guidance, given volatile energy prices and uncertainty in global commodity flows. Any adverse developments in cross-border rules, labor action, or regulatory outcomes could restrict growth, raise costs, or undermine competitive advantages over the medium term.

3. Cost Inflation and Safety Incidents Could Undermine Efficiency Gains

Rising expenses and safety incidents may offset historic productivity improvements. In the first quarter of 2026, incident and accident costs rose by over $30 million year over year, largely due to weather-related disruptions and elevated claims. While car velocity and fuel productivity reached record levels, purchased services and material expenses jumped 9%, and other costs (including snow removal and advisory fees) increased substantially. If cost inflation or safety-related charges persist, or if further labor and regulatory risks materialize, the company’s margin improvement efforts could stall and investor confidence in normalized earnings power may weaken.
