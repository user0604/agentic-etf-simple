Ultralife Posts 6% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 7:40 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Ultralife reported a business loss amid operational disruptions and ongoing weakness in key segments.
Adjusted EBITDA dropped sharply and the company posted a GAAP net loss for the quarter.
Management is focused on backlog conversion and margin improvement.

Ultralife (NASDAQ:ULBI) posted disappointing results for the first quarter as operational challenges and weak Communications Systems sales offset progress in other parts of the business. The designer and manufacturer of advanced batteries and communications systems faced production setbacks and increased expenses, leading to a net loss. The earnings release, issued May 8, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, highlights a period marked by both revenue decline and sharp margin pressure. Overall, the quarter reflected ongoing volatility despite a record sales backlog.

Notable Developments
Production disruptions at Newark and Raynham, including weather and power outages, cut into output and contributed to the net loss.
Communications Systems revenue fell 25.7% year over year, with gross margin for this segment dropping to 22.8% from 29.5% last year.
Backlog reached a new high at $115.1 million, up 21.2% year over year, supporting management’s focus on future recovery.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide quantitative forward guidance, but reiterated its goal to restore profitability and convert backlog into revenue. Success will depend on improving factory efficiency, reducing the impact of operational setbacks, and strengthening order flow in Communications Systems. Investors should watch how quickly the company turns its record backlog into sales and whether margin pressures can be reversed in later quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s Battery & Energy Products segment, which includes lithium and rechargeable battery systems for commercial, industrial, and defense applications, saw sales dip 4.7% year over year. Weaker demand in oil and gas and industrial markets offset growth in medical battery sales during the period.

The Communications Systems segment, supplying products like radio amplifiers and military accessories, remained a key weak spot with ongoing order delays and lower factory utilization. This segment’s revenue contribution shrank further and margins narrowed, underlining the importance of improved execution in coming quarters.

Operating expenses rose 10.5%, including a 23.3% spike in research and development spending, which management says supports new products for both defense and medical applications. One-time costs related to consulting and disruption also weighed on results, further affecting operating leverage.

What Ultralife does

Ultralife designs and manufactures advanced lithium batteries, chargers, and communication systems for energy, defense, and commercial customers worldwide.
