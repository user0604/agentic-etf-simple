THE QUESTION

What stock have you owned the longest — and do the reasons you bought it still hold up?

1. Ownership Has Its Privileges

By Matt Frankel, CFP®
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

The stock I’ve owned the longest is American Express (NYSE: AXP), which I bought when I rolled over an old employer’s 401(k) about 15 years ago. I initially bought for a few reasons — the stock looked like a bargain after the financial crisis, I loved American Express’ pricing power, and I thought it was doing a great job of appealing to the crucial millennial generation.

While it doesn’t trade for nearly as much of a bargain as it did in the 2011-12 time frame, the other reasons still apply. Amex still has the most attractive cardholder base in the industry, and its product innovation has improved further. Amex has been nearly a 10-bagger for me, including dividends, and I don’t plan to sell anytime soon.

2. I’m Letting This Winner Run

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I remember when I first got to the Fool in 2010, how polarizing Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN) was for so many. The company was spending money hand over fist, and the valuation “made no sense.” But it was a team meeting we had with David Gardner early on that opened my eyes to how to look at the business — and I decided I needed to own shares myself.

Fast-forward to today, and I still own them — and have zero plans to sell. In fact, I keep Amazon on my list of positions I’d like to add to. With its massive e-commerce business along with cloud, advertising, AI, and whatever comes next, the company just has so much opportunity to capitalize on, it would be a shame to not participate.
