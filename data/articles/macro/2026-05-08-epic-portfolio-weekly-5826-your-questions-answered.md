Market Movers: Two Stories, One Theme

This week served up a useful reminder that earnings season rewards clarity – and punishes anything that looks like a question mark.

Team Rule Breakers recommendation Shopify (NASDAQ: SHOP) delivered a standout Q1: 34% revenue growth, operating income nearly doubled, and EPS came in 90% above consensus. And yet shares fell more than 15% after the report. The culprit was guidance – management called for "high-twenties" percent revenue growth in Q2, signaling deceleration. At ~85x forward earnings, any hint of a slowdown seemingly spooks the market. For long-term investors, this looks like noise. Shopify has a well-documented history of under-promising and over-delivering, and its AI initiatives are still in early innings.

Sterling Infrastructure (NASDAQ: STRL) told the opposite story. Shares surged more than 50% after a Q1 that didn't just beat expectations – it obliterated them. Adjusted EPS of $3.59 topped estimates by $1.40, and revenue of $825.7 million beat forecasts by nearly $234 million. Full-year guidance was equally stunning: sales of $3.7 billion-$3.8 billion versus the $3.14 billion Wall Street had modeled, and adjusted EPS of $18.40-$19.05 against a consensus of just $13.83. When a company beats and raises by those margins, the market rerates fast. Team Hidden Gems' recommendation of Sterling is – by a wide margin – the early standout performer in Epic Portfolio, up an astounding 85%+ in just a month.

Speaking of Sterling...

One month in, the Epic Portfolio is already telling the story we expected it would – and doing it in vivid color. After that incredible earnings report, Sterling Infrastructure is up more than 85%. TransMedics (NASDAQ: TMDX) and Shopify – two companies we continue to believe in – are each down more than 20%. When we built the case for 50 stocks, we wrote that a handful of positions pulling back would be inevitable, and that a few outsized winners would likely carry the day.

This is the math we've always believed in. Big winners are rare, unpredictable, and often sudden. You can't time them – you can only make sure you're holding them when they arrive. Owning 50 stocks isn't about diluting conviction. It's about staying calm when volatility hits some positions, because other positions are picking up the slack. Sterling struck early. And because we own it alongside 49 other high-quality businesses, short-term noise from any one of them stays exactly that... noise. We think that's the emotional and financial power of genuine diversification.
