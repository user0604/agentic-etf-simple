Corvex (NASDAQ:MOVE) completed its shift into the AI infrastructure business, signaling a new chapter for the company. The emerging AI platform provider saw strong early contracted demand but only modest initial revenue from its new segment. Reporting results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 19, 2026, Corvex navigated the close-out of legacy operations and the start-up of its AI cloud platform. Overall, the quarter reflected early momentum in bookings but underscored the transitional nature of the period.

Notable Developments
The acquisition of Corvex OpCo transformed the company’s focus to supplying GPU-accelerated AI cloud infrastructure, with most revenue now coming from this new business line.
Deferred revenue jumped from $0.01 million to $4.4 million, indicating strong early bookings for contracted AI compute, though recognized revenue for the period remained modest.
Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $(1.6) million from $(4.9) million in the prior year, reflecting lower ongoing losses and improved cost discipline in the new operational structure.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue specific financial guidance for the next quarter or full year, citing the ongoing integration of the acquired AI platform business. Its main priority remains scaling the AI cloud offering, converting contracted deferred revenue into recognized sales, and demonstrating commercial traction. Investors should closely monitor revenue recognition trends and whether the booked contract value translates into actual earned revenue in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

The shift to a GPU-accelerated AI cloud platform was completed with the Corvex OpCo acquisition on March 19, 2026. Of the $0.51 million total revenue, $0.48 million came from AI platform and services in just 12 days, demonstrating immediate post-acquisition commercialization. The connected devices and services—representing the company’s legacy business—generated $0.04 million and continue to be phased out.

Deferred revenue at period end soared to $4.4 million, reflecting customer contracts for future AI compute services that have yet to be recognized as income. Management explained this growth signifies a rapid ramp-up in customer interest for the company’s core offering of secure, scalable GPU-based computing power.

On cost, operating expenses were $5.4 million and general and administrative costs made up more than $3.3 million of this total. While net cash used in operations held steady at $4.3 million year over year, the cash balance increased sharply to $29.3 million following the business combination—providing greater funding for the AI infrastructure rollout.
