Key Takeaways
EuroDry returned to profitability this quarter as charter rates surged.
Revenue rose year over year.
Management expects improved profitability to continue but did not provide concrete financial guidance.

EuroDry (NASDAQ:EDRY) rebounded to profitability, driven by a sharp increase in vessel charter rates despite operating a smaller fleet. The international drybulk shipping company specializes in transporting commodities like iron ore, coal, and grain using Panamax, Ultramax, Kamsarmax, and Supramax vessels. The latest results, released on May 20, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, reflected strong top-line growth and improved operational performance. Overall, the period showed meaningful progress but left some uncertainty with limited forward guidance and a shrinking operational fleet.

Notable developments
Revenue climbed 38.9%, with average daily charter rates for vessels doubling compared to the same quarter last year.
The fleet contracted to 11 vessels from 12.8, yet operational efficiency improved with utilization reaching 99.7%.
EuroDry committed $74 million for future fleet renewal, ordering two new Kamsarmax eco vessels alongside two previously ordered Ultramaxes.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management pointed to continued momentum, noting that “has increased during the last month and a half,” with expectations that stronger charter market conditions will impact the next quarter. However, no updated guidance or specific financial targets were provided. Investors should watch the delivery and financing of new vessels and any movement in average charter rates, as these are central to future earnings growth.

Quarterly highlights

Charter rates more than doubled year over year, as the average ship in service earned $14,416 per day, enabling a swing to positive net income after last year’s loss. The revenue figure captured this upsurge, even as the total number of vessels operated was lower due to earlier fleet downsizing.

Operational metrics showed high efficiency, with almost every available day used for cargo movement and total vessel operating expenses per day held essentially flat. Periodic cost spikes from maintenance were visible, such as a jump in drydocking costs, but overall cost control supported improved cash generation.

On the strategic front, EuroDry is moving toward a younger, more environmentally efficient fleet by investing in four newbuildings scheduled for delivery through 2028, which is expected to reduce average fleet age and improve regulatory compliance over time. Management continued a share repurchase program, reflecting ongoing confidence in the company’s balance sheet and equity value.

What EuroDry does

EuroDry operates a fleet of drybulk shipping vessels that transport cargoes such as iron ore, coal, and grain worldwide under charters.
