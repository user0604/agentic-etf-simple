1. MELI Is Growing Like It's Still a Start-Up

MercadoLibre (NASDAQ: MELI) just dropped its first-quarter results, and the top line is screaming: $8.8 billion in revenue, up 49% year over year. There's a catch, though, and shares sank 7% after hours. Operating income fell 20%, as management keeps shoveling money into long-term growth bets. They're not apologizing for it, and Motley Fool analyst Buck Hartzell doesn't think they should. He recently wrote of MELI's increased spending, "It's exactly what you want to see because it will eventually lead to more scale and higher margins in the future." MELI is recommended in Rule Breakers and in Stock Advisor (Team RB).

Flywheel is spinning: Since lowering Brazil's free-shipping threshold last year, items sold jumped 56% year over year (YOY), and unique buyer growth hit a five-year high.
Mercado Pago, a real bank: With 83 million monthly active users and a $14.6 billion credit portfolio up 87% YOY, this no longer looks like a payments sidebar.

2. TTD Posts 12% Revenue Growth in Q1

Shares of The Trade Desk (NASDAQ: TTD) fell 14% after hours after the company reported that it grew Q1 revenue 12% to $689 million. Net income dropped to $40 million from $51 million a year ago. Margins compressed across the board. CEO Jeff Green blamed macro headwinds but stayed upbeat, pointing to new partnerships and platform upgrades. Q2 guidance calls for at least $750 million in revenue. TTD is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers.

Sticky customers: "It feels as if the bad news just keeps rolling in for The Trade Desk," Motley Fool contributor Jason Hall wrote in March, but customer retention has stayed above 95% for over a decade. That's practically unheard of in adtech and is a reminder of why TTD's moat is real.
Eating its own cooking: TTD repurchased $164 million of its own stock in Q1, with $327 million still in the buyback tank.
3. Airbnb Raises Outlook on Travel Demand

Airbnb (NASDAQ: ABNB) has just raised its full-year outlook again. The short-term rental giant now expects low- to mid-teen sales growth, up from at least low double digits. Q1 revenue climbed 18% to $2.68 billion, topping estimates, and gross booking value jumped 19% to $29.2 billion. The Iran conflict dented bookings in parts of Europe and Asia Pacific, but the Americas more than picked up the slack. ABNB is recommended by both Team Hidden Gems and Team Rule Breakers.

People are still traveling -- a lot: Domestic U.S. demand was strong, and trips are being booked further in advance, a healthy sign for future quarters.
Reserve Now, Pay Later is catching on fast: The feature already accounts for about 20% of gross booking value, and Q2 guidance of $3.54 billion to $3.60 billion came in ahead of Wall Street's $3.46 billion estimate.
