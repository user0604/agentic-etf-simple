Key Takeaways
Manchester United posted higher revenue but recorded a significantly larger quarterly loss.
Broadcasting revenue rose 57.1% year over year in the quarter.
Management gave new guidance for higher full-year revenue and profits, highlighting financial discipline and team performance.

Manchester United increased its revenue, driven by broadcast and merchandise growth, but posted a much larger loss due to rising costs and one-off charges. The prominent global football club, recognized for its strong brand and storied team, released financial results for its third fiscal quarter on May 28, 2026. Despite gains in key areas, escalating expenses for management changes and finance costs weighed on profitability, resulting in a mixed overall picture for the period.

Notable Developments
Revenue climbed 18.1% due to stronger broadcasting fees, offsetting a 5.2% dip in matchday revenue following fewer home games than last year.
Broadcasting revenue surged 57.1% on better men’s team Premier League placement and higher international rights values, while merchandising also rose sharply with e-commerce gains.
Total operating expenses rose 10.5%, including £16.7 million in exceptional costs from the change of head coach and staff, contributing to a wider net loss.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year revenue target to between £655 million and £665 million, and its adjusted EBITDA expectations to between £200 million and £210 million, citing team improvements and cost controls. The new men’s head coach’s recent appointment is expected to provide stability, but future quarterly results will depend heavily on securing new sponsorships and managing costs. Investors should closely monitor progress on sponsorship deals and stadium investment plans.

Quarterly Highlights

Broadcasting income was the strongest performer this quarter, reflecting the men’s team’s higher league finish as well as growth in the value of Premier League media rights. This helped offset a drop in matchday revenue, which fell due to three fewer home matches compared to the year-ago period.

Commercial revenue grew 10.3% year over year. Strong merchandise and licensing sales drove the increase, particularly via e-commerce, even as sponsorship revenue slipped following the end of the Tezos training kit deal. Management noted a one-off credit helping retail revenue, underlining that future gains may require further commercial innovation.

Expenses went up thanks to higher amortization from increased investment in the playing squad and exceptional charges from replacing the manager. Net finance costs also spiked due to unfavorable foreign exchange moves on debt.
