Unitil Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 11:36 AM

Comments

Unitil (NYSE:UTL) is a regulated utility serving electric and natural gas customers throughout New England, with a growth strategy anchored in expanding its rate base, pursuing strategic acquisitions, and investing in infrastructure modernization. Recent years have seen the company execute accretive deals, achieve robust customer growth, and prioritize stable, sustainable shareholder returns in a heavily regulated sector.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Robust Rate Base Growth and Smart Acquisitions Accelerate Earnings Power

Persistent rate base expansion enables above-average regulated earnings growth – Over the past five years, Unitil's average annual rate base growth has been 8.1%, near the top end of its long-term guidance range of 6.5% to 8.5%. This momentum was supercharged by the acquisitions of Bangor Natural Gas and Maine Natural Gas, raising the consolidated rate base by 17% year over year in 2026. Importantly, none of the benefits from the pending Aquarion Water acquisition are yet factored into current capital plans or guidance, suggesting potential further upside. In regulated utilities, a growing rate base typically translates into higher allowed earnings through rate-making mechanisms, positioning Unitil for continued 5% to 7% annual earnings-per-share growth and enhancing its long-term investment appeal.

2. Market Dominance in Maine Translates Into Margin and Customer Expansion

Unitil’s dominant footprint supports sticky revenue and scale benefits – Following recent acquisitions, Unitil now serves approximately 90% of Maine’s natural gas customers, making it the state’s largest gas utility. In the first quarter of 2026, customer additions totaled 7,100 year over year, the majority from newly acquired territories. This market share delivers pricing stability and broader cost recovery through the regulatory process and leverages economies of scale to improve margins—examples include a $10.3 million year-over-year gain in gas gross margin in Q1 2026, primarily from customer growth and higher rates. This entrenched market presence reduces competitive threats and provides a stable foundation for ongoing investment and returns.

3. Constructive Regulatory Outcomes Support Predictable and Growing Cash Flows

Favorable regulatory rulings drive higher authorized returns and reduce volatility – In 2026, Unitil locked in several constructive rate case outcomes, such as a $13 million base rate hike for its New Hampshire electric operations, with an authorized return on equity (ROE) of 9.45%, higher than those of many regional peers. The approval of multiyear rate plans and accelerated cost-recovery mechanisms ensures timely returns on new infrastructure spending. Additionally, with all electric and more than half of gas customers on decoupled rate structures, Unitil is largely insulated from weather-driven volume swings and demand shocks, supporting earnings stability and a confident reaffirmation of its 2026 EPS guidance and long-term growth outlook.

4. Disciplined Capital Allocation and Conservative Balance Sheet Safeguard Dividends

Prudent financial management underpins dividend growth and credit strength – Unitil continues to allocate capital effectively, maintaining a balanced mix of equity and debt while funding a rising capital program—now budgeted at $1.2 billion through 2030, up 20% over prior plans. The company’s 2026 annualized dividend of $1.90 per share represents a 5.6% increase, with management consistently targeting a payout ratio between 55% and 65%. Recent fixed-rate debt issuance preserves funding predictability in a higher interest rate environment, while at-the-market equity program flexibility supports additional growth initiatives. This approach helps ensure ongoing access to capital markets, stable investment-grade ratings, and robust dividend coverage even as the company pursues expansion.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Acquisition Integration and Regulatory Hurdles Could Delay Growth Upside

Deal uncertainty clouds the forecast for future earnings expansion – While the pending Aquarion Water acquisition is positioned as highly complementary, its completion is contingent on several regulatory milestones. Connecticut approval has been obtained, but unresolved issues in Massachusetts, such as restrictive risk and stay-out provisions, could lead Unitil to exclude these assets or delay transaction closure. Any setbacks could limit near-term rate base and earnings expansion versus management’s scenario analysis. Such delays or adverse conditions may also result in higher transaction costs or divert attention from core operational execution, potentially dampening investor confidence in the company’s growth story.

2. Rising Interest Expenses Threaten Margin Expansion as Borrowing Costs Normalize

Higher debt-service obligations could erode returns on new investments – Unitil’s recent senior note issuance at interest rates of 5.62% and 5.87% (for tenors out to 2041)—higher than prior vintages—reflects the broader trend of tightening monetary policy. These comparatively elevated borrowing costs, necessary to fund expansion and refinance maturing obligations, may drive up overall weighted average cost of capital. If interest rates remain elevated, the spread between allowed regulatory returns and actual financing costs could narrow, potentially pressuring margins and limiting the profitability of future capital projects, particularly if regulators are slow to reflect higher funding expenses in base rates.

3. Geographic and Regulatory Concentration in New England Heightens Risk of Adverse Policy Shifts

Unitil’s earnings are heavily reliant on a limited set of jurisdictions – As a predominantly New England–focused utility, Unitil depends on favorable regulatory environments in just a few states. Changes in regional energy policy, political attitudes toward utility returns, or heightened scrutiny of infrastructure investment recovery could lead to stricter rate case outcomes or required cost absorption by the company. For instance, while only 0.5% of Unitil’s rate base is affected by recent Federal Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) return-on-equity cuts, future state-level or federal directives could further restrict returns, challenge decoupling mechanisms, or delay cost recovery, placing earnings growth and shareholder returns at risk relative to more diversified peers.
