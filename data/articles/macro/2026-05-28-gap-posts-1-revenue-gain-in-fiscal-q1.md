Gap (NYSE:GAP) posted a business win this quarter, led by robust growth at its core Gap brand, even as other brands showed mixed results. The company, a major American apparel retailer with brands including Old Navy, Banana Republic, and Athleta, reported first-quarter results for the 13 weeks ended May 2, 2026, in a release dated May 28, 2026. Overall, the period showed operational improvement, with sales growth, effective inventory control, and increased shareholder returns, but also margin pressure and uneven brand performance.

Notable developments
Gap brand achieved double-digit comparable sales growth of 10%, its strongest result in over 20 years, with net sales rising to $796 million.
Gross margin fell by 1.3 percentage points to 40.5% due to tariffs, while revenue climbed 1.0% and comparable sales were positive for the ninth straight quarter.
The company returned $464 million to shareholders through stock buybacks and a 6% increase in its quarterly dividend.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management increased its adjusted earnings per share forecast for fiscal 2026 to $2.30-$2.40, citing improved tariff conditions and ongoing efficiency efforts. The most important thing for investors to watch is progress in turning around Athleta and reviving online channel growth, as these areas remain significant performance risks. Revenue growth guidance for the full year was trimmed to 1%-2%.

Quarterly highlights

The Gap brand delivered standout performance, with sales up 10% and similar gains in comparable sales, driven by demand in denim and kids’ products. This improvement offset mixed results at other brands.

Gross margin, which measures profit after the cost of goods sold but before administrative expenses, decreased by 1.3 percentage points. The decline was mainly from higher tariff expenses, but the underlying merchandise margin improved due to better pricing and inventory management.

Free cash flow was a positive $78 million versus a negative $223 million the previous year, reflecting improved net income and careful management of inventory and expenses. The company returned $464 million to shareholders, including a higher dividend and share repurchases.

What Gap does

Gap operates a portfolio of clothing and accessories brands — Old Navy, Gap, Banana Republic, and Athleta — serving customers through more than 2,400 retail stores and online platforms worldwide.
