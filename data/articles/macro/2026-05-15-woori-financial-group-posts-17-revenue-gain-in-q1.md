Key Takeaways
Net income declined slightly as higher credit losses and costs offset new insurance gains.
Revenue rose year over year.
Outlook remains cautious with no explicit forecast and rising credit risk to monitor.

Woori Financial Group (NYSE:WF) reported mixed first quarter results, as higher costs and credit losses modestly outweighed gains from new insurance operations and revenue growth. The diversified Korean financial group, known for its broad retail and institutional banking footprint, released its latest financial results on May 15, 2026, covering the quarter ended March 31. Overall, the period showed stable core revenues, a large non-cash revaluation gain, and pressure on expense control.

While insurance contributions and fee income continued to expand, profit measures dipped compared to the same period last year. The quarter was defined by steady revenue growth, a notable one-time gain from asset revaluation, and rising expenses that weighed on profitability.

Notable Developments
Net income slipped 2.3% as general and administrative expenses and credit impairments both rose at a quicker pace than revenue.
The firm declared a quarterly cash dividend of ₩220 per share, with payout scheduled for May 29, 2026.
Impairment losses on credit rose 20.9% year over year, reaching ₩526,782 million, highlighting ongoing credit risk concerns.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide specific projections for the coming quarters. It highlighted the group’s broad-based service capabilities and diversification following recent insurance acquisitions. The most important aspect for investors to monitor next is the trend in credit loss expenses, given a significant year-over-year increase in impairment losses this period.

Quarterly Highlights

The period featured further progress in service diversification, with consolidated net insurance income—added after the acquisition of Tongyang Life and ABL Life—helping to broaden the revenue base. Insurance operations are now a visible contributor, supporting year-over-year growth in revenue and fee income.

Profitability was under pressure, as both credit loss provisions (which cover expected bad loans) and general and administrative expenses outpaced gains in net interest income. This led to declines in both operating income and net income relative to the prior year quarter.

The company’s one-off gain on revaluation of premises and equipment drove total comprehensive income sharply higher, but this was a non-recurring event and not related to daily business operations or cash flow.

What Woori Financial Group Does

Woori Financial Group is a diversified financial services company in South Korea, offering banking, insurance, credit cards, asset management, and related services through a broad network of branches and digital platforms.

For those tracking the stock, the next key will be how cost control and credit quality trends shape upcoming results.
