Key Takeaways
Moderate business win as new specialty contracts boost high-value segments, but losses persist.
Revenue rose 8% year over year while gross margin improved slightly.
Guidance reaffirmed, with near-term focus on scaling new CDMO projects and digital D2C expansion.

Emerging biotech firm BioHarvest Sciences (NASDAQ:BHST) delivered moderate progress in its latest quarter as new specialty ingredient contracts fueled growth in high-value segments, yet operating and net losses continued. The innovator in botanical synthesis and nutraceuticals saw rising adoption of its flagship dietary supplement and milestones reached in contract manufacturing. The results, reported on May 14, 2026 for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, showcased revenue and customer gains but also highlighted ongoing cost pressure. Overall, the period reflected steady advancement, with new contracts pointing to future growth, even as profitability remains elusive.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 8% compared to the prior year, driven by growth in both direct-to-consumer dietary supplements and new contract manufacturing projects.
Cash and equivalents totaled $19.2 million, providing flexibility for investment and ongoing operating losses in light of a $(2.6) million net loss.
Contract development and manufacturing revenue jumped 135% year over year, but this segment remains loss-making and is still ramping up toward material scale.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year revenue guidance of $42–$48 million, expressing confidence in both direct-to-consumer and contract manufacturing growth. The most important development to monitor is the company’s progress in turning early-stage CDMO (contract development and manufacturing organization) projects into recurring, royalty-generating revenue streams over the next several quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Direct-to-consumer revenue continued to dominate, with VINIA—the company’s red grape cell dietary supplement—accounting for 95% of total sales. VINIA’s customer base reached 90,000 active users by late April, and a new hydration-focused supplement has generated nearly $1 million in sales since its introduction late last year.

The CDMO segment delivered rapid growth off a small base, as new contracts for specialty plant compounds in fragrance and saffron completed key milestones and contributed to the 135% year-over-year increase in CDMO sales. Despite these wins, the segment posted a $1.3 million operating loss.

Gross margin improved slightly to 59% in the first quarter of 2026, as sales and marketing spending rose to $4.1 million, compared to $3.7 million in the same period last year.

What BioHarvest Sciences Does

The company uses proprietary plant cell technology to develop and produce functional ingredients and dietary supplements for the nutraceutical and wellness markets.

Members are encouraged to monitor further CDMO contract progress and updates on BioHarvest’s digital direct-to-consumer expansion in upcoming quarters.
