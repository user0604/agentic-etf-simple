Hawaiian Electric Reports 22% Profit Drop in Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 4:18 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Business win as wildfire litigation settlement payments commence, but profit pressures intensify.
Earnings per share (EPS) fell 21.7% as rising operations and maintenance costs offset almost flat revenue.
Outlook hinges on higher 2027 rate rebasing to address new structural costs.

Wildfire liability payments weighed on results as the leading Hawaiian utility and holding company Hawaiian Electric Industries (NYSE:HE) navigated a cost-burdened start to the new year. The company provides electricity to approximately 95% of Hawaii’s population, primarily through its regulated utility subsidiary. In its first-quarter 2026 update released May 8, the company reported lower core earnings as it absorbed higher costs and made its first annual payment under the Maui wildfire settlement agreement.

Overall, the quarter reflected significant legal progress for the business but brought sharply lower profitability in its utility operations.

Notable Developments
The company made the first scheduled $479 million payment related to the Maui wildfire tort settlement, marking a milestone in resolving claims linked to last year’s fires.
Net income for the electric utility segment, on a non-GAAP core basis, fell to $36 million, down 28% from the previous year, mainly due to operations and maintenance and insurance cost increases.
Moody’s upgraded the company’s credit rating after settlement completion, but both the parent and its utility remain below investment grade, signaling ongoing credit risk.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue earnings guidance for fiscal 2026 but expects operations and maintenance expenses to remain high, with relief likely to come only after 2027 rate rebasing. It is a transitional year, and capturing increased costs in customer rates will depend on regulatory proceedings later this year. The single most important thing for investors to watch is the outcome of the 2027 rate rebasing effort and whether the Public Utilities Commission approves the sought-after recovery of new costs.

Quarterly Highlights

The first annual payment in the Maui wildfire settlement was made in April and is part of four-yearly payments totaling $1.9 billion. Despite the settlement, the company’s credit ratings remain below investment grade, which affects borrowing capacity and adds ongoing uncertainty.

The core utility business reported much lower profit, with net income for this segment down 28% due to sharply higher operations and maintenance spending. Key drivers included $7 million more in storm-related costs, $6 million higher insurance premiums, and $4 million in increased power supply expenses, only partly offset by higher revenue under the state’s performance-based regulation.

While the company reiterated its long-term plan to expand renewable energy and maintain grid resilience, this quarter saw little new progress on actual project development or emissions reduction metrics. The business also filed an updated wildfire mitigation plan, a regulatory requirement for continued utility operation in high-risk areas.

What Hawaiian Electric Industries Does

Hawaiian Electric Industries supplies regulated electricity services to nearly all of Hawaii’s population, operating across multiple islands through its electric utility subsidiary.

To stay informed, watch for the results of the company’s 2027 rate rebasing proposal and upcoming regulatory decisions.
