Earnings at Brookfield Business Partners (NYSE:BBU) softened as declines in revenue and fewer large asset sale gains weighed on results. A leading global business services and industrials investor, it reported its latest financial results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed steady segment progress and ongoing operational execution but with headline figures down from a year ago.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped $313 million to $6.44 billion, driven mainly by the deconsolidation of a healthcare operation that reduced segment revenue by $441 million.
Industrials delivered higher adjusted EBITDA of $320 million, up from $304 million, helped by Clarios’ strong cash flows and a $1.04 billion tax refund.
Quarterly cash dividend held steady at $0.0625 per Class A share as the company continued its capital return policy.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its focus on operational improvement and disciplined capital recycling, aiming for a minimum 15% return on new investments. No new financial targets or updated guidance were issued for the remainder of the year, signaling a “wait and see” approach. Investors should monitor results in the Business Services and Infrastructure segments, as well as progress with asset sales and deployment of new investments like DeployCo and Fosber.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue fell as the company exited its healthcare services business, leading to a reduction of $441 million in Business Services revenue compared to the prior year. Infrastructure Services also reported lower earnings, reflecting the loss of contribution from the sold shuttle tanker operation last year. Adjusted EBITDA for the combined segments remained broadly stable, with Industrials outperforming due to ongoing strength at Clarios and gains from tax credits.

Within segment detail, Business Services generated consistent cash flow, supported by growth in the residential mortgage insurer Sagen (EBITDA up to $65 million), but dealer software services earnings dropped sharply due to higher IT investments and lower ownership share. In Infrastructure Services, EBITDA fell by $14 million to $90 million, largely from the absence of last year’s $114 million asset sale gain, though underlying operations such as Lottery Services saw modest growth and new contract ramp-ups.

Brookfield redeployed capital into new sectors, completing the acquisition of a 37% stake in Fosber, a machinery firm, and committing $150 million into DeployCo, an AI-focused portfolio company. Strong balance sheet liquidity was highlighted, with cash rising nearly $1 billion to $4.52 billion since year-end and a stable net debt-to-capitalization ratio at 72%.
