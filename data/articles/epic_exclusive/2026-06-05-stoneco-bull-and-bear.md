StoneCo Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 5, 2026, 4:45 PM

Comments

StoneCo Ltd. (NASDAQ:STNE) is a Brazilian financial technology company that provides digital payments, banking, and credit solutions primarily to small and medium-sized businesses. The company’s growth strategy centers on evolving from a pure payment processor into an integrated financial platform, leveraging technology to deepen merchant relationships, cross-sell bundled services, and enhance capital efficiency. StoneCo is committed to disciplined capital allocation, technological innovation, and navigating Brazil’s macroeconomic and competitive landscape to drive long-term value creation.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Platform Transition Drives Higher Client Stickiness and Cross-Sell Potential

The shift to a full financial services platform unlocks deeper engagement and higher revenue per client. StoneCo’s ongoing migration from a single-product merchant acquirer to a holistic financial partner represents a strategic inflection point. By integrating payments, banking, and credit into a unified digital platform, the company is increasing cross-sell opportunities and engagement. Management reports that clients using two or more products generate significantly higher average monthly revenue compared to single-product users. This is supported by the new Average Revenue Per Active Client (ARPAC) metric and a focus on enhancing client lifecycle value through workflow integrations and platform upgrades. As cross-channel adoption improves, the company stands to benefit from a more loyal, profitable client base, positioning StoneCo to expand ARPAC and reduce churn over time.

2. Deposit Growth and Funding Efficiency Strengthen Profitability Amid Rate Volatility

Substantial growth in average daily retail deposits reduces StoneCo’s funding costs and supports long-term margin improvement. The company’s retail deposit franchise reached BRL 10.1 billion at the end of Q1 2026, up 22% year over year, with average daily deposits climbing 26% year over year. Strategic efforts to deepen deposit penetration—through enhanced platform engagement and workflow integration—have meaningfully reduced StoneCo’s cost of funds from 100% of Brazil’s Interbank Deposit Certificate (CDI) rate in early 2025 to 87% in early 2026. This shift is crucial given Brazil’s volatile interest rate environment, as every 100 basis point movement in the Selic rate impacts StoneCo’s pre-tax earnings by approximately BRL 200–250 million. Continued deposit growth not only stabilizes funding but also provides a durable advantage over competitors reliant on wholesale funding, directly benefiting financial margins.

3. Conservative Credit Underwriting and Shift Toward Secured Lending Mitigate Risk

The pivot to secured and government-backed credit products is intended to improve portfolio quality and lower risk intensity. StoneCo’s Q1 2026 results show a 14% sequential expansion in the total credit portfolio, reaching BRL 3.2 billion. However, after confronting elevated non-performing loans (NPLs) in new 2025 merchant cohorts, management acted swiftly—tightening underwriting models, reducing average loan ticket sizes, and prioritizing secured working capital and BNDES (Brazilian Development Bank)-backed lending. Although short-term cost of risk remains high (21.9% in Q1), coverage ratios are robust at 229%, and early post-adjustment cohorts are demonstrating improved repayment trends. By moving to more collateralized products and conservative provisioning, StoneCo is reducing the likelihood of uncontrolled credit losses, setting the stage for credit growth to recover with lower risk and more predictable returns as the macro environment stabilizes.

4. Proactive Capital Returns Demonstrate Discipline and Confidence in Value Creation

StoneCo’s return of BRL 3.6 billion to shareholders in 2026—equivalent to a 27% yield—highlights strong capital stewardship and surplus generation. Following the divestiture of its Linx software business, StoneCo promptly returned proceeds through an extraordinary dividend and significant share buybacks, with additional repurchases planned throughout the remainder of the year. This disciplined capital allocation signals management’s willingness to prioritize shareholder returns over risky expansion or value-destructive M&A when immediate reinvestment opportunities are lacking. The resulting capital ratio, even post-distribution, remains comfortably above the company’s 17% internal buffer, providing ample flexibility to fund future organic growth or weather macro shocks. This approach underpins StoneCo’s transition to a more mature, capital-efficient, and shareholder-aligned business model.

5. Strategic Execution and Operational Agility Bolster Resilience in Tough Macro Conditions

Management’s rapid response to credit setbacks and focus on execution, rather than external macro relief, elevates StoneCo’s credibility and adaptability. Despite facing challenging Brazilian macroeconomic conditions—marked by higher Selic rates (now projected at 14% for 2026) and ongoing small business stress—StoneCo maintained its full-year guidance, forecasting better performance in the second half as operational initiatives ramp up. Management has demonstrated an ability to promptly identify and address operational headwinds, such as elevated merchant churn and credit provisioning, through accelerated decision-making, policy adaptation, and transparent disclosure. By focusing on what it can control—retention, product and pricing simplification, AI-driven cost efficiencies—the company positions itself to outperform peers amid economic turbulence and to quickly rebound as the environment improves.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Persistent Client Churn and Weak Payment Volume Growth Signal Market Share Pressures

Elevated merchant churn and modest TPV (Total Payment Volume) growth threaten StoneCo’s scale and competitive standing. In Q1 2026, StoneCo’s TPV grew just 3% year over year, and its active client base declined by 5% sequentially, driven by both intentional pruning for profitability and continued competitive churn. Analyst downgrades and recent news cite weak TPV growth and take-rate compression, raising concerns about stagnating or shrinking payments franchise. While management frames client base reductions as strategic, the ongoing loss of less engaged or lower-ARPAC customers could put the brakes on network effects and leave StoneCo vulnerable to faster-growing fintech rivals or traditional banks eager to capture market share through price competition, especially as Instant Payment (PIX) volumes rise and the industry matures.

2. Deteriorating Credit Quality and High Cost of Risk May Erode Profitability

Recent spikes in non-performing loans and elevated credit provisions expose StoneCo to sustained earnings headwinds. The company’s Q1 2026 results revealed a concerning uptick in 15–90 day delinquency (up 60 basis points sequentially) and NPLs over 90 days (now 7%, up from 5.2%), particularly in micro and small business lending. Cost of risk soared to 21.9%, forcing StoneCo to take a conservative provisioning posture. Although management is shifting toward secured and government-backed credit, the lingering effects of previous automated lending missteps could continue to drag on financial results for several quarters. Prolonged credit stress, especially if Brazil’s macro environment remains challenging, could undermine StoneCo’s return on equity and threaten the viability of its credit expansion strategy.

3. Margin Compression from Competitive and Macro Pressure Could Limit Long-Term Growth

Intensifying competition, pricing pressure, and high interest rates are squeezing StoneCo’s operating margins and dampening investor confidence. The company’s adjusted gross profit margin contracted to 41.6% in Q1 2026 (from 44.4% a year earlier), as both operating expenses and credit cost provisions rose. News reports emphasize take-rate compression and muted revenue growth, with shares hitting a new 52-week low in May 2026. With Brazil’s interest rates above initial expectations (Selic now closer to 14%), StoneCo’s net interest margins are under persistent strain—each 100 basis point rate increase hits pre-tax earnings by up to BRL 250 million. These headwinds, combined with the risk of ongoing ARPAC weakness and a business mix gravitating toward lower-margin products, suggest a structural challenge to sustaining historical growth rates and returning to higher profitability in a crowded fintech market.
