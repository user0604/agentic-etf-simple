Exagen Posts 12% Revenue Gain in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Record revenue growth, but profitability remains elusive.
Revenue increased 12% year over year.
Management maintained full-year revenue guidance at $70–$73 million.

Exagen (NASDAQ:XGN) delivered record revenue driven by further gains in its core autoimmune disease testing franchise, but continued to report an operating loss. The diagnostic company specializes in advanced laboratory tests for autoimmune and connective tissue diseases, anchored by its AVISE® CTD product line. For the first quarter of fiscal 2026, reported on May 11, 2026, revenue grew but operating costs kept the company in the red. Overall, results showed strong sales and improving adjusted EBITDA, but profitability remains out of reach.

Notable developments
Sales of AVISE® CTD drove a 10% increase in test volumes, supporting record revenue and a trailing-twelve-month average selling price of $444 per test.
Adjusted EBITDA loss improved 14% to $(2.2) million compared to the prior year, indicating progress on operational efficiency targets.
Cash and cash equivalents ended the quarter at $21.5 million, bolstered by typical seasonal patterns in revenue cycle management.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 revenue guidance, targeting $70 to $73 million in total sales. It remains focused on increasing test volumes, disciplined cost controls, and further improvements to adjusted EBITDA. Investors should watch whether ongoing growth in test sales and average price can translate to reduced losses in coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

AVISE® CTD, an autoimmune diagnostic test, remained the growth engine, with both test volume and average selling price increasing over last year.

AVISE® CTD accounted for 91% of company revenue in 2025 and continues to benefit from payer negotiations that have raised reimbursement rates.

Operationally, gross margin held steady at 59.0%, reflecting effective revenue cycle management. Operating expenses—including research and development investment—grew at a slower rate than sales but remain high as a proportion of revenue.

The quarter ended with a strong cash position, though working capital fluctuated due to timing in claim collections, typical for the first quarter. No new product launches or major new customer wins were disclosed this period.

What Exagen does

Exagen specializes in laboratory-developed diagnostic tests that help clinicians identify and manage complex autoimmune and connective tissue diseases.
