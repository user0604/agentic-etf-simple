Earnings Preview: CuriosityStream

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

CuriosityStream (NASDAQ:CURI), a streaming and licensing company focused on factual and educational video content, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 earnings on Thursday, May 14, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of 2025, CuriosityStream reported revenue of $19.2 million, a 36% increase from the prior year, and adjusted free cash flow of $4.3 million. The company’s licensing revenue reached $9.8 million, while subscription revenue was $9.1 million for the quarter. Gross margin expanded to 60%, up from 52% a year ago. Adjusted EBITDA was positive at $1.1 million, marking the fourth consecutive quarter of positive results. For the full year, revenue rose 40% to $71.7 million, and adjusted free cash flow grew 46% to $13.9 million. These results were at the high end of guidance and met or exceeded analyst expectations for the period.

Positives
Strong licensing momentum driven by AI demand: The company saw a 352% increase in content licensing revenue for the year, mainly from supplying video and data assets to organizations developing artificial intelligence (AI) models. This shift diversified revenue streams and reduced reliance on traditional subscription business.
Consistent cash generation and dividend payments: CuriosityStream posted its eighth straight quarter of positive operating cash flow and paid out $22 million in dividends during 2025, demonstrating financial stability and shareholder returns.
Improved gross margins and cost discipline: Gross margin rose to 60% in Q4, supported by reduced general and administrative expenses (excluding noncash charges) and a 33% year-over-year reduction in nondiscretionary costs.
Repeat and expanding licensing partnerships: Nearly all large language model (LLM) partners renewed deals, and the company expects its roster of licensing partners to more than double in 2026, laying groundwork for future growth.
Negatives
Declining direct-to-consumer subscription revenue: Direct-to-consumer (DTC) revenue fell 24% year over year, reflecting a shrinking DTC subscriber base that was only partly offset by growth in partner-driven subscriptions.
Net loss driven by nonrecurring costs: The company reported a net loss of $6.4 million for 2025, primarily due to higher stock-based compensation and incentive adjustments. Without these charges, earnings would have been positive.
Bundled distribution headwinds: Revenue from bundled distribution partners declined 14% for the year, as some affiliate agreements were revised or not renewed, and the broader linear pay-TV industry continued to contract.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Licensing revenue mix and AI partner expansion: Investors should track whether licensing revenue continues to outpace subscription revenue, as management expects. Watch for updates on new and repeat AI training deals and the pace of onboarding new licensing partners.
Subscription growth from new pricing and partnerships: The company began rolling out a price increase on March 1, 2026, and expects to launch on 12 to 20 new platforms this year. Monitor the impact of these changes on subscriber churn and overall subscription revenue, especially as the price increase gradually takes effect over annual renewal cycles.
Progress on cost controls and margin trends: Gross margin improvements and disciplined spending were key in Q4. Look for continued cost management, especially regarding general and administrative expenses excluding noncash items, and any updates on the run rate for 2026.
Cash flow and dividend sustainability: The company has guided for double-digit growth in both revenue and cash flow for 2026 and intends to continue regular dividend payments, subject to liquidity requirements under its new $10 million credit facility. Investors should assess whether cash generation remains strong enough to support these payouts.
Guidance updates and deal timing clarity: Management indicated that revenue from large licensing deals can be lumpy and that guidance may be narrowed later in the year. Watch for any revised guidance or commentary on the cadence of licensing revenue and the timing of major contract deliveries.
