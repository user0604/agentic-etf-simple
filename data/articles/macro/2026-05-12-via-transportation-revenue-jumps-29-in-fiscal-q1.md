Notable developments
Revenue reached $127.4 million, beating the company’s outlook and rising 29% from the prior-year period.
Adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to ($5.8 million), up 30% from the same quarter in fiscal 2025, reflecting improved operating leverage despite elevated expenses.
The customer base expanded to 838, up 23%, with organic growth and the completed Downtowner acquisition both contributing.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management is guiding for second quarter revenue in the range of $132.5 million to $134.0 million, targeting 24% to 25% growth from a year ago, and narrowing the adjusted EBITDA loss to between ($3.0) million and ($4.0) million. Full-year 2026 guidance calls for similar momentum and a first quarter of positive adjusted EBITDA by year-end. The most important factor for investors to monitor is the mix of higher-margin software revenue versus services, as this will influence the company’s ability to achieve sustained profitability.

Quarterly highlights

The revenue figure surpassed company guidance, pointing to strong demand for the TransitTech platform from new and existing city and transit authority partners. This top-line performance was helped by broadening service offerings and disciplined customer acquisition, as seen in the increased customer count and growth from both organic channels and the Downtowner acquisition.

Profitability showed steady progress, with the adjusted EBITDA loss shrinking by 30% year over year. Operating expenses remained high, especially in sales, marketing, and administration, which together increased more than revenue during the quarter.

Adjusted gross margin ticked down slightly to 40%. Management noted a goal of lifting margins over time by expanding the higher-margin software portion of its platform revenue and supporting AI-powered features.
