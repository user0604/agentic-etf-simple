Key Takeaways
Copa delivered a business win with double-digit growth in revenue and profits. Earnings per share rose 20.5% compared to the prior year. Management guides for lower operating margins next quarter as higher fuel costs are only partially offset.

Copa (NYSE:CPA) posted a strong quarter driven by healthy travel demand and higher capacity. The leading Panama-based airline specializes in connecting destinations across the Americas via its extensive network and efficient fleet. The results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, released on May 14, 2026, showed significantly improved profitability and operational performance. Overall, the period featured solid execution despite the emerging headwind of rising fuel prices.

Notable developments
Revenue climbed 17% year over year as passenger traffic continued to recover, with 4.1 million passengers carried in the quarter.
Operating margin improved to 24.6%, reflecting growth in revenue and a disciplined approach to costs outside of fuel.
The board declared a second quarterly dividend of $1.71 per share, payable June 15, 2026.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects a drop in operating margin for the second quarter, guiding for a range between 8% and 12% due to a projected 80% to 90% jump in jet fuel prices compared to the prior year period, with only about half of that increase likely to be recovered through higher ticket prices. Key areas for investors to monitor will be fuel price trends and the company’s ability to pass on cost increases through fares as the year progresses.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue saw robust growth as the company increased its available seat miles by 14.0%, serving over 6 million passengers and registering a load factor of 87.2%, which is a key airline metric that shows the percentage of seats filled on flights.

Costs excluding fuel (a measurement known as ex-fuel CASM for cost per available seat mile) were kept in line with management’s targets, down 1.0% year over year, but total operating expenses rose as fuel prices increased by 7.5% and fleet expansion continued.

The company expanded its fleet, ending the quarter with 127 aircraft and finalizing a significant new order for Boeing 737 MAX planes to support future network growth and operational efficiency.

What Copa does

Copa operates as a leading Latin American airline, offering scheduled passenger and cargo services to destinations throughout North, Central, and South America, as well as the Caribbean, using a hub in Panama City.
