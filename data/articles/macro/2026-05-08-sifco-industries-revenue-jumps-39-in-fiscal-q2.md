Notable Developments
Profitability rebounded: net income from continuing operations turned positive, reversing last year’s loss as gross profit grew by $4.1 million.
Working capital increased as inventories rose over 60% and contract assets rose 15.7% since fiscal year-end, supporting production for new orders.
Liquidity improved as revolver borrowings dropped to $2.8 million from $8.0 million, though cash remained low at $0.3 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management cited strong demand and rising backlog but did not issue detailed revenue or earnings targets for the rest of fiscal 2026. Key factors to watch are the company’s ability to manage working capital and maintain profit margins, especially as hiring and supply chain constraints persist.

Quarterly Highlights

Profitability improvements stood out as earnings per share swung from a net loss last year to a significant profit this quarter. Adjusted EBITDA, a measure that excludes non-cash and one-time items, also posted a strong gain, reflecting improved operating efficiency.

The company credits these gains to its focus on core aerospace and energy sectors after divesting its European operations. Sales momentum remained strong, with revenue up sharply versus the prior year as demand from aerospace and energy customers spurred growth in the company’s main product lines—precision-forged and machined components used in engines and other critical systems. Management highlights increasing backlog, although no detailed customer or segment breakout figures were disclosed.

Operational challenges persist, however. Inventories and receivables increased rapidly, boosting working capital needs, while management notes that labor availability for its skilled workforce is still a constraint. While the company's borrowings declined, continued low cash balances could be a concern if market volatility or supply chain issues disrupt future production.
