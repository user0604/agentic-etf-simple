Zscaler Beats Q1 but Cash Story Sours Fast

Matt Frankel, CFP

May 28, 2026, 8:48 AM

Comments (1)
8.0

Zscaler (NASDAQ:ZS) delivered a quarter where the headline numbers looked great, then management buried two disclosures underneath that investors decided mattered far more. Revenue grew 25% year over year to $850.5 million and adjusted earnings per share of $1.08 beat consensus by roughly 7%, while non-GAAP operating margin hit a company record of 23%. Annual recurring revenue, the contracted revenue Zscaler is locked in to collect over the next twelve months, also climbed 25% to $3.53 billion, giving management cover to nudge full-year revenue and earnings guidance higher.

The clean beat unraveled once investors looked past the top line. Stripping out the recent Red Canary acquisition, a managed detection business that management itself admits has higher customer turnover than Zscaler's core platform, organic ARR growth slowed to 21%. The bigger blow came from free cash flow, where the company slashed its full-year margin guidance from a 26.5%–27% range down to 22.8%–23.3%, a roughly 370 basis-point cut. Zscaler attributed the reduction to a spike in memory, storage, and processor prices, and said it is pulling forward data center hardware purchases into the fourth quarter to lock in costs before they climb further. For a business long sold to investors as capital-light cloud software, a sudden hardware-driven margin hit is the kind of surprise that forces a re-rating.

Shares confirmed that re-rating, falling about 31.5% on Wednesday to close at $126.41 and erasing roughly $9 billion in market value, even as the S&P 500 finished essentially flat and the Nasdaq notched another record close. With the broader market quiet, the move is unmistakably about Zscaler itself, and specifically about the free cash flow guidance cut, since the operating results were strong enough that nothing else explains a one-day drop of that size. Underwhelming fourth-quarter revenue guidance of $875–$878 million, just shy of the roughly $879 million Wall Street expected, and management's acknowledgment of sales team turnover and tougher new-customer acquisition only sharpened the concern that Zscaler's premium valuation is harder to defend when the cash story turns murky.

A combination of slowing growth concerns and rising capital spending has been a fairly common story lately, especially in the software industry. DoorDash (NASDAQ:DASH) is another example from the Hidden Gems universe where there are clearly investor fears that a recent surge in spending won't produce strong returns.

In Zscaler's case, if the company can successfully become "the cybersecurity platform for the AI era," it could end up being money well spent. There are still some investor questions surrounding whether AI could be a disruptive force, or if Zscaler will be a net beneficiary. I tend to land in the latter camp – agentic AI will create massive cybersecurity challenges, and Zscaler is in a strong position to capitalize. However, the bottom line is that the increased hardware spending and lower free cash flow margins left investors with more questions than answers, and that's why the stock has been under pressure.
