Earnings Preview: Titan Machinery

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 11:02 AM

Comments

Titan Machinery (NASDAQ:TITN), a dealer of agricultural and construction equipment, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2027 on Thursday, May 21, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2026 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026, Titan Machinery reported total revenue of $641.8 million, down 14.6% year over year, reflecting weaker demand in domestic agriculture, construction, and Europe, partially offset by growth in Australia. The company posted a net loss of $36.2 million, or $1.59 per diluted share, which included a significant non-cash tax valuation allowance. On an adjusted basis, the net loss was $32.5 million, or $1.43 per diluted share. These results were in line with management’s expectations and analyst consensus. Gross profit margin improved to 13.5% from the prior year’s 6.7%, mainly due to fewer inventory impairments and better inventory health. Notably, the company exceeded its inventory reduction target, achieving a $201 million decrease for the year.

Positives
Significant inventory reduction achieved: The company more than doubled its initial $100 million inventory reduction goal, which has improved inventory quality and reduced interest expense. This positions the business for better margins when demand recovers.
Improved gross profit margin: Gross profit margin nearly doubled compared to the prior year’s quarter, reflecting healthier inventory and fewer impairments. This shows operational discipline despite a tough demand environment.
Stability in parts and service revenue: Parts and service, which are less cyclical than equipment sales, contributed over half of gross profit dollars and provided stability during the equipment downcycle.
Expense management: Operating expenses declined slightly year over year, with headcount and discretionary spending down, demonstrating effective cost control in a challenging market.
Negatives
Continued weakness in domestic agriculture demand: Same-store sales in the domestic agriculture segment fell 22.8%, driven by low commodity prices and weak grower profitability, which directly impacts equipment purchases.
Non-cash tax valuation allowance impacted net results: The quarter’s net loss included a $0.78 per share non-cash tax charge, which was larger than previously expected and affected reported results, even though it did not impact cash flow.
Australia and Europe remain challenged: While Australia saw some improvement, elevated input costs and prior-year volume declines continued to weigh on results. Europe’s growth was mainly currency-driven, with underlying demand still below historical averages.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2027's Report
Revenue trends by segment: Management expects Q1 revenue to decline around 20% year over year, with domestic agriculture down 15% to 20%, Europe down 20% to 25% due to the Germany exit, Australia up 10% to 15%, and construction flat to up 5%. Watch for how these trends play out versus guidance and consensus sales estimates of $485.6 million.
Equipment margin improvement: The company is guiding for full-year equipment margins to rise to about 8.4% from 7.3% last year. Q1 results will offer an early look at progress on this front, especially as inventory health continues to improve.
Update on inventory and aged equipment: Management has shifted its focus from inventory reduction to optimizing product mix and reducing aged inventory. Investors should monitor progress on further lowering aged equipment, as this impacts both margins and interest expense.
Performance of parts and service business: With equipment sales expected to remain weak, stability in the higher-margin parts and service segment will be critical for supporting overall profitability and absorption rates.
Status of restructuring and international operations: The Germany divestiture is largely completed, with only minor wind-down activities expected in Q1. Any updates on restructuring costs, impairment charges, or performance in Australia and Eastern Europe could influence outlook for the remainder of the year.
