Costco Wholesale (NASDAQ:COST), a leading membership-based warehouse retailer, is expected to report earnings for its fiscal third quarter of 2026 on Thursday, May 28, 2026, after the market closes.

Q2 FY2026 Recap

In the second quarter of fiscal 2026, Costco Wholesale reported net sales of $68.24 billion, marking a 9% increase from the same period last year. Net income reached $2.035 billion, or $4.58 per diluted share, which represented a nearly 14% year-over-year increase. These results beat analyst estimates for both sales and earnings per share. Comparable sales grew 7.4%, with digitally-enabled comparable sales up 22.6%. Membership fee income rose 14% to $1.355 billion, supported by both new member sign-ups and the September 2024 membership fee increase. The company ended the quarter with 82.1 million paid members and 40.4 million executive memberships. Gross margin improved, while selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) expenses as a percentage of sales were slightly higher.

Positives
Strong digital sales growth: Digitally-enabled comparable sales rose over 22%, reflecting success in e-commerce initiatives and digital enhancements that are resonating with members.
Membership fee income growth: Membership fee income increased 14%, driven by both new sign-ups and the recent fee increase, supporting recurring revenue and profitability.
Gross margin improvement: Gross margin as a percentage of net sales increased, aided by higher profitability in ancillary businesses like pharmacy and gasoline, as well as a nonrecurring legal settlement.
Consistent traffic and transaction growth: Shopping frequency increased 3% and the average transaction size also grew, indicating healthy member engagement and spending.
Negatives
Higher SG&A expenses as a percentage of sales: SG&A costs rose slightly, impacted by increased general liability reserves and central operating costs, which offset some of the efficiency gains from technology investments.
Slight decline in U.S. and Canada renewal rates: The renewal rate in these regions dipped by 10 basis points, mainly due to a higher proportion of online sign-ups, which tend to renew at a lower rate than in-warehouse sign-ups.
Slower membership growth: Paid membership growth was 4.8% year over year, below the pace seen in prior quarters, as fewer new warehouses opened in entirely new markets.
What to Watch in Q3 FY2026's Report
Sales momentum and digital performance: Investors should look for continued growth in both overall sales and digitally-enabled sales, especially given the strong March sales report showing an 11.3% year-over-year increase.
Membership trends and renewal rates: Watch for updates on paid membership growth and renewal rates, particularly whether digital-focused retention strategies are helping to stabilize or improve renewal percentages.
Gross margin and cost management: Monitor how gross margin trends, especially in the context of ongoing price reductions on key items, and whether SG&A expenses remain in check despite inflationary pressures and investments in technology.
Progress on new warehouse openings and real estate initiatives: The company is targeting 28 net new openings in fiscal 2026 and has announced more creative approaches to entering urban markets. Updates on the pace and location of openings will be important for long-term growth expectations.
Impact of tariffs and supply chain dynamics: Pay attention to any commentary on how new global tariffs, potential tariff refunds, and geopolitical events (such as those in the Middle East) are affecting costs, inventory, and pricing strategies in the near term.
