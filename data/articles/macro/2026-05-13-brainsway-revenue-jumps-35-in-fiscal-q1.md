BrainsWay (NASDAQ:BWAY) began 2026 with a clear business win as it accelerated shipments and recurring revenue growth. The company, a developer of noninvasive neurostimulation devices for psychiatric and neurological disorders, reported its first-quarter results on May 13, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Notable Developments
The company shipped a record 117 Deep TMS (transcranial magnetic stimulation) systems, up 44% year over year, raising its installed base to about 1,820 units.
Gross margin remained steady at 75%, indicating that cost controls kept pace with revenue and shipment growth.
Cash used in investing activities increased, causing the cash balance to drop by roughly $9 million to $58.9 million over the quarter.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $66–$68 million, aiming for up to 30% annual growth and operating margins of 13%–14%. It highlighted upcoming regulatory filings as key milestones, specifically an FDA submission for treating post-traumatic stress disorder in depression patients. Investors should monitor progress on payer adoption and how effectively new investments translate into higher recurring revenue and operating cash flow.

Quarterly highlights

Shipments of Deep TMS systems reached a new high, reflecting growing acceptance among clinics and stronger relationships with mental health networks. The company cited payer adoption and broader support for its protocols as helping to drive usage higher, with remaining performance obligations rising 25% from the prior year period to about $75 million—a measure of total future contracted revenue yet to be recognized.

Profitability improved across several measures as both net income and operating income more than doubled year over year. Adjusted EBITDA, a measure of earnings that strips out non-cash expenses and one-time items, also jumped, showing a stronger recurring profit base.

BrainsWay made sizable investments in external ventures, including a $6 million loan to Neurolief, plus stakes in two other neuroscience startups. While these moves expand its addressable market, they contributed to a notable reduction in cash, driven also by higher trade receivables and investment activity, which will be worth monitoring next quarter.

What BrainsWay does

BrainsWay develops and markets noninvasive brain stimulation devices used to treat psychiatric and neurological disorders in clinical and hospital settings.
