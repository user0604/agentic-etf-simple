Eversource Energy Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 11:09 AM

Comments

Eversource Energy (NYSE:ES) is a leading regulated utility serving approximately 4.6 million electric, gas, and water customers across New England. The company’s long-term growth strategy centers on investing heavily in regulated transmission and distribution infrastructure, supporting grid modernization, and enabling the region’s ambitious clean energy transition. Eversource pursues a disciplined capital allocation plan, leveraging predictable regulated returns and maintaining focus on balance sheet strength, cash flow durability, and a steadily growing dividend.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Transmission and Grid Modernization Investments Provide Durable Rate Base Growth

Sustained capital deployment in critical infrastructure is set to drive compounding earnings power. Eversource Energy has committed to $26.5 billion in capital expenditures through 2030, with $5.1 billion targeted for 2026 alone—including major grid investments to support regional reliability and electrification. These projects are directly aligned with state and federal policy mandates for decarbonization, leading to robust rate base expansion. In Q1 2026, non-GAAP earnings per share (EPS) rose to $1.73, up $0.23 year over year, primarily on rate base growth. As state regulators increasingly back forecast-based rate-setting and advanced metering infrastructure rollouts, Eversource is well positioned for multi-year, inflation-protected growth in its investable base, which supports long-term earnings visibility and downside protection for investors.

2. Constructive State Regulation and Securitization Bolster Cash Flow and Liquidity

Supportive state policies are enhancing cash flow resilience and balance sheet flexibility. Recent approvals—including Connecticut’s $100 million storm reserve fund and authorization of storm cost securitization laws—mean Eversource is expected to recover about $2 billion in deferred storm costs in Connecticut and New Hampshire over the next 12–18 months. This cash influx will help mitigate regulatory lag, reduce interest expense, and minimize new equity needs. State regulators have also approved the sale of the Aquarion Water business, further fortifying the company’s capital structure and enabling portfolio optimization. These constructive outcomes underscore a favorable regulatory environment at the state level, providing predictability for capital investment reimbursements and ongoing rate base growth.

3. Revolution Wind and Clean Energy Investments Anchor Long-Term Strategic Value

Landmark clean energy projects strengthen Eversource’s strategic positioning in a transforming industry. The Revolution Wind offshore project is 95% complete and on track for commercial operation in the second half of 2026, bringing 704 megawatts of new renewable capacity by year-end. The company is directly enabling New England’s clean energy future by interconnecting more than 2.6 gigawatts of new supply from wind and hydroelectric sources, critical for meeting forecasted 15–50% electricity demand growth through 2045. These projects are not only essential for meeting policy targets, but their inclusion in the regulated rate base locks in stable, long-lived returns, while cementing Eversource as a preferred partner for energy transition investments in the region.

4. Prudent Financial Strategy and Resilient Dividend Appeal to Defensive Investors

Strong cash flow coverage, conservative leverage, and an attractive dividend yield reinforce Eversource’s defensive profile. Eversource’s balance sheet remains robust, with funds-from-operations to debt ratios of 14.2–14.5%, well above rating agency downgrade thresholds, and $1.5 billion of recent hybrid financing was five times oversubscribed. The company increased its annual dividend by 5.2% in early 2026, supporting an annualized yield near 4.6%—notable in a volatile market climate. Management projects $800 million to $1.1 billion in new equity over five years but enjoys flexibility on timing, given expected cash inflows from asset sales and securitizations. This financial discipline supports capital expenditures while reducing dilution risk, making the stock appealing for long-term, income-focused investors.

5. Macro Tailwinds from Regional Electrification and Policy Demand Fuel Long-Run Opportunity

Surging regional electricity demand and coordinated state support provide structural growth engines. Massachusetts’ recent executive order and ISO New England’s projections call for up to 50% growth in electricity usage by 2045, reflecting broad policy consensus around electrification and clean energy adoption. Eversource is directly involved in advancing new substations, transmission links, and modern grid programs to accommodate this trend. As a result, the company stands to benefit from both the volume and value of utility infrastructure needed to power electric vehicles, heat pumps, and data-driven commercial growth. These long-term trends give Eversource a multi-decade runway to grow rate base and match broader utility sector outperformance as electrification accelerates across the region.

Potential Bear Cases
1. FERC Transmission ROE Reset Creates Earnings Uncertainty and Regulatory Overhang

Lower federal returns and unresolved litigation could suppress profitability for years. The Federal Energy Regulatory Commission’s (FERC) March 2026 order reducing base allowed return on equity for transmission to 9.57% (from 10.57%) represents a direct threat to Eversource’s highest-growth business segment. The company is pursuing appeals and updated filings (seeking an 11.39% ROE), but the timeline for final resolution could stretch into late 2027, prolonging investor uncertainty. As transmission comprises a major driver of consolidated earnings, adverse rulings or extended refund periods may pressure profitability, limit upside, and keep valuation multiples compressed until clarity is achieved on the ultimate allowed return and potential retroactive liabilities.

2. Large Capital Plan Faces Risk from Delayed Regulatory Approvals or Cost Overruns

Execution and regulatory recovery risks threaten earnings growth and balance sheet health. Eversource’s ambitious $26.5 billion capital expenditure plan depends on timely approvals, effective project management, and reliable cost recovery through regulated rates. Major initiatives such as Advanced Metering Infrastructure in Connecticut and large grid modernization projects await final regulatory sign-offs, while sector inflation and supply chain volatility risk exceeding original budgets. Any delays, denied recovery, or unanticipated cost increases would undermine both near-term earnings and long-term return on invested capital. Further, such setbacks could force incremental equity or debt issuance—raising dilution and leverage risks that would diminish the defensive attributes investors expect from a regulated utility.

3. Prolonged Regulatory Lag and Policy Shifts May Hamper Long-Term Growth Targets

Persistent friction with state or federal regulators could constrain projected returns and dividend growth. Eversource maintains a long-term non-GAAP EPS growth target of 5–7% annually, but the basis for this outlook is now being recalibrated amidst shifting regulatory landscapes. Connecticut and Massachusetts agencies continue to press for affordability, strict cost oversight, and lower allowed returns, raising the bar for future rate case success. Meanwhile, policy transitions at ISO New England or wavering state support for large infrastructure investments could lead to further regulatory lags or unfavorable rulings. Such headwinds put pressure on the company to contain costs, sustain payout growth, and deliver the long-term compounding that utility investors seek. If not managed adeptly, these factors could lead to further guidance resets and reduced investor confidence in the earnings trajectory.
