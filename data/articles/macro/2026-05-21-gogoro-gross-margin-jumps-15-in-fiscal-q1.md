What Gogoro does

Gogoro operates an electric vehicle ecosystem focused on battery swapping services and smart electric scooters, mainly serving urban mobility needs in Taiwan.

Notable developments
The energy (battery swapping) business produced $36.6 million in revenue, up 6.2% year over year, while hardware revenue fell 9.8%.
Gross margin rose sharply to 20.4%, mainly due to the end of costly battery upgrade programs and improved network utilization.
Positive operating cash flow of $3.1 million reflected better working capital control and disciplined spending, as operating expenses fell by $2.5 million from last year.
Quarterly highlights

The energy unit, which runs the battery swapping network, saw a 4% year-over-year increase in subscribers and higher retention, driving stronger recurring revenue and service margins. The retirement of first-generation batteries helped lower costs, with management expecting further benefits once newer batteries launch.

Hardware and vehicle sales underperformed, with nearly a 10% year-over-year revenue drop as the company shifted sales toward more affordable, entry-level electric scooters. This diluted the average selling price, even though unit registrations climbed nearly 33% over last year.

International and non-Taiwan sales declined, leaving the company dependent on Taiwan, which accounts for about 95% of revenue. The lack of new details on the Foxconn partnership or expansion plans means investors still await concrete news on new market growth and potential manufacturing gains.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its 2026 revenue guidance of $285-$305 million, with profitability in the energy business expected later this year. It plans to invest $30 million in network upgrades and new technology, aiming to boost the performance and efficiency of its platform. Investors should focus on whether recurring revenue growth and margin improvement in the battery swapping business can offset hardware segment weakness and drive company-wide profitability.
