Key Takeaways

Natera (NASDAQ:NTRA) delivered a standout quarter driven by higher test volumes and margin progress. The leading provider of advanced molecular diagnostics saw its commercial execution translate into a raised annual outlook for revenue and gross margin. Reporting on May 7, 2026, for the first quarter, Natera moved ahead on multiple operational fronts while remaining structurally unprofitable. Overall, the quarter showed broad-based growth but deepening net losses.

Notable developments
Revenue climbed 38.8% year over year to $696.6 million, driven partly by improved average selling prices and higher test volumes.
Gross margin reached 64.7%, rising 1.6 percentage points from the prior-year period, aided by ongoing cost optimization efforts.
Oncology testing remained the highest growth segment, with the number of oncology tests processed increasing 54.4% over the prior year.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its 2026 full-year revenue guidance to $2.74–$2.82 billion, alongside a revised gross margin range of 64–66%. It expects continued strong demand across its core businesses and projects a positive net cash inflow for the year. Investors should watch whether ongoing product innovation and reimbursement wins can offset rising expenses to drive lasting improvements in margins and cash generation.

Quarterly highlights

Natera processed over one million tests in the period for the first time, solidifying its position as a key player in both oncology and women's health diagnostics. Notably, oncology test volumes rose 54.4%, underscoring demand for its Signatera molecular residual disease platform and recent advances in its cancer monitoring portfolio.

Gross margin improvement reflects greater operational efficiency and pricing gains, as management cited "continued progress in reducing cost of revenues associated with tests processed." The revenue increase outpaced test volume growth, implying a benefit from higher average selling prices and a favorable product mix.

Net losses widened to $85.1 million, or $0.60 per share, mainly as result of higher research and development and selling, general, and administrative costs. However, the quarter marked a shift to positive net cash inflow, an important financial milestone as the company invests heavily in product development and clinical trials.

What Natera does

Natera develops and sells advanced genetic tests for oncology, women’s health, and organ health, helping physicians diagnose, monitor, and manage patients based on genetic information.
