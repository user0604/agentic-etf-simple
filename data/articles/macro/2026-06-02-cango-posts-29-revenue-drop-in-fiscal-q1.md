Cango Posts 29% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 6:18 AM

Key Takeaways
Cango posted a significant loss as heavy non-cash charges and lower Bitcoin prices hit performance.
Revenue fell sharply year over year.
Management did not provide an outlook and continues to focus on cost controls and new AI infrastructure initiatives.

Cango (NYSE:CANG) posted its largest-ever quarterly loss after writing down the value of its mining machines and digital assets. The digital infrastructure and former auto platform operator is navigating a major transition, shifting from Bitcoin mining into early-stage artificial intelligence (AI) compute services. In its results for the first quarter (released June 2, 2026), revenue contracted sharply while management highlighted cost controls and heavy debt repayment. Overall, the period was marked by steeper losses and shrinking operations, but also by significant reduction in long-term debt.

Notable Developments
Total revenue declined by 29% year over year to $102.0 million as the company cut operating mining capacity and faced lower Bitcoin prices.
Net loss reached $261.1 million, primarily driven by non-cash impairment charges on mining machines and changes in the fair value of Bitcoin assets.
Long-term debt was reduced from $557.6 million at year-end to $30.6 million after significant asset sales and repayments.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue guidance for the next quarter, emphasizing continued focus on cost discipline, liquidity, and launching its EcoHash AI compute platform. Investors should watch for updates on new customer traction and potential revenue from EcoHash, as well as progress improving cash reserves and reducing cash burn.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s revenue came in well below the prior period as mining output dropped and Bitcoin prices fell.

Cango produced 1,266 Bitcoin in the first quarter of 2026 following its decision to reduce total mining hashrate for cost efficiency.

Operating costs ballooned to $356.4 million, with over $200 million related to asset impairments and fair value changes as a result of lower Bitcoin market prices. This resulted in the net loss for the period rising to $261.1 million, significantly higher than last year’s $28.3 million loss.

Management took decisive action to deleverage, selling Bitcoin reserves to repay long-term debt and ending the quarter with just $7.2 million in cash. No material revenue was reported from the newly launched EcoHash AI initiative, which remains in the pilot phase.

What Cango Does

Cango operates digital infrastructure for Bitcoin mining and is developing services in artificial intelligence computing.
