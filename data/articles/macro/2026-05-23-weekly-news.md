1. Nvidia Beats; Wall Street Shrugs

Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA) grew revenue by 85% and earnings per share by 214% in its most recent quarter, fueled by continued strong demand for data center chips. Management is expecting more of the same in the quarters to come, while also announcing a new $80 billion share repurchase program and boosting the quarterly dividend from $0.01 to $0.25. But Wall Street was largely unimpressed, sending Nvidia shares down slightly after earnings and down 4.5% for the week.

"It's hard to imagine what Nvidia needs to do to impress the market." Seth Jayson is bemused by the relative excitement around lesser artificial intelligence (AI) companies: "There are piles of 'AI' plays out there trading way above reality based on imagined excellence, meanwhile the most consequential AI company on the planet is drawing yawns."
"The market needs a straitjacket!" On The Morning Show, Tim Beyers called Nvidia's quarter strong and the reaction "irrational." But he said the reaction is also a warning for investors in all AI-related stocks, arguing that expectations have gotten out of hand.
2. SpaceX Reaches for the Stars

Elon Musk's SpaceX filed its long-awaited Form S-1 this week ahead of an expected June initial public offering. The company, a combination of Musk's space ambitions, xAI, and the X social media site, lost $4.28 billion on revenue of $4.69 billion in the first quarter of 2026. But it has big hopes for the future, describing its mission as building "the systems and technologies necessary to make life multiplanetary" and to "understand the true nature of the universe."

"The largest actionable TAM in human history." Of all of the massive numbers in the filing, Lou Whiteman was focused on SpaceX's massive $28.5 trillion estimated total addressable market. For context, that's just a few trillion shy of U.S. gross domestic product. Most of that opportunity is in AI, Whiteman notes, which is also currently the source of much of SpaceX's cash burn. He's more optimistic about the space side of the operation for now.
"You're paying more than 100 times sales for a company that lost $5 billion last year." On Motley Fool Hidden Gems Investing, Jon Quast, Matt Frankel, and Tyler Crowe break down the good and the bad in the filing, but each concludes they are passing on the stock for now.
3. Lululemon Gets a Workout

A growing feud between Lululemon Athletica's (NASDAQ: LULU) board and founder Chip Wilson became very public this week when the company wrote a letter to shareholders, criticizing Wilson for having "outdated perspectives" and "troubling conflicts of interest" that the board says could derail a Lulu turnaround plan. The letter is a response to Wilson's effort to replace directors with what he calls "highly qualified independent nominees." Investors are taking notice, sending Lululemon shares up 7% for the week.

Who's in charge here? Lululemon shares have lost more than half their value over the past five years, and the company arguably needs a fresh start. The conflict is between two competing visions of what that reboot looks like: Wilson's board overhaul or the board's plan to bring in Nike (NYSE: NKE) exec Heidi O'Neill as CEO.
"Lululemon ... appears, to me, to be talking out of both sides of their mouth." Jim Gillies has strong opinions here, and he is siding with Wilson. The board has yet to articulate a turnaround plan, he notes, and with O'Neill not scheduled to take over until September, he finds it hard to believe input from the company's largest shareholder would stand in the way of progress.
4. SaaS Stocks State Their Case

Shares of Workday (NASDAQ: WDAY) and Zoom Communications (NASDAQ: ZM) have both been cut in half in recent years, victims of competitive pressures and, more recently, fears that new AI tools would eat into their businesses. Their quarterly results do not suggest the end is near. Workday grew earnings by 19% and revenue by 13%, saying that AI-driven product adoption is accelerating. And Zoom posted better-than-expected 8% earnings and 5% revenue growth. Investors cheered both sets of results, sending Zoom up 5% and Workday up 3% for the week.

"Who knows, maybe there's a comeback in the making here!" Yasser El-Shimy was impressed by Zoom's 184% surge in AI Companion paid users and the company's success in retaining enterprise users.
Builders versus sellers versus measurers: On Motley Fool Hidden Gems Investing, Jon Quast does a deep dive into the different types of software-as-a-service (SaaS) stocks and which categories could be most vulnerable. He's most worried about finance and marketing companies.
5. Cava Serves Up Good News

Inflation and macro concerns have weighed on restaurants this earnings season. But Cava Group (NYSE: CAVA) proved the exception to the rule, growing revenue by 32% in the quarter, powered by 9.7% same-restaurant sales and 20 new store openings. Cava is gaining traction in the Midwest, with 459 locations. Management also raised guidance for the quarter. Investors ate up the results, sending Cava shares up 5% for the week.

"We believe our guidance appropriately balances the momentum we are seeing in the business today, with a prudent view of the external environment." Company CFO Tricia K. Tolivar said the company remains cautious given the macroeconomic backdrop and forecasts "more moderate" gains in the quarters to come.
"A delicious Mediterranean bowl is apparently still near the top of the list." Tim Beyers called the same-store sales number "an enormous improvement" over last quarter's disappointment and said it could be a sign that Cava is succeeding in winning over increasingly choosy consumers.
