Fluor Posts 81% EPS Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 7:14 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Profitability and bookings fell sharply as project overruns and legal charges weighed on results.
Adjusted (non-GAAP) earnings per share dropped by more than 80% year over year, while revenue declined 8% in Q1 2026.
Management narrowed its full-year guidance and stressed the need for stronger awards in the second half.

Large project overruns and a significant legal charge in the Mission Solutions segment caused a difficult quarter for Fluor (NYSE:FLR). The global engineering and construction services leader, active in energy, infrastructure, and government contracting, released first quarter results on May 8, 2026, for the period ending March 31. Overall, the period was marked by steep drops in both profit and new contract awards, but strong operating cash flow and asset sales provided some financial cushion.

Notable Developments
The Urban Solutions segment grew revenue to $2.44 billion but posted a segment profit collapse to $6 million, due to a $37 million cost overrun on a large mining project.
Mission Solutions moved to a $71 million segment loss, as a $96 million legal charge stemming from a 2013 lawsuit erased last year's $5 million segment profit.
Company-wide new contract awards fell 54% to $2.7 billion, with both Energy Solutions and Urban Solutions seeing fewer bookings and declining backlogs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership narrowed its full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $525–$560 million, cutting the top end mainly due to project cost growth and international scheduling risks. Operating cash flow outlook remains steady at $300 million. Investors should pay close attention to the pace of new awards and gross margins in the Urban Solutions and Energy Solutions segments in the remainder of the year, as management projects improvement in the back half.

Quarterly Highlights

The Urban Solutions division, which handles projects in infrastructure, life sciences, mining, and technology, delivered higher revenue but almost no profit due to the significant mining project overrun. While top-line growth reflected ongoing strength in core markets, project execution issues weighed heavily on segment profitability.

In the Mission Solutions group—focused on U.S. government and nuclear contracts—revenue fell and legacy legal costs damaged results, with the segment reporting a $71 million loss. The charge highlights the lingering risks from older contracts and legal disputes in the government services business.

Across all business lines, total new contract awards—the value of signed new work—dropped steeply from the prior year. Nearly all new bookings are structured as reimbursable contracts, which lower the risk of future losses but often yield slimmer profit margins.

What Fluor Does

Fluor delivers engineering, procurement, construction, and project management services for customers in energy, infrastructure, government, and advanced technology industries worldwide.
