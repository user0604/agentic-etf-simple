1. Roku Revamps the Home Screen

★ ROKU is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Roku (NASDAQ: ROKU) unveiled its first major home-screen redesign in a decade, introducing an AI-powered personalized main screen that learns user habits and shifts throughout the day based on who's watching. The platform reaches more than 100 million households, and the revamp opens the door to new advertising opportunities.

Personalization at the core: The new layout stores historical data to sharpen recommendations over time, factoring in things like location in the house and time of day. Features include a "Top Pick for You" section pulling from viewing history and major sporting events, plus a quick access bar for most-used apps.
Ads are the real business: Advertising drives the majority of Roku's revenue -- $4.15 billion of last year's $4.74 billion total came from its platform segment. Executives say the redesign creates room to "think creatively" about new ad formats without disrupting the viewing experience.
2. Robinhood Hands Your Wallet to AI

Robinhood (NASDAQ: HOOD) unveiled tools today that let AI agents trade stocks and make purchases on users' behalf -- one of the first attempts to bring autonomous finance to retail investors instead of just hedge funds. CEO Vlad Tenev framed it as the next step in the company's mission to "democratize finance for all."

How it works: The new Agentic Trading product lets users connect third-party AI assistants to rebalance portfolios, monitor themes like AI stocks, or execute strategies automatically. A separate Agentic Credit Card lets AI agents hunt for deals and buy things using designated virtual cards. Stock trading goes live first, with options, crypto, and futures to follow.
Guardrails for the inevitable mishap: Putting autonomous trading in retail hands without institutional risk controls is a bold call. Robinhood is keeping agentic accounts separate from main portfolios, limiting AI access to capital users specifically allocate, and providing spending limits, manual approvals, and an emergency disconnect button.
3. Lululemon, Chip Wilson Call a Truce
★ LULU is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB)

Lululemon Athletica (NASDAQ: LULU) ended its boardroom battle with founder Chip Wilson, agreeing to two of his board picks in exchange for his pledge to stop publicly criticizing the company for 18 months. Shares are up 4% today on the news.

The deal: Former On Holding (NYSE: ONON) co-CEO Marc Maurer and former ESPN chief marketing officer Laura Gentile join the board after the June 25 annual meeting, with a third director jointly selected by October 1. Wilson, who owns 8.7% of the company, also agreed to a cap on his stake.
A reset moment: The truce clears the runway for incoming CEO Heidi O'Neill, a former Nike (NYSE: NKE) executive who starts in September when her non-compete expires. Lululemon's stock has tumbled more than 60% over the past year as North American sales weakened and rivals Alo and Vuori chipped away at market share.
