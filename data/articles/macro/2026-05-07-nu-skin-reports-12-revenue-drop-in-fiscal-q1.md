Key Takeaways
Nu Skin Enterprises reported continued declines across revenue, earnings, and core operating metrics. Earnings per share (EPS) fell year over year, with revenue at the low end of guidance. Management kept its full-year outlook unchanged, expecting improvement as new initiatives roll out.

Nu Skin Enterprises (NYSE:NUS) faced another challenging period as both sales and key operating metrics declined. The global beauty and wellness company saw revenue drop to the bottom of its expected range for the first quarter, with persistent weakness in customer and sales force numbers. The earnings release, published May 7, 2026, covered results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and suggested that efforts to stabilize performance remain a work in progress. Overall, the quarter reflected broad-based declines, tempered only by management’s confidence in future initiatives.

Notable Developments
Total revenue declined 12.0% to $320.6 million, hitting the low end of management’s guided range for the quarter.
Customer count shrank by 14%, with all major geographies posting lower numbers, highlighting ongoing distributor and consumer challenges.
The dividend payment remained steady at $2.9 million for the quarter, while the company allocated $5.0 million for share repurchases.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 revenue and earnings guidance, signaling belief in a second-half recovery as product launches, such as the Prysm iO wellness platform, expand more broadly. The most important factor to monitor going forward is whether customer and sales force metrics stabilize or improve as these initiatives roll out, particularly in core and emerging markets.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue continued to decrease across all major regions, with the Americas dropping 16.3%, South Korea down 22.1%, and most other areas showing single- to double-digit declines. Sales in Nu Skin’s Rhyz segment declined 16.4%, limiting any offset from diversification efforts.

Gross margin held steady at 67.9% (Non-GAAP), but operating margin slipped to 3.6% from 6.4% the previous year, as lower sales volumes pressured overall profitability. Expenses as a percentage of revenue, especially selling costs, climbed, underlining pressures on cost management as revenue softened.

Customer engagement remained under pressure, with total customers and active affiliates falling sharply compared to the prior year. Management pointed to early signs of improved paid affiliate activation from new platform launches as a possible future tailwind, but these had not yet driven measurable recovery in the quarter.
