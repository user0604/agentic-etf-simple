Cloudflare (NYSE:NET) posted a business win as it reported healthy growth in revenue and operational improvements while launching a significant organizational restructuring. Notable Developments
Revenue rose 34% compared to the same period last year, surpassing management’s stated outlook for the quarter.
Gross margin (Non-GAAP) fell by 4.3 percentage points year over year to 72.8%, reflecting higher costs for supporting rapid growth and AI-driven traffic.
Cloudflare announced a major workforce reduction of about 1,100 roles as it transitions to an "agentic AI-first operating model."
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year fiscal 2026 revenue and earnings outlook after outperforming its own guidance in the first quarter. The company now expects up to $2.813 billion in annual revenue. Investors should closely monitor Cloudflare’s ability to restore or stabilize margins as it implements restructuring and invests in AI capabilities.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue growth was robust at 34% year over year, supported by strong customer demand across its connectivity cloud and security offerings. Management noted particular momentum in current remaining performance obligations, a metric that measures revenue contracted but not yet recognized. Free cash flow also improved significantly, with a 59.0% jump from last year, providing financial resources for ongoing expansion.

Profitability faced pressure, as Non-GAAP gross margin declined to 72.8% from 77.1% a year ago, reflecting increased network and infrastructure spending as well as shifts in customer mix. Operating margin held steady at around 11%, but elevated stock-based compensation and other investment-related costs are likely to weigh on results in the near term.

A standout event in the quarter was the announced reduction of approximately 1,100 jobs, aimed at accelerating Cloudflare’s adoption of an AI-driven operating model. The company expects to incur $140–$150 million in restructuring charges, mostly in the second and third quarters, as it completes this transition. Leadership claims that this strategy will accelerate innovation, but the effects on operations and company culture remain to be seen.
