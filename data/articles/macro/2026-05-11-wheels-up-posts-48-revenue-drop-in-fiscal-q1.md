Wheels Up Experience (NYSE:UP) posted another period of substantial losses but achieved a major operational milestone by finishing its fleet upgrade far sooner than planned. The private aviation platform modernized its aircraft lineup and improved reliability, while access to fresh financing provided additional stability. The earnings release, published May 11, 2026, covers results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period showed progress in operations but highlighted ongoing financial pressures including shrinking revenue, persistent negative margins, and high cash outflows.

Notable Developments
It finished the transition to modern Bombardier Challenger 300 and Embraer Phenom 300 jets in April, 18 months ahead of plan.
Service reliability hit record levels, as completion rate rose to 98.9% and on-time performance improved by 8 percentage points year over year.
The company secured up to $200 million in new financing, including a $100 million loan led by Delta Air Lines, providing short-term liquidity.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership expects the completed fleet upgrade to bring further efficiency, grow premium bookings, and support improved margins. However, management did not issue specific revenue or profit targets for the coming quarters. Investors will be watching whether the higher-spend Signature Membership tier and new aircraft can reverse the downward revenue and margin trend while helping reduce operating cash burn.

Quarterly Highlights

The company completed its fleet transition well ahead of schedule, moving to a fleet consisting entirely of premium Challenger and Phenom jets. This led to a spike in aircraft reliability and led to a reduction in costly flight delays, as the three-hour delay rate fell to 2.0% from 5.0% last year.

The Signature Membership program, which now accounts for about a third of all members, fueled premium gross bookings per flight leg up 32%, offset by a 28% drop in total flight legs flown. Core membership and flight revenue both declined versus the previous year, and overall adjusted contribution margin dropped sharply, driven by sale of non-core businesses and fleet transition costs.

The company doubled operating cash outflows year over year, ending the period with $54 million in cash. Ongoing net losses and higher debt have increased the company’s dependence on outside financing, including the new major funding round from Delta and AIP Capital.

What Wheels Up Experience Does

Wheels Up Experience operates a membership-based private aviation platform, offering on-demand private jet flights and related travel services.
