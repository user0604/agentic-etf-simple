Notable Developments
Revenue climbed to $28.2 million, reflecting moderate year-over-year growth across billboard and broadband services.

Cash generated from operations increased to $3.9 million, helping fund share repurchases of 375,286 shares for $4.8 million during the quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue specific financial guidance for upcoming quarters, emphasizing continued discipline in capital allocation and operational execution. Investors should closely track bottom-line profitability and the influence of major equity holdings, such as Sky Harbour, on reported earnings. The most important metric to monitor is whether operating losses can be narrowed as revenue edges higher.

Quarterly Highlights

Steady, incremental revenue growth was seen in both the billboard rentals and broadband services, with each segment adding to overall stability even as insurance results remained mostly flat. The insurance business saw premiums earned edge down slightly, while insurance commissions rose modestly. This resulted in total revenue moving up just under 2%, reflecting the collection of several stable but slow-growth businesses.

Profitability worsened at the headline level, as net loss per share increased to $(0.07) from $(0.02) in the year-ago period. Higher depreciation and non-cash investment losses, especially from Sky Harbour holdings, masked solid performance in the core business lines. The valuation of certain investments rose, but those gains were not recognized in quarterly income. Instead, volatility in these holdings produced a drag on reported results as losses were booked under the equity method of accounting. The equity method means Boston Omaha reports its share of an affiliate's profits or losses on its own income statement, not just realized sale gains or losses.

On the balance sheet side, cash flow from operations rebounded by nearly 49% compared to the prior year, helping fund active buybacks. Book value per share remained nearly unchanged, signaling relative protection of shareholder equity despite net reported losses. Leverage was little changed, and overall financial health remained solid, with more than $48 million in unrestricted cash and investments.
