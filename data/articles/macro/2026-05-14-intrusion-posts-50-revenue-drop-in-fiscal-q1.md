Intrusion (NASDAQ:INTZ) suffered a significant business setback as delays in government contract awards weighed heavily on its quarterly results. The cybersecurity firm, recognized for its proprietary threat intelligence and real-time network protection technologies, struggled to maintain momentum amid these challenges. On May 14, 2026, the company reported results for the first quarter, showing a marked widening in losses and a steep drop in revenue. Overall, the period highlighted mounting pressure around execution and financial stability with declines across most operating metrics.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell by 50% as a key federal contract remained delayed, and management reaffirmed its expectation of recovering delayed revenue in future periods.
Operating expenses increased to $4.2 million, reflecting the company’s ongoing investment in sales and product development despite weakening revenue.
Intrusion closed the period with $1.4 million in cash, later securing $3 million in additional financing to support near-term operations.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management continues to forecast a return to profitability by the end of fiscal 2026, although the precise recovery timeline for the delayed government revenue is now unclear. Investors should watch for the pace of government contract resumption and actual revenue recognition from signed deals, as these remain pivotal to both cash flow and overall business health.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue dropped by half versus the prior year, chiefly due to timing issues with an important U.S. government contract. This outcome contrasts with earlier management statements that expected the delayed contract revenue to be recognized in the first half; the quarter showed that recovery had not occurred by March 31.

Operating expenses rose by nearly a quarter, driven by higher sales and marketing as well as development costs. The absence of revenue progress, however, pushed net loss to $3.6 million, which is a steeper loss rate than full-year 2025’s average.

Liquidity emerged as a pressing issue, with cash reserves falling to $1.4 million by quarter’s end and the company opting for an additional secured financing deal in April. Intrusion highlighted in its earnings release that new business wins, such as a $4 million state contract and the launch of the P.O.S.S.E. cyber defense program for law enforcement, are key future growth drivers, but those wins have not yet translated into recognized revenue.
