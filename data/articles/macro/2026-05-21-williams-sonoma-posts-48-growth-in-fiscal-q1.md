Williams-Sonoma posted a broad-based gain as all brands showed positive comparable growth.

Williams-Sonoma (NYSE:WSM) achieved growth across all major brands this quarter, showing momentum at the start of fiscal 2026. The specialty home furnishings retailer, known for iconic banners such as Pottery Barn, West Elm, and Williams Sonoma, reported first-quarter results on May 21, 2026, for the period ending May 3, 2026. Overall, the quarter saw higher sales and continued strong consumer demand even as some margin pressure remained.

Notable Developments
Every major brand reported positive comparable sales, with West Elm standing out for 8.5% comp growth and Pottery Barn Kids & Teen rising 4.5%.
Operating margin dipped to 16.2%, below last year’s level, mainly due to ongoing tariff costs and higher operating expenses.
Operating cash flow was $156 million, and the company returned $373 million to shareholders, including an increased dividend payout.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its fiscal 2026 outlook for revenue growth of 2.7% to 6.7% and an annual operating margin of 17.5% to 18.1%. It noted that higher tariffs and related inventory costs will continue to affect margins in the first half of the year. The main factor to monitor is whether operating margins recover later in the year as tariff impacts fade and supply chain efficiencies gain more traction.

Quarterly Highlights

West Elm, the modern furniture and home goods brand, delivered a sharp acceleration with 8.5% comparable revenue growth and 7.8% total sales growth, reflecting successful product launches and collaborations. Pottery Barn Kids & Teen remained strong with 4.5% comp growth, sustaining momentum from prior quarters. The core Williams Sonoma brand posted 5.0% comp growth, underscoring ongoing demand for kitchen and cooking products.

While revenues and overall free cash flow rose, merchandise inventories increased 9.0% from a year earlier, driven by an estimated $60 million in higher tariff costs as products flowed through the supply chain. The operating margin, which measures profit as a percentage of revenue, slipped to 16.2%, below the full-year outlook, due primarily to these cost pressures.

The company returned $373 million to shareholders through share repurchases and dividends during the quarter, with the quarterly dividend rising to $0.76 per share. The total store count remained steady at 506 locations, a sign that growth is shifting to digital and comparable sales rather than new openings.

What Williams-Sonoma Does

Williams-Sonoma is a home furnishings retailer that sells cookware, furniture, and home accessories through brands including Pottery Barn, West Elm, and Williams Sonoma, operating both stores and a large e-commerce platform.
