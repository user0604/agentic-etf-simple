Key Takeaways
The company delivered significant operational gains in power generation and oil and gas, but deep negative cash flow and large hedge losses cut into overall progress.
Earnings per share rose 81.3% year over year, while revenue climbed 84.1% to $807.8 billion pesos in the first quarter.
Management gave no formal financial outlook; investors should watch for future updates on capital project execution and the evolving regulatory environment.

Pampa Energía (NYSE:PAM) saw strong results in its core energy and hydrocarbon businesses, yet overall gains were overshadowed by a steep operating cash deficit and heavy losses from derivative financial instruments. The leading integrated electricity, oil and gas, and petrochemical producer in Argentina reported its first quarter results on May 13, 2026, covering the period ended March 31, 2026. While core operations made clear year-on-year progress, unusually large hedging and currency impacts reduced equity and liquidity growth in the period.

Overall, the quarter highlights major advances in revenue and profit from electricity generation and oil and gas, but also raises questions on financial resilience as capital outlays, cash burn, and regulatory uncertainty persist.

Notable developments
Electricity generation revenue rose 90% to $392.99 billion pesos as installed capacity increased to 5,472 megawatts.
Oil and gas segment revenue jumped 118% year over year and saw a $28.75 billion loss from hedging activity.
Operating cash flow reversed from a positive $93.89 billion to negative $330.23 billion pesos.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management provided no formal guidance for full-year financials or segment targets. The company continues with large-scale capital projects, and future performance will depend on regulatory approvals, completion of new investments, and ongoing market conditions. Investors should monitor the company's liquidity position and progress on projects like Rincón de Aranda, especially given the persistent volatility in Argentina’s regulatory and economic environment.

Quarterly highlights

Electricity generation and transmission segments posted strong revenue and profit growth, with improved tariffs and 5,472 megawatts of installed capacity. This figure represents around 12% of Argentina’s power supply and supports the company’s strategy of vertical integration.

The oil and gas division saw a surge in segment revenue, reflecting both rising production and higher Brent crude oil prices. However, hedge strategies capped realized benefits and led to substantial mark-to-market losses in equity and comprehensive income, erasing most of the period’s equity gains.

Despite robust operating income, free cash flow was deeply negative, driven by higher capital expenditures and a significant rise in receivables. The cash position at quarter-end fell to $326.1 billion pesos, down sharply from year-end levels, highlighting increased reliance on future collections or external funding.

What Pampa Energía does

Pampa Energía is a diversified Argentine energy company that generates electricity, produces and explores for oil and gas, and manufactures petrochemical products.
