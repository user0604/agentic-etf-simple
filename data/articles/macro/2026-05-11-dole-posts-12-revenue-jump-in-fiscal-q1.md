Fresh produce company Dole (NYSE:DOLE) posted robust sales gains but saw profitability shrink under cost pressure. As a major supplier of globally sourced fresh fruits and vegetables, the group reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 11, 2026. The numbers reflect healthy demand but also ongoing cost inflation, with overall results showing top-line strength but lower earnings compared to last year.

Overall, the quarter highlighted resilient sales momentum, but rising fruit costs — especially in bananas and pineapples — squeezed margins and profits.

Notable Developments
Group revenue jumped 11.6 %, reflecting strong consumer demand, favorable currency shifts, and higher pricing in core markets.
Profitability weakened, with adjusted EBITDA down 4.3 % and Fresh Fruit profits falling 17 % due to fruit sourcing cost inflation.
Free cash outflows improved by nearly $91 million year over year, as lower working capital needs reduced operating cash strain.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management kept its forecast for at least $400 million in adjusted EBITDA for fiscal 2026, underscoring confidence in pricing adjustments and operational improvements as the year progresses. Leadership cautioned that shipping and fuel costs may rise further in the next quarter, particularly for the Fresh Fruit segment. Investors should closely monitor whether planned price increases and supply chain actions help restore profit margins in the second half.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure rose strongly across all regions, fueled by robust demand and higher pricing for bananas, pineapples, and plantains. The Diversified Fresh Produce segment in Europe, Middle East, and Africa (EMEA) saw revenue climb 14.6 %, with currency effects accounting for much of the gains in markets like France and Germany.

Profitability, however, moved in the opposite direction. In Fresh Fruit, profits fell sharply even as sales rose, as higher sourcing prices — especially for bananas — could not be fully passed along to customers. This segment remains the company’s largest and plays an outsized role in overall earnings.

Cash generation improved, as lower seasonal working capital outflows and smaller capital expenditures contributed to a much smaller free cash outflow compared to the prior year. The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.085 per share, signaling continued capital discipline.

What Dole Does

Dole is a leading global supplier and marketer of fresh fruits and vegetables, operating across production, sourcing, packaging, and logistics.
