Key Takeaways
The company's rapid revenue and store growth was overshadowed by a steep rise in net loss compared to the prior year.
Revenue rose 33.4% over the prior year.
Management maintains a focus on store expansion and growth investment, while warning that higher costs will continue to impact profitability.

Discount grocer BBB Foods (NYSE:TBBB) posted another quarter of strong sales and store growth, but reported a much larger net loss as costs increased sharply. The chain, one of Mexico’s largest discount supermarket operators serving low- and middle-income households, continues to expand rapidly and build out its store network. The company released first quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 27, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. Overall, the quarter’s results reflected robust top-line growth but persistent pressure on the bottom line due to rising administrative and expansion costs.

Notable Developments
Total revenue climbed 33.4%, supported by 123 net new store openings and double-digit same-store sales growth.
Net loss worsened to Ps.558.3 million as share-based compensation expenses rose sharply and administrative costs nearly doubled.
Cash generated from operating activities was up 64%, reflecting strong sales and the company’s use of negative working capital.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated its goal to prioritize growth and market penetration over near-term profitability, with no explicit forward guidance on profits for the year. Investments in new stores, talent, and infrastructure are set to continue, and the company aims to open hundreds more locations by the end of fiscal 2026. Investors should watch ongoing trends in expense growth and the impact of large non-cash compensation charges on future results.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s aggressive expansion strategy added 123 net new stores during the quarter, bringing the total to 3,469 and confirming its long-term plan to widen its footprint across Mexico. This expansion, alongside higher sales from existing stores, was a key contributor to the 33.5% revenue increase year over year.

Operating costs, especially administrative expenses driven by share-based compensation, surged this period. Specifically, non-cash share-based payment expense was Ps.721.5 million, a 238% increase over the same period last year, and was the primary reason for the swing to an operating loss and a much larger net loss.

Despite these losses, operating cash flow remained strong, rising 64% from a year ago. The business continued to benefit from its model of negative working capital, with quick inventory turnover and supplier payment terms providing solid liquidity even as expenses trended higher.

What BBB Foods Does

BBB Foods operates a large chain of discount grocery stores focused on serving value-conscious consumers in Mexico with a wide assortment of essential products.
