Reinsurance Group of America (NYSE:RGA) benefited from favorable claims experience and higher investment income, leading to stronger results for the quarter. The global reinsurance provider, which specializes in life and health reinsurance and financial solutions, released its earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter brought steady growth and positive contributions from nearly every geography.

Notable Developments
Adjusted operating income per share rose to $6.97, a 23.1% jump compared to the prior-year quarter, supported by higher investment income and premium growth.
Net premiums increased 14.3% to $4.60 billion due to broad-based growth across all regions and a $103 million foreign currency benefit.
The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.93 per share, and RGA repurchased $50 million in stock during the period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reported that many regions and segments exceeded its own expectations and described its ongoing business pipeline as “healthy,” reiterating confidence for the rest of 2026 and beyond. However, it did not provide new specific guidance for full-year earnings or return on equity. Investors should watch for updates on the company’s tax rate and monitor whether the current momentum in investment income and premiums persists in future quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

All major geographies contributed to the company’s higher net premiums, with particularly strong earnings growth in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa (EMEA), as well as Asia Pacific. The company’s adapted product mix, including traditional life and health reinsurance as well as financial solutions, helped fuel this broad-based expansion. In the U.S. and Latin America, traditional business earnings were essentially flat year over year, but this was offset by substantial gains in the financial solutions segment.

Investment income, which refers to earnings generated from the company’s investment portfolio, grew 19.3% compared to the same quarter last year, owing to both higher yields and a larger invested asset base. A higher average return on investment provided a meaningful lift to both earnings and return on equity. This reliance on investment income underscores the importance of tracking market interest rate movements in assessing the company’s future profitability.

The company’s effective tax rate increased to about 24.9%, noticeably higher than its previously guided range of 22 to 23%. Management cited the impact of where profits were earned by jurisdiction and a larger-than-expected valuation allowance on tax credits. This tax rate development introduces some uncertainty for earnings in upcoming quarters.
