Key Takeaways
CapsoVision reported stable commercial momentum but faced mounting cost pressures. EPS loss narrowed year over year. Management highlighted upcoming FDA milestones as the key near-term focus.

CapsoVision (NASDAQ:CV) maintained revenue stability but experienced margin pressure and higher costs in the latest quarter. The medical device company develops capsule endoscopy solutions used for imaging the digestive tract. Its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 were released on May 14, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter reflected steady demand from major healthcare customers but featured a rise in spending ahead of anticipated regulatory and product milestones.

Notable Developments
Revenue was stable as contract renewals from major health systems offset a slight decline in overall procedure volumes, which fell 3% from the prior year.
Gross margin declined from 54% to 48%, which the company attributed to increased costs from U.S. government tariffs affecting manufacturing inputs.
Operating expenses rose 22% year over year, driven mainly by a 43% increase in research and development investment supporting product enhancements and clinical studies.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management emphasized that anticipated U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) clearance for its AI-enabled capsule feature remains its main near-term objective. It did not provide detailed financial guidance but suggested upcoming regulatory decisions will be an important turning point. The most significant factor for investors is whether the company secures timely approvals for new products targeting expanded clinical applications.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue remained unchanged from the prior-year quarter, reflecting stable demand from key U.S. gastrointestinal network customers, including Indiana University Health and Providence Health Systems. While total capsules used declined, this was offset by a modest increase in average selling prices.

Profitability weakened as gross margin fell 6 percentage points, which the company said was due to higher manufacturing costs linked to tariffs rather than reductions in product pricing. Higher expenses were driven by research and development work on AI-enhanced CapsoCam Plus, a capsule device designed to record 360-degree digestive-tract images, and ongoing clinical trials for CapsoCam Colon aimed at colorectal screening.

The company finished the period with a cash balance of $17.9 million following a $14 million equity raise in March, providing increased financial flexibility while investments in new technologies and regulatory submissions continue to push up operating losses.

What CapsoVision does

CapsoVision develops and markets capsule-based imaging solutions for gastrointestinal diagnostics, including AI-enabled panoramic visualization technologies.
