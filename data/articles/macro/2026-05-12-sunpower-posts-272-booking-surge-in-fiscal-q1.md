Key Takeaways
SunPower experienced a business setback as revenue fell short of its own guidance, with a Q1’26 revenue of $72.8 million, down 9% from its $80 million guidance.
Bookings surged 271.5% year over year to a record 4,446 jobs, reflecting the impact of recent acquisitions.
Management guided for a return to profitability by Q3 2026, supported by record bookings and aggressive cost cuts.

SunPower (NASDAQ:SPWR) struggled this quarter as sales failed to meet its targets, with Q1 2026 revenue at $72.8 million compared to its $80 million guidance. The company, a national provider of residential solar installations and digital management tools, faced these challenges as it worked to absorb recent acquisitions and implement cost-saving actions. The results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (released May 12, 2026), highlight the complexity of integrating new businesses and managing costs amid tightening cash conditions. Overall, the quarter showed mixed progress: strong customer bookings, but deeper operating losses and an urgent need for operational discipline.

Notable developments
Revenue of $72.8 million was $7.2 million below the company’s $80 million Q1 2026 guidance.
Record bookings surged 271.5% year over year to 4,446 jobs, driven largely by recent acquisitions of regional solar firms.
Immediate cost-cutting actions, including a reduction in workforce and a shift to a four-day workweek, are expected to reduce operating expenses by $9.9 million per quarter beginning in Q2 2026.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expects revenue to modestly recover to $75 million in Q2 2026, with further improvement forecast for Q3. It aims to reach break-even operating profit if revenue meets or exceeds $76 million later this year. The key factor for investors is whether the company can successfully convert its backlog of new bookings into actual revenue as integration challenges remain high.

Quarterly highlights

Booking activity set a new record, more than tripling from the previous year, as the company integrated sales operations from acquired businesses—including Sunder Energy, Ambia Energy, and Cobalt Power Systems. This growth expands SunPower’s national reach but puts pressure on its installation and back-office teams.

Operating income, excluding non-cash costs and one-time charges, fell to a $12.9 million loss as spending on staff, integration, and system upgrades ran ahead of incoming cash. However, gross margin improved by 10 percentage points year over year, indicating progress on pricing and cost control in completed jobs.

SunPower ended the period with a slim $9.5 million in cash, making it vulnerable to further delays or disruptions in revenue collection. Management has responded by reducing headcount by over 100 employees and cutting back on less profitable sales channels to preserve liquidity and focus on core operations.

What SunPower does

SunPower provides residential solar installation services and operates a digital platform to manage the design, sale, and installation of rooftop solar energy systems across the United States.
