Healthcare's Labor Gap Fuels Covista

The theme from Covista's fiscal third quarter can probably be summarized as, "We did what we said we were going to do."

Jim Gillies

May 11, 2026, 9:23 AM

Comments
9.7

If you've spent any time watching my appearances on Fool24 or are a member of Hidden Gems Canada, where I spend most of my days, you'll know that my "schtick" is the small, the perhaps strange, the misunderstood. Basically, companies that don't get a lot of love around Fooldom aside from me.

One such company reported last week, and let's just say that early results validate that I've chosen to recommend it twice in HG Canada. I'm talking about Covista (NYSE: CVSA), the for-profit education company with a focus on medical professions (nurses, doctors, nurse practitioners, veterinarians). If that company name doesn't seem familiar, you may remember Covista under its prior name, Adtalem Global Education, with the name (and ticker) switch becoming official in February of this year.

The theme from Covista's fiscal third quarter can probably be summarized as, "We did what we said we were going to do." Enrollment surpassed 100,000 across Covista's three main segments (Chamberlain, Walden, and Medical/Veterinary) for the first time, up 6.8% year-over-year, an 11th straight quarter of growth. Notably, enrollment at Walden, which has stalled out for a few quarters (and was the catalyst for a sharp sell-off two quarters back that led to our opportunistic second official Gems recommendation of Covista/Adtalem) has picked back up. The strength of the quarter led to increased revenue and earnings guidance.

The opportunity for Covista is, in my opinion, large. AI might be reshaping a ton of industries and workflows ... including those within the medical profession. But it's not going to fluff your pillows, change your dressings, draw your fluids, or empty your bedpans. Technocrats may fantasize about fully robotic surgeries ... but they seem disinterested in human connection at bedside. I don't think those are going to be supplanted by AI. And I think that's going to persist.

The most interesting thing on the quarterly conference call from CEO Stephen Beard was that there are approximately 700,000 healthcare related jobs posted every month in the U.S. At any given time, there are scarcely 300,000 unemployed healthcare workers to fill those positions. Hence the need for what Covista offers.

Oh, and they're a shareholder-friendly company as well, producing considerable cash flows above what's necessary for their business, and so the fate of excess cash is to steadily improve the balance sheet and to buy back shares, shrinking the share count. And the price, while not as great as it was six months back, is still pretty decent -- enough to warrant a starter position, I think.
