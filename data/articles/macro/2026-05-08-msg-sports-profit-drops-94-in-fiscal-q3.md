MSG Sports Profit Drops 94% in Fiscal Q3

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 7:53 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
MSG Sports saw underlying fan demand remain strong, but profits sharply declined as expenses surged.
Revenue rose compared to the prior year, but operating income dropped 94% year over year.
Management is exploring a spin-off of the Knicks and Rangers, and investors should watch cost controls and playoff progress.

Madison Square Garden Sports (NYSE:MSGS) reported robust ongoing fan interest, but profit margins sharply contracted as expenses outweighed modest revenue growth. The owner of the New York Knicks and New York Rangers, two major professional sports franchises, continues to rely on live events, ticketing, and media rights. The company released its fiscal third quarter results for the period ended March 31, 2026, on May 8, 2026.

Despite solid per-game demand, profit dropped substantially due to rising payroll costs and league financial obligations. Overall, the quarter reflected healthy consumer appetite but exposed significant new pressures on profitability for MSG Sports.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 2% as all key per-game categories, like tickets and merchandise, saw higher averages, even with five fewer home games played.
Operating income fell 94%, driven by a $38.2 million jump in direct operating expenses, mainly from higher player pay and league-related costs.
The company announced it is weighing a potential spin-off to separate the Knicks and Rangers into distinct public firms, which could change future reporting and structure.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide numerical financial guidance for upcoming quarters. Its main message focused on continued demand strength for the franchises, ongoing cost pressures, and the ongoing evaluation of spinning off the Knicks and Rangers. Investors should closely watch expense trends, deferred revenue levels, and the impact of team playoff progress on short-term profits.

Quarterly Highlights

Despite only a small increase in revenue, MSG Sports benefited from higher national media rights payments, which added $27.0 million thanks to the NBA’s new contract and an initial NBA Cup payment. This partially offset lower local TV fees, which dropped by $4.0 million primarily due to a reduction in the number of games exclusively available to MSG Networks, and were also impacted by newly renegotiated MSG Networks deals that introduced an 18% reduction in annual rights fees.

Direct operating expenses climbed 12%, mainly reflecting player payroll increases, NBA luxury tax charges, and higher league revenue sharing. These expense jumps were much greater than the modest revenue gain, causing both operating and adjusted operating income to fall sharply from the year-ago period.

Deferred revenue, which serves as an indicator of future ticketing and sponsorship pipeline, fell by $62.2 million over the past year, raising questions about upcoming quarters’ cash flow unless largely driven by timing. Operating cash flow for the first nine months of fiscal 2026 dropped to $5.0 million, down from $41.9 million in the prior-year period.

What Madison Square Garden Sports Does

The company owns and operates the New York Knicks (NBA) and New York Rangers (NHL), generating revenue from live events, media rights, sponsorships, and merchandise sales.
