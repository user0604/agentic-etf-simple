May Hiring Defies Rate, War Pressures

A resilient labor market kept hiring strong as service sectors picked up steam. However, stubborn energy and tech costs continue to strain household budgets and keep pressure on policymakers.

Snapshot
Headline payrolls: The U.S. economy added a robust 172,000 nonfarm payroll jobs in May, matching April's solid pace (+179,000) and dramatically exceeding the consensus forecast of 80,000 job additions.
Unemployment and wages: The unemployment rate held steady at 4.3%, matching expectations and tracking within its tight historical range, while average hourly earnings rose 0.3% for the month and 3.4% year-over-year.
Sector drivers: A surprise hiring boom in leisure and hospitality led the expansion with 70,000 jobs, alongside consistent gains in local government (+55,000) and healthcare (+35,000), which offset a 22,000 job decline in financial activities.
Upward revisions: Backdata painted an even stronger picture of labor demand, as combined payroll figures for March and April were revised upward by a substantial 93,000 roles.
Analysis & Implications
The blow-out payroll numbers reveal an economy that is successfully absorbing severe geopolitical shocks, giving the Federal Reserve ample leeway to prioritize its brewing inflation battle over growth concerns.
With April's headline consumer inflation sitting at 3.8% and wholesale inflation spiking to 6.0%, a 3.4% annual wage growth means workers are experiencing a contraction in real purchasing power, a trend that could eventually soften consumer spending.
The combination of a tight labor market and compounding price shocks—driven by a 40% surge in retail gasoline and rising AI hardware costs—substantially elevates the probability of a defensive quarter-point interest rate hike at the Fed's June 17 meeting.
Fixed-income and equity markets reacted poorly to the threat of higher-for-longer borrowing costs, evidenced by an immediate spike in Treasury yields and a sharp slide in stock futures as traders repriced rate expectations for the back half of the year.
