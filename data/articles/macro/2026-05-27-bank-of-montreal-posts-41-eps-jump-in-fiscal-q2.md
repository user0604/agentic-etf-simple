Key Takeaways
Overall, it was a business win as net income and earnings climbed sharply from last year. EPS (earnings per share) increased 41% year over year. Management repeated its long-term focus on growth, returns, and digital innovation, with caution on capital and divestiture impacts.

Bank Of Montreal (NYSE:BMO) delivered a robust quarter as strong revenue, sharply higher profitability, and better credit performance lifted results. The major North American bank operates across Canada and the United States, servicing personal, commercial, wealth management, and capital markets clients. The earnings report released on May 27, 2026, covered the second quarter, showing broad improvements alongside ongoing investment and portfolio changes. Overall, the quarter showed solid growth, with notable gains in fee businesses and risk controls, partially offset by higher costs and lower capital ratios.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 10% to $9.57 billion, driven by double-digit growth in fee-based businesses and single-digit growth in lending.
The provision for credit losses fell 30% compared to last year, reflecting improved loan quality and reduced reserves on performing loans.
The bank declared a quarterly dividend increase of 5%, raising it to $1.71 per share for the period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management has reinforced its focus on enhancing returns, investing in technology, and expanding its North American business. It did not issue specific future-quarter targets but highlighted ongoing attention to cost control, credit management, and digital innovation. Investors should closely monitor the impact of the planned divestiture of transportation finance assets and the bank’s ability to maintain strong capital levels in the coming periods.

Quarterly Highlights

Profitability rose across every major business line, with Wealth Management and Capital Markets earning particular strength on higher fee income and activity. The bank saw broad-based growth in core Canadian and U.S. personal and commercial lending, while non-interest revenue from services such as investment management, trading, and payments helped lift the overall revenue figure.

Credit quality improved, as seen in the reduction in the provision for credit losses, with the largest declines coming from U.S. banking and capital markets operations. This line item, which represents funds set aside to cover potential loan losses, was a key driver behind the year over year net income gain.

The board approved a quarterly dividend increase, highlighting confidence in the bank’s capital position and earnings power. However, the Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) capital ratio dipped slightly and will be worth tracking, especially as the upcoming sale of transportation finance assets will trigger a significant one-time charge next quarter.
