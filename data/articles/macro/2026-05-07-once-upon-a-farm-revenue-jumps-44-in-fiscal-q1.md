Key Takeaways
Strong revenue growth driven by new baby snacks and improved retail productivity.
Revenue rose 43.7% year over year to $72.7 million.

Once Upon A Farm (NYSE:OFRM) accelerated its sales growth as new products and expanded retail initiatives spurred higher demand. The leading producer of organic baby and kids’ snacks and pouches reported its first-quarter results on May 7, 2026. Overall, the quarter marked strong progress in top-line growth and margin expansion, but costs remain elevated as the business invests for scale.

Notable developments
Revenue grew 43.7% from the prior year as new baby product sales more than doubled, led by baby snacks up 121.7%.
Gross margin improved to 40.8%, rising 3.1 percentage points with lower cooler slotting fees and increased scale helping profitability.
Post-IPO, the company erased all debt and boosted its cash balance to $99.9 million from $10.9 million, increasing financial flexibility.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised the full-year 2026 net sales target to $313–$323 million, up from prior guidance, and set an improved adjusted EBITDA range of $2–$4 million. The most important factor for investors to monitor next is whether the company can deliver positive adjusted EBITDA for the rest of the year as it continues to invest heavily in new products and distribution.

Quarterly highlights

Product innovation played a significant role in the quarter as baby-focused lines, such as dry baby snacks, delivered triple-digit sales growth, with baby snacks up 121.7% year over year. Kid pouches and kid snacks saw slower expansions, with the former growing 3.9% and the latter growing by 15.7% over the same period last year.

Strong execution in retail and supply chain brought a reported 11% increase in cooler productivity quarter over quarter. The company’s cash position improved sharply thanks to IPO proceeds, enabling it to pay down all outstanding debt and add significant liquidity for future investments.

Despite the higher revenue and gross profit, selling, general, and administrative costs surged, with a notable portion due to one-time IPO expenses and stock-related payouts. Even after adjusting for these, ongoing operating costs as a percentage of revenue rose, underscoring persistent investment in growth and infrastructure.

What Once Upon A Farm does

Once Upon A Farm produces and markets organic, cold-pressed baby food pouches, snacks, and meal solutions aimed at young children and their families.
