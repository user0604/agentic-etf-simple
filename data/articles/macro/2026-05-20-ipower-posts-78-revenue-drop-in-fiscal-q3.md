Key Takeaways
iPower posted a business loss as it continued its transition to an asset-light model, leading to sharply lower sales.
Non-GAAP net loss improved as cost reductions offset most of the revenue drop.
Management did not provide forward guidance, and investors are watching for execution on new AI initiatives and e-commerce recovery.

The business reset at iPower (NASDAQ:IPW) meant another tough quarter, as rapid cost cutting helped offset much weaker sales but did not yet deliver new revenue streams. The online retailer and supply chain platform, primarily focused on e-commerce sales of home, gardening, and pet products, reported financial results for its fiscal third quarter (the period ended March 31, 2026; results released May 20, 2026). Despite progress on expense discipline and balance sheet stability, overall the period marked continued disruption as the company pivots away from divested operations into new strategic areas.

Notable Developments
Revenue declined 78% year over year, reflecting the impact of the Global Product Marketing divestiture and a shift to an asset-light operating model.
Operating expenses fell 74% year over year, with management highlighting sequential reductions as well.
Post quarter-end, management committed up to $3 million to its new AI infrastructure investment strategy.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled an ongoing focus on cost controls and pursuing “higher-quality revenue opportunities,” but did not provide specific financial guidance for the next quarter or year. With the shift toward AI infrastructure investment now underway and sublease income expected to grow, the most important factor for observers is whether new initiatives can replace lost e-commerce and service revenue and help stabilize core operations.

Quarterly Highlights

Cost reduction was a defining theme, as operating expenses dropped from $7.2 million to $1.9 million, delivering much narrower non-GAAP losses compared to the prior year. This emphasis on “expense discipline” reflects a deliberate choice by management to prioritize solvency and flexibility over near-term growth.

The company’s e-commerce platform, which relied on Amazon for over 80% of sales, saw substantially lower volume following the business reset. There were no updates on product category mix, specific channel traction, or new product introductions, leaving questions about progress in its main sales engine.

Early in its new strategy, iPower disclosed an initial commitment of up to $3 million to a yield-bearing instrument in the AI infrastructure space. Management also pointed to more than $2.6 million in contracted sublease income through 2028, providing support for fixed costs while repositioning its business model.

What iPower Does

iPower is an online retailer and platform operator that sells a range of home, gardening, and pet products mainly through e-commerce channels, while exploring investments in AI infrastructure assets.
