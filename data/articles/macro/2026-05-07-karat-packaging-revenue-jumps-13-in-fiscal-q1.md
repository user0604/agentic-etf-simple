Key Takeaways
Karat Packaging posted strong sales growth, but rising tariffs compressed margins.
Revenue rose year over year.
Management expects continued growth but cautions on cost and trade policy risks.

Karat Packaging (NASDAQ:KRT)’s sales climbed as it expanded in sustainable products and landed another large customer, but rising input and import costs eroded margins. The foodservice packaging supplier focuses on eco-friendly and custom-disposable goods for restaurant chains, distributors, and e-commerce channels. Reporting financial results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026, the company delivered robust top-line momentum but saw profitability under pressure. Overall, the quarter featured strong demand for sustainable goods, but inflation and tariffs weighed on profits.

Notable Developments
Revenue jumped 12.9% year over year, reaching $116.9 million, mostly due to strong demand in the chains and distributors channel and eco-friendly product sales.
Gross margin declined to 35.5% from 39.3% the previous year, with import duties and tariffs rising to $10.5 million, or 13.8% of sales.
Dividend payments held steady at $0.45 per share, while free cash flow slipped 5.1% to $6.3 million, reflecting higher costs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects continued revenue growth of 8–10% for the next quarter and low double-digit gains for the full year, with gross margin guidance between 34% and 36%. Reported guidance excludes any potential impact from anticipated tariff refunds, highlighting ongoing trade policy uncertainty. Investors should closely monitor the outcome of tariff changes and management’s ability to pass higher costs through pricing and maintain margin levels.

Quarterly Highlights

Eco-friendly product sales increased by nearly 17%, as the company expanded its paper bag lineup and secured another national chain contract. This helped drive strong overall top-line gains, particularly in the chains and distributors segment, which grew 15.1% and remains Karat Packaging’s largest channel.

Profitability showed mixed signals, with gross margin falling to the low end of guidance due to tariffs and higher inventory adjustments. Import costs reached $10.5 million, almost triple the prior year, and cut into earnings even as adjusted EBITDA (non-GAAP) improved 5.0%.

Cash flow generation remained positive, supporting a steady quarterly dividend at $0.45 per share. However, free cash flow dipped from a year ago, reflecting the impact of higher operating and input costs on overall financial flexibility.

What Karat Packaging Does

Karat Packaging manufactures, imports, and distributes foodservice packaging products, including environmentally friendly disposables, to restaurant chains, distributors, and retailers across North America.
