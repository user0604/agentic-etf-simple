SpaceX IPO: Vision Priced In, Proof Still Pending

SpaceX is pricing at $135 a share and $1.8 trillion in valuation — here's why one Fool isn't buying in.

Lou Whiteman

12:03 PM

Comments
10.0

TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

We now know the details of SpaceX’s IPO pricing. The question is, is it a buy?

SpaceX said it intends to sell 555.6 million shares at $135 apiece, looking to raise about $75 billion in an offering that would value the company at about $1.8 trillion.

Valuation comparisons are notoriously difficult. That is particularly true in a case like this, where there is no company that does exactly what SpaceX does. But a quick sum-of-the-parts analysis asks more questions than it answers. Here’s the tale of the tape:

Starlink: The most mature part of the business today, Starlink generated $3.26 billion in revenue in the first three months of 2026. At $13 billion a year, and valued at the same 35 times sales that Amazon (NASDAQ:AMZN) recently agreed to pay to acquire Starlink competitor Globalstar (NASDAQ:GSAT), and Starlink could be worth about $500 billion.
Space: The rocket business did $612 million in sales in the first quarter. Annualized to $2.4 billion and applying Rocket Lab’s (NASDAQ:RKLB) 95 times sales multiple, the space unit could be worth about $225 billion.
xAI: The AI business did just $818 million in first-quarter revenue, and a lot of its comparables are not publicly traded. But SpaceX only bought xAI a few months ago, so we can use the $250 billion purchase price as its valuation.

I’d note that if I were a banker trying to sell this IPO, I think I can justify those multiples based on those comparisons. But as an analyst, I’m not sold that Globalstar is worth anything near 35 times sales or Rocket Lab worth 95 times. T-Mobile (NASDAQ:TMUS), which provides the same services as Starlink (with lower capex) is only valued at two times sales.

That said, even with those generous multiples, we are still struggling to get to $1 trillion, which is 40% below the IPO target valuation.

Can the gap be closed? For me, as an individual investor, the answer is no. I will not be buying into this IPO.

That said, a similar analysis of Tesla (NASDAQ:TSLA), especially in its early years, would have yielded a similar result. There have always been an ample number of investors willing to buy into Elon Musk’s vision of the future, confident that, given enough time, Musk will be able to create value. And with Tesla, it has proven to be a wise bet.

For SpaceX to justify its valuation, a lot of things will have to go right for the company from here.
