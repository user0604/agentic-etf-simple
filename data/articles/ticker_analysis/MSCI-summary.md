MSCI Adds Texas Stock Exchange to Global Index Eligibility

MSCI is expanding the scope of its flagship global indexes by adding the Texas Stock Exchange to its list of eligible U.S. trading venues. The move ensures the company's investment decision support tools reflect the evolving landscape of American capital markets as new exchanges compete for corporate listings. As a major provider of indexes used for exchange-traded funds (ETFs) — pools of securities that trade on an exchange — MSCI updates its eligibility criteria to maintain benchmark accuracy. The company utilizes rules to "fast-track" large initial public offerings (IPOs), which are the first time a company's stock is sold to the public, to ensure indexes represent companies that matter to the economy.

MSCI Company Overview
MSCI provides critical data and specialized analytics that help institutional investors manage risk and assess market performance across global asset classes. The company generates revenue primarily through recurring subscription fees and asset-based licenses for its proprietary stock market indexes and sustainability ratings. By integrating these unique benchmarks and risk models directly into the technical workflows of asset managers and banks, the firm establishes high switching costs that ensure long-term client retention.

MSCI Grows Revenue as Index Business Attracts More Clients

MSCI helps investors by creating stock market indexes, portfolio analysis tools, and data about companies’ sustainability. The company is known for its strong focus on innovation, especially in using artificial intelligence to improve its products. Over the past five years, MSCI’s stock returned 20.2%, trailing the S&P 500’s 71.9% gain by 51.7 percentage points.
