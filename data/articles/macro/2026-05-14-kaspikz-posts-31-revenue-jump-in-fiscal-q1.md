Kaspi.kz (NASDAQ:KSPI) booked sharp revenue gains but reported flat earnings as higher costs offset revenue growth and investment in Turkey continued. The leading Kazakhstan-based fintech, e-commerce, and payments ecosystem provider posted its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026, showing strong platform expansion but persistent profit margin pressure. Overall, the quarter saw the company deliver on topline targets, but rising operational and credit costs limited bottom-line improvement.

Notable Developments
The Marketplace platform delivered 49% revenue growth in the first quarter.
Net income remained flat despite 31.5% revenue growth due to a 56% rise in cost of goods and a 46% increase in interest expenses.
The quarterly dividend was maintained at KZT 850 per share, unchanged from the previous period.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its full-year guidance for gross merchandise value, total payments volume, and adjusted EBITDA growth, targeting 20% for Marketplace, 15% for Payments, and 5% for Fintech loan originations. The most important area to watch is expense growth, especially cost of goods and loan provisioning, as continued increases could offset further revenue gains.

Quarterly highlights

Marketplace revenues accelerated, with Turkey (primarily Hepsiburada) now making up over a quarter of consolidated revenue. Investments in technology, logistics, and merchant engagement drove this growth, but also led to increased operational costs as the integration progressed.

The Payments platform recorded slower revenue growth (7%) as product mix shifted toward lower-margin offerings such as QR and B2B payments. While transaction volume expanded, the per-transaction revenue ("take rate") remained under pressure and management described margins as at a practical floor.

Fintech platform revenues climbed 25%, driven by about a 40% jump in interest revenue and a larger loan book, but provision expenses rose more than a quarter year over year. The credit risk profile shifted with a higher non-performing loan ratio, but management highlighted an increase in secured lending and a high coverage ratio to help manage potential losses.
