Key Takeaways
Revenue dropped sharply as the company transitions away from legacy energy services.
EPS loss narrowed while operating expenses increased year over year.
Management expects a sharp revenue ramp and positive adjusted EBITDA in the second half as new contracts are executed.

Duos Technologies Group (NASDAQ:DUOT) reported a revenue trough as it pivots from legacy businesses toward AI-focused infrastructure, with first-quarter results showing continued transition pains. The provider of modular edge data centers and AI computing services saw a significant drop in revenue for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (reported on May 19, 2026), but reiterated expectations for strong growth in the rest of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period reflected a company in reset mode, laying groundwork for a sharp rebound if new deployments ramp as planned.

Notable Developments
Revenue declined 45% as legacy energy contracts wrapped up, but new Technology Solutions and hosting segments began contributing.
Gross margin rose 23% despite lower sales, helped by non-cash revenue from equity stakes and sharp cost reductions.
The company completed a major $65 million equity raise, with $15 million in customer prepayments received after quarter end to support new deployments.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed a goal of more than $50 million in revenue for fiscal 2026, with most sales expected in the second half tied to infrastructure and GPU hosting contracts. It expects adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) to turn positive as new projects come online. The most important thing for investors to watch is the timing and execution of these data center and GPU deployments, which are critical to hitting the full-year targets.

Quarterly Highlights

The largest factor in the shrinking revenue was the wind-down of legacy service agreements, which led to a sharp gap before recognition of new infrastructure revenue. However, Duos posted initial revenue from its Technology Solutions division this quarter, and continued to generate hosting revenue that began in prior periods, indicating early traction in its new business model.

Despite weaker sales, gross margin increased as the company benefited from high-margin income tied to its equity interest in New APR, and cut costs in old business lines. Management noted that these unusually high margins are unlikely to continue, with future project margins forecast in the 70–80% range as new deployments scale up.

Operating expenses rose by 69% because of expanded sales, marketing, and administrative spending to support growth in digital infrastructure. With a strengthened cash position and customer prepayments in place, management said it has the liquidity needed to execute a rapid expansion in the back half of the year.
