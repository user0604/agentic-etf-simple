Quarterly highlights

The period saw total net loss drop more than 57% compared to the prior year, achieved through deep cuts to research, development, and administrative spending. This allowed the company to redirect spending toward its most promising clinical programs.

Clinical progress centered on icovamenib, an oral small molecule targeting menin, a protein involved in diabetes. New 52-week data from its ongoing Phase II trial showed potential for the drug to preserve insulin production, particularly in recently diagnosed type 1 diabetes patients.

BMF-650, a GLP-1 agonist (a drug that mimics a natural hormone to help regulate blood sugar and appetite), continues Phase I testing for obesity, with initial results expected in the second quarter of 2026. The company collaborated with major U.S. academic centers, but did not report progress on new commercial partnerships or additional revenue sources.
