Key Takeaways
Olaplex reported modest revenue growth but swung to a net loss as operating expenses surged.
Adjusted EBITDA fell 26.1% while revenue nudged up 2.5% compared to the prior-year quarter.
No new financial guidance was issued due to the pending Henkel acquisition, reducing future transparency.

Olaplex (NASDAQ:OLPX) posted a small sales gain in its latest quarter, but profitability fell sharply as spending and merger costs increased. The professional haircare company, known for its patented technology and salon-focused products, released results for the first fiscal quarter on May 11, 2026. Overall, the period showed progress in some channels but highlighted ongoing challenges in the U.S. market and signaled more uncertainty as it moves forward with an acquisition.

Notable developments
Professional channel sales increased 12.3% year over year to $38.8 million, driven by new product launches like No. 3 PLUS.
Specialty retail channel sales dropped 13.3%, while U.S. sales fell 3.5%, pointing to regional and channel weakness.
SG&A expenses surged 37.4% to $66.0 million, mainly from higher marketing spend and acquisition-related costs.
Management outlook and what to watch

Due to entering a definitive acquisition agreement with Henkel AG & Co. KGaA, management did not provide updated financial guidance and will end regular earnings communications. Investors should closely watch upcoming announcements about the acquisition process and the effect of shifting business focus on longstanding transformation plans.

Quarterly highlights

The Professional channel led the quarter with double-digit sales growth, reflecting continued success in connecting with salon professionals and launching targeted products. The Direct-To-Consumer business also grew nearly 14%, showing resilience in its online and direct distribution strategy.

Margins improved at the gross profit level, mainly from strong sales mix and scaling of new launches. However, a sharp increase in selling, general, and administrative costs, including up-front marketing and merger expenses, overwhelmed these gains and led to an adjusted EBITDA decline.

Specialty retail and U.S. sales contracted, while international markets improved. Management’s transformation plan paused as focus shifted to the impending Henkel acquisition, with future visibility now limited due to the suspension of formal guidance.

What Olaplex does

Olaplex develops and sells professional haircare products focusing on patented technology for hair repair and maintenance, serving salon, retail, and direct channels worldwide.
