1. Acquisitions Aren’t Always Good

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I remember vividly when Teladoc Health (NYSE: TDOC) announced its acquisition of Livongo for a whopping $18.5 billion in 2020. At the time, Teladoc Health was making great progress in the burgeoning virtual healthcare space, and having followed the company for a while, I was impressed. It broke down regulatory barriers and questions that came with healthcare delivery in a virtual setting, and as a user I was (and still am) impressed with its utility. But the news of that acquisition, at that price, made me very uncomfortable. I really questioned it but decided I would give it a chance at least, particularly as we were in the throes of the Covid years. But man, oh man — I should have trusted my gut on that one.

2. Fastly: When There’s Little Product With the Promise

By Tim Beyers
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Fastly (NASDAQ: FSLY) fascinated me. I loved the idea of an “edge cloud” where immense compute power would be distributed globally via small clusters of hardware positioned close to users.

Think of it as the exact opposite of the large-scale data centers we’re building for AI. Instead of a small network of mega-facilities, Fastly and its peers would deliver intelligent, interconnected nodes of compute almost anywhere you could fit a server.

Phenomenal idea. Trouble was — and to a degree, still is — the idea lacked product-level proof. I bought a vision built on too much hype and not enough hard evidence. That was a costly mistake I am working hard to never repeat.

3. The List Goes On and On

By Matt Frankel, CFP®
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Without question, the investment where (in retrospect) I ignored some pretty big red flags was Boston Omaha (NYSE: BOC). And to be honest, there were too many to list here. One was the outrageous bonuses management took from the company a few years ago, as a technicality in the incentive structure allowed the co-CEOs to collect 15 years’ worth of bonus payments at once.

Second is the complete lack of communication with shareholders when a “shut up and trust the process” attitude hadn’t been earned whatsoever. Third was the chasing of trends, like sponsoring a SPAC at the height of the 2021 bubble, despite supposedly being a long-term compounder. I ended up selling my entire position at a loss, but if I had paid more attention to these things, I would have gotten out much sooner.

4. Trees Don’t Grow to the Sun

By Anthony Schiavone

In 2021 I recommended Sun Communities (NYSE: SUI), a REIT that specialized in owning manufactured housing communities, RV parks, and marinas. The stock price fell from about $200 at the time of the recommendation to $125 today.

I overestimated the quality of Sun’s management team, extrapolated growth rates forward to justify a premium valuation, and the gap between cash flow growth and dividend growth was the red flag that I missed. Once growth stalled, the misallocation of capital from previous acquisitions became evident.

The lessons I learned from this mistake were:

Good times don’t last forever
Management should be incentivized on “per share” financial metrics
Dividend growth is the metric REIT investors should focus on.

(I still own Sun Communities and it’s still recommended in a service I help run. The company has made significant progress correcting past mistakes while returning to meaningful dividend growth).
