ZEVASKYN’s early commercial rollout gained steam as more patients received treatment, but high operating costs kept the company in the red. The rare disease gene therapy developer Abeona Therapeutics (NASDAQ:ABEO) reported its first-quarter 2026 financials on May 13, 2026, covering the first full quarter of commercial activities since its first-in-class product’s launch. Overall, the quarter delivered tangible signs of revenue growth and infrastructure progress, but ongoing high expenses delayed a move toward profitability.

Notable Developments
First quarter product revenue reached $8.7 million, driven by three patient treatments for ZEVASKYN, compared to zero revenue the year before.
Operating costs rose sharply as commercial activities expanded, with selling, general, and administrative expenses nearly doubling year over year to $19.5 million.
By quarter-end, six qualified treatment centers (QTCs) were active, and payer coverage reached 95% of commercially insured U.S. lives.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled that further expansion of qualified treatment centers and streamlining treatment processes remain top priorities for 2026. The company did not issue new guidance, but indicated that achieving higher patient treatment rates and controlling costs are essential for future profitability. The most critical factor to watch is the pace of new patient treatments, as hitting the target of at least 3.5 treatments per month is needed to approach break-even.

Quarterly Highlights

Product revenue growth was a standout, as the company treated three patients, up from just one the previous quarter and none a year ago. This shows steady, albeit gradual, market adoption for ZEVASKYN, the autologous cell-based gene therapy for recessive dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa (RDEB).

While the revenue figure showed material progress, the cost of scaling commercialization was high. Selling, general, and administrative expenses surged nearly 100% from the prior year, reflecting investments in personnel, training, and launch activities for ZEVASKYN.

The shift in company focus was visible in the pipeline strategy, as Abeona deprioritized ophthalmology programs and in-licensed a new autologous engineered T-cell therapy (ABO-701) for prostate cancer. This led to a one-time $7.0 million R&D expense but overall research spending declined as resources concentrated on commercial execution.

What Abeona Therapeutics Does

Abeona Therapeutics develops and manufactures gene therapies for rare genetic disorders, with its first approved product treating chronic skin wounds in patients with RDEB.
