Key Takeaways

Earnings per share loss narrowed to $3.13 from $5.28 year over year.

Lifeward (NASDAQ:LFWD) struggled in the first quarter with a steep revenue drop and continued adjusted operating losses. The leading provider of wearable robotic exoskeletons and rehabilitation devices saw mixed progress as it focused on tightening expenses and expanding its medical device pipeline through acquisitions. The company released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 15, 2026, highlighting significant supply chain disruptions and working capital constraints. Overall, the quarter emphasizes the importance of restoring product shipments and stabilizing underlying sales as the company pivots to a broader biomedical strategy.

Notable Developments
Revenue dropped 22% as AlterG product sales fell 38% due to supply chain challenges. ReWalk Personal Exoskeleton sales, in contrast, rose 11% year over year.
The company completed its Oratech acquisition, bringing in $6.5 million in cash and a new clinical pipeline for oral insulin.
Adjusted operating expenses fell 12%, supporting a 33% reduction in operating cash burn for the quarter despite flat operating losses.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled improved visibility for order shipment recovery during the second and third quarters following the resolution of supply chain constraints. Investments from the Oratech acquisition and new capital support future product launches, including a Phase 2 clinical study for oral insulin. Investors should monitor the pace at which AlterG sales and broader revenue shipments rebound, as margins and long-term profitability will depend on these gains.

Quarterly Highlights

The most immediate concern for the quarter was the substantial drop in overall revenue, weighed down by slower AlterG product shipments amid working capital issues. This segment, which specializes in anti-gravity rehabilitation treadmills, posted a 38% year-over-year decline, sharply impacting the top line and causing underutilization of factory capacity, which in turn compressed gross margin by nearly eight percentage points.

While AlterG sales pulled results lower, the company reported strong performance for the ReWalk Personal Exoskeleton, a wearable robotic system for mobility-impaired users. Revenue from this product grew 11% year over year, boosted by expanding Medicare Advantage reimbursement and broader international sales support. In contrast, MyoCycle functional electrical stimulation bikes remained flat, with no notable growth or decline.

Strategically, Lifeward closed its Oratech acquisition, adding $6.5 million in cash and a new biomed pipeline focused on oral insulin. The quarter also included new capital raises, lifting unrestricted cash to $11.4 million and strengthening the balance sheet for future clinical programs and the scale-up of neurorehabilitation products.
