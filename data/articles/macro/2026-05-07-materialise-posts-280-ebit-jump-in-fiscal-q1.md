Profitability improved at Materialise (NASDAQ:MTLS) in Q1 2026 as its gross and operating margins expanded despite revenue staying essentially unchanged. The 3D printing and software provider reported stable sales, but noted better operational efficiency driven by tight cost control and a sharper focus on its core Medical and Software businesses. This earnings release, issued May 7, 2026 for the first quarter, showed management holding steady on its outlook while beginning to reshape its business portfolio after certain divestitures. Overall, the period reflected disciplined cost management with moderate gains in profitability but pressured revenue growth outside the Medical segment.

Notable Developments
Medical segment sales grew 6.7%, but Software revenue slipped 1.4% and Manufacturing dropped 8.1% year over year.
Free cash flow remained positive and the company invested €2.3 million to repurchase 511,513 shares during the period.
Divestitures of the RapidFit and Eyewear business units are expected to lead to impairment charges next quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its 2026 full-year outlook for €273 to €283 million in revenue and €10 to €12 million in adjusted EBIT, already reflecting the loss of divested business contributions. The biggest area to monitor next is whether near-term revenue growth rebounds, especially as the company’s Manufacturing unit seeks to recover after recent declines and the Medical segment faces softer growth than in previous periods.

Quarterly Highlights

The Medical segment remained the company’s strongest, recording year-over-year revenue growth and accounting for just under half of total sales. Nonetheless, profit margins in Medical moderated compared to especially robust quarters in the past year, indicating possible pressure on segment profitability going forward. Meanwhile, the Software business saw significant improvement in adjusted EBITDA, even as the effort to shift toward a cloud-based, recurring revenue model caused near-term revenue contractions.

Manufacturing revenues fell by 8.1% year over year, reflecting continued headwinds in industrial and prototyping demand. However, cost reductions saw this unit return to positive adjusted EBITDA, compared with a loss in the prior year period. Management continues to reposition this segment towards higher-value niche markets, such as aerospace and defense, but this strategy is still in early stages.

On the financial side, the company’s strong cash generation supported share buybacks while also reducing gross debt. Free cash flow remained healthy, and net cash rose to €72.8 million. Shareholders’ equity was little changed, while operating expenses held broadly stable.
