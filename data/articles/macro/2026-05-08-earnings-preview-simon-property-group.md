Earnings Preview: Simon Property Group

The Motley Fool Team

May 8, 2026, 9:02 AM

Comments

Simon Property Group (NYSE:SPG), a leading owner and operator of shopping malls and premium outlets, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on Monday, May 11, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

Simon Property Group finished 2025 with record real estate funds from operations (FFO) of $4.8 billion, or $12.73 per share. For the fourth quarter, real estate FFO was $3.49 per share, representing 4.2% growth year over year. Portfolio net operating income (NOI) increased 5.1% in the quarter, and domestic property NOI grew 4.8%. Malls and premium outlets ended the year at 96.4% occupancy, while The Mills portfolio reached 99.2% occupancy. Average base minimum rents for malls and premium outlets rose 4.7% to $60.97 per square foot. Retailer sales per square foot for the year were $799, up 2% from the prior year. The company completed over $2 billion in acquisitions, finished more than 20 major redevelopment projects, and opened a new premium outlet in Indonesia. Simon returned $3.5 billion to shareholders through dividends and share repurchases. These results met or exceeded most analyst expectations for the quarter.

Positives
Record real estate FFO and NOI growth: The company delivered its highest-ever real estate FFO and posted solid NOI growth, which signals strong operational performance and cash flow generation.
High occupancy and rising rents: Occupancy ended the year at over 96% for malls and premium outlets, and average base rents increased, reflecting resilient tenant demand and effective leasing strategies.
Active acquisitions and redevelopment: Simon completed several significant property acquisitions and over 20 redevelopment projects, positioning the portfolio for future growth and value creation.
Strong balance sheet and liquidity: The company ended the year with more than $9 billion in liquidity and maintained an A-rated balance sheet, providing flexibility for future investments and debt management.
Negatives
Rising interest expense: Higher market interest rates and new debt issuances increased interest expense, which is expected to be a headwind in 2026.
Modest dip in occupancy from acquisitions: The addition of recently acquired assets slightly reduced overall occupancy rates, though management expects to improve these figures through leasing efforts.
Tariff-related retailer pressure: Management noted that tariffs are creating challenges for some tenants, particularly smaller retailers, which could impact rent collections and tenant stability in 2026.
Write-down of certain investments: The company recorded a non-cash loss related to a reduction in the carrying value of some equity interests, reflecting ongoing restructuring in certain platform investments.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Leasing momentum and occupancy trends: Investors should monitor whether Simon can maintain or improve high occupancy levels and continue signing new leases at attractive rents, especially at recently acquired or redeveloped properties.
Impact of tariffs and retailer bankruptcies: The full effect of tariffs on retailer profitability is expected to be more visible in 2026. Watch for any signs of increased tenant distress, bad debt, or bankruptcies that could affect rental income.
Contributions from redevelopment and new projects: Management previously guided to approximately $30 million in incremental NOI from projects stabilizing in 2026. The pace and success of these contributions will be important for earnings growth.
Progress on Simon Plus loyalty program: The loyalty initiative, launched in late 2025, is still in its early stages. Updates on membership growth, retailer participation, and any impact on shopper traffic or sales could be early indicators of its potential.
Guidance updates and capital allocation: Investors should look for any revisions to 2026 guidance, especially regarding property NOI growth and interest expense, as well as updates on share repurchases and dividend policy following the new $2 billion repurchase authorization.
