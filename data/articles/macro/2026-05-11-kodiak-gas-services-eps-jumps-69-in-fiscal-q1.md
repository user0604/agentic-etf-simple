Key Takeaways
Kodiak Gas Services booked a business win by raising its full-year guidance after integrating its new power segment.
Adjusted earnings per share rose 68.6% year over year.
Management now expects stronger results, but higher capital spending and rising debt add pressure.

Kodiak Gas Services (NYSE:KGS) capitalized on robust demand and strategic expansion, increasing its annual outlook after acquiring a new distributed power unit. The leading natural gas compression and power infrastructure provider reported first-quarter results on May 11, 2026, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026. While new growth opportunities emerged, the quarter also saw rising leverage and one-time expenses weighing on stated profit numbers.

Overall, the quarter reflected strong operational progress but underscored higher capital commitments and cash flow volatility.

Notable Developments
Adjusted earnings per share climbed to $0.59, partly due to removing one-time costs linked to debt refinancing and acquisition activity.
Contract Services — Kodiak's core segment — recorded revenue of $307 million, up 6.2%, with utilization reaching a high 98% and gross margin expanding 10.7% year over year.
The Distributed Power Solutions acquisition closed at the start of April, adding new growth streams that led management to raise its full-year outlook for adjusted EBITDA and power revenue.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance by about 13%, now projecting $820 to $860 million as it integrates the new power business. The company's focus turns to successfully deploying new power assets, sustaining high compression utilization, and converting profit growth into free cash flow. Investors should monitor rising capital spending and higher debt, which could affect future cash generation.

Quarterly Highlights

Adjusted net income, excluding debt refinancing and acquisition expenses, rose 59% year over year to $52.0 million.

The core Compression Infrastructure segment sustained very strong utilization at 98% while revenue climbed to record levels, reflecting robust demand in leading U.S. gas production regions.

At the same time, capex for growth surged, free cash flow declined from the prior year, and the leverage ratio increased to 3.6 as Kodiak ramped up spending for both compression and power expansion.
