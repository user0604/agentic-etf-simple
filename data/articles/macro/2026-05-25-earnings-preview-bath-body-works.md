Earnings Preview: Bath & Body Works

The Motley Fool Team

May 25, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Bath & Body Works (NYSE:BBWI), a leading specialty retailer focused on personal care and home fragrance products, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on Wednesday, May 27, 2026, before the market opens. Investors will be watching closely for updates on the company’s ongoing transformation efforts and progress toward its growth targets.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Bath & Body Works reported net sales of $2.7 billion, down 2.3% from the prior year. Adjusted earnings per share (EPS) came in at $2.05. Both figures were ahead of management’s guidance and beat analyst estimates. The company’s performance improved as the quarter progressed, thanks to targeted promotions and a rebound in consumer demand after a slow start. However, management acknowledged that results remain below the company’s long-term standards and highlighted the need for continued transformation under its "Consumer First Formula" strategy.

Positives
Sales and earnings beat analyst expectations: Both net sales and adjusted EPS exceeded consensus, which suggests the company’s recent adjustments are having a positive short-term effect.
International business showed strong growth: International net sales rose 8.6% year over year, and system-wide retail sales grew 13%, indicating healthy global demand and successful partner-led expansion.
Inventory management improved: Inventory was down 5% from the prior year, and management described inventory levels as "clean" heading into spring, which reduces risk of markdowns and supports future margins.
Early success from new product launches: The new moisturizing hand soap, featuring updated formulas and packaging, saw strong consumer reviews and sell-through, supporting the company’s innovation focus.
Negatives
Body care category underperformed: Body care sales declined mid-single digits, with seasonal collections—especially Holiday Traditions—failing to resonate with consumers, highlighting the need for more disruptive innovation.
Gross margin declined: Adjusted gross profit rate fell by 100 basis points, mainly due to tariffs, showing ongoing cost pressures that may persist into 2026.
SG&A expenses increased: Selling, general, and administrative expenses rose as a percentage of sales, reflecting higher wage costs and investments in technology and transformation initiatives.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Progress on the Consumer First Formula: Investors should look for evidence that new product innovation, brand refresh efforts, and expanded distribution are starting to drive customer growth, especially among younger demographics.
Sales performance versus guidance and consensus: Management guided Q1 net sales to decline 6% to 4% year over year, with consensus sales estimates at about $1.36 billion. Actual results versus these expectations will be a key focus.
Impact of Amazon and new distribution channels: The Amazon launch (completed Feb. 20, 2026) is intended to reach new and lapsed customers. Early sales and consumer engagement data from this channel will be important to watch.
Gross margin trends and tariff impacts: Management expects about a 150 basis point headwind from tariffs in Q1. Investors should monitor whether product cost pressures are being offset by cost savings and operational improvements.
International growth and geopolitical risks: International sales are expected to rise mid- to high single digits for the year, but ongoing geopolitical uncertainty, especially in the Middle East, could impact results. Updates on store openings and sales mix will be relevant.
