Yext Posts 25% Margin Gain in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 2, 2026, 8:22 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Yext continued its shift toward larger enterprise clients and away from smaller customers.
Adjusted EBITDA margin improved compared to the same period a year ago.
Management expects further operating efficiencies and margin expansion throughout fiscal 2027.

Yext (NYSE:YEXT) continued its strategic pivot toward high-value enterprise customers, accepting some revenue contraction to focus on larger, more profitable accounts. The digital presence management provider reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2027, released on June 2, 2026, showing improved profitability through cost discipline alongside a deliberate decline in total revenue. Overall, the quarter delivered strong margin expansion but further revenue contraction as the company doubled down on its enterprise strategy.

Notable developments
Profitability improved, with adjusted EBITDA margin rising to 25% and non-GAAP operating expenses falling to 54% of revenue.
Annual recurring revenue from customers with contracts of $50,000 or more increased by 1%, now making up 91% of total ARR.
The Scout AI product exceeded adoption expectations, playing a bigger role in new wins and contract renewals.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management foresees more operating efficiency gains and aims for further adjusted EBITDA margin expansion toward 30% over the rest of fiscal 2027, but it has not issued revenue or ARR guidance. The most important factor for investors to monitor is whether ARR among high-value enterprise clients returns to sustained growth and helps offset the decline from smaller customers.

Quarterly highlights

Profitability gains stood out in the quarter, with tighter cost control, especially in sales and marketing, helping push adjusted EBITDA and operating margins higher even as absolute revenue ticked lower. Non-GAAP operating expenses dropped to 54% of revenue, and free cash flow remained robust at $37.0 million.

The company's AI-driven Scout platform continued to gain momentum, speeding up both new customer expansion and upsells within large accounts. Management described Scout as central to renewal discussions and as a differentiating product, providing customers with actionable intelligence for digital marketing strategies.

Yext repurchased $140 million worth of shares in the quarter, reducing its outstanding share count and authorizing an additional $100 million in buybacks. Net leverage remained low, and management reiterated its intent to limit shareholder dilution by tying equity incentives to performance benchmarks.
