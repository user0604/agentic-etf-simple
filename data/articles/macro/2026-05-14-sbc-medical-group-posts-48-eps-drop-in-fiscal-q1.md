Sbc Medical Group Posts 48% EPS Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 7:00 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Sbc Medical Group reported lower profitability as it overhauled its franchise fee structure.
Earnings per share dropped 48% while revenue fell 9% year over year.
Management reaffirms its expansion plans but gave no specific outlook for upcoming quarters.

Sbc Medical Group (NASDAQ:SBC)’s sharp profit drop was the main headline as it pushed forward with a sweeping change to its franchise model. The diversified healthcare services provider, active in aesthetic and broader medical clinics, implemented a major revision to its franchise fee structure in April 2025, which weighed on short-term results. The company released its financial results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 14, 2026, showing that while customer and franchise growth continued, core earnings metrics declined materially. Overall, the quarter reflected operational resilience but underlined near-term financial pressure from the new business model.

Notable Developments
Revenue declined by 9%, to $43.1 million, as the company rolled out a new franchise fee model in April 2025.
Net income margin compressed by 19 percentage points to 26%, reflecting the impact of lower revenues and higher costs of revenue.
The franchise network expanded by 33 locations year over year, with the number of customers rising 10% to 6.76 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide specific financial guidance for the next quarter or year, but reiterated its focus on international expansion, artificial intelligence investment, and broadening its brand portfolio. It signaled that execution of these strategic changes is its priority. Investors should watch for stabilization in margin trends as the new business model matures.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure was driven down by the planned franchise fee reform, which management described as a "strategic structural reform" to support longer-term growth. The services segment provided some offset, with non-franchise revenues up sharply from the prior year, but not enough to prevent an overall decline.

Profitability measures fell sharply, with net income margin dropping to 26% and EBITDA margin to 43%. The decline in earnings per share and net income was partly amplified by the absence of one-off items that had boosted the prior year's results.

Despite these financial headwinds, the company’s operational footprint strengthened: franchise clinic locations increased to 284 and total customers over the past twelve months reached 6.76 million, both up year over year. The repeat customer rate remained steady at 72%.
