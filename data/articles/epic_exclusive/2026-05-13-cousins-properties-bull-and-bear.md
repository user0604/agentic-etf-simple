Cousins Properties Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 3:54 PM

Comments

Cousins Properties (NYSE:CUZ) is a real estate investment trust specializing in the ownership, development, and management of premium, lifestyle-oriented office properties across high-growth Sun Belt markets such as Atlanta, Austin, Charlotte, Nashville, Dallas, and Phoenix. The company’s growth strategy centers on capital recycling—acquiring trophy assets at discounts to replacement cost, strategic dispositions, proactive development, and relentless leasing focus—targeting high-credit, revenue-generating tenants from sectors like technology, finance, and professional services.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Market Leadership in Sun Belt Trophy Offices Drives Outsized Pricing Power

Cousins’ dominant position in the highest-growth Sun Belt urban submarkets enables enduring rent growth and favorable lease economics. The company’s portfolio features a concentration of new and redeveloped Class A and trophy office properties in Midtown Atlanta, Uptown Charlotte, and Austin’s Domain, directly benefiting from demographic migration and corporate relocations. As of Q1 2026, occupancy rates for the portfolio reached 88.9%, and total leased percentage was 91.8%. Management cites a “flight to quality,” with nearly all positive net absorption since 2010 occurring in newly delivered buildings, and supply set to tighten further as conversions and minimal new construction could result in shortages by 2030. This scarcity positions Cousins to push rents and attract top-tier, revenue-generating tenants, reinforcing pricing power for years to come.

2. Robust Leasing Momentum and Rent Roll-Ups Point to Sustainable Earnings Growth

Leasing velocity in early 2026 has been among the highest in company history, signaling ongoing demand and resilience. In Q1 2026, Cousins executed 932,000 square feet of leases—one of the strongest quarters on record, with 52% representing new or expansion activity and an average lease term of 6.6 years. The weighted average cash leasing spread was a positive 15.2%, marking an extraordinary 48 consecutive quarters of second-generation cash rent roll-ups. Average net rent grew 18% year over year to $44.54. With a late-stage leasing pipeline of 1 million square feet (twice the year-ago level) and minimal lease expirations—just 8.3% through 2027—this strong rent growth profile directly supports continued growth in funds from operations (FFO) and underlying earnings stability.

3. Prudent Capital Management Creates Flexibility for Shareholder Returns

Cousins’ disciplined capital allocation and balance sheet management enable both internal and external value creation. During Q1 2026, the company acquired 300 South Tryon in Charlotte for $317.5 million while funding the purchase by disposing of noncore assets like Harborview Plaza ($39.5 million at a 9% capitalization rate) and placing 111 Congress in Austin under contract. Management also repurchased 3.9 million shares at an average of $23.36 each and increased the buyback authorization to $500 million, demonstrating conviction in the company’s undervalued equity and balance sheet strength. New $500 million unsecured bonds were issued at a 5% yield, and a $1.2 billion unsecured credit facility was established. This flexibility supports opportunistic investment, share repurchases, and stable dividend payouts—expected to remain in the low- to mid-70% of funds available for distribution—offering robust shareholder returns ahead.

4. Return-to-Office Trends and Sun Belt Migration Catalyze Long-Term Demand

Macro tailwinds from accelerating return-to-office mandates and ongoing Sun Belt migration provide a durable platform for demand. Major employers are refocusing on in-person collaboration, and population inflows into Sun Belt markets are gathering pace, driven by favorable tax climates and the relocation of corporate headquarters. Recent marquee lease wins, such as the renewal of a Fortune 10 tenant at Domain 8 in Austin (273,000 square feet) and new leases with Oracle in Nashville and KPMG in Atlanta, underscore the sustained appetite for best-in-class space. As new construction remains minimal and conversions reduce future office supply, Cousins’ high-quality, amenity-rich assets are poised to capture outsize demand as the market rebalances by 2030 and beyond.

5. Sector-Leading Fundamentals and Peer Outperformance Support Valuation Rerating

Cousins’ operational discipline and financial outperformance stand out in a challenged office real estate landscape. The company has delivered three consecutive years of FFO growth, achieving a 3.9% compound annual growth rate since 2023, alongside rigorous expense control (1.95% average annual growth over four years) and unmatched leasing spread consistency. Broker consensus as of late April 2026 remains a moderate buy, with leading analysts favoring Cousins’ Sun Belt focus and asset quality over less specialized or coastal office landlords. This peer outperformance on key earnings and operational metrics positions the company for a potential valuation recovery as leasing markets normalize and investor confidence returns to premium Sun Belt office assets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Hybrid Work and Tenant Uncertainty Prolong Leasing Softness and Limit Rent Growth

Persistent caution among large tenants regarding future office space needs could dampen leasing velocity and effective rent growth for longer than anticipated. Despite strong recent leasing, management acknowledges that overall tenant decision cycles remain extended, with a wait-and-see approach prevalent in the broader Sun Belt office market. Hybrid work policies, sublease competition, and changing occupancy strategies among key technology and finance customers could slow or reverse the recovery in office demand. If tenants defer long-term commitments or reduce space footprints, Cousins may face challenges backfilling expiring leases or maintaining robust rent roll-ups—especially if recent trends toward concessions and longer rent-free periods persist through and beyond 2026.

2. Elevated Capital Expenditures and Temporary Leverage Spikes Impact Near-Term Cash Flows

High levels of leasing activity and active portfolio recycling introduce near-term cash outlays and balance sheet risk. Management projects that second-generation capital expenditures will be higher in 2026 as a result of the surge in new leasing, which, while ultimately value accretive, creates lumpiness in cash flow in the interim. Net debt to earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) temporarily rose to 5.66 times in Q1 2026, above the company’s low-5x target, driven by large transactions and the timing of asset sales. While this is expected to normalize, any delays in dispositions or further debt-funded activity could keep leverage elevated and reduce financial flexibility, potentially constraining distributions or buybacks during periods of market stress.

3. Prolonged Public-Market Discount to Private Asset Values Limits Shareholder Upside

Persistent skepticism regarding the office sector and a sluggish capital-markets backdrop could keep Cousins’ shares trading below intrinsic value for an extended period. While the company has increased its buyback program on the belief that shares are undervalued, the broader market has yet to fully rerate Sun Belt office REITs to reflect the improved fundamentals seen in leasing and earnings. Limited investor appetite for office exposure, ongoing macro uncertainty, and the gradual pace of net asset value recovery since the pandemic may continue to weigh on share price performance, regardless of strong operational execution. Without clear and sustained evidence of sectorwide normalization, market sentiment alone could cap total returns, making even best-in-class execution insufficient to drive outsized appreciation in the near term.
