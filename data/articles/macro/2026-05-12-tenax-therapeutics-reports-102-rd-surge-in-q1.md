Tenax Therapeutics (NASDAQ:TENX) posted a wider loss as it ramped up spending to advance its lead drug candidate. Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed that cash and cash equivalents are expected to fund operations through at least the first quarter of 2028, with no announced changes to study timelines. The topline data readout from the LEVEL Phase 3 trial, expected in the third quarter of 2026, is the most important upcoming milestone for the company.

Quarterly highlights

The LEVEL Phase 3 trial for the oral therapy levosimendan (TNX-103) completed patient randomization in March, keeping the company on schedule for a major clinical readout later this year. This milestone is significant because there are currently no approved therapies for pulmonary hypertension with heart failure with preserved ejection fraction (PH-HFpEF), the patient group targeted by the trial. The LEVEL-2 global pivotal trial is ongoing, with enrollment expected to finish by the end of 2027.

Research and development spending more than doubled as the company ran two late-stage studies at once and added new clinical and commercial leaders. General and administrative costs declined, helped by lower incentive-based stock awards, showing some cost restraint outside of research activities. This pattern of higher research spending with tighter overhead is common for biotech firms preparing for commercial launch.

The company added new executives, including a Chief Financial Officer, Chief Commercial Officer, and an Executive Vice President of Clinical Development and Strategy. These hires show preparation for future commercialization if late-stage trials succeed and reflect a move to build infrastructure beyond drug development.

What Tenax Therapeutics does

Tenax Therapeutics develops and seeks regulatory approval for new therapies targeting serious pulmonary and cardiovascular conditions where few or no approved treatments exist.
