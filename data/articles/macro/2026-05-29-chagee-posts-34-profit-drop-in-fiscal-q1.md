Key Takeaways
Modest revenue growth offset by a sharp drop in profitability.
Earnings per share fell 39.8% as the company expanded its mix of company-owned locations.
Management provided no formal outlook but signaled a focus on stabilizing same-store growth and profitability.

Chagee (NASDAQ:CHA)’s bottom line came under significant pressure as gains in revenue failed to overcome sharply higher costs and ongoing same-store sales declines. The leading specialty tea retailer shifted more of its network to company-owned stores, but the increase in operating expenses eclipsed modest revenue growth. The results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, released on May 29, 2026, highlight the company’s struggle to balance growth and profit amid tough economic and competitive conditions. Overall, the quarter marked mixed results as overseas expansion momentum continued but domestic weakness persisted.

Notable Developments
Revenue from company-owned stores rose 230%, but franchised store revenue dropped 12.9% as domestic market pressures continued.
Operating expenses jumped 16.6% year over year, outpacing revenue growth and weighing on profitability metrics like net margin.
Same-store sales in China declined 16.1%, an improvement over the prior quarter but still a key concern for ongoing performance.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide an explicit formal outlook for coming quarters, referencing previous guidance to hold revenue and profit roughly flat while aiming to stabilize same-store sales. The most important factor for investors is whether Chagee can return its same-store sales in China to growth, as ongoing declines challenge both revenue mix and margin prospects.

Quarterly Highlights

The revenue figure inched higher, supported by strong growth in company-owned locations, which now contribute a larger share of total sales. However, the franchised teahouse segment, which still accounts for over three-quarters of revenue, contracted as sluggish local demand and competition hurt performance.

Profitability declined substantially, with operating income and net income falling more than 30% year over year. This was driven mostly by higher costs tied to the rapid company-owned store buildout and greater general and administrative expenses, especially those linked to international growth initiatives and share-based compensation.

International expansion remained a highlight, with overseas sales and new locations rising sharply. Overseas gross merchandise value (GMV) in overseas markets grew 139% year over year, but remains a small part of Chagee’s global total, so international growth cannot yet fully offset weakness in the core China market.

What Chagee Does

Chagee operates a large network of teahouses selling specialty tea drinks, with both franchised and company-owned stores in China and international markets.
