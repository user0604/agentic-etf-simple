Dillard's Posts 54% EPS Jump in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Profitability was boosted by a large one-time legal settlement.
Earnings per share grew 54.4% to $16.04 and revenue rose 2.6% to $1.568 billion for the first quarter of fiscal 2026.
Management projects further capital investment but rising costs and limited digital disclosure deserve attention.

Dillard's (NYSE:DDS) reported a strong start to the year as a legal settlement powered notable profit gains. The national department store operator, known for its private label brands and extensive store network, continued to see steady retail sales and improved product margins. The results, released on May 14, 2026 and covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026, showed the company benefiting from sound merchandising but facing higher expenses and the absence of clear e-commerce information. Overall, the quarter combined disciplined operations with cost pressures and an unusual legal windfall.

Notable Developments
Net income included an after-tax gain of $79.6 million ($5.10 per share) from a legal settlement, boosting profitability beyond normal retail operations.
All merchandise categories saw sales growth, led by strong gains in home, accessories, lingerie, and shoes, with moderate increases in apparel and slight improvement in cosmetics.
Operating expenses were $444.0 million (28.3% of sales) compared to $421.7 million (27.6% of sales), with the increase largely due to higher payroll and payroll-related expenses.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management plans $130 million in capital expenditures for fiscal 2026, up from $93 million in the prior year. It expects lower depreciation and flat or slightly lower rental and interest expenses. Investors should monitor expense growth and the company’s disclosure on digital and credit card initiatives in future quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The legal settlement made a major contribution to reported profit, inflating the headline earnings figure compared to underlying operations. Excluding the one-time gain, core net income would have increased at a more modest rate.

Dillard's sustained positive retail sales across all merchandise categories. Stronger results in home, ladies’ accessories, lingerie, and shoes indicate continued strength in areas where private label brands are often featured.

Operating costs, especially payroll and related expenses, grew faster than sales. Inventory rose in line with sales, showing controlled stock but highlighting an area to watch as seasonal volumes build later in the year.

What Dillard's does

Dillard's operates a chain of department stores across the United States selling apparel, accessories, cosmetics, and home goods, with a focus on private label and exclusive merchandise.
