Key Takeaways
Business win: Kornit Digital delivered revenue at the top end of its guidance, supported by strong recurring revenue growth.
Performance: Annual recurring revenue rose sharply year over year.
Outlook: Management expects higher revenue and margin improvement in the next quarter as recurring contracts expand.

Kornit Digital (NASDAQ:KRNT) edged revenue to the top of its forecast range on recurring business momentum but continued to face pressure on margins and net income. The digital textile-printing technology specialist reported first quarter 2026 results on May 13, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the period showed strong progress in annual recurring revenue, but profitability remained a challenge as gross margin compressed.

Notable developments
Annual recurring revenue almost doubled, reaching $26.8 million, as more customers adopted the subscription-based AIC model.
Gross profit margin fell by 4.2 percentage points to 41.0%, mainly due to tariffs and product mix shifts.
Cash flow from operations remained positive, at $6.3 million, extending a streak of ten quarters of positive operating cash flow.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management projects second quarter revenue of $51 million to $55 million and expects adjusted EBITDA margin to improve from the current quarter's result. The company highlights increasing backlog and recurring contract pipeline as reasons for stronger performance in the latter half of fiscal 2026. Investors should monitor whether expanded recurring revenue can stabilize or lift profit margins, given ongoing cost pressures and margin declines.

Quarterly highlights

The recurring revenue model showed rapid growth, supported by strong adoption of the All-Inclusive Click (AIC) program. AIC lets customers pay for technology as a service, increasing sales predictability and customer retention.

Gross margin, which measures the share of revenue left after production costs, dropped 4.2 percentage points compared to the same period last year. Higher tariffs and changes in the mix of products sold contributed to this margin pressure despite gains from the recurring business model.

Cash flow from operations remained positive and slightly improved year over year, helping support ongoing investments in new technology and innovation. This positive cash flow performance came even as net losses persisted, reflecting careful working capital management and a growing share of predictable, service-driven business.
