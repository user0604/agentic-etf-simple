1. Okta, AI-Era Bouncer, Pops 20%

Okta (NASDAQ: OKTA) beat fiscal first-quarter estimates Thursday, with revenue of $765 million (up 11% year over year) topping the $752 million expected, and earnings per share (EPS) of $0.91 beating the $0.85 consensus. Shares jumped more than 17% in after-hours trading. Okta is recommended in Stock Advisor (Team RB).

The agentic AI angle: CEO Todd McKinnon says the proliferation of artificial intelligence (AI) agents is driving demand for identity security tools -- but AI isn't yet a majority of revenues. He called it "plumbing ... for the next five and 10 years," signaling a long runway ahead.
Q2 guidance lands in line: The company guided for $790 million to $794 million next quarter, versus the $791 million consensus. Remaining performance obligations -- a key measure of subscription backlog -- also topped estimates, suggesting demand is building.
2. Costco Tops Estimates on 11% Sales Jump

Costco (NASDAQ: COST) just dropped a tasty Q3: $70.53 billion in revenue topped estimates of $69.81 billion, net sales surged 11.6% year over year, and EPS landed right on the dot at $4.93. Adjusted comparable sales rose 6.6%, with top categories including pharmacy, home furnishings, and -- oddly enough for a warehouse store -- gold and jewelry. The Stock Advisor (Team HG) rec was down about 1% on Thursday and was flat after hours.

Bulk buyers, bulk loyalty: Paid memberships climbed 4.1%, app and website traffic exploded 37%, and digital sales leaped nearly 21%.
Tariff plot thickens: After a Supreme Court ruling invalidated some Trump tariffs, Costco said it would pass any refunds directly to shoppers as lower prices. "The tariff refund saga is interesting, kind of like waiting for a rebate you might never get," Motley Fool analyst Seth Jayson recently said.
3. MongoDB Raises Outlook After Blowout Q1

Rule Breakers rec MongoDB (NASDAQ: MDB) reported a strong quarter, posting revenue of $687.6 million (up 25% year over year) and promptly raised full-year guidance. The bar is already high, and MongoDB has a history of clearing it. "MongoDB almost always exceeds its internal guidance and the Street consensus by a wide margin," Rule Breakers analyst Tim Beyers wrote in March.

Atlas flywheel spinning: Cloud revenue jumped 29% -- the growth engine behind the guidance raise -- as enterprises pile AI workloads onto MongoDB's platform with no signs of slowing.
Bears should see the cash flow statement: Free cash flow nearly doubled to $197.5 million, and non-GAAP (generally accepted accounting principles) operating income jumped from $87.4 million to $123.2 million, "demonstrating we can drive durable revenue growth while simultaneously expanding margin," in the words of MDB President and CEO CJ Desai.
