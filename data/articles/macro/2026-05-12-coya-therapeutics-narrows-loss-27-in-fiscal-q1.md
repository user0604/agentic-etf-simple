Coya Therapeutics (NASDAQ:COYA) navigated the first quarter of fiscal 2026 with incremental clinical advances and a stronger balance sheet, but operating losses persisted. The biotech company focuses on developing Treg-based therapies for neurodegenerative diseases and autoimmune conditions. Its results for the period ended March 31, 2026 (announced on May 12, 2026) offered evidence of execution against clinical milestones but highlighted ongoing funding needs. Overall, the quarter reflected progress with lead drug development and stable collaboration revenue, offset by high expenses and lack of commercial product sales.

Notable Developments
The lead drug candidate COYA 302 for ALS entered full recruitment in its Phase 2 trial, maintaining a projected topline readout in the first quarter of 2027.
Cash and cash equivalents rose to $50.7 million due to a private placement, outpacing operating cash burn for the quarter.
Research and development expenses declined 21.6% while general and administrative costs increased, largely due to non-cash stock option charges linked to a board transition.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its guidance for key clinical milestones, including the completion of trial enrollment and initiation of studies in frontotemporal dementia later in 2026. It highlighted recent FDA Fast Track Designation as important for COYA 302’s regulatory path. The most important area for investors to monitor next will be progress on clinical trial enrollment and any updates on cash runway as development costs persist.

Quarterly Highlights

In clinical development, the company’s Phase 2 ALS trial of COYA 302 is now fully recruiting after protocol changes widened patient eligibility. The FDA granted Fast Track Designation to COYA 302 for ALS, and an Investigational New Drug application was accepted for use in frontotemporal dementia, which should support future studies.

Financially, cash on hand climbed to $50.7 million by the end of the quarter, supported by an $11.1 million private placement led by Dr. Reddy’s Laboratories and Greenlight Capital. Collaboration revenue from the Dr. Reddy’s partnership remained steady at $0.3 million, reflecting ongoing research and development services.

Expenses shifted during the period, with research and development down to $4.1 million, a decline associated with the timing of laboratory activities, while general and administrative costs went up to $3.8 million, primarily due to a one-time non-cash stock option charge. Net loss narrowed slightly compared to the prior year quarter.

What Coya Therapeutics Does

Coya Therapeutics develops immune cell therapies that target regulatory T cells for the treatment of neurodegenerative and autoimmune diseases.
