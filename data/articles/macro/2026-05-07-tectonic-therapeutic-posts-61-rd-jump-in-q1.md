Notable developments
Clinical progress stayed on track: TX45 advanced through late-phase enrollment in its largest trial, while TX2100 moved through early-stage safety testing with topline data expected later in 2026.
Research and development spending jumped 61%, now at $20.9 million, driven mainly by growing trial activity and workforce costs.
Cash reserves ended at $236.9 million, with management stating this will fund operations through late 2028 and cover several planned clinical milestones.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its forecast for a cash runway into the fourth quarter of 2028, emphasizing that advancing the TX45 and TX2100 programs remains the priority. The next major event for investors is the release of topline data from the TX2100 Phase 1a study by the end of the third quarter of 2026. Investors should closely monitor upcoming clinical trial results, as they are likely to shape the company's path forward and its access to new capital or partnerships.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s largest pipeline asset, TX45 (an Fc-relaxin fusion protein for pulmonary hypertension), neared full enrollment in its pivotal APEX Phase 2 trial for heart failure-related hypertension. Leadership reported that topline results for this study remain on schedule for late 2026 or early 2027, in line with previous guidance.

In parallel, the company initiated a second Phase 2 study of TX45 for a related lung condition, screening its first patient in March 2026. Meanwhile, TX2100 (a VHH-Fc fusion antagonist antibody) dosed healthy volunteers in a Phase 1 safety trial, with full data expected by the end of September 2026—potentially a key inflection point for its clinical program.

Rising operating costs reflected ramping research activity. Research and development expenses grew to $20.9 million from $13.0 million a year ago, mainly due to increased clinical trial spending and added staff, while net loss also widened year over year.

What Tectonic Therapeutic does

The company develops biologic drugs targeting G protein-coupled receptors (GPCRs) using its proprietary GEODe™ platform to address diseases with unmet needs.
