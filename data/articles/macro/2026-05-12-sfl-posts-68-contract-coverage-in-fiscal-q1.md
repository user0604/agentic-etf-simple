SFL (NYSE:SFL) delivered steady performance this quarter, with a higher cash dividend and continued focus on long-term charter contracts supporting income stability. The global shipping and offshore asset owner manages a diverse fleet of vessels and drilling rigs, generating revenues across container, tanker, car carrier, bulk, and energy markets. On May 12, 2026, it reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026.

Overall, the period saw reliable operations and improved net income, though some gains came from asset sales and derivative movements rather than core shipping activities.

Notable Developments
The quarterly dividend was increased to $0.22 per share, marking the 89th consecutive payout to shareholders.
Total operating revenues were $174 million in Q1 2026, with approximately 87% from shipping operations and 13% from energy assets.
Short-term debt rose to $723 million from $606 million at the end of the prior quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

The company’s leadership highlighted the stability of its $3.7 billion fixed-rate charter backlog and noted that future capital investments are expected to be covered through available credit. Management remains focused on securing long-term charter contracts and prudent capital allocation. The most important area for investors to track is whether short-term liabilities continue to outpace new incoming contracts or asset deployments as debt rollovers increase.

Quarterly Highlights

The increase in the dividend reflects management’s confidence in steady cash flow and backlog visibility, underlined by 68% of contracts secured with investment-grade counterparties. Revenue in Q1 2026 was $174 million, with container, tanker, and car carrier assets operating at 99% or 100% utilization.

Asset sales and favorable market movements contributed materially to net income in the quarter, including an $11.5 million gain from selling a Suezmax tanker and additional gains on derivatives and investments. Profitability improvements over the prior quarter were not entirely from recurring ship operations but also from these non-operational items.

Liquidity remained sound with total available funds at $288 million (counting undrawn facilities), even as short-term debt built up. The energy segment continued to lag with only 50% utilization, and new longer-term contracts begin only in 2027, which introduces some near-term risk.

What SFL Does

SFL operates a diversified fleet of commercial vessels and offshore drilling rigs, leasing them to shipping and energy companies under medium- and long-term contracts.
