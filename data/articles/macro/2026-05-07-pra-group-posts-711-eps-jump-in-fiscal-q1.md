PRA Group (NASDAQ:PRAA) delivered a notable win in the first quarter, as recovery efforts and operational focus lifted profitability. The leading debt buyer and collector purchases and manages portfolios of nonperforming loans, recovering money through a mix of call center, digital, and legal strategies. Its latest results, released May 7, 2026 and covering the quarter ended March 31, signal continued progress in its long-term initiatives despite pockets of expense growth. Overall, the quarter showed financial improvement with strong U.S. and European contributions.

Notable developments
Total cash collections increased 11% to $551.9 million, led by continued gains in U.S. legal collections and strong European recoveries.
Operating expenses rose to $211.3 million, as investments in legal collection costs outweighed compensation savings from workforce reductions.
Portfolio purchases of $220.9 million, though down year over year, were described by management as “in line with expectations” and supported by disciplined buying strategies.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management is keeping its focus on disciplined portfolio purchases, operational improvements, and technology modernization, as outlined in its "PRA 3.0" strategy. No explicit short-term guidance was given, but continued investment in digital and AI tools is expected. Investors should closely monitor collection cost trends and the pace of new portfolio acquisitions in upcoming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

Collections efficiency stood out, with the cash efficiency ratio rising to 61.8%—its highest in recent quarters. This measure shows how much cash is retained from collections after operating expenses, highlighting the company’s operational improvements.

PRA Group prioritized profitability over volume in its portfolio acquisitions, investing $220.9 million in line with its return objectives and management expectations. Strong purchase price multiples in both the U.S. and Europe reflected continued discipline over purchasing strategy.

The quarter saw further action on cost control, including compensation savings from headcount reductions, but operating expenses still increased, driven by a 45% jump in legal collection costs as the company pursues higher-certainty recoveries through the courts.

What PRA Group does

The company purchases portfolios of nonperforming loans and seeks to recover amounts owed through a combination of call center activity, legal processes, and digital engagement.
