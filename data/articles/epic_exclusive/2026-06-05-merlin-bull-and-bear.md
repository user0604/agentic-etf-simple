Merlin Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

Jun 5, 2026, 4:44 PM

Comments

Merlin, Inc. (NASDAQ:MRLN) specializes in AI-powered autonomous flight systems designed to retrofit large, multi-crew aircraft. The company’s growth strategy centers on pioneering autonomy for both defense and commercial cargo aviation, leveraging defense contracts as foundational proof points and charting an ambitious path toward broader market adoption. Merlin aims to solve acute pilot shortages and enable safer, more efficient air operations through scalable autonomy platforms.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Military Validation of Core Technology Accelerates Broader Adoption

Major defense milestones greatly de-risk certification barriers – Merlin recently completed the Critical Design Review (CDR) for its C-130J autonomy program with United States Special Operations Command in June 2026, advancing to the aircraft integration phase. Successful navigation of this pivotal military review is a key industry validator: it signals that Merlin’s autonomy stack meets stringent Department of Defense airworthiness standards. This technical endorsement does not just unlock a $105 million prime contract but creates a path for future defense programs and bolsters credibility for later commercial Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) applications. Industry precedent shows that Department of Defense-cleared aviation technology often translates into higher trust and faster regulatory pathways for civil aviation, positioning Merlin to benefit from risk reduction and first-mover advantages over less proven competitors.

2. Entry into Commercial Cargo Markets Could Multiply Addressable Revenue

Expansion into cargo aviation dramatically increases market scope – Merlin’s unveiling of its new product, Condor, marks a shift from a purely defense focus to targeting the fast-growing commercial cargo segment. The company’s technology is now positioned for retrofit on large civilian freighters, addressing a total addressable market projected to grow at a compound annual rate exceeding 32% through the late 2020s. By focusing on solving real-world pilot shortages and air network inefficiencies, Merlin’s commercial cargo ambitions open the door to a global customer base—including airlines and logistics operators. Successful commercial adoption could scale Merlin’s revenue opportunity far beyond initial military contracts, matching the trajectory of other dual-use aviation innovators who moved from government to commercial customers.

3. Anchor Government Partnerships Provide High Credibility and Long-Term Revenue Visibility

Long-cycle defense contracts underpin Merlin’s financial foundation – The company’s relationship with United States Special Operations Command, highlighted by its C-130J program progression, exemplifies a durable anchor partnership. Defense contracts in aviation are typically multi-year, milestone-based, and subject to less cyclicality than commercial orders. This stability enables higher visibility into near-term and medium-term revenue, supports longer-term research and development cycles, and makes it easier to attract strategic partners and capital. If Merlin can successfully execute on its current defense milestones, history suggests such anchor relationships can snowball into additional program awards in adjacent domains, further strengthening the business model and dampening the risk of early revenue volatility.

4. Technology-Agnostic Platform Unlocks Wide Retrofitting Opportunity

Merlin’s product flexibility allows rapid scaling across aircraft types – By focusing on a retrofittable autonomy core, the Merlin Pilot—recently showcased in the newly announced Condor system—the company is not locked into a single chassis or aircraft original equipment manufacturer. This enables Merlin to pursue a range of large, multi-crew aircraft in both defense and commercial fleets worldwide. Industry trends increasingly favor flexible, software-driven upgrades to legacy fleets due to the high cost and long cycles of full aircraft replacement. Merlin is one of the first to credibly demonstrate this platform approach, which could allow for outsized scale if commercial pilot shortages and operator demand continue as projected. This technological flexibility enhances competitive positioning and increases the likelihood of mass adoption compared to bespoke or single-model autonomy solutions.

5. Active Communication and Milestone Execution Boost Investor Confidence

Disciplined execution and improved transparency foster trust and strategic optionality – The company held its first earnings call as a public entity in May 2026 and is scheduled to participate in high-profile investor conferences, affirming a commitment to transparency and long-term shareholder engagement. Management has demonstrated the ability to communicate strategy and milestone progress, particularly around high-stakes certification events and new product launches like Condor. For a high-growth, pre-scaleup aviation technology company, consistent and credible public communication is a key lever to attract institutional investors, strategic partners, and top-tier talent—all crucial accelerators at this phase. This approach signals Merlin’s intent to execute in line with or ahead of industry timelines, a factor that markets increasingly reward when execution risk is high.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Pace of Revenue Recognition Remains Tied to Contract Milestones, Not Recurring Sales

Delayed revenue transformation may hinder near-term valuation – Merlin’s Q1 2026 results and related commentary point to increased operating investment, yet material contract revenue recognition has not accelerated, remaining largely contingent on achieving specific technical and regulatory project milestones. This milestone-centric model is typical for early-stage defense technology plays, but it may postpone meaningful cash inflows and prolong unprofitability. Without a path to recurring or diversified commercial sales—and given the absence of revealed backlog size or new contract wins outside defense—the company may face valuation setbacks if milestone payments are delayed or if pipeline conversion to revenue falls short of expectations for successive quarters.

2. High Burn Rate Coupled With Limited Cash Visibility Elevates Financing and Dilution Risk

Intensifying operating investments raise sustainability concerns – In Q1 2026, Merlin reported higher operating expenses to accelerate technology and certification progress. For aviation technology companies advancing toward costly certification and go-to-market phases, sustained high spending without immediate revenue or balance sheet transparency could necessitate frequent capital raises. If the company needs to raise additional funds at lower valuations or in adverse market conditions, current shareholders could face meaningful dilution and the business may lose operational flexibility at critical moments. Heavy reliance on external capital markets is a classic risk in advanced mobility sectors.

3. Customer Concentration and Execution Dependence Pose Structural Risks

Reliance on a single anchor defense contract limits diversification – The company’s fortunes remain tightly linked to its C-130J program with United States Special Operations Command, with no announced commercial wins or multiple major defense customers. This exposes Merlin to risk should the anchor contract encounter delays, modifications, or early termination—a not-uncommon occurrence in large government procurement projects. Customer concentration also reduces negotiating power and increases uncertainty regarding future cash flows. Until Merlin can demonstrate successful execution, revenue recognition, and client expansion in commercial or other defense verticals, investors must weigh the risk of unwelcome project shocks or strategic overdependence on one customer relationship.
