Key Takeaways
Target delivered a business win with broad-based gains across all merchandise categories.
Comparable sales rose 5.6%, marking a turnaround from last year’s decline.
Management raised its full-year sales growth outlook and expects stronger operating margins in fiscal 2026.

Target (NYSE:TGT) posted a clear rebound in the first quarter, led by growth across every major product category and a sharp recovery in customer traffic. The mass-merchandise retailer, which operates nearly 2,000 stores in the US and sells groceries, apparel, home goods, and essentials, reported results for its first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 20, 2026. Overall, the quarter marked a notable return to sustained growth after last year’s challenging period.

Notable Developments
All six core merchandise categories posted year-over-year sales increases, with hardlines up 14.6% and beauty up 9.6%.
Digital sales advanced 8.9%, while same-day delivery services grew by more than 27%, showing continued growth in omnichannel capabilities.
Non-merchandise revenue, including advertising and memberships, expanded nearly 25%, highlighting the success of new profit streams.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised its full-year forecast to approximately 4% sales growth for fiscal 2026, up from a prior 2% target. Operating margin is expected to improve slightly, reflecting ongoing investments in stores and technology. Investors should monitor margins closely as spending on payroll and infrastructure continues alongside growth initiatives.

Quarterly Highlights

Merchandise sales growth spanned every core category, suggesting Target’s new merchandising approach and product selection are attracting more shoppers. Beauty grew 9.6%, while hardlines (toys, electronics, and sporting goods) saw a 14.6% increase; home goods and essentials also delivered positive results.

The company’s omnichannel strategy, using stores as fulfillment centers, supported robust digital, delivery, and in-store pickup performance. Over 97% of merchandise sales were fulfilled from stores, and digital sales contributed 20.3% of merchandise sales, up from 19.8% the year before.

Profitability is recovering, with non-merchandise areas like advertising, the Target Circle 360 loyalty program, and marketplace operations now driving a larger share of profits. These digital businesses recorded double-digit growth and helped raise the overall adjusted margin rate, though higher spending on wages and technology remains a headwind.

What Target Does

Target is a US-based discount retailer that offers a wide range of products including groceries, clothing, electronics, and household essentials, both in stores and online.
