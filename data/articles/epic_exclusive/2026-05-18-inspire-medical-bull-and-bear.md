Inspire Medical Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 18, 2026, 7:13 AM

Comments

Inspire Medical Systems, Inc. (NYSE:INSP) develops and markets implantable therapeutic devices for obstructive sleep apnea, providing a surgical alternative to continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) treatment. The company’s growth strategy focuses on clinical innovation, expanding its network of implanting centers, strengthening payer reimbursement, and broadening patient access through digital engagement and targeted commercial investments—all while navigating industry coding and policy changes that impact reimbursement and procedural growth.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Clinical Evidence and Product Differentiation Underpin Long-Term Adoption

Robust clinical results and product innovation support durable demand for Inspire therapy – Inspire Medical Systems has consistently delivered strong clinical data, most recently highlighting real-world outcomes such as low device revision rates (1.7% in 2024) and explant rates below 1%. Its latest-generation Inspire V device, now used in over 90% of U.S. centers, offers shorter operating times and improved responder rates versus previous versions. Major data releases in 2026—including the 5,000-patient ADHERE registry and outcomes demonstrating lower cardiovascular and respiratory adverse events—reinforce payer support and differentiate its product. As more physicians transition to Inspire V and new trials (like the PREDICTOR study) broaden eligibility pathways, the device’s clinical appeal and commercial moat are strengthened, increasing the likelihood of sustained, long-term adoption beyond present reimbursement volatility.

2. Balance Sheet Strength Supports Strategic Investment and Resilience

Strong financial health enables Inspire to weather short-term turbulence and invest in future growth – As of March 31, 2026, the company reported $400 million in cash and investments with no debt, and generated $12.8 million in operating cash flow in the first quarter of 2026. This financial flexibility allows Inspire Medical Systems to invest in education, field support, and new product development even amid lower near-term revenues and elevated reimbursement risks. With capital expenditures guided to $40–$45 million for the year and a 17.5% adjusted EBITDA margin in Q1 2026, management is well-positioned to defend market position, accelerate innovation, and support commercial partners during policy transitions. The absence of debt ensures that adverse cash flow scenarios are survivable, buying time for operational recovery and future expansion.

3. Reimbursement Stabilization Offers Path to Revenue Recovery and Upside Surprise

Reimbursement clarity could unlock pent-up procedural demand and drive a growth rebound – While ongoing uncertainty surrounding procedure coding (especially for Inspire V devices) and the federal WISER prior authorization program has suppressed quarter-to-quarter revenue, management anticipates these policy headwinds will dissipate as billing teams adapt and payer guidelines stabilize. Specific stopgap measures—like expanded field reimbursement teams and proactive center education—are accelerating this learning curve. Should clarity and confidence return sooner than anticipated, there exists a pipeline of deferred but engaged patients (‘hot leads’) ready for scheduling, offering the potential for sharp volume and revenue uptick in late 2026 or 2027 as prior authorizations recover. Historical management commentary suggests that funnel health can rapidly normalize once center and provider confidence in reimbursement is re-established.

4. Leading Market Position Enhances Competitive Defensibility and Expansion Potential

Inspire’s established procedural footprint and brand recognition create high barriers to entry – The company maintains leadership in the surgically implanted hypoglossal nerve stimulation market, operating across more than 280 U.S. territories. Despite competitive entrants such as Nyxoah, management notes that coding and WISER-related disruptions—not competitive share loss—are behind current headwinds. The high installed base, extensive provider training initiatives, and long-standing payer relationships create switching costs for physicians and hospitals. Furthermore, Inspire’s focus on existing center utilization and selective new center openings, even during reimbursement turmoil, position it to recapture growth rapidly once external conditions normalize, leveraging its first-mover advantage and clinical dataset to expand market share domestically and internationally.

5. Product Pipeline and Operational Flexibility Position Company for Future Growth

Investment in next-generation devices and channel capability supports renewed expansion – Inspire Medical Systems continues to innovate, as seen by progress toward Inspire 6—which adds sleep/wake detection—and by maintaining Inspire IV inventory for centers with reimbursement concerns. Management has proactively adjusted its commercial field force, optimizing cost structure while ensuring clinical coverage and account support. The resumption of direct-to-consumer marketing and digital tools is designed to revive patient pipeline growth following a temporary slowdown in 2025. These strategic and operational capabilities enable rapid scaling as bottlenecks resolve, while device portfolio advances provide further levers for differentiation, clinical adoption, and potential expansion into adjacent therapy areas or international markets.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Reimbursement Instability Could Cause Persistent Revenue Erosion

Sustained coding confusion and policy volatility threaten procedural volumes and patient conversion – The most acute risk is that reimbursement headwinds remain unresolved well into or beyond 2026. Lack of a dedicated single-lead CPT code until at least 2028, inconsistent payer policies (including the push from some commercial plans toward unlisted codes), and shifting Medicare Administrative Contractor interpretations have created significant administrative friction. The WISER program’s expansion and the ongoing use of AI-based prior authorization in multiple states directly suppress pipeline flow and center productivity, with management flagging a potential $120–$150 million revenue headwind for the full year 2026 and an 8.3% drop in share price following new guidance. If continual uncertainty causes deferred patient demand to become permanently lost, Inspire may struggle to return to prior growth rates or achieve forecasted volumes—even if clinical support remains intact.

2. GLP-1 Pharmaceutical Adoption and New Therapies Undermine Addressable Market

Emerging obesity drugs and evolving standards of care may reduce demand for surgical solutions – The recent approval and rapid uptake of GLP-1 class obesity drugs (such as semaglutide and tirzepatide) introduce a new source of uncertainty. Some sleep apnea patients are postponing surgery to trial pharmaceutical options first, which could shrink the near-term eligible patient pool for Inspire’s devices. While management suggests that weight loss could eventually make more patients eligible for Inspire therapy, the transition could delay or permanently displace demand as non-surgical alternatives gain favor. If these drugs become standard frontline therapy or if future device competitors successfully enter the market, the company’s long-term addressable market size and growth rate could materially decline.

3. Sustained Financial Underperformance and Legal Risks Could Deter Investment

Profitability headwinds and shareholder litigation may pressure stock performance and confidence – Management reduced full-year 2026 guidance, now projecting an adjusted diluted earnings per share range of $0.75 to $1.25 and revenue of $825–$875 million, marking an expected 9–11% year-over-year decline. The volatility of GAAP financials, evidenced by a Q1 2026 diluted EPS of $(0.39) and an outsized GAAP tax rate of 571.2% due to stock-based compensation swings, increases uncertainty for investors. The adverse reaction to earnings—a sharp share price drop and analyst downgrades—was compounded by a shareholder investigation launched in April 2026. Regulatory or legal setbacks could tie up resources, distract from execution, and extend the period of financial underperformance, making it more challenging for the company to maintain investor confidence or attract long-term capital in a volatile reimbursement environment.
