SmartRent Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 26, 2026, 11:06 AM

Comments

SmartRent, Inc. (NYSE:SMRT) provides enterprise software and hardware solutions that enable rental property owners and operators to connect, automate, and manage their portfolios through Internet of Things (IoT) platforms. The company’s growth strategy centers on expanding its installed IoT base, increasing recurring software-as-a-service (SaaS) revenue, deepening customer wallet share, and executing Vision 2028—a multi-year operating plan aimed at scaling sales channels, launching data-driven solutions, and simplifying platform offerings to drive sustainable profitability.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Expanding Recurring Revenue Base Signals Improved Profitability and Predictability

Rising recurring revenue is transforming the business model – In the first quarter of 2026, annual recurring revenue (ARR) rose 9% year over year to $60.9 million, now comprising approximately 39% of the company’s total revenue. Management’s successful renegotiation of legacy contracts—delivering a 33% price uplift on the first three renewals—supports stronger average revenue per unit (ARPU) and an increase in the proportion of high-margin SaaS sales over time. As a third of the installed base is still on early, lower-priced contracts scheduled for renewal, recurring revenue should continue to scale, improving cash flow visibility and earnings resilience as one-time hardware-driven volatility recedes. This structural pivot to SaaS enhances long-term value and positions the company for continued gross margin expansion.

2. Large Untapped Installed Base within Existing Customers Creates Compelling Upside

Significant white space supports sustained growth runway – As of March 2026, SmartRent had deployed IoT solutions across 911,000 rental units—a figure that grew 10% year over year—covering just 15% of a 6 million-unit portfolio collectively controlled by its current 600 enterprise customers. With 85% of these portfolios not yet converted, the path to expand within known customers dwarfs current deployment levels. Incremental penetration through the “March to 1 Million” initiative, cross-selling new solution modules, and leveraging hardware refresh cycles provide numerous levers for ARR and ARPU growth. Capturing even a modest additional share of existing accounts could double revenue without the need for costly new-customer acquisition, boosting operating leverage and scalability in the process.

3. Go-to-Market Expansion and VAR Channel Unlock Access to New Markets

Channel strategy widens addressable opportunity and accelerates bookings – In early 2026, the company launched a Value-Added Reseller (VAR) program aimed at more efficiently capturing small and mid-market property operators that are costly to reach with a direct sales force alone. By scaling to 8–10 VAR partners over the next year, supplementing a direct sales team that is also slated to double its headcount, SmartRent will be equipped to cover broader geographic and segment white space. This go-to-market transformation should increase new unit bookings, accelerate time-to-market, and allow for cost-effective expansion—critical advantages as the industry’s adoption of IoT-driven property management matures and competition intensifies.

4. Structural Cost Actions and Gross Margin Gains Position the Company for Sustainable Profitability

Operating leverage is beginning to materialize after 2025 restructuring – Despite a 6% year-over-year dip in total revenue to $38.7 million in Q1 2026, the company’s gross margin improved significantly, rising 630 basis points to 39.1% through cost realignment and a favorable revenue mix shift toward software. Professional services margins reached breakeven for the third straight quarter following recurring losses just a year prior, while adjusted EBITDA returned to positive territory at $0.4 million, compared to a $6.4 million loss (non-GAAP) in the prior year. Pairing reduced operating expenses (down 32% year over year) with a robust $99 million in cash and no debt, the business is now positioned to self-fund growth investments with expanding cash flow—a crucial milestone for long-term value creation.

5. Product Innovation and AI-Driven Platform Evolution Enhance Competitive Differentiation

Investments in AI and analytics can deepen customer retention and open new revenue streams – The company’s Vision 2028 strategy emphasizes leveraging its large installed device base to power advanced AI and data analytics solutions, providing customers with actionable ROI and unique operational insights. Ongoing rollouts of next-generation software, expansion of the device and analytics suite, and smooth execution of hardware refresh programs keep SmartRent at the technology forefront in an evolving sector. These innovations are expected to boost customer stickiness, support up-selling, and defensively strengthen SmartRent’s position in a market where property owners increasingly prioritize digital automation and intelligence for efficiency and tenant experience.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Bookings Volatility and Execution Hurdles Could Delay Revenue Growth

Timing and ramp risks threaten near-term topline expansion – In the first quarter of 2026, new unit bookings declined 9% year over year due to a combination of ramping new enterprise sales hires, focus on legacy contract renewals, and customer delays attributed to macroeconomic caution. Although management characterizes these as temporary, sustained underperformance or slow productivity ramps from newly added sales and VAR partners could prolong soft bookings and hinder progress toward revenue and ARR growth objectives. If transition pain continues, competitive catch-up and market share expansion could stall, making shortfalls in top-line performance more likely.

2. Hardware Margin Pressure and Churn in Key Solutions May Temper Profitability

Shifting solution mix exposes underlying margin risk – While overall gross margin improved in Q1 2026, hardware segment margins fell to 18.2%, down roughly 760 basis points year over year due to adverse mix and lower volumes. At the same time, churn in the Smart Operations module contributed a negative $0.11 ARPU impact to SaaS revenue, partially offsetting gains from new deployments. Success in managing hardware margin volatility and mitigating churn—especially as the installed base ages and hardware refresh cycles accelerate—will be essential. Persistent margin pressure and churn in higher-value modules could diminish the expected profitability inflection the company aims to deliver.

3. Prolonged Net Losses Increase Pressure on Operational Execution and Cash Preservation

Sustained unprofitability tests balance sheet and market confidence – For the first quarter of 2026, SmartRent reported a net loss per share of ($0.02), consistent with expectations but underscoring continued operating losses. Despite a $99 million cash balance and an undrawn $75 million credit facility, recurring net losses—paired with increased investments in salesforce and channel ramp—place a premium on discipline in expense management and timely realization of ARR and margin gains. Should the company fail to reach positive free cash flow on a sustained basis or if bookings and SaaS mix lag, the balance sheet cushion could erode, leading investors to question the path to durable profitability and self-sufficiency.
