Key Takeaways
Surf Air Mobility posted a strong quarter for its high-growth charter business but remains unprofitable overall.
Revenue rose year over year.
Management raised its full-year profit outlook, but net losses and cash outflows remain key risks.

Surf Air Mobility (NYSE:SRFM) delivered a solid quarter for its private air charter segment, but the company remains unprofitable overall. The technology-driven regional air mobility platform grew its On Demand charter revenue and kept total revenue at the high end of its outlook for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, as reported on May 11, 2026. Overall, the period showed both financial improvements and ongoing challenges as the company works to scale its platform.

Notable Developments
On Demand private charter revenue reached a record $10.1 million, rising 77% year over year, while scheduled service revenue continued to decline by 13%.
Adjusted EBITDA loss improved to $12.3 million, above company guidance, helped by cost controls and software-enabled margin gains.
Management reaffirmed full-year revenue outlook and improved its forecast for full-year Adjusted EBITDA loss by about 40%.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its full-year 2026 revenue goal of $128 million to $138 million and sharply improved its Adjusted EBITDA loss outlook to a range of $30 million to $25 million. The profit outlook boost reflected faster cost reductions enabled by its proprietary SurfOS software and expanding higher-margin charter business. Investors should now watch for continued scaling of the charter segment and evidence of successful external commercialization of SurfOS, which is planned for later in 2026.

Quarterly highlights

On Demand private charter was the quarter’s standout, notching a 77% jump in revenue with particular growth in long-haul and international flights, and strong improvements in revenue per flight. BrokerOS, Surf Air Mobility’s platform for third-party brokers, helped drive these results, lifting active brokers from 6 to 29 by April and boosting broker productivity and speed.

Scheduled airline service revenue dropped 13% as the company exited unprofitable routes. Still, operational metrics including completion rates and on-time performance improved, and investment continued in new aircraft and facilities, particularly in Hawaii following the partnership with electric plane maker BETA Technologies.

The SurfOS platform advanced both internally and as a future product, with new artificial intelligence tools rolled out and early revenue generated from a booking “take rate”. The company trimmed operating expenses by introducing workflow automations and preparing for SaaS (software-as-a-service) commercialization in the second half of the year.

What Surf Air Mobility does

Surf Air Mobility is a technology-driven regional air mobility company offering scheduled flights, private charters, and air mobility software, with a focus on sustainable electric aviation.
