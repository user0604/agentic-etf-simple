Key Takeaways
Cavco Industries delivered solid revenue and profit growth, offset by challenges in housing margins and flat order backlog.
Earnings per share increased 21.3%, and revenue rose 8.2% compared to the same period last year.
Management signaled ongoing investment and a cautious stance due to uncertainty in demand and rising costs.

Cavco Industries (NASDAQ:CVCO) delivered operational progress but faced pockets of margin pressure and flat order backlogs this quarter. The leading factory-built home maker saw higher profits and revenue, helped by contributions from financial services and a recently acquired retail business.

The latest results cover the fiscal fourth quarter, announced on May 22, 2026, tracking the company's performance through March 28, 2026. Overall, the period showed top-line growth with record home sales, tempered by continued factory-built housing margin challenges and only modest improvement in orders.

Notable Developments
Net revenue rose to $550.1 million, driven by higher average selling prices and the American Homestar acquisition.
Gross profit margin in the core factory-built housing segment declined to 21.2%, while financial services delivered improved profitability.
Quarter-end backlog remained nearly flat year over year, finishing at $195 million compared to $197 million in the previous period.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management indicated a focus on investment in new manufacturing capacity and further operational integration of recently acquired businesses. While the executive team highlighted a strong year for home sales and financial services, it cautioned that market demand remains uncertain and that input costs could continue to pressure margins. Investors should watch for any gains in factory-built housing orders, margin stabilization, and progress from new plants in the quarters ahead.

Quarterly Highlights

Net revenue increased 8.2% year over year as higher average selling prices per home and expanded company-owned retail operations offset fairly stable unit sales. The acquisition of American Homestar and a favorable product mix contributed to this revenue growth, even as total homes sold were nearly unchanged for the quarter.

Margins in the factory-built housing segment declined compared to the prior year period, dropping by 1.1 percentage points. This resulted from rising costs of materials and a shift toward products and channels that do not always bring the same profit per dollar of sale. In contrast, the financial services segment, including insurance and lending, experienced a significant gain in gross profit margin, helped by higher insurance premiums and a drop in claims.

Quarter-end order backlog was essentially unchanged, reflecting only modest improvement in factory orders late in the period. While March orders picked up, the full quarter’s backlog ended at $195 million, showing no broad-based momentum in demand as the business enters the next fiscal year.

What Cavco Industries Does

Cavco Industries designs and builds factory-built homes and provides related insurance and consumer financing in the United States.
