Key Takeaways
Nayax delivered strong revenue and adjusted EBITDA growth, offset by lower hardware margins and negative free cash flow.
Revenue climbed 32% year over year.

Nayax (NASDAQ:NYAX) maintained rapid top-line growth as it expanded its global payments platform, but margin pressure from hardware promotions and negative free cash flow continued. The leading commerce enablement and payments solutions provider serves unattended retail and cashless payment markets worldwide. These first-quarter fiscal 2026 results, released on May 12, 2026, showed progress on the company’s growth strategy, though operational challenges persisted.

Overall, the period reflected strong recurring revenue gains with some headwinds in hardware profitability and continued investment dragging on cash flow.

Notable Developments
Recurring revenue rose 27% to $79.3 million, accounting for 74% of total revenue and supporting the company’s SaaS-plus-payments model.
Hardware margin fell to 33.1% from 39.5% due to promotional pricing for new VPOS Media point-of-sale devices in Europe, leading to overall gross margin stagnation.
Free cash flow remained negative at ($6.0 million), impacted by increased infrastructure investment and timing of payment settlements.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year fiscal 2026 goals: revenue of $510 million to $520 million and adjusted EBITDA of $85 million to $90 million. It expects improved free cash flow conversion on a full-year basis, targeting 40% of adjusted EBITDA. Investors should watch the evolution of hardware margins and free cash flow as new product launches and infrastructure spending continue.

Quarterly highlights

Recurring revenue sustained its role as the main growth driver, increasing 27%, supported by gains in both payment processing and SaaS segments. The average revenue per user (ARPU) climbed 14% year over year, underpinned by expansion into higher-value verticals such as electric vehicle charging and amusement facilities.

The gross margin remained largely flat as higher payment and software margins counteracted significant hardware margin declines. Management attributed the drop in device margins mainly to aggressive European promotions for the latest screen-based VPOS Media point-of-sale devices.

While the company’s installed device base and customer count expanded by double digits, negative free cash flow persisted. Management pointed to timing effects and investment in new infrastructure but confirmed its goal to reach positive free cash flow as collections catch up and operational leverage improves.

What Nayax does

Nayax provides global cashless payment processing, point-of-sale systems, and software for unattended retail and consumer engagement.

Stay tuned for future quarters as Nayax continues to focus on recurring revenue growth and margin improvement.
