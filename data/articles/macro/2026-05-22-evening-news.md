1. Walmart's New CEO Is Not Messing Around

John Furner has been Walmart's (NASDAQ: WMT) CEO for three months, and he's already swinging the axe. Tom Ward, chief operating officer (COO) of Sam's Club, is out after nearly two decades. So is Cedric Clark, who ran U.S. store operations, The Wall Street Journal reported on Friday. Neither position is small. Shares were down less than 1% on Friday.

Old guard, meet the door: Furner already installed new leadership across all three of Walmart's major units when he took over. It's all part of the "new challenges to tackle," with "all eyes on him to continue McMillon's success," as Motley Fool Chief Investment Officer Andy Cross noted recently.
Ward's long run comes to an end: Nearly 20 years at Walmart -- not a bad run. Sam's Club has been a standout performer for Walmart lately, so whoever fills his shoes has a tough act to follow.
2. SEC Puts Crypto Stock Trading Plan on Ice

Putting stocks on the blockchain turns out to be harder than it sounds. The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) was set to roll out a sweeping exemption that would let crypto platforms trade tokenized versions of U.S. stocks -- think your favorite equity, but living on a pseudonymous blockchain. Then the stock exchanges called. Now the plan is on pause.

"Nobody is asking for this": That's a direct quote from a partner at Chatham, New Jersey, brokerage Themis Trading, who polled clients on trading stocks 24/7 via crypto platforms. Enthusiasm was muted.
The North Korea problem is real: An NYU Stern professor warns that tokenized stocks could land on lax know-your-customer (KYC) platforms, potentially letting sanctioned foreign entities hold shares and collect dividends. As he put it: "You can't pay a dividend when you don't know who owns the token."
3. IMAX Sale Buzz Sends Shares Up 15%

Somebody might be buying IMAX (NYSE: IMAX), and Wall Street is very excited about it. Shares jumped more than 15% on Friday after reports of preliminary sale talks. At about a $2.1 billion market cap, analysts say the premium big-screen brand is a steal: "one of the most defensible moats in entertainment for what amounts to a rounding error on the balance sheet of any major studio."

Bidding war waiting to happen: Netflix (NASDAQ: NFLX), Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL), Sony (NYSE: SONY), and private equity are the names analysts keep floating. Studios are largely ruled out, since nobody wants a competitor controlling their IMAX release windows.
Numbers make the case: IMAX hit a record $1.28 billion at the global box office in 2025, up 40%-plus over 2024, but the stock still hasn't reclaimed pre-pandemic valuations, which is exactly the kind of disconnect that makes acquirers salivate.
