PagerDuty (NYSE:PD) reported stronger profit margins as cost controls took hold, but growth in new recurring contracts and customer spend remained soft. The digital operations management platform helps enterprises automate incident response and maintain digital services by integrating AI-driven solutions with cloud workflow tools. The company released its first quarter fiscal 2027 results on May 28, 2026, for the period ending April 30, 2026. Overall, the quarter highlighted improved profitability, though muted growth in recurring revenue and customer usage signals areas for investor attention.

Notable developments
Profitability improved, with non-GAAP operating margin up to 24.6%, supported by lower operating expenses and higher free cash flow.
Annual recurring revenue (ARR) was flat at $496 million, and the net retention rate decreased to 97% from 104%, indicating customer churn and contraction.
A new $100 million share repurchase authorization was announced, as management highlighted confidence in long-term cash flow.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its revenue guidance for fiscal 2027, setting a range of $488.5 million–$496.5 million and raising its non-GAAP earnings forecast slightly. The outlook expects little near-term growth, with profitability gains largely from spending discipline rather than higher sales. Investors should closely watch whether new AI-powered products and usage-based pricing reverse the stagnation in recurring revenue and customer retention in coming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s profitability metrics improved across the board, with a year-over-year jump of 33.3% in non-GAAP earnings per share and free cash flow up by 42.1%. Lower operating costs, including reduced research and development and sales and marketing spend, contributed to higher earnings even as revenue growth stayed modest.

Annual recurring revenue, which measures the contracted value of subscription-based services, stayed flat at $496 million. The dollar-based net retention rate, which shows how much recurring revenue existing customers continue to generate, dropped to 97%, reflecting lower expansion in customer contracts and some ongoing churn, especially outside the largest enterprise segment.

PagerDuty continued investing in product development, announcing new AI integrations and workflow automation features to strengthen its Operations Cloud offering. Despite these product advances, the company acknowledged the ongoing challenge in translating innovation into higher subscription revenues. The new $100 million buyback follows several quarters of strong cash generation, and a recent CEO transition marks a shift as the company seeks renewed growth momentum.

What PagerDuty does

PagerDuty provides a digital operations platform that helps organizations automate incident response and maintain the reliability of digital services using AI and cloud-based workflow tools.
