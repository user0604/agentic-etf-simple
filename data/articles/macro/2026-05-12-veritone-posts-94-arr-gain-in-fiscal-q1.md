Key Takeaways
Veritone reported a mixed quarter as revenue and customer count both declined.
Net loss per share improved to $(0.21), while annual recurring revenue rose 9.4% from the prior year.
Management reaffirmed full-year revenue and profit guidance despite near-term challenges.

Veritone (NASDAQ:VERI) delivered a transitional quarter, with revenue and customer numbers both falling compared to the prior year. The artificial intelligence software provider is focusing on its data refinery business and new public sector contracts to drive future growth. The first-quarter results, released on May 12, 2026, covered the period ending March 31 and showed progress in recurring revenue, but only modest improvement in losses and cash outflow. Overall, the quarter showed mixed progress, with some bright spots in core growth areas but ongoing challenges in meeting ambitious full-year targets.

Notable developments
Software Products & Services revenue fell 4.6%, and total customer count declined 8.2%, reflecting softness in talent acquisition solutions.
Annual recurring revenue increased, driven by a 50% rise in consumption-based contracts compared to the prior year.
Cash and equivalents dropped to $15.1 million from $27.4 million last quarter, while management began a cost-cutting initiative targeting a 30% reduction in operating expenses.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 outlook, maintaining projected revenue of $130 million to $145 million and a significant reduction in non-GAAP net loss. The company highlighted a large pipeline of potential deals and recent strategic wins with major technology partners. Investors should watch for acceleration in revenue and changes in subscription customer trends as the year progresses.

Quarterly highlights

Growth in recurring revenue was fueled by a sharp increase in consumption-based contracts, which are agreements where customers pay based on usage rather than a fixed subscription fee. These contracts can be less predictable but contributed to the rise in annual recurring revenue.

The Veritone Data Refinery, the company's artificial intelligence data product line, secured new bookings and expanded its pipeline, supported by deals with industry leaders such as Google, NVIDIA, and a multi-year cloud partnership with Oracle. These relationships could help secure future revenue if the pipeline converts to signed contracts.

Despite growth in public sector sales and certain product lines, customer attrition in core software products and a decline in legacy managed services led to lower overall revenue and a wider non-GAAP net loss. The company is working to address these issues through cost reductions and aims to achieve operating profitability by the end of fiscal 2026.
