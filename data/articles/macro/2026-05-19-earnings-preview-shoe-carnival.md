Earnings Preview: Shoe Carnival

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Shoe Carnival (NASDAQ:SCVL), a leading U.S. retailer of family footwear, is expected to report its first quarter fiscal 2026 earnings on Thursday, May 21, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

The fourth quarter of fiscal 2025 showed Shoe Carnival delivering results that were generally in line with analyst expectations. Net sales for the quarter were $254.1 million, down 3.4% from the prior year. Comparable store sales declined 3.5%. Diluted earnings per share (EPS) came in at $0.33, matching the consensus estimate. Gross profit margin held steady at 34.9%, reflecting disciplined pricing even in a highly competitive holiday environment. The company ended the year with a debt-free balance sheet and over $130 million in cash and securities.

Positives
EPS met expectations: The company reported $0.33 per diluted share, which matched analyst consensus, indicating stable performance despite a challenging retail environment.
Strong balance sheet: Shoe Carnival ended the year with no debt for the 21st consecutive year and maintained over $130 million in cash and marketable securities, giving it flexibility to manage through near-term headwinds.
Shoe Station e-commerce growth: The Shoe Station banner’s online sales continued to outperform, showing strong consumer demand beyond the physical store footprint and validating the brand’s resonance in the market.
Gross margin discipline: Gross profit margin remained flat year over year, demonstrating management’s focus on profitability over chasing unprofitable sales during the holiday season.
Negatives
Declining comparable store sales: A 3.5% drop in comparable store sales, led by mid-single-digit declines at Shoe Carnival stores, signals ongoing pressure on in-store traffic and consumer demand.
Mixed performance from rebannered stores: The 101 stores converted to the Shoe Station format showed significant variability, with some underperforming expectations, prompting a slowdown in the rebanner rollout for further analysis.
Inventory build-up: Merchandise inventories rose 14% year over year to $439.6 million, mainly due to opportunistic pre-tariff buys, but this elevated inventory creates near-term margin risk as it is worked down through promotions.
SG&A expense deleverage: Selling, general and administrative expenses increased to 30.6% of sales, up from 29.6% a year ago, reflecting higher costs related to rebanner investments and lower sales volume.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Gross margin pressure from inventory reduction: Management has signaled that working through elevated inventory will require increased promotional activity, which may compress gross margins in the first half of the year.
Impact of the Jordan brand launch: The rollout of Nike’s Jordan brand to over 60% of stores, with a full rollout by mid-April, could help drive athletic category sales, especially in legacy Shoe Carnival stores, and will be a key metric to watch.
Pace and performance of rebannered stores: Only 21 additional stores are planned for rebannering before back-to-school 2026, a slowdown from prior plans. Investors should look for updates on how these stores perform and how management is refining the rebanner strategy.
Comparable store sales trends by banner: Shoe Station’s e-commerce channel has outperformed, but in-store sales have been mixed. Watch for commentary on whether assortment adjustments and targeted marketing are starting to improve in-store comps, especially as management aims for better performance in the second half of the year.
Cost control and SG&A trends: Management expects SG&A expenses to decrease by $12 million to $14 million in fiscal 2026. Progress on this front will be important to offset margin pressures and support earnings during this transition year.
