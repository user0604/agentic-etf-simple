NewLake Capital Partners (OTC:NLCP) maintained portfolio stability with full rent collection, but continued to face pressure on underlying income due to vacant properties and tenant risks. The real estate investment trust, which owns and leases properties to state-licensed cannabis operators, reported results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Notable developments
Full rent was collected across the portfolio, including from tenants undergoing restructuring, even as revenue fell by 6.8% compared to the prior year.
Three cultivation properties in Pottsville, Pennsylvania; Sparks, Nevada; and Fitchburg, Massachusetts remained vacant during the quarter, resulting in no rental income from those sites.
The dividend was held steady at $0.43 per share, representing a 90% payout of adjusted funds from operations, up from about 85% a year earlier.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management continues to take a cautious approach, citing ongoing tenant credit risks and an uncertain regulatory landscape in the cannabis sector. No changes have been made to growth or risk assumptions based on potential regulatory shifts, and the company is focusing on maintaining defensive balance sheet strength. Investors should watch for updates on re-leasing activity at the three vacant properties and on the progress of at-risk tenants, such as Cannabist, which has entered restructuring proceedings.

Quarterly highlights

The company reported full rent collection for the period, including all payments from tenants facing financial pressure. This was achieved despite Cannabist's bankruptcy restructuring, where all four associated leases stayed current and remain supported by a combined $482,000 in security deposits held by the company.

Revenue declined compared to the prior year, with management attributing the drop to vacancies at three cultivation properties. While a key lease in San Diego was successfully transferred and extended to a new tenant, keeping rent terms flat, there was no notable progress on re-leasing the vacant sites.

On the capital management front, the balance sheet remained strong with only 1.6% debt to gross assets, $107.2 million in liquidity, and no debt maturities until May 2027. The maintained dividend of $0.43 per share pushed the adjusted funds from operations payout to the high end of management’s comfort range, reflecting a delicate balance as cash flows ease.
