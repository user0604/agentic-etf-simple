Oshkosh Posts 56% Profit Drop in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Oshkosh reported a tough start to the year, with profitability falling sharply as sales held steady.
Non-GAAP diluted earnings per share dropped 55.7% year over year.
Management maintained its full-year outlook, expecting a recovery in the second half.

Oshkosh (NYSE:OSK) started the year on a weak note, as profit margins fell sharply while sales remained nearly flat. The leading manufacturer of specialty vehicles and equipment saw both its Access and Vocational segments underperform, while the Transport segment delivered stronger results. The company released its quarterly results on May 8, 2026, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the period showed significant margin pressure, though the company kept its full-year guidance unchanged.

Notable developments
Profitability fell due to lower sales volume and unfavorable cost mix, with operating income dropping over 50% from the prior-year period.
The Access segment saw operating margin contract by more than seven percentage points, reflecting cost and volume challenges.
The company raised its quarterly dividend by nearly 10% to $0.57 per share, continuing its focus on shareholder returns.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management kept its full-year forecast for 2026, expecting adjusted earnings per share of $11.50 and net sales of about $11.0 billion. It projects a back-half recovery, with the first quarter seen as the low point due to seasonal and volume factors. Investors should pay close attention to margin and volume improvements in the upcoming quarters, especially in the Access segment where delivery and profitability have disappointed.

Quarterly highlights

The Access segment, which covers products like aerial work platforms and telehandlers for construction sites, posted revenue slightly lower than last year and a steep drop in profit margin. Management attributed these declines to weaker sales volumes and pressure from an unfavorable mix between product types and costs.

The Vocational segment, which includes fire apparatus, airport vehicles, and refuse/recycling trucks, also saw lower revenue and sharp margin contraction. Fire truck production improved but deliveries missed internal targets, partly due to unexpected weather and travel disruptions. Refuse and recycling vehicle sales declined the most among its product families in this segment.

Transport, which comprises defense vehicles and postal delivery trucks under government contracts, stood out as a positive with higher delivery vehicle sales helping segment revenue grow more than 10%. However, overall defense revenue declined, reflecting program transitions, even as the company continues to ramp up production for the Next Generation Delivery Vehicle contract with USPS.

What Oshkosh does

Oshkosh designs and manufactures specialty vehicles and equipment for industries including defense, fire and emergency, construction, and municipal services.
