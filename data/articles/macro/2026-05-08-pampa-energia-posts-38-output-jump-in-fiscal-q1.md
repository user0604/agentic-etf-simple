Key Takeaways
Production and profitability improved, but cash flow fell sharply due to heavy investment.
Oil and gas production reached a record 100.6 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day, up 38.4% year over year.

Pampa Energía (NYSE:PAM) delivered strong output gains and improved profitability, but its cash burn widened as it pushed investments higher. The leading Argentine energy company reported surging oil and gas production along with margin improvement for the first quarter, as detailed in results released May 8, 2026 (covering its fiscal Q1 2026 period). However, expanded capital expenditures and volatile commodity prices sharply hurt free cash flow.

Overall, the quarter showed solid operational progress, but liquidity pressures and pricing risks remain in focus.

Notable Developments
Oil and gas production hit a new record at 100.6 thousand barrels of oil equivalent per day, 38% above the prior year quarter.
Free cash flow worsened to negative $402 million amid heavy investment, compared with negative $119 million a year earlier.
Adjusted EBITDA margin increased to 48%, up seven percentage points versus last year, reflecting efficiency gains and a better business mix.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management plans to continue prioritizing capital spending on development assets, especially Rincón de Aranda, aiming to lift production while keeping leverage moderate. No explicit full-year guidance was provided. The most important factor for investors will be how the company balances heavy investment, cash preservation, and regulatory risks in Argentina’s complex market.

Quarterly Highlights

Record oil and gas output was underpinned by growth at fields like Rincón de Aranda, which alone produced 25.3 thousand barrels per day by the quarter’s end--about six times more than one year ago. Rincón de Aranda continues to attract major capital, with $1.5 billion in spending earmarked until production reaches a 45-thousand-barrel plateau.

The company’s free cash flow showed a deficit of $402 million. This resulted from a surge in capital expenditures and greater working capital needs, offsetting profit gains from stronger production and margin improvement.

The electricity generation and transmission business contributed half of consolidated adjusted EBITDA. The segment saw margin compression, as adjusted EBITDA margin in the power business dropped to 47% (from 61% in the prior year), mainly due to changes in volume and pricing. However, new power market rules allowed for higher gas output and improved spot market profitability within the period.
