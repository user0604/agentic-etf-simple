Carlyle Credit Income Fund Reports 35% Q2 NAV Drop

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 4:17 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Net asset value fell sharply as market volatility hit CLO valuations.
Core net investment income per share declined from the prior quarter.
Management remains defensive, focusing on portfolio stability and dividend coverage.

Carlyle Credit Income Fund (NYSE:CCIF) reported a significant drop in net asset value as volatility in the collateralized loan obligation (CLO) market weighed on results. The closed-end fund, which invests primarily in CLOs backed by senior secured loans, released its earnings for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, on May 19, 2026. Overall, the period saw tighter profitability and a reduced margin of dividend coverage, despite ongoing efforts to manage risk and maintain payouts.

Notable developments
Net asset value per share dropped to $3.34 from $5.17 in the prior quarter, a decrease of 35% due to lower CLO valuations and market volatility.
Core net investment income per share fell to $0.29, narrowing the coverage of the monthly dividend of $0.06 per share.
New CLO investments totaled $1.5 million, down from $13.1 million in the previous quarter, reflecting a more cautious approach to portfolio deployment.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management emphasized a defensive strategy, focusing on disciplined underwriting and active risk management as market volatility continues. The main priority is to maintain stable dividend coverage and preserve capital. Investors should monitor net asset value and portfolio yield in upcoming quarters, as further declines could affect future payouts.

Quarterly highlights

The sharp decline in net asset value per share was the most notable event, with the fund’s NAV falling by $1.83 per share compared to the prior quarter. This drop was attributed to ongoing volatility in the CLO market and tighter valuations for underlying loan assets.

Core net investment income, which excludes non-recurring items, decreased from the previous quarter, resulting in a tighter margin for dividend coverage. The monthly dividend remained at $0.06 per share through August 2026, but management noted that coverage is now less robust.

Investment activity slowed, with only $1.5 million deployed into new CLO positions during the quarter. This conservative approach, along with ongoing resets and refinancing of preferred shares, highlights management’s focus on risk control and capital preservation.

What Carlyle Credit Income Fund does

Carlyle Credit Income Fund is a closed-end fund that seeks to generate income and preserve capital by investing in a portfolio of U.S. collateralized loan obligations and related fixed-income securities.
