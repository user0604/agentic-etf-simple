Berkshire Bets $6.8B on Housing Rebound

Greg Abel's first big acquisition says a lot about where Berkshire thinks the housing market is headed.

Nicholas Sciple

Jun 1, 2026, 9:24 AM

Comments
7.0

Is Berkshire Hathaway (NYSE: BRKB) betting on a housing rebound? After this weekend's news, I think the question is worth asking.

Berkshire announced it is buying homebuilder Taylor Morrison (NYSE: TMHC) for $72.50 a share in cash. That works out to roughly $6.8 billion in equity value, or about $8.5 billion including debt, and it is a 24% premium to Friday's close. It is the first multibillion-dollar acquisition of the Greg Abel era, and the company expects the deal to close in the second half of the year.

Taylor Morrison is no small bolt-on. It runs more than 350 communities across 21 markets in 12 states, sells to entry-level, move-up, and resort buyers, and offers its own mortgage, title, and insurance services. CEO Sheryl Palmer and her team are staying on, and the company will go private once the deal closes. Abel said he wants to unify Berkshire's site-built homebuilding into a single platform over time, and Taylor Morrison hands him a national operator to build that platform around.

Here is what makes the deal more than a one-off. Berkshire was already tilting toward housing before this weekend, and you can see it in the 13F it filed last month. In the first quarter, Berkshire added more than 40% to its Lennar Class A stake and another 31% to the Class B shares, and it held onto its position in NVR. These are small positions, a rounding error next to Apple (NASDAQ: AAPL) or American Express (NYSE: AXP). But the direction is what counts here, and the direction was up.

That matters because the rest of the quarter was a cleanup. The portfolio shrank from roughly $274 billion to $263 billion, and Berkshire exited 16 positions entirely, including names as good as Visa (NYSE: V), Mastercard (NYSE: MC), Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN), and Domino's (NASDAQ: DPZ). When a firm is selling almost everything and still chooses to add to homebuilders, even in small size, those additions are worth a closer look.

The footprint runs deeper than the public stocks, too. Berkshire already owns Clayton Homes, a range of building products businesses, and Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices, one of the largest brokerage networks in the country. Taylor Morrison slots right in.

I would stop short of calling it a wholesale sector bet. Berkshire actually cut its Nucor (NYSE: NUE) steel position in the quarter, so this is not buy everything with a hammer near it. But the conviction in homebuilders specifically is hard to miss, and it is backed by nearly $400 billion in cash and a CEO willing to write a multibillion-dollar check on his very first big swing.

So is Berkshire calling the bottom in housing? It hasn't said so out loud. But for a sector that's seen a meaningful slowdown in recent years, the most patient buyer in the market just placed a sizable and deliberate bet on the rebound. That is worth paying attention to.
