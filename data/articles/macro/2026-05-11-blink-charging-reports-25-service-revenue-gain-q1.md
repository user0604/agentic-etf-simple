Blink Charging Reports 25% Service Revenue Gain Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 4:43 PM

Key Takeaways
Profitability improved as cost controls narrowed losses, but sales of charging hardware declined sharply.
Service revenue rose 25% year over year, helping margin expansion despite flat total revenue.
Management reaffirmed full-year revenue and margin guidance for 2026.

Blink Charging’s owner-operated service model drove a major improvement in profitability, yet sales of its charging hardware fell steeply. The leading electric vehicle (EV) charging equipment and services provider highlighted persistent cost discipline and a focus on recurring revenue in its first quarter 2026 earnings release, published on May 11, 2026, covering results for the quarter ended March 31. Overall, the period showed big progress on margins and cash flow, but total revenue remained flat year over year, raising questions about future growth drivers.

Notable Developments
Service revenue made up 64% of total revenue, rising to $13.3 million from $10.7 million year over year, even as product (hardware) revenue dropped 26%.
Non-GAAP gross margin improved by about 2 percentage points, reflecting the greater share of high-margin service revenue in the mix.
Net cash from operating activities turned positive at $0.7 million, a change of $13.7 million compared to the year-ago period’s cash burn.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership reaffirmed its target of $105–$115 million in revenue and a GAAP gross margin of roughly 35% for fiscal 2026. It stated that momentum in service revenue and expansion of owner-operated stations will be crucial to meeting these goals. Investors should monitor whether recurring revenue and hardware sales accelerate in the coming quarters to support this outlook.

Quarterly Highlights

Service revenue accounted for nearly two-thirds of total revenue in the period, offsetting a sharp drop in product revenue. Charging service and cloud-based network fees climbed rapidly year over year.

Operating losses and net loss narrowed considerably due to aggressive expense control under the BlinkForward initiative. Spending fell across compensation, general and administrative, and other areas, helping drive a positive result for cash from operating activities.

Management credited further investments in owner-operated direct current (DC) fast charging equipment as a factor raising service revenue and margin, but also noted that higher running costs impacted overall profitability. Scale and utilization will be key to improving this balance as the network grows.

What Blink Charging Does

Blink Charging operates and manages a global network of EV charging stations, offering both hardware and software-driven charging services for electric vehicles.
