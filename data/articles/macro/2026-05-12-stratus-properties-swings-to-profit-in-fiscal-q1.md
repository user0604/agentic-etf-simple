Stratus Properties Swings to Profit in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 8:27 AM

Key Takeaways
Stratus Properties shifted toward liquidation after significant asset sales, transforming its operating model.
Earnings per share swung from a loss to a profit as results benefited from one-off property sales.
Management’s focus remains on the execution and shareholder approval of its liquidation plan, with uncertainty around timing and value as asset sales progress.

Stratus Properties (NASDAQ:STRS) recorded a major profit bounce after selling key real estate assets, as it began moving toward an orderly shutdown of its core business. The real estate developer and operator is now focused on liquidating its property portfolio, pending a shareholder vote on its full dissolution plan. Reporting results for the first quarter on May 12, 2026, Stratus showed large, one-time gains but a shrinking revenue base as recurring operations wound down. Overall, the period was defined by strategic transformation, not ongoing business performance.

Notable Developments
The Board approved a full liquidation plan in March, pending stockholder approval, with estimated payouts of $29.73 to $37.69 per share.
Asset monetization accelerated with the $60.8 million Kingwood Place sale, producing a pre-tax gain, net of noncontrolling interests, of approximately $13.4 million, while revenue from leasing dropped sharply as the property portfolio shrank.
The company boosted its cash balance to $73.5 million by quarter-end, even as capital expenditures decreased as investment in new projects slowed.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management’s focus has moved entirely to obtaining shareholder approval and executing the liquidation, aiming to maximize property sale value and return proceeds to investors. There is no forward financial guidance, as future activity will hinge on contract closings and regulatory sign-offs. Investors should closely monitor the outcomes and timing of asset sales and the shareholder vote in June.

Quarterly Highlights

The most significant change in the period was the company’s strategic pivot: in March, the Board committed to fully liquidating Stratus, projecting shareholder payouts once all assets are sold. This plan is subject to a vote, and if it does not pass, the company may need to pursue a more conventional and much smaller real estate operation.

Asset sales became the main earnings driver, with the Kingwood Place transaction accounting for a large portion of reported profits in the quarter. At the same time, leasing operations and recurring revenue streams continued to shrink, as more properties exited the portfolio—reflected in a 24.8% drop in total revenue year over year in the first quarter.

Financial discipline was evident in the lower capital spending and rising cash balance, supported by real estate sale proceeds. Share buybacks continued through late April, with 235,421 shares repurchased for $5.2 million, though management’s emphasis is now on efficient wind-down and capital return from property sales rather than ongoing operations.

What Stratus Properties Does

Stratus Properties develops, owns, leases, and sells residential and retail real estate, primarily in central Texas, with a recent shift to liquidating its holdings and returning proceeds to stockholders.

For those following Stratus Properties, closely watch the shareholder meeting and status of key property sales as the liquidation plan unfolds.
