U-Haul Posts 52% EPS Drop in Fiscal Q4

The Motley Fool Team

May 27, 2026, 4:20 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
U-Haul reported a disappointing quarter as losses grew and profitability declined.
Adjusted EBITDA for the Moving and Storage segment rose 2.6% on an adjusted basis, while overall diluted EPS fell sharply.
Management expects some relief ahead from lower planned truck spending, but storage occupancy and fleet costs remain top challenges.

Losses mounted for U-Haul (NYSE:UHAL) as cost pressures and empty storage space dragged down its latest results. The leading do-it-yourself moving and storage provider posted a wider net loss for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (with results released May 27, 2026), reflecting higher depreciation and sluggish occupancy in recently built self-storage locations. Overall, the period saw modest revenue growth but continued strain on margins and profitability.

Notable Developments
Despite higher revenue, net loss widened to $127.8 million, as higher fleet depreciation and poor equipment resale weighed on profitability.
Self-storage revenue grew 7.1% year over year, but average occupancy continued to decline—to 71.0% by quarter end.
The company declared a $0.05 per share dividend for non-voting shares and launched a $350 million share repurchase authorization.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management indicated future spending on rental trucks will decrease, aiming to control rising depreciation. The biggest factor to watch is how quickly new self-storage units fill and whether cost pressures from excess space and elevated fleet expenses moderate in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue rose 3.1%, with self-moving equipment rentals up 1.6%, reflecting modest growth despite a flat pricing environment for one-way moves. Costs tied to operating a large rental fleet increased, with depreciation and disposal charges up sharply year over year, resulting in a wider net loss for the quarter.

Self-storage revenue climbed 7.1% on new site openings and resilient pricing, but occupancy fell—from 77.0% to 71.0% at quarter end—due to continued rapid expansion of storage capacity outpacing demand. Management expects the drag from unrented space to persist until more units are rented than added to the system.

The company maintained steady performance in its insurance business, which now makes up nearly 6% of consolidated revenue, providing some balance to pressure in the core moving and storage operations. U-Haul’s board authorized a sizable share repurchase and continued its modest regular dividend on non-voting shares.

What U-Haul Does

U-Haul is North America’s leading provider of self-service moving truck rentals and storage, operating a large network of company-run stores and independent dealers.
