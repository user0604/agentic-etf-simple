Alumis Posts 90% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 4:19 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Alumis delivered positive clinical results but saw significantly reduced revenue due to the absence of prior licensing income.
Research and development expenses declined year over year, narrowing the net loss for the quarter.
Management reaffirmed key regulatory milestones for its lead drug candidate, with sufficient cash to fund operations into late 2027.

Alumis (NASDAQ:ALMS) reported major progress in its clinical pipeline but posted sharply lower revenue following the end of non-recurring licensing income. The innovative immunology company focuses on developing TYK2 inhibitors for immune-related disorders. For the period ended March 31, 2026 (first quarter 2026, results announced May 14, 2026), it achieved robust late-stage data for its lead candidate while maintaining a strong cash position. Overall, the quarter showed meaningful advancement on clinical and operational fronts, but commercial revenue remains minimal.

Notable developments
Phase 3 envudeucitinib trials in plaque psoriasis delivered leading skin clearance results, with up to 68.0% of patients achieving substantial improvement measures by Week 24.
Research and development costs fell to $81.5 million from $96.6 million, aided by the completion of major trial enrollment.
Cash and investments reached $569.5 million at March 31, 2026, giving Alumis operating runway into late 2027.
Management outlook and what to watch

Alumis management reaffirmed its timelines for submitting a new drug application for envudeucitinib in plaque psoriasis in the fourth quarter of 2026. Investors should closely follow the upcoming Phase 2b results in systemic lupus erythematosus, expected in the third quarter of 2026, which will be a major indicator of the broader potential for its TYK2 franchise.

Quarterly highlights

Positive Phase 3 results for envudeucitinib, Alumis’s lead oral TYK2 inhibitor for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis, showed clear advantages: at Week 24, PASI 90 skin clearance response rates were recorded at 68.0% and 62.1%, with complete clearance (PASI 100) in up to 41.0% of patients. These outcomes also included significant improvements in quality of life and rapid itch relief, with a safety profile matching earlier trial phases.

The company further reduced both research and development as well as general and administrative expenses year over year, which narrowed the net loss by 6.0%. This was mainly because it concluded major enrollment and topline reporting in its late-phase psoriasis trials and reduced prior-year merger costs.

Cash and marketable securities climbed to $569.5 million, boosted by an early-2026 equity offering, and are expected to cover operational needs into the fourth quarter of 2027. Alumis completed its strategic review of lonigutamab and is now exploring strategic alternatives for this asset, focusing efforts on its TYK2 pipeline and upcoming regulatory submissions.

What Alumis does

Alumis develops targeted therapies with a primary focus on TYK2 inhibitors for immune-mediated diseases such as psoriasis and systemic lupus erythematosus.
