Sylvamo (NYSE:SLVM) posted a net loss as it worked through a transition year marked by major supply chain changes and investment projects. The global producer of uncoated freesheet paper, with operations in North America, Latin America, and Europe, released its first quarter results on May 8, 2026. The period reflected planned disruptions and higher costs, with results aligning with management’s earlier guidance for a challenging start to fiscal 2026.

Notable developments

During the quarter, Sylvamo’s revenue declined compared to the prior year, reflecting both planned North American supply transitions and cost pressures across all regions. The company’s adjusted EBITDA margin narrowed, and operating profit in North America dropped as it faced transition costs, tariffs, and the end of a significant supply agreement. Free cash flow was more negative than the prior year, impacted by inventory build related to supply chain changes and the payment of annual incentives.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated that fiscal 2026 will be a transition year, with stronger profitability and cash flow expected in the second half as projects at the Eastover mill are completed and supply stabilizes. The most important factor for investors to monitor is the execution of capital investments and the pace at which regional price increases are realized, especially in Europe and North America.

Quarterly highlights

North America saw the largest impact, with revenue and profit declining due to the end of the Riverdale agreement, higher import costs, and ongoing strategic investments. Management responded to new U.S. tariffs on European imports by shifting to lower-cost Brazilian sourcing, which helped reduce some financial impact.

In Europe, the business continued to face pressure, with operating losses deepening and adjusted EBITDA margin falling compared to the prior year. The company cited lower sales prices, higher input costs such as energy and chemicals, and reliability issues as key drivers, while noting that recent price increases will only be realized later in the year.

Latin America’s results also weakened, affected by seasonal demand and higher costs for maintenance and operations. Management began a lean transformation initiative in the region aimed at improving future efficiency, with plans to expand similar programs in North America next.

What Sylvamo does

Sylvamo produces uncoated freesheet papers used in office printing, educational, and professional documents, with major mills and operations in North America, Latin America, and Europe.
