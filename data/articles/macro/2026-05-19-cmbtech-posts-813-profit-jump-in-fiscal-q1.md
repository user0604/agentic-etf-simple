Notable Developments
More than 70% of the quarter’s profit came from selling shipping vessels, with $267.4 million in gains on disposals.
Fleet modernization advanced with seven newbuilds delivered and the sale of ten older ships to lock in favorable prices.
Contract backlog grew $109 million to $3.26 billion, reflecting new and extended long-term charters.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management emphasized that the quarter’s exceptional earnings stemmed from rare market conditions and warned about uncertainties in shipping supply and demand ahead. It continues to focus on balance sheet strength and converting current momentum into long-term charters. Investors should watch for progress securing recurring revenue sources and updates on green and dual-fuel segments as volatile spot markets and asset sale gains may not repeat.

Quarterly Highlights

The quarter’s results were shaped by capitalizing on a “red-hot” tanker market, including high-value sales of eight very large crude carriers and two capesize dry bulk vessels, leading to significant one-time gains. Day rates soared for several vessel types: spot rates for tankers and dry bulk ships roughly doubled from the prior year, directly boosting revenue.

The fleet grew more modern and efficient with the arrival of seven newbuilds across vessel classes, positioning the company to benefit from high fuel prices thanks to eco-efficient designs. Notably, offshore energy ships were able to fix contracts at excellent daily rates during the period.

Although the main profit drivers were one-off sales, the firm closed the quarter with increased cash on hand and a larger order backlog. This financial strength supports potential investments in its green fuel initiatives, although no direct revenue contribution from hydrogen or ammonia activities was reported this quarter.
