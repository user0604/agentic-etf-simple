Key Takeaways
Green Dot delivered strong revenue growth in its core B2B and Money Movement Services businesses. Earnings per share climbed 98% year over year on higher revenue. Management offered no financial guidance as the acquisition process continues.

Prepaid and fintech platform Green Dot (NYSE:GDOT) reported a jump in fee-based business and payment activity, powered by ongoing strength in its business-to-business and money movement services. The company, a major provider of banking and money transfer technology to retailers and employers, delivered its first-quarter update on May 11, 2026, for the quarter ended March 31. Despite clear gains in topline growth and segment profit, Green Dot’s consumer business remained weak and the company gave no earnings outlook as it awaits a pending acquisition. Overall, the period showed a successful pivot toward its scalable growth segments, while legacy areas continued to face headwinds.

Notable Developments
B2B Services revenue grew 22%, becoming the largest contributor to company sales as new embedded finance partners came online.
Money Movement Services—such as tax refund and cash transfer programs—expanded 18.6%, supported by the addition of a large franchise tax partner.
Consumer Services revenue fell 9%, and segment profit dropped 24%, as retailer declines and reduced marketing weighed on that line of business.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide guidance for fiscal 2026, citing the pending acquisition by Smith Ventures and CommerceOne. In the release, leadership focused on maintaining growth through new financial partnerships and product innovation. The most important thing for investors to watch is whether B2B and Money Movement Services can continue to offset ongoing declines in consumer-facing segments.

Quarterly Highlights

B2B Services, which include embedded business banking and payroll technology, recorded strong revenue gains as Green Dot deepened relationships with key platform partners and added new business customers. Segment profit increased modestly, and the company reported encouraging account growth in this channel.

Money Movement Services saw solid growth from a robust tax season and the launch of a new franchise partner, helping to further diversify the company’s sources of fee income. Integration plans with Stripe point to further expansion opportunities in digital payments.

The consumer segment continued to struggle, with lower retail activity and active account numbers contributing to the drop in both revenue and profit. While the company made some efforts to stabilize the direct-to-consumer business with late-2025 marketing, these have yet to produce a turnaround.
