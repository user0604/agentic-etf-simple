Notable developments
Piedra del Águila hydroelectric complex joined the company's portfolio with a new 30-year concession at a cost of USD 245 million, increasing hydroelectric coverage.
Revenue grew 63% year over year, driven by higher power sales under renewed market rules and successful operation of new and existing thermal and renewable plants.
Financial leverage rose sharply, as loans and borrowings climbed 51% from year-end, mainly to fund the hydroelectric acquisition, and cash balances decreased as acquisition costs were paid out.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide explicit updated financial guidance for fiscal 2026 or segment performance targets in the earnings release. Instead, it emphasized integration of new assets, the importance of adapting to Argentina’s regulatory and macroeconomic shifts, and the need to manage higher short-term financing. The most important thing for investors to follow next is how Central Puerto handles debt repayment and the impact of Argentina’s shifting economic policies on future profitability.

Quarterly highlights

The period saw a milestone acquisition as Central Puerto secured the 30-year Piedra del Águila hydroelectric concession, boosting hydro capacity by 1,440 megawatts and reinforcing its position as one of Argentina's largest private generators. This purchase, finalized in January 2026, contributed to the company’s installed capacity of 4,809 megawatts spanning thermal, hydro, and wind assets.

Revenue climbed to ARS 343.6 billion, up 63% year over year, primarily from increased energy sales under Argentina’s updated electricity market rules, which now base pricing on marginal costs and allow for more market-based contracting. Earnings also benefited from diversified energy sources -- with both conventional and renewable generation contributing -- but margins narrowed as operating costs and administrative expenses outpaced the revenue growth.

To finance growth, loans and borrowings surged to ARS 745.1 billion, a 51% increase from year-end 2025, while operating cash inflow declined despite high net income. This reflects both the acquisition of the hydro asset and continued investments in plants, which heightened short-term financial risk and will require close monitoring as new debt matures.
