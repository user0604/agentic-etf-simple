Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management cited this as a transition quarter, with restructuring efforts now largely complete. They expect improved performance in future periods as core markets become the focus and transition costs fall away. Investors should watch for evidence of revenue stabilization, margin improvement toward the 50% goal, and positive impacts from regulatory shifts—especially any benefits from federal cannabis rescheduling and hemp regulations in Nevada.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue fell by nearly one quarter compared to the same period last year, a direct result of the California market exit and slower sales in core states. Florida, which now leads in contribution after California's exit, continued to feel the effects of price compression despite expansion efforts and operational investments.

The gross margin improvement, driven by streamlined operations and exiting loss-making markets, did not yet reach the "north of 50%" level management had targeted. Management had previously projected stronger improvement beginning this quarter, highlighting a gap between prior expectations and actual results.

The net loss worsened due to an outsized income tax provision, likely linked to federal tax penalties on cannabis companies still facing Schedule I restrictions. On the plus side, adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed marginally, and operating expenses dropped 19%, showing the early effects of cost cuts and a refocused business footprint.
