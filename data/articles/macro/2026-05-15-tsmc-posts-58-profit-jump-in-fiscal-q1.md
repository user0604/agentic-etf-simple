Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (NYSE:TSM) posted robust quarterly gains as customers continued to shift production toward its latest technologies. The leading global chip manufacturer, known for enabling cutting-edge electronics, reported solid results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 (released May 15, 2026), with high adoption of its 3-nanometer and 5-nanometer processes supporting broad-based improvement. Overall, the quarter showed across-the-board expansion in revenue and margins driven by premium chip manufacturing.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased by 35.1% in New Taiwan dollars to NT$1,134.10 billion, reflecting strong demand for advanced chips such as those made with the 3-nanometer process.
Gross margin for the quarter was 66.2%.
Newer process technologies (3-nanometer and 5-nanometer) together made up 61% of total wafer revenue, highlighting customer migration to TSMC's most advanced products.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects continued revenue growth for the next quarter, forecasting between $39.0 billion and $40.2 billion in U.S. dollars. It expects margins to remain strong, but signals they could level off compared to this period’s improvement. The most important thing to monitor will be customer demand for the newest chip technologies, given their outsized contribution to current results.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s revenue jump was powered by demand for its latest manufacturing processes, especially 3-nanometer technology, which alone reached a 25% share of wafer revenue. This shift shows that TSMC’s customers, such as makers of artificial intelligence chips and advanced electronics, are rapidly embracing next-generation designs.

Profitability metrics expanded sharply year over year, with gross margin at 66.2% and operating margin at 58.1% in the first quarter of 2026. This resulted in net income increasing at a faster pace than sales, reflecting operational leverage as production scaled up on higher-value work.

Looking forward, management’s revenue outlook implies continued business strength. However, guidance for flat to only slightly higher margins means investors should watch for signs that efficiency gains and premium pricing may be reaching a plateau if costs or competition increase.
