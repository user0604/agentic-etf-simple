Spruce Biosciences Cuts Q1 Loss by 12%

Key Takeaways
Spruce Biosciences made progress advancing its lead therapy while reducing its net loss.
Net loss improved to $12.3 million, with R&D expenses down year over year.
Management reaffirmed its plan for a regulatory filing by late 2026 and expects its cash to last into the second half of 2027.

Spruce Biosciences (NASDAQ:SPRB) made steady operational progress in the first quarter, continuing to advance its lead drug candidate while tightening expenses. The rare disease-focused biotech develops therapies for conditions with significant unmet need, and in the period ending March 31, 2026 (earnings released May 13, 2026), its results showed disciplined cost control and clinical momentum. Overall, the quarter marked a step forward operationally, though the company remains fully reliant on a single development program and has no current product revenue.

Notable developments
Spruce reaffirmed that its planned Biologics License Application (BLA) for TA-ERT, a treatment for MPS IIIB, remains on schedule for submission in the fourth quarter of 2026.
Research and development spending fell by nearly 30% year over year, mainly due to the absence of one-time charges seen in the prior year, with cost focus now centered on advancing TA-ERT.
As of April, the company raised $69.0 million in gross proceeds, strengthening its cash position and extending its funding runway into the second half of 2027.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reiterated its regulatory timeline, with the goal of submitting the TA-ERT therapy for approval by late 2026. The company expects its cash resources, bolstered by the recent offering, to be sufficient to fund operations and obligations into the second half of 2027. The single most important factor for investors will be the clinical and regulatory progress of TA-ERT, as Spruce has no commercial products and all future revenue prospects hinge on this program.

Quarterly highlights

During the quarter, Spruce presented new long-term clinical data for its lead therapy, TA-ERT, demonstrating the drug’s ability to stabilize cognitive function and reduce key biomarkers in patients with MPS IIIB, a rare genetic disease. This supported the company’s goal of meeting regulatory requirements for its planned submission in late 2026.

On the financial front, overall research and development costs declined sharply versus the previous year, reflecting both operational discipline and a narrowing focus since discontinuing other prior programs. General and administrative expenses increased, with higher spending directed toward building pre-commercial infrastructure and expanding leadership in preparation for a potential product launch.

The company reported a net loss of $12.3 million for the quarter, a notable improvement from the prior year. Its balance sheet was further strengthened after quarter-end, with a public offering raising significant additional capital and new lending facilities arranged without restrictive covenants. This positions Spruce with runway beyond anticipated drug approval timelines.

What Spruce Biosciences does

Spruce Biosciences develops therapies targeting rare and genetic diseases with severe unmet medical need, focusing primarily on its lead treatment for MPS IIIB.
