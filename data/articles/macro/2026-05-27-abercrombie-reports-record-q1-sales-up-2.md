Abercrombie & Fitch (NYSE:ANF) set a new record for first-quarter sales, but profit margins narrowed and earnings fell compared to the prior year. The global specialty apparel retailer, which operates the Abercrombie, Hollister, and abercrombie kids brands, released its fiscal 2026 first-quarter results on May 27, 2026. The company reported steady sales growth but noted rising costs and weaker performance in Europe, the Middle East, and Africa.

Overall, the period reflected disciplined sales growth but also highlighted ongoing challenges for profit margins and international operations.

Notable Developments
Net sales climbed 2%, reaching a record $1.11 billion, as the Americas and Asia-Pacific both posted growth despite Europe, Middle East, and Africa sales dropping 10%.
Operating margin fell by 1.3 percentage points to 8.0%, as profitability declined due to higher selling and administrative costs along with tariff pressures.
Net cash from operating activities improved to $44 million from a negative result last year, reflecting better inventory control and cash management.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership reiterated its full-year goals for 3% to 5% sales growth and double-digit operating margins, keeping guidance unchanged despite the quarter’s revenue record. Management cited ongoing uncertainty in Europe and the impact of tariffs as key reasons for caution. Investors should watch for any developments in EMEA performance and further progress in digital transformation and brand growth.

Quarterly Highlights

The $1.11 billion revenue figure marked Abercrombie & Fitch’s fourteenth straight quarter of sales growth. Performance in the Americas segment was strong, growing 3% to $900 million, while Asia-Pacific saw an even larger jump of 24%, partially offsetting the sharp 10% decline in EMEA.

Profitability came under pressure as operating margin declined to 8.0%, down 1.3 percentage points year over year. Higher selling expenses, continued investment in marketing, and new tariffs were highlighted as factors driving this contraction, despite some improvement in cost of goods sold as a percentage of revenue.

Cash flow improved significantly, with net operating cash moving from a $4 million outflow last year to a $44 million inflow. Inventory levels were managed closely, ending slightly lower than the prior year and contributing to stable store operations and promotional discipline.
