Enterprise customer expansion drove a solid quarter for Similarweb (NYSE:SMWB), but recent declines in customer retention rates highlight areas for caution. The digital intelligence platform provider, which delivers web analytics and AI-powered data to businesses worldwide, released its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 13, 2026. Overall, the period reflected healthy progress on commercial goals but highlighted lumpy revenue streams and the need to improve retention trends.

Notable developments
Revenue reached $73.9 million, at the very top end of management’s $72–74 million guidance range for the quarter.
The number of customers with annual recurring revenue of $100,000 or more grew to 461 (up 11.9%), and now accounts for 64% of ARR, compared to 61% a year earlier.
Dollar-based net retention rate for these larger customers slipped to 103%, from 111% in the prior year quarter, reflecting less expansion or increased churn from this group.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised the lower end of its 2026 full-year forecast for both revenue ($307–315 million) and non-GAAP operating profit ($17–19 million), pointing to continued growth in AI-driven data deals and larger, multi-year client contracts. Looking ahead, investors should closely watch whether one-time AI and data training agreements become recurring revenue, as well as whether retention rates recover.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue growth accelerated, supported by expanded demand for AI-related data solutions and the launch of a major new retail intelligence product. The company’s platform saw improved sales productivity and the addition of a major “seven digit” contract with a global tech customer for AI data.

The customer base continued to shift toward larger, multi-year enterprise deals. Multi-year contracts now make up 64% of all annual recurring revenue, up sharply from 52% a year ago, suggesting greater predictability and customer commitment.

However, cash flow from core operations dropped substantially and net retention rates fell—both signs that the company’s success in landing large deals has not yet translated into stable, recurring expansion from existing clients. This “lumpiness” in large-data contracts remains a central question for sustainable growth.

What Similarweb does

Similarweb provides digital intelligence and data analytics tools that help companies analyze web traffic, online trends, and competitors across global digital markets.
