Dropbox Posts 8.6% EPS Gain in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:24 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Dropbox delivered a modest business win by beating its internal targets despite sluggish user metrics.
Non-GAAP EPS rose 8.6% while revenue increased 0.8% year over year.
Management signals steady performance but cautions that revenue and user growth are likely to remain flat in the near term.

Cloud collaboration provider Dropbox (NASDAQ:DBX) topped its own financial targets in the first quarter by focusing on retention and platform improvements, even as growth in users and annual recurring revenue stalled. The company, known for its file storage and productivity services, released its quarterly results on May 7, 2026, covering the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter revealed stability in revenue and cash flow but continued pressure on margins and user growth.

Notable Developments
Revenue grew to $629.5 million, up 0.8% year over year, with growth of 2.0% excluding the wind-down of the FormSwift business.
Operating margins declined, with GAAP operating margin sliding to 27.5%.
Unlevered free cash flow (a measure of cash generated before financing costs) rose 35.6% to $236.4 million, reflecting fewer one-time outflows and tight spending controls.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reiterated that it expects flat revenue and continued investment in platform innovation for the remainder of fiscal 2026, with operating margins likely to settle near 39–39.5% for the year. Investors should pay close attention to user metrics and the rollout of AI-enabled products like Dash, as these are critical to future growth but have yet to materially impact revenue or user counts.

Quarterly Highlights

The company stabilized its revenue stream despite reporting a slight decline in paying users, down to 18.09 million from 18.16 million the prior year. However, average revenue per user increased, offsetting much of this drop and supporting the revenue figure.

Profitability metrics showed some stress: both gross and operating margins contracted from the year-ago period. The company attributed these pressures to ongoing investment in its Dash platform, an AI-powered universal search feature, and higher interest expenses linked to greater borrowing.

Free cash flow was notably strong during the quarter, bolstered by the absence of previous year’s one-time costs like lease buyouts and workforce reductions. The company reported a cash and short-term investment balance of $1.289 billion at quarter end.
