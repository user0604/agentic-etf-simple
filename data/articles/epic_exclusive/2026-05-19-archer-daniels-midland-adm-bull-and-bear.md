Archer-Daniels-Midland: Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 4:59 PM

Comments

Archer-Daniels-Midland (NYSE:ADM) is a global leader in agricultural origination, processing, and nutrition solutions. The company connects crops to markets and transforms them into products for food, feed, industrial, and renewable fuel end markets. ADM’s long-term growth strategy emphasizes expanding in value-added nutrition segments, leveraging biofuel market tailwinds, driving operational efficiency, and investing in sustainability and innovation to position the business for secular trends in food security, ESG, and decarbonization.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Disciplined Capital Allocation and Dividend Track Record Underpin Shareholder Value

ADM’s commitment to capital discipline and dividend growth establishes a reliable foundation for long-term total returns. The company paid its 378th consecutive quarterly dividend in June 2026, marking a record 53 years of uninterrupted and growing dividends. In Q1 2026 alone, $254 million was returned to shareholders via dividends, backed by resilient cash flow from operations of $442 million (before working capital changes). Capital expenditure guidance for 2026 stands at $1.3 to $1.5 billion, supporting both maintenance and strategic growth initiatives while keeping leverage manageable at 2.2x, a level projected to normalize at 2x by year-end. This financial prudence gives ADM flexibility for bolt-on mergers and acquisitions or potential future buybacks, ensuring the company can balance reinvestment and return of capital regardless of commodity cycles, a critical feature for business-focused, income-oriented investors.

2. Regulatory Tailwinds in Biofuels and Renewable Policies Enhance Earnings Visibility

Recent U.S. policy clarity and incentives have significantly improved margin outlooks for ADM’s biofuel and feedstock businesses. The Environmental Protection Agency’s March 2026 finalization of Renewable Volume Obligations for 2026-2027 has boosted market confidence, directly increasing both domestic and export demand for corn and soy used in renewable diesel and ethanol. RIN (Renewable Identification Number) price strength has lifted utilization and margins across the sector. Additionally, the introduction of the Section 45Z tax credit is expected to contribute $150 million in full-year 2026 segment income. These stable and predictable regulatory structures support capacity utilization, allow ADM to better forecast margin environments, and reduce one-off volatility, underpinning the company’s raised 2026 adjusted earnings per share guidance of $4.15 to $4.70, up from $3.60 to $4.25 previously.

3. Carbohydrate Solutions Segment Delivers Breakout Profit Growth from Ethanol

ADM’s Carbohydrate Solutions segment is generating both cyclical and structural profit gains from robust ethanol margins and innovation. In Q1 2026, segment operating profit surged by 48% year over year to $356 million, fueled by favorable ethanol economics, effective risk management, and policy incentives. The Vantage Corn Processors (dry milling ethanol) subsegment alone contributed $127 million in operating profit, up $94 million versus Q1 2025, while base ethanol EBITDA margins improved both sequentially and annually. As global mandates for higher biofuel content expand (with U.S. ethanol exports forecast at 2.4 billion gallons in 2026, nearly 17 billion gallons when combined with domestic use), ADM enjoys favorable end-market dynamics. Ongoing innovation and grind diversification efforts position the business to sustain and grow these profits even as the product mix and regulatory environment evolve.

4. Decarbonization and Advanced Nutrition Investment Open New High-Margin Growth Channels

ADM is systematically shifting toward higher-margin, innovation-driven platforms anchored in sustainability and functional nutrition. The company sequestered approximately 300,000 metric tonnes of carbon dioxide in Q1 2026 and is investing in renewable natural gas and sustainable aviation fuel as next-generation growth drivers. In nutrition, segment operating profit jumped 42% to $135 million in Q1 2026 despite flat revenue, reflecting the positive impact from advanced flavors, biosolutions, and the recovery of the Decatur East plant. Human nutrition profits rose 39% on increased flavor sales and operational optimization. These moves demonstrate ADM’s ability to leverage R&D and portfolio development to generate value from secular trends in health, wellness, and sustainability — markets with less exposure to raw commodity cycles and higher price resiliency.

5. Cost Efficiency and Supply Chain Optimization Initiatives Support Margin Expansion and Resilience

ADM’s multi-year cost reduction and productivity programs are enabling the company to combat input price volatility and improve earnings quality. The company is targeting $500 to $750 million in aggregate cost savings over the 2025–2028 period, driven by further automation, artificial intelligence deployment, and supply chain enhancements. Q1 2026 saw record global crush production with improved throughput and lower downtime, signaling operational execution despite commodity and transportation headwinds. These efficiency projects not only unlock higher incremental margins in strong demand periods but also provide downside protection when markets are turbulent, supporting more stable cash flow and underpinning shareowner confidence through cycles.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Repeated Commodity Price Volatility and Mark-to-Market Swings Can Obscure True Profitability

Mark-to-market and timing impacts can introduce significant earnings swings and hinder investor confidence in underlying earnings stability. In Q1 2026, ADM recorded $275 million in net negative mark-to-market and timing effects, particularly in its Ag Services & Oilseeds segment, representing a more than twelvefold increase over the prior year’s comparable quarter. Although management expects much of this to reverse in subsequent quarters as inventory and hedging positions settle, such volatility creates challenges for forecasting, valuation, and perceived earnings quality. The company’s practice of excluding future mark-to-market impacts from guidance further raises uncertainty, leaving open the possibility of recurring negative surprises in periods of rapid commodity price movement.

2. Weaker Demand and Persistent Margin Compression in Core Starches and Sweeteners Underline Risks to Segment Profitability

Secular declines and volume pressure in starches and sweeteners threaten ADM’s ability to grow or even sustain profitability in legacy segments. While Carbohydrate Solutions delivered headline profit growth thanks to ethanol, the starches and sweeteners subsegment grew operating profit by just 11% in Q1 2026, trailing the broader segment, with ongoing margin and volume headwinds reflecting lifestyle changes and evolving customer preferences. Management continues to reference “meal demand” and the need for “grind diversification,” signaling concern over the sustainability of this foundational business. If innovation or alternative uses fail to offset soft traditional end markets, ADM’s overall earnings base could become increasingly concentrated — and vulnerable — in fewer, more volatile profit drivers.

3. Macro and Geopolitical Instability Remain Persistent Wildcards for Revenue, Costs, and Export Markets

Global disruptions and policy shocks have the potential to upend operating assumptions, dampen demand, or increase costs with little warning. The company’s raised 2026 outlook is supported by recent regulatory wins and market stabilization, but significant uncertainty remains from areas such as Middle East conflict, U.S.-China trade relations, and ongoing geopolitical risks. ADM explicitly references confidence in margin improvement even against the backdrop of continued macro and geopolitical uncertainty, yet these uncontrollable factors can reduce export volumes, trigger supply chain bottlenecks, or cause cost spikes in energy and logistics — any of which could offset or reverse progress in cost efficiency and margin trends. Investors must recognize that even a diversified multinational like ADM is not immune to external shocks that disrupt global agricultural markets.
