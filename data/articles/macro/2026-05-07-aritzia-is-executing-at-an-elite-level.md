Aritzia Is Executing at an Elite Level

The fashion retailer continues to fire on all cylinders, posting accelerating growth and raising the bar for what investors should expect going forward.

Nicholas Sciple

May 7, 2026, 10:35 PM

Comments
8.0

Aritzia (OTC: ATZAF) continues to execute across every metric you could want from a retailer. Like James Carville debating Will Ferrell in the 2003 film Old School, "We have no response. That was perfect."

Net revenue jumped 32.6% to $1.19 billion in Q4, with comparable sales growth of 27.7% on top of last year's 26% comp. The U.S. business grew 37.8% to $755 million and now represents nearly 64% of the mix, while Canada still managed 24.3% growth. Retail was up 35% on the back of 14 new boutiques opened over the last 12 months (including a flagship in Manhattan's Flatiron district), and digital climbed 29.2% with help from the new Aritzia app.

Margins held up well against trade-policy pressure. Gross margin expanded 90 basis points to 43.3% even after absorbing higher tariffs and the elimination of the de minimis exemption. SG&A leverage drove another 110 basis points of operating margin improvement. Adjusted EBITDA margin reached 18.6%, up 60 basis points, and adjusted EPS came in at $1.15, a 38.6% increase year-over-year.

For the full year, Aritzia delivered $3.7 billion in revenue, growing 35.2% and pulling forward the Fiscal 2027 revenue target of $3.5 to $3.8 billion that management set back in 2022. They hit it a year early. Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 260 basis points to 17.5%, despite absorbing a matching 260-basis-point tariff and de minimis headwind.

Capital spending is climbing alongside the revenue base. The cumulative capex plan for Fiscal 2024 through Fiscal 2027 is now approximately $900 million, up from a prior $750 million assumption. The extra $150 million covers additional real estate work in FY26 and FY27, plus boutique projects pulled forward from FY28. Fiscal 2027 capex alone is guided to roughly $250 million, with $210 million tied to new and repositioned boutiques. Given new-store payback periods of 12 to 18 months and an identified opportunity for 180-plus U.S. locations against 76 today, leaning into real estate looks like the right call.

The balance sheet keeps getting stronger. Cash sits at $592 million, more than double last year's $286 million, with no debt drawn. Free cash flow more than quintupled to $487 million. Management put $145 million toward buybacks during the year and announced a fresh normal course issuer bid.

Guidance for Fiscal 2027 calls for revenue of $4.4 to $4.6 billion (19% to 24% growth), gross margin expansion of another 150 to 200 basis points, and adjusted EBITDA margin around 19% (compared to 17.5% in FY 2026). Q1 alone is tracking to $900 to $925 million, up 36% to 39%.

With the long-term plan announced in 2022 complete, CEO Jennifer Wong said a fresh strategic plan is coming this fall. Aritzia keeps doing what it said it would do, ahead of schedule despite real tariff pressure. We see plenty of room left for the stock to run.
