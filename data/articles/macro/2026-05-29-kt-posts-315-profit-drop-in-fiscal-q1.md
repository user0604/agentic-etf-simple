Notable Developments
Segment revenue for the core information and communications technology (ICT) business increased 3.3%, but segment profit fell by 21.5% as costs rose.
Operating expenses grew 2.3% year over year, outpacing revenue and mainly driven by higher employee costs and promotional spending.
The board approved a quarterly dividend of ₩600 per share, maintaining stability on shareholder returns even as net income dropped 31.5% from last year.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year financial forecast and emphasized continued investment in cybersecurity and network infrastructure. However, it noted persistent pressure on costs and limited progress in new digital and business-to-business services. Investors should closely watch improvement in operating profit and signs of stabilization in margin performance over the upcoming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The ICT business remained the company’s largest revenue source, but operating income in this segment decreased sharply, affected by increased labor and sales promotion costs. While real estate operations delivered a notable jump in both revenue and profit — up 73.5% and 221%, respectively — this rebound started from a weak base last year and may not be repeatable.

The company’s consolidated number of wireless subscribers climbed 10.3% year over year to 29.16 million, but its reported churn rate (customers leaving for other providers) rose to 1.7%. At the same time, average monthly revenue per user slipped, reflecting a competitive market and possible pricing pressures.

Outside of its main telecom activities, KT’s satellite TV and finance units both posted lower segment profits. In the period, the company began the process to spin off a content channel unit, signaling ongoing reevaluation of its business portfolio.

What KT does

KT is a leading Korean telecommunications and digital services provider, offering mobile, broadband, media, IT, and related solutions for consumers and businesses.
