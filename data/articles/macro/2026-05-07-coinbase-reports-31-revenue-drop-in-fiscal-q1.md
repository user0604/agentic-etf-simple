Key Takeaways
Coinbase Global reported a sharp revenue decline and a return to net loss as crypto activity slowed.
Adjusted EBITDA dropped approximately 67% year over year.
Management outlined ongoing cost controls and product expansion while warning of market volatility ahead.

Crypto exchange operator Coinbase Global (NASDAQ:COIN) felt the pinch as weak crypto trading volumes led to a big fall in revenue and pushed the company back to a net loss. The leading U.S. crypto platform highlighted strategic progress in expanding its suite of products and maintaining strong trust with customers. It released first quarter results on May 7, 2026, for the period ending March 31. Overall, the quarter underscored resilience in operating performance against considerable industry headwinds but saw financials weighed down by depressed markets.

Notable Developments
Total revenue fell 31.0% year over year as crypto market trading activity dropped and transaction revenue dropped 40% from last year.
Subscription and services revenue provided a buffer, making up 44% of net revenue and reaching $584 million during the quarter.
Management reported achieving or beating its own cost and revenue guidance for the period, with technology and administrative expenses slightly below expectations.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management signaled continued investment in new products but expects operating expenses to decrease due to headcount reductions and ongoing cost discipline. For the second quarter, leaders forecast stable or slightly lower subscription and services revenue and more muted transaction revenues given weak crypto volumes. Investors should focus on the sustainability of revenue from newer products, expense control execution, and ongoing market share shifts as the most important factors for future periods.

Quarterly Highlights

The sharp fall in transaction revenue reflected a combined 23% quarter over quarter and 40% year-over-year decline. This was driven by lower crypto asset prices and trading activity industry-wide, though the company outperformed total spot market volume trends.

Stablecoin and subscription product revenues helped cushion these declines, contributing $584 million and forming a growing line of recurring revenues. The company’s U.S. dollar stablecoin (USDC) business reached an all-time high in on-platform balances, with over 25% of all outstanding USDC held within company products.

On the product side, new offerings such as derivatives and prediction markets scaled quickly, each reaching annualized revenue run rates above $100 million. The retail derivatives line crossed $200 million in run rate, and decentralized finance integrations doubled decentralized exchange trading volume in just one quarter.
