Spine and musculoskeletal device maker Globus Medical (NYSE:GMED) delivered a strong start to 2026, underpinned by revenue growth, expanding profit margins, and progress on integrating recent acquisitions. The company, a leading designer and manufacturer of medical devices for musculoskeletal disorders, released its first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, the quarter demonstrated business strength, although the integration of acquired businesses and sustained margin gains remain key watch items.

Notable Developments
Base business revenue, excluding Nevro, increased 13.2%, while total worldwide net sales grew 27.0% thanks to recent acquisitions.
Adjusted gross margin improved by 1.9 percentage points to 69.2%, as operational and manufacturing efficiencies took hold.
The company raised its full-year non-GAAP EPS guidance to $4.70–$4.80 and reaffirmed revenue guidance at $3.18–$3.22 billion.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed revenue guidance for fiscal 2026 and increased its earnings per share outlook, expressing confidence in continued profitability and successful acquisition integration. The most important area for investors to monitor is the ongoing performance and margin improvement of recently acquired businesses, particularly Nevro, as their profitability still lags the core business.

Quarterly Highlights

Organic growth continued as base business sales rose by double digits, even without the added contribution from Nevro. The U.S. market remained the largest driver, supported by a stronger sales force and sustained demand for core spine products.

Profitability advanced, with adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 32.3%. Improvements came from both higher sales volume and operational discipline, though integrating lower-margin businesses remains a challenge for overall margin targets.

The integration of Nevro led to additional revenue, but with an adjusted EBITDA margin around 12%, further work is needed to bring acquired operations up to the company's standards. Management has named ongoing cost structure improvements and cross-selling as priorities in this area.
