Key Takeaways
Evogene reported a significant business setback as key revenue streams declined and losses increased.
Revenue decreased sharply year over year, with net loss widening on higher financing costs and lower sales.
Management anticipates operational progress and incoming cash from asset sales, but near-term revenue outlook remains uncertain.

Evogene (NASDAQ:EVGN) reported a challenging start to fiscal 2026 as former core businesses wound down, pushing revenue sharply lower and widening losses. The computational biology and generative AI innovator is focusing on its ChemPass AI™ platform and new collaborations, striving for future commercialization. The results for the quarter ended March 31, 2026, were announced on May 20, 2026, and highlighted both operational cost discipline and the urgent need to turn technical partnerships into tangible revenue. Overall, the period reflected progress on refocusing but exposed a weak top-line and heightened losses.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell to $0.3 million, down from $2.3 million, as prior sales from Casterra operations nearly vanished.
Operating loss increased to approximately $3.2 million from $3.0 million last year, as cost cuts in research and administration were offset by lower revenue.
Net loss almost doubled to approximately $5.9 million due to one-time financing expenses tied to warrant inducement, even as total cash reserves edged higher to approximately $13.1 million.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management remains focused on strengthening the ChemPass AI™ platform and deepening its network of partnerships in both pharma and agriculture. Evogene expects to receive cash distributions from sold or shuttered subsidiaries in the next quarter, which could support its liquidity. Investors should closely watch whether recent collaborations convert into milestone or recurring revenue in the coming periods.

Quarterly highlights

The collapse in revenue came mainly from the near-total halt of Casterra seed sales, with former streams contributing almost nothing in the quarter. The company’s new focus is on generative AI and small-molecule drug and agchemical discovery, supported by technical advancements such as its expanded ChemPass AI™ collaboration with Google Cloud.

Operating expenses dropped year over year, with research and development and general expenses each lower than the prior period as cost-control measures took hold. Despite these reductions, losses grew as the revenue shortfall outpaced savings and substantial non-operating costs came from warrant-related fundraising.

Strategic exits from non-core subsidiaries continued, with Evogene set to receive cash dividends from Lavie Bio and Biomica in the next quarter. Meanwhile, AgPlenus refocused on in-house fungicide programs after ending its partnership with Bayer, a setback for Evogene's collaboration-driven model.

What Evogene does

Evogene uses artificial intelligence technologies to develop products for the life sciences sector, focusing on pharmaceuticals and agriculture.
