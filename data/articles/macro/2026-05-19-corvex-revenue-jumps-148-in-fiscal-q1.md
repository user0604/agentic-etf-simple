Corvex (NASDAQ:MOVE) entered a new phase as it shifted its business to focus on AI cloud infrastructure following a major acquisition. The AI cloud provider completed the purchase of Corvex OpCo and exited its legacy device business during the period ending March 31, 2026 (results announced May 19, 2026). The quarter showed early signs of demand for its computing platform, but also included integration costs and continued operating losses. Overall, the period marked foundational change, with strong expansion in contracted business but realized revenue and profit still lagging.

Notable developments

Revenue reflected only 12 days of operations from the acquired AI platform business, while the deferred revenue balance increased to $4.4 million as of period-end.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management expressed optimism about defining the “next era” of AI infrastructure but did not issue a quantitative outlook for the rest of fiscal 2026. With a growing pipeline and $4.4 million in deferred revenue—funds billed but not yet earned—future recognition trends will be crucial. Investors should track how fast contracted business materializes as reported revenue and whether cash reserves support the company's scale-up ambitions.

Quarterly highlights

The shift from legacy devices to exclusive focus on AI cloud infrastructure was evident, with $475 thousand of AI platform and services revenue included for only part of the quarter. Overall revenue rose sharply year over year, reflecting the impact of the new business model and partial benefit from recent client wins.

Deferred revenue climbed from $12 thousand to $4.4 million, indicating that customers are contracting for AI infrastructure capacity to be delivered and recognized over future periods. This expansion points toward sustainable future revenue, but actual conversion rates will dictate growth in coming quarters.

Operating expenses stayed flat at $5.4 million but changed in makeup, with lower technology and marketing spend offset by higher general and administrative costs tied to the transaction and new operations. The decline in adjusted EBITDA loss to $(1.6) million reflects tighter spending and an improving underlying margin profile, though net loss remained significant and cash burn elevated due to investments in infrastructure.
