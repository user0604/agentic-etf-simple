Key Takeaways
Virco Mfg. saw losses widen as key sales metrics declined compared to last year.
Gross margin narrowed significantly from the prior-year period.
Management expects a return to normal seasonality, with stronger results likely during the peak summer quarters.

School furniture producer Virco Mfg. (NASDAQ:VIRC) faced a difficult off-season as it reported a wider quarterly loss amid lower sales and tighter margins. The leading designer and manufacturer of furniture and fixtures for educational institutions noted a return to typical seasonal patterns, but the year-over-year comparison showed marked declines. The latest results, released on June 3, 2026, cover the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and set the stage for a pivotal summer selling period.

Overall, the quarter reflected normalization of demand after post-pandemic disruptions, with lower revenue and profitability but strategic changes to inventory and cash management.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell to $30.7 million as order backlogs normalized, leading to a decline of 9.1% year over year.
Gross margin contracted by 6.1 percentage points, attributed largely to lower factory output and reduced absorption of fixed overhead costs.
The company improved its cash balance to $3.7 million and lowered inventory by 7.7% as it managed working capital more tightly.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide specific financial guidance but emphasized the return to “traditional pattern” seasonality, with losses expected in both the first and fourth quarters and profitability peaking midyear. The next quarters, driven by summer demand from schools, are expected to be more indicative of the company’s annual performance. Investors should monitor sales momentum and margin recovery as school deliveries accelerate in the summer months.

Quarterly Highlights

First quarter revenue dropped compared to last year as the company worked through the end of its prior-year backlog, signaling a return to historical sales seasonality. Management noted that incoming order rates remained roughly flat, but with a slightly higher backlog heading into the peak season.

Gross margin decreased meaningfully as reduced production volumes impacted the spread of fixed costs and there was limited ability to offset lower sales with expense reductions. According to management, most of this change resulted from fewer shipping days compared to the prior year when pandemic-driven backlog extended into the first quarter.

Virco continued to lower inventory levels and ended the quarter with improved liquidity, providing flexibility for the coming months. Nearly 80% of its order backlog now involves full-service projects, including design, planning, and installation, supporting its position in the market for integrated solutions.

What Virco Mfg. Does

Virco Mfg. designs, manufactures, and distributes furniture and fixtures for educational institutions throughout the United States.
