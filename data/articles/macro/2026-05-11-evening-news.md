1. Hims & Hers Raises the Bar

Hims & Hers (NYSE: HIMS) had a busy first-quarter 2026: Revenue hit $608 million (up 4%), subscribers climbed 9% to nearly 2.6 million, and the company raised full-year guidance to $2.8 billion to $3.0 billion. Oh, and they want to be the world's largest consumer health platform by 2030. No small ambitions here. Shares are down 9% after hours.

The margin hangover: Gross margin slipped from 73% to 65%, and the company swung from $49.5 million in net income to a $92 million net loss -- a trade-off worth watching, as HIMS invests heavily in GLP-1 products and new markets.
The moonshot number: Management is targeting $6.5 billion in revenue and $1.3 billion in adjusted EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) by 2030 -- roughly 10 times today's revenue run rate. Believers, take note.
2. Texas AG Sues Netflix for Spying on Kids

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton filed suit against Netflix (NASDAQ: NFLX) on Monday, accusing the streamer of secretly collecting and selling user data to advertisers. The company has publicly claimed otherwise; the complaint quotes former CEO Reed Hastings saying in 2020, "We don't collect anything." Paxton wants Netflix to purge illegally collected data and pay up to $10,000 per violation. "My read on the suit," wrote CMFFrankDip, Ticker Guide for Netflix, "is that Netflix is using viewing data to target people with programmable ads -- this is done by most every media company."

"When you watch Netflix, Netflix watches you": That's the AG's own language in the complaint -- and it covers children, too, with the suit alleging Netflix deliberately engineered its platform, including autoplay, to maximize screen time and data harvesting.
Billions at stake: Netflix allegedly sold viewer habits to commercial data brokers and adtech firms for years, generating substantial revenue -- now potentially subject to per-violation civil fines.
3. Don't Sleep on PACS; This Quarter Slaps

PACS Group (NYSE: PACS), which operates more than 320 skilled nursing and assisted living facilities across 17 states, just dropped a quarter that's hard to argue with. Revenue climbed 11.2% to $1.42 billion, net income nearly tripled to $80.7 million, and management liked what it saw enough to hike full-year adjusted EBITDA guidance to $605 million to $625 million -- about 22% above 2025's result at the midpoint. "If the business lives up to its potential," Motley Fool co-founder and CEO Tom Gardner wrote in the fall, I think this could be a $50-$60 stock in 5 years."

Lapping the industry isn't a fluke: Mature facilities hit 94.8% occupancy against an industry average of 79%. That's not luck -- that's a moat doing its job.
Now they can buy back stock, too: The board has approved a $250 million repurchase program -- and the CFO basically winked that they would've used it in Q1 if they could have.
