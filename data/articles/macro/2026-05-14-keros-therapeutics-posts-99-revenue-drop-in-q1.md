Keros Therapeutics Posts 99% Revenue Drop in Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 14, 2026, 8:11 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Keros Therapeutics recorded a significant business setback as revenue and profitability fell sharply following the transition of its lead asset.
Revenue dropped over 99% year over year, with the company posting an EPS loss instead of prior profit.
Management reiterated a strong cash runway into 2028 as pipeline progress now depends on external partnerships and internal clinical development.

Keros Therapeutics (NASDAQ:KROS) posted a steep loss as its revenue base shrank following the transfer of key development responsibilities to Takeda Pharmaceuticals. The clinical-stage biotech, focused on rare blood and muscle diseases, is now in a leaner operational phase prioritizing its therapeutic pipeline. The results, released on May 14, 2026 for the first quarter, confirmed a planned strategic shift but also exposed the volatility linked to one-time licensing revenue from the prior year. Overall, the quarter reflected disciplined spending but heightened risk and uncertainty about future milestones.

Notable Developments
Revenue fell from $211.2 million to just $0.4 million as license revenue from its Takeda agreement ended, exposing heavy reliance on one-off deals.
Operating costs declined sharply, with research and development expense dropping 66.9% due to project handoffs and cost-cutting.
Cash at quarter’s end totaled $281.5 million, which management expects will fund operations through the first half of 2028.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management affirmed that its cash and cash equivalents should fund planned operations into 2028. The primary area to watch is progress on its two lead clinical programs: elritercept, now under Takeda’s control for Phase 3 in myelofibrosis, and rinvatercept, Keros' main internal drug candidate.

Quarterly Highlights

The loss of licensing revenue caused the company’s revenue to contract by 99.8%, with the period showing almost no new income except for minor service work. This sharp drop illustrates how dependent the company was on a single milestone achieved in the prior-year period.

Spending dropped meaningfully as research and development expense fell to $16.1 million, a decrease of nearly $33 million year over year. This cost-cutting followed the transfer of elritercept (a protein therapeutic for blood diseases) to Takeda and internal restructuring that began in 2025.

Pipeline updates centered on external progress: Takeda now leads elritercept’s trials for anemia in myelofibrosis, while Keros continues to develop rinvatercept for Duchenne muscular dystrophy and amyotrophic lateral sclerosis. However, Keros disclosed no new clinical or regulatory milestones from its own programs in the period.

What Keros Therapeutics Does

Keros Therapeutics develops medicines targeting rare hematological and neuromuscular conditions using protein engineering and a focus on TGF-beta signaling pathways.
