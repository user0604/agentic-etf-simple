Huawei Unveils New Chip - and a Bold Claim

6:34AM

Chinese tech giant Huawei has announced a new chip design approach called "LogicFolding" to develop advanced semiconductors despite U.S. sanctions. The technology will debut this fall in its Kirin smartphone chips, potentially impacting Apple's (NASDAQ: AAPL) iPhone sales and narrowing Nvidia’s (NASDAQ: NVDA) window to sell chips in China.

Huawei claims this multi-layer architecture, based on its "Law of Tau," could deliver capabilities equivalent to 1.4-nanometer technology by 2031. For context, global leader TSMC (NYSE: TSM) has started volume production of 2-nanometer chips. A true 1.4-nanometer process represents a major leap that uses up to 30% less power.

However, industry analysts remain skeptical. They warn that while the design increases efficiency, Huawei still faces unproven manufacturing scales, tough heat management issues, and a lack of access to advanced ASML (NASDAQ: ASML) lithography machines.

Huawei's semiconductor president, Tingbo He, acknowledged that challenges remain as the company begins a decade-long development path.

Oil Markets Near 'Tank Bottoms' in Asia

6:00AM

Global oil markets are rapidly approaching minimum operating levels due to the Iran war and disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, warned veteran strategist Jeff Currie on Monday. Speaking at the UBS Wealth Conference in Singapore, Carlyle’s (NASDAQ: CG) chief strategy officer of energy pathways cautioned that headline inventory figures are misleading because much of the stored oil is required just to keep pipelines running safely.

According to Currie, Asia is already hitting these "tank bottoms," with Singapore seeing explosive prices shift from jet fuel to diesel. Europe is expected to face similar supply issues within a month once temporary U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve exports dry up. The United States could see its own shortages by July.

International Energy Agency chief Fatih Birol supported this outlook, warning the market could enter a "red zone" by July or August. Currie dismissed temporary fixes like suspending the U.S. gasoline tax, stating only an increase in physical oil supply can solve the crunch.
