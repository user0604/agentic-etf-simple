Key Takeaways
Revenue grew but profit margins declined.
Adjusted EPS fell to EUR 0.50, down 9% year over year.
Management reaffirmed its full-year guidance, including targets for higher adjusted gross profit and EBITDA margins for fiscal 2026.

Comfort footwear maker Birkenstock (NYSE:BIRK) reported steady revenue growth, but margin and profit figures moved lower as cost pressures mounted. The global footwear and lifestyle company reported its results for the fiscal second quarter on May 13, 2026, covering the three months ended March 31. Overall, the quarter saw resilient sales but weaker profitability as external factors impacted margins.

Notable developments
Revenue rose 8% on a reported basis, with every geographic segment advancing in constant currency terms.
Profitability weakened, as adjusted gross margin fell 3.1 percentage points year over year to 54.6%.
The company opened five new retail stores, bringing the total to 111 worldwide as of March 31, 2026.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its fiscal 2026 guidance, targeting constant-currency revenue growth of 13 to 15%, an adjusted gross profit margin of 57.0 to 57.5%, and an adjusted EBITDA margin of 30 to 30.5%. It expects earnings to recover in the second half of the year as operational and pricing measures take hold. Investors will want to watch whether margin recovery materializes as planned, especially if cost and currency headwinds persist.

Quarterly highlights

The revenue figure advanced to EUR 618 million, with the Asia-Pacific segment leading the way at 22% growth year over year on a reported basis. Channel growth was supported by both business-to-business (B2B) and direct-to-consumer (DTC) sales, reflecting a broader product assortment and further store openings.

Margins came under pressure, with adjusted gross margin down to 54.6% and adjusted EBITDA (a measure of core profitability before the impact of interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization) declining 1% year over year. Management cited unfavorable foreign exchange rates and new U.S. tariffs as key factors weighing down profitability.

The company continued its retail expansion, adding five stores in the quarter for a total of 111 globally, and noted strong growth for closed-toe footwear, especially in the Asia-Pacific market, where average selling prices are highest.

What Birkenstock does

Birkenstock designs, manufactures, and sells premium footwear and lifestyle products through a mix of wholesale partners, direct-to-consumer stores, and online channels.
