Alico Posts 33% EBITDA Jump in Fiscal Q2

The Motley Fool Team

May 11, 2026, 4:19 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Shift in business focus as legacy citrus operations wound down and land sales drive profitability.
Adjusted EBITDA rose 32.6% year over year.
Management maintains guidance for $14 million Adjusted EBITDA and expects next key development milestone later this year.

Alico (NASDAQ:ALCO) shifted decisively away from its traditional citrus farming roots, capitalizing on land sales to post a return to profitability. The leading Florida land management and development company continues a strategic transition as it exits citrus and invests in property entitlement, with the period reported on May 11, 2026 (covering the quarter ended March 31) marking significant progress. For the quarter, one-time asset gains outweighed shrinking ongoing revenues, underlining a business in the midst of transformation.

Overall, the period showed much-improved profitability alongside a continued reduction in legacy agricultural activity.

Notable Developments
Q2 revenue dropped 70.3% as citrus operations wound down, but net income swung positive with $11.4 million, primarily from $26.9 million in land sale proceeds.
Land Management and Other Operations revenue more than doubled, up 113.1% year over year, signaling early growth in leasing and diversified activities.
Entitlement progress continued as the company secured Collier County approvals for the key Corkscrew Grove Villages project, a milestone for future land development.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed full-year guidance for approximately $14 million in Adjusted EBITDA and flagged a year-end cash balance target of about $40 million, slightly lowered due to share repurchases. The company emphasized its focus on advancing permitting and development milestones for Corkscrew Grove Villages. Investors should monitor the status of state and federal permits and the ability to maintain strong land sale momentum as citrus exits reach completion.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s financial results reflect its transition: while revenue is sharply down due to the citrus wind down, the profit picture has turned positive on the back of substantial land sales. Gains from 2,950 acres sold in the quarter made up most of this positive swing, even as ongoing operating cash flow remained modest.

Recurring revenue from land management, including farm, sod, and grazing leases, showed further growth but remains small next to one-off land transactions. Land Management and Other Operations contributed $1.5 million, up from $0.7 million year over year, as more of the farmable land is now under lease, holding steady at about 97% utilization.

The strategic transformation advanced with successful regulatory approvals for the Corkscrew Grove Villages development. This county-level entitlement marks a key step, but major state and federal permits are still needed before construction can begin, likely in 2028 or 2029 if all requirements are met. The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.05 per share.

What Alico Does

Alico owns, manages, and develops large land holdings in Florida, focusing on land sales, leasing, and real estate development as it transitions out of citrus agriculture.
