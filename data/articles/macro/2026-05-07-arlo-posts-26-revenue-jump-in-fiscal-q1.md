Key Takeaways

Arlo Technologies (NYSE:ARLO) delivered a record-setting quarter, highlighting its successful shift to a subscription-focused business model. The smart home security specialist achieved strong growth in subscriptions and improved profitability, partly due to margin expansion and continued investment in technology. The earnings release, issued on May 7, 2026, covers results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and points to steady progress with a few areas to watch, such as inventory and product gross margin.

Overall, the quarter showed robust results as Arlo extended its lead in high-margin recurring revenue, even as certain product and inventory metrics warrant attention going forward.

Notable Developments
Subscriptions and services revenue reached $90.1 million, up from $68.8 million last year, now accounting for nearly 60% of total revenue.
Product gross margin remained negative at -4.6% but improved from the prior year’s -7.7%, reflecting continued focus on subscriber acquisition through hardware sales.
Cash and short-term investments rose by $14.4 million year over year, totaling $167.5 million, with $8.0 million used for share repurchases.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management forecasts second-quarter revenue between $145 million and $155 million, with non-GAAP EPS set at $0.17–$0.23. The company highlights ongoing investment in innovation, integration of new acquisitions, and the potential for some inventory build in key channels. Investors should watch the evolution of product margins, subscriber growth, and any signals of shifting hardware demand.

Quarterly Highlights

Arlo’s subscription and services business continues to be its growth engine, driving recurring revenue and high margins. Subscription revenue set a new record, hit a year-over-year growth of over 30%, and now represents almost 60% of total sales.

The company remains committed to technology leadership, as seen in its acquisition of Aloe Care Health, which brings new AI-powered features and positions Arlo in the aging-in-place care market. Research and development spending grew to $22.8 million this quarter, up from $16.2 million the year before.

Product gross margin stayed negative but saw notable improvement both year over year and sequentially. This reflects Arlo’s deliberate approach to using hardware sales as a way to acquire subscribers for its high-margin software and services, but continued negative margins remain an area needing attention if device volumes shift or conversion rates change.

What Arlo Technologies Does

Arlo Technologies designs and sells connected smart security products and cloud-based services, focusing on subscription software for home and business security solutions.
