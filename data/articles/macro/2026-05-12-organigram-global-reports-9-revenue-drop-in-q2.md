Notable Developments
Revenue declined nearly 9% year over year, with core Canadian recreational cannabis sales dropping by $6.6 million to $50.0 million.
Adjusted EBITDA—a non-GAAP profitability metric—fell sharply, primarily due to lower recreational cannabis sales, while operating expenses remained flat as a proportion of net revenue.
The company recorded a $5.8 million impairment charge related to U.S. regulatory risks in its hemp-derived product segment, highlighting external headwinds.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management updated its fiscal 2026 outlook to forecast over $350 million in annual revenue after the Sanity Group acquisition. The company expects international sales will drive most future growth, but these projections depend on successful integration and better execution in Canada. Investors should closely monitor progress in growing overseas revenue, especially through the new German subsidiary.

Quarterly Highlights

Canadian recreational cannabis remained the largest revenue source, accounting for more than 80% of quarterly sales, but saw a significant decline from the previous year. This drop outweighed modest gains from international markets and was a key reason for the overall revenue decrease.

Profitability compressed as adjusted gross margin fell to 31%, down 2 percentage points from the prior year. The margin squeeze was due to a less favorable product mix, higher returns on unsold inventory, and increased operating costs—including a $5.8 million asset impairment from U.S. hemp regulatory changes.

Operating cash use improved to $6.76 million, a positive when compared to the $16.59 million outflow last year. However, cost discipline remains a challenge, with operating expenses rising 23%, largely as a result of new marketing investments and one-time regulatory charges.
