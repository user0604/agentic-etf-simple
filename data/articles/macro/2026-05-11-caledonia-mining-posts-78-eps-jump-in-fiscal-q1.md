Caledonia Mining Posts 78% EPS Jump in Fiscal Q1

Key Takeaways
Caledonia Mining posted stronger profits despite lower gold production, as high gold prices offset operational challenges.
Earnings per share rose 77.8% year over year.
Management reaffirmed full-year production guidance and remains focused on operational improvement and project development.

High gold prices lifted profits for gold miner Caledonia Mining (NYSEMKT:CMCL), even as its main Zimbabwe operation faced significant production setbacks. The company, which operates the Blanket Mine and is progressing toward developing the larger Bilboes project, reported first quarter 2026 results on May 11, 2026. Overall, the period showed resilience in financial results despite a notable drop in production output and higher mining costs.

Notable developments
Gold production at the Blanket Mine dropped 20.9% to 14,767 ounces, while companywide gold sales fell 28.9% to 13,784 ounces.
The average realized gold price surged 66.3%, which drove an 18.3% increase in revenue and boosted profitability metrics despite higher costs.
The quarterly dividend was maintained at $0.14 per share, underlining management’s confidence in future cash flows.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its annual production guidance of 72,000–76,500 ounces at Blanket Mine, expecting operational improvements to shift output toward the second half of the year. Cost pressures are projected to ease as higher grades are accessed and production increases. Investors should closely monitor progress on these operational changes and updates on Bilboes project funding and development.

Quarterly highlights

The sharp rise in the company’s average realized gold price, reaching $4,816 per ounce, allowed Caledonia Mining to post higher earnings even though its gold output fell markedly compared to the prior year. This price-driven effect helped offset a rise in the all-in sustaining cost per ounce, a key mining industry measure that includes both operating and capital costs for each ounce produced.

Operational difficulties at Blanket Mine caused the head grade (the amount of gold per ton of ore) to drop from 3.1 grams per ton to 2.5 grams per ton year over year, and management implemented new shift patterns and hired contractors in response. The cost per ounce sold climbed to $2,765, the result of fixed costs being divided among fewer ounces and ongoing investment in underground development.

Looking forward, plans to commission a new ball mill and to accelerate access to higher-grade areas are intended to restore production volume and efficiency. At the same time, Caledonia Mining retained a solid financial position with $170 million in cash and continues to invest in exploration and growth, including progressing the Bilboes project, which aims to significantly increase the company’s future gold output at a lower cost per ounce.
