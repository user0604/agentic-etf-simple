Lyft Posts 17% Rider Growth in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 7, 2026, 4:23 PM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Lyft delivered strong growth in riders and cash flow, but margin improvement was modest.
Active riders rose 17% to 28.3 million compared to the prior year.
Management projects continued growth but highlights the need to watch cost discipline and successful integration of new businesses.

Ride-hailing platform Lyft (NASDAQ:LYFT) showed robust user growth and strong cash generation in its latest quarter, even as profit margins remained thin. The company is a major player in on-demand transportation, matching riders and drivers in cities across North America and beyond. Reporting results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026, Lyft posted year-over-year increases in most major operating and financial metrics. Overall, the quarter marked clear operating progress, but ongoing cost growth and questions around profitability add complexity to the outlook.

Notable Developments
Active rider growth was strong, increasing 16.9% to 28.3 million, marking the sixth straight quarter with double-digit increases.
International expansion accelerated as Lyft closed its acquisition of Gett’s UK business and announced upcoming launches in London and Nashville for its new autonomous vehicle (AV) fleet program.
Sales and marketing expenses jumped 50% year over year, raising questions about future margin expansion even as free cash flow reached $287.3 million in the quarter.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects continued top-line gains, calling for gross bookings growth of 18% to 21% and higher adjusted operating profit in the next quarter. The company also emphasized greater international contributions and ongoing investments in autonomous driving and technology. Investors should watch for results from international business integration and clearer signs of cost control, particularly in non-core expenses.

Quarterly Highlights

Lyft saw its rider base climb, with 28.3 million active riders in the quarter, which the company attributes to growing demand for its ride-hailing service and successful partnerships. Rides in North America linked to partners like Chase and DoorDash accounted for a record 27% of total trips, underlining the significance of these collaborations for user acquisition and engagement.

The acquisition of Gett UK extended Lyft’s reach into London, Europe’s largest ride-hail market, and the company confirmed plans to launch its Flexdrive autonomous vehicle fleet in Nashville later in 2026. However, details about the immediate financial impact of these international moves remain limited in the release.

While revenue and free cash generation strengthened, operating costs—especially in areas like sales, marketing, and general and administrative spending—rose rapidly. Adjusted EBITDA margin was little changed at 2.7%, suggesting that profitability gains are lagging behind the company’s expanding scale.
