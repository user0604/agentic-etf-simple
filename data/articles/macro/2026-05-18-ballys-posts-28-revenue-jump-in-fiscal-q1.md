Key Takeaways

Bally’s (NYSE:BALY) posted strong top-line growth on the back of acquisitions and digital gains, though profit margins at its traditional casinos did not keep pace. The diversified gaming and resort operator, which runs casinos, a growing digital platform, and lottery services worldwide, released results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 18, 2026. The period showed both headline revenue expansion and rising investment in major U.S. projects, but also revealed operational and integration risks that remain in focus.

Overall, the quarter brought notable revenue gains but uneven profitability across business units.

Notable developments
Headline revenue rose 28.3%, reflecting the impact of integrating Queen’s casinos and expanding digital gaming through Intralot B2C, where revenue growth reached 31.0%.
Significant capital was deployed, with $500 million paid for a New York casino license and new debt facilities established to support ongoing project and acquisition investments.
North America Interactive revenue increased 35.9% year over year, but the segment remained unprofitable with a negative $7.1 million in adjusted EBITDAR.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management expressed confidence in ongoing growth and integration projects, stating that it will "leverage our operational expertise and capital resources to deliver on our highly anticipated growth projects." Investors should watch for improvement in core casino profit margins and overall group profitability as new developments come online and integrations mature.

Quarterly highlights

The company’s revenue rose sharply, driven by contributions from new casino and digital acquisitions, such as Queen’s four properties and expanded Intralot digital gaming. Core digital segments, including Intralot B2C and North America Interactive, saw revenue increases above 30%, fueled by player growth and new market launches.

Despite this growth, the main casino segment’s profitability did not improve alongside revenue, partly due to heavier competition and added costs from integrating Queen’s operations. The company noted specific competition in regions like Shreveport and Dover that weighed on performance.

Bally’s moved ahead with major, multi-year property projects in Chicago, the Bronx, and Las Vegas, committing significant funds and taking on refinancing activities to manage its nearly $4.4 billion in long-term debt. The period included the completion of a license payment for the Bronx casino.
