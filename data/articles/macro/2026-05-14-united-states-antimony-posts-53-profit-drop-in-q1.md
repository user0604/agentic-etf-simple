Key Takeaways
United States Antimony posted a steep net loss as profitability and volumes declined during a heavy investment phase.
Gross profit fell over 50% from the prior year on higher costs and lower sales volumes, despite rising antimony prices.

United States Antimony (NYSE:UAMY) reported a challenging start to the year as swift investments and rising costs led to a significant net loss. The leading domestic producer and processor of antimony and zeolite saw most key financial metrics fall in the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (release date: May 14, 2026). Overall, the period was marked by major capital spending and inventory builds intended to enable future contract fulfillment and capacity expansion.

Notable Developments
Gross profit declined sharply, dropping over 50% year over year to $1.1 million as cost per pound of antimony rose 69%.
Inventory ballooned to $22.0 million, up more than 500% from a year earlier, reflecting preparations for upcoming contract shipments and facility expansion.
No revenue was recognized from recently secured government contracts in the period, with management expecting fulfillment to ramp up as new plant capacity comes online.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 gross revenue guidance of $125 million. It characterized the first quarter as an “investment phase,” emphasizing that revenue and margin expansion depend on the timely commissioning of newly expanded facilities and the start of government contract deliveries. The key factor for investors to monitor next is how quickly the Thompson Falls smelter expansion and Montana in-house antimony production contribute to revenue and margins.

Quarterly Highlights

The antimony segment saw sales volumes drop roughly 23% from the prior year, but average selling prices rose 22%. Higher purchase and processing costs drove gross margin lower, and the company noted that its own lower-cost mined ore was not yet included in this period’s results.

Inventory growth accounted for much of the cash outflow, with antimony inventory value jumping from $3.6 million a year ago to $22.0 million. This working capital build aims to support future contract deliveries but presents a risk if there are delays or price swings.

The company recognized no revenue yet from its long-term contracts with the U.S. government, as antimony deliveries are set to begin following completion of the Thompson Falls expansion, which is “nearing completion and is expected to come online over the next few weeks.” Management views the majority of annual revenue as likely to fall in the second half of the year.

What United States Antimony Does

United States Antimony produces and processes antimony and zeolite products used in flame retardants, batteries, specialty metals, and environmental applications.
