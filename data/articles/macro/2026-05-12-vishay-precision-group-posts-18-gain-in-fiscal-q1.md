Vishay Precision Group (NYSE:VPG) posted an encouraging start to fiscal 2026 as surging customer orders and Sensors segment strength fueled higher revenue. The precision measurement specialist provides sensors, weighing solutions, and measurement systems for industries like electronics, aviation, and manufacturing. In its earnings report released May 12, 2026, for the quarter ending April 4, the company showed robust sales but only limited profits and ongoing margin pressures. Overall, the quarter delivered clear sales momentum, but cash flow and margin expansion stayed muted.

Notable Developments
Revenue reached $84.4 million, up 17.6% year over year as demand grew, especially in the Sensors segment, which itself rose 23.1%.
Adjusted gross margin rose to 39.0%, while adjusted operating margin remained at 1.9%, reflecting continued high expenses for growth and restructuring.
Order bookings hit $102.1 million, marking a 25.5% increase from the previous quarter, and the book-to-bill ratio (orders to shipments) was 1.21, suggesting healthy future sales.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects second quarter revenue between $85 million and $90 million, anticipating continued order strength, particularly in Sensors. However, no formal guidance was given for earnings or margin rates. Investors should watch whether rising costs and spending on future growth will limit improvement in both margins and free cash flow in upcoming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

The Sensors segment stood out for its rapid expansion, with revenue climbing 23.1% and booking activity at near-record levels. Demand came from semiconductor equipment, data centers, aviation, and emerging fields like humanoid robotics—where $1.0 million in orders were booked, and engagement began with a fourth robotics developer.

Adjusted gross profit margin improved modestly overall, driven by higher sales and efficient manufacturing in Sensors. Still, adjusted operating margin did not improve compared to last year since the company continued investing heavily in restructuring and new growth areas.

Although order momentum was strong and the cash balance remained high at $82.5 million, adjusted free cash flow (operating cash after capital expenses) was negative for the quarter, with higher capital spending offsetting operating profit. This signals a need to monitor whether new business translates into actual cash generation.
