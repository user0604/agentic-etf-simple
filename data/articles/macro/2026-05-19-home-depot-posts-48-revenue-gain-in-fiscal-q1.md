Home improvement retailer Home Depot (NYSE:HD) delivered stable sales but continued to grapple with shrinking profit margins and lower earnings per share this quarter. The leading North American home improvement chain reported first-quarter results on May 19, 2026, for the period ended May 3, 2026. Overall, the quarter met Home Depot's own expectations, with revenue rising and diluted earnings per share declining compared to the prior year.

Notable Developments
Sales reached $41.8 billion, an increase of 4.8% year over year, while comparable sales improved slightly to 0.6% growth.
Profit margins tightened, with adjusted operating margin dropping to 12.3% from 13.2% and adjusted diluted EPS falling to $3.43.
Inventory rose by 5.9% from the prior year, mainly due to recent acquisitions and higher inventory costs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year forecast, expecting total sales growth between 2.5% and 4.5%, and adjusted operating margin between 12.8% and 13.0%. Profit margin pressure is expected to be greatest in the first half of the year, with margins improving as the effects of recent acquisitions annualize. Investors will want to keep a close watch on profit margins and sales to professional customers in the quarters ahead.

Quarterly Highlights

While total revenue increased, most of the sales gains came from acquisitions and new locations rather than stronger performance at existing stores. Comparable sales, a key measure that reflects sales at stores open at least a year, improved only modestly to 0.6% growth, signaling that underlying demand is still limited.

Profitability continued to face challenges, with both GAAP and adjusted operating margins declining from the prior year. This margin compression came as Home Depot absorbed higher costs related to its recent GMS and SRS acquisitions, which have also contributed to increased intangible asset amortization expenses.

Inventory levels climbed nearly 6%, which management attributes mainly to acquisition activity and increased costs. While the company maintains it is prepared for upcoming sales periods, the rise in inventory could become a concern if sales growth slows or demand remains soft.

Home Depot is one of the largest home improvement retailers in North America, supplying building materials, tools, appliances, and related services to both consumers and professionals across its network of stores.
