Consumer electronics trade-in platform ATRenew (NYSE:RERE) outperformed its revenue guidance, growing both sales and profitability during the most recent quarter. The leader in China’s secondhand electronics market saw results above the top end of its own forecasts for the three months ending March 31, 2026 (reported May 19, 2026). Overall, the quarter demonstrated broad-based growth, but some financial warning signs showed up in the company’s cash and inventory balances.

Notable Developments
Revenue surpassed the upper end of management guidance by RMB200.1 million, showing higher-than-expected demand for pre-owned electronics.
Net income jumped 215.7% year over year, supported by cost control and stronger product sales through online channels.
Cash and equivalents dropped 21.4% from the prior quarter, as inventory levels increased by 38.3% to RMB1,486.2 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management forecasts next quarter revenue between RMB6,240 million and RMB6,340 million, projecting 25.0% to 27.0% year-over-year growth, a slower rate than this quarter.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue for the quarter exceeded management’s stated goal, coming in nearly 3.4% above the upper end of the RMB5,960 million outlook. Growth came mainly from proprietary product sales, reflecting expanded online demand and continued investments in fulfillment networks and technology.

Profitability improved at a faster pace than revenue, with adjusted operating income rising by 70.2% year over year. Key expense areas, including fulfillment and marketing, grew slower than revenue, which helped expand profit margins and support operational efficiency.

The company’s cash balance shrank quarter over quarter, while inventories rose sharply—signs that rapid growth is putting pressure on working capital. Management extended the share buyback program, with $39.4 million authorization remaining, and repurchased $2.7 million worth of shares in the period.

What ATRenew Does

ATRenew operates a technology platform that enables consumers and businesses in China to trade in, recycle, and purchase pre-owned electronics such as smartphones and other devices.
