Broadwind Posts 7.5% Revenue Drop in Fiscal Q1

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 7:10 AM

Comments
Key Takeaways
Broadwind continues its strategic pivot, with rising orders in core segments offset by declining heavy fabrication sales.
Revenue fell 7.5% year over year.
Management expects a leaner, higher-margin business to emerge after exiting wind tower production.

Broadwind (NASDAQ:BWEN)’s transformation continued as the company accelerated its exit from wind tower manufacturing while expanding its core Gearing and Industrial Solutions lines. The diversified precision manufacturer, known for supplying high-value gears, fabricated structures, and supply chain solutions for power and infrastructure markets, reported first-quarter results on May 12, 2026, covering the period ended March 31. Overall, this quarter showed real momentum in core markets but underscored short-term challenges from winding down wind-related operations.

Notable Developments
The Heavy Fabrications segment saw a 35% revenue drop, linked to facility sales and the wind-down of wind tower production.
Gearing and Industrial Solutions segments both grew sharply, with Gearing up 42% and Industrial Solutions up 64% in revenue year over year.
Total orders rose 23% compared to last year, while core segment backlogs also reached new highs.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Broadwind management confirmed that it will complete its exit from the wind tower business by the third quarter, expecting a more stable core focused on specialty gearing and gas turbine components. The most important issue for investors to monitor is whether the company can translate order momentum in Gearing and Industrial Solutions into consistent profitability, while reducing reliance on a handful of large customers.

Quarterly Highlights

The sale of the Abilene facility marks Broadwind’s most decisive move away from the wind market, as management works to redeploy capital into higher-growth, less cyclical opportunities. Heavy Fabrications, historically tied to wind towers, saw its sales drop considerably and will be removed as a reporting segment after the transition.

The Gearing segment recorded robust demand from power generation, mining, and industrial customers, with orders up 66% and backlog now totaling $30.5 million. Operating losses in the segment narrowed substantially compared to last year as utilization improved.

Industrial Solutions, which provides natural gas turbine kits and related supply chain services, posted 64% revenue growth and a record $43.3 million backlog. Strong orders came from both new and existing customers in the natural gas sector, helping improve segment margins and operating income.

What Broadwind Does

Broadwind manufactures precision gears, supply-chain solutions, and large fabricated structures for customers in power generation, mining, oil and gas, and critical infrastructure industries.
