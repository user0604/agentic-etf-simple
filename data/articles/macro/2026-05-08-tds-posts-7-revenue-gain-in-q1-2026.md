Key Takeaways
Reported elevated profits driven by one-time gains from spectrum sales.
Revenue increased 7% year over year, with free cash flow improving but remaining negative.
Management reaffirmed its full-year outlook, with emphasis on ramping fiber build and stabilizing operations.

Telephone and Data Systems (NYSE:TDS) recorded a strong headline result this quarter, fueled by significant gains from asset sales. The diversified telecommunications and infrastructure operator is focused on expanding high-speed broadband through its TDS Telecom unit and growing its tower leasing business at Array. The company released earnings for the quarter ended March 31, 2026 (disclosed May 8, 2026), which highlighted improved cash reserves from $1.0 billion in spectrum sales, but recurring operational challenges remain. Overall, the quarter showcased material progress on asset monetization, while telecom and fiber operations continue to need higher growth rates.

Notable Developments
Revenue rose 6.6% year over year in the March 2026 quarter, while earnings were mainly driven by a large gain from the sale of wireless spectrum.
Free cash flow improved by $26.6 million year over year but remained negative, reflecting heavy investment in fiber network expansion.
Management affirmed full-year guidance for both the telecom and infrastructure businesses, signaling reliance on faster fiber build and stable tower tenancy.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership has reaffirmed its full-year 2026 revenue and adjusted OIBDA (operating income before depreciation and amortization) targets for both business units. The most critical point for investors in coming quarters will be monitoring the pace of fiber network expansion compared to the company's stated annual goal.

Quarterly Highlights

TDS Telecom increased its marketable fiber service addresses to 1.1 million but delivered 40,000 new addresses in the quarter, slower than the rate needed to meet the full-year target of 200,000 to 250,000. Residential fiber net additions grew to 10,900, and average residential revenue per connection saw a modest increase, even as churn rose to 1.3% from 0.9% a year ago, pointing to heightened competitive pressures.

Array, the tower-focused subsidiary, saw site rental revenue leap 92% year over year, driven by leasing deals with large wireless carriers. The company now manages 4,452 towers and notes improving application volume for colocation, but future growth remains somewhat dependent on converting current lease-up opportunities.

Free cash flow, while less negative, continues to signal the high capital required for network construction. Meanwhile, the company closed the quarter with $1.37 billion in cash, nearly quadrupling its balance from last year after major spectrum asset sales, but warned that operating income gains were heavily reliant on these one-time transactions.
