Crescent Capital BDC (NASDAQ:CCAP), a lender to U.S. middle-market companies, is expected to report its first-quarter 2026 financial results on Wednesday, May 13, 2026, after the market closes.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

In the fourth quarter of fiscal 2025, Crescent Capital BDC reported net investment income (NII) of $0.45 per share, which met analyst expectations and continued to cover the regular dividend. The company’s net asset value (NAV) per share declined to $19.10 from $19.28 in the prior quarter, reflecting unrealized losses from specific portfolio companies. The investment portfolio remained stable at $1.6 billion in fair value, spread across 184 companies, with a continued focus on first lien, sponsor-backed loans. Non-accruals increased during the quarter but improved on a pro forma basis after January events. The Board declared a $0.42 per share dividend for Q1 FY2026, maintaining the payout level. Management highlighted a stable portfolio with strong sponsor support and ongoing efforts to preserve capital and earnings power in a competitive private credit environment.

Positives
Dividend coverage remains strong: Net investment income covered the dividend by 107%, supporting confidence in the current payout level.
Portfolio diversification and credit quality: The portfolio remains highly diversified, with 91% in first lien positions and 99% in sponsor-backed companies, which helps limit concentration risk.
Balance sheet flexibility improved: The company extended debt maturities, with over 90% of committed debt now maturing in 2028 or later, enhancing financial flexibility.
Active origination platform: Crescent’s private credit platform committed over $1.7 billion in the fourth quarter, and add-on investments in existing companies remain a key driver of activity.
Negatives
Net asset value declined: NAV per share fell again, driven by unrealized losses on certain portfolio companies, which may concern investors focused on capital preservation.
Non-accruals increased: The percentage of debt investments on non-accrual status rose to 4.1% at cost (2% at fair value), above the company’s historical average, due to two new non-accruals in Q4.
Lower investment income: Interest income and overall investment income declined year over year, primarily due to lower base rates and some restructuring activity.
Dividend supplement not paid: The supplemental dividend was not paid for Q4 because of the NAV-based measurement test, reflecting recent NAV declines.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Dividend policy and fee structure review: Management and the Board are actively evaluating the base dividend level and fee structure in response to lower expected base rates. Investors should look for updates or changes, as management indicated a more detailed announcement is likely in the Q1 report.
Net asset value stability: Continued monitoring of NAV per share will be important, especially given recent unrealized losses and higher non-accruals. Any improvement or further decline could impact investor sentiment.
Non-accrual and credit trends: Investors should track the percentage of portfolio on non-accrual, especially after the January restructuring and sale that reduced non-accruals on a pro forma basis. Updates on credit quality and recoveries will be key.
Origination activity and deployment pace: The company noted increased deal activity in early 2026. The pace of new investments and repayments, as well as any changes in spreads or deal structures, will affect earnings power.
Supplemental dividend status: The supplemental dividend policy remains in place but was not triggered for Q4. Investors should watch for any changes in NAV or earnings that could affect the likelihood of a supplemental payout in Q1.
