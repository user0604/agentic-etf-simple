1. Winning Never Gets Old

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

While the market’s reaction was fairly muted, I think you have to call Nvidia’s (NASDAQ: NVDA) latest report a win. It delivered a (another) record-breaking quarter with $82 billion in revenue, up 85% year-over-year, driven by strong demand for Blackwell and Rubin AI architectures. And the company is expanding its reach with the Vera processor and is now forecasting $1 trillion in revenue through 2027.

I appreciate the dividend boost, but I also think the $80 billion share buyback announcement is bluster. Since 2021, the company has spent over $130 billion on share repurchases, and the share count is down just 2%. I fully understand this is not unique to the company, and stock-based compensation is a thing. But I also think it could do more with the dividend.

2. Going Beyond the GPU

By Meilin Quinn
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Nvidia's latest earnings made me more optimistic on the stock, for two reasons. First, Nvidia guided to roughly $20 billion in CPU revenue this year, which would make it a bigger data center CPU supplier than Intel (NASDAQ: INTC) or AMD (NASDAQ: AMD) by year-end. Behind that growth is its new Vera chip, and with it Nvidia now reaches across the full AI data center stack, from GPUs to CPUs to networking.

Second, Nvidia’s networking business tripled year over year to $15 billion, led by InfiniBand revenue that quadrupled. Networking has been Nvidia’s fastest-growing business for several quarters now.

Together, these wins (a new revenue stream in CPUs and a networking business now bigger than most chip companies) give Nvidia more growth runway to support the lofty expectations priced into its stock.

3. Win. And It Wasn’t Close.

By Jose Najarro
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

Nvidia reported Q1 FY27 earnings of $81.6 billion in revenue, up 85% year over year. The stock barely moved.

That reaction tells you more about expectations than results. Nvidia guided Q2 at $91 billion, which works out to roughly 95% year-over-year growth. That is not deceleration. That is acceleration, compounding into a larger number every single quarter.

While the revenue story was running, Nvidia also announced an $80 billion share repurchase authorization. The company is not just growing. It is confident enough in its own cash generation to hand it back to shareholders at scale.

Win does not cover it.
