Key Takeaways
Valens Semiconductor delivered a modest business win as revenue outpaced its targeted range but overall growth remained muted.
Loss per share widened to $(0.05) on a non-GAAP basis, while revenue was stable over the prior year period.
Management guides for higher revenue next quarter, but profitability remains out of reach for now.

Valens Semiconductor (NYSE:VLN) beat its own guidance for revenue and gross margin in the latest quarter, but top-line growth stagnated and losses deepened. The semiconductor company, best known for its HDBaseT connectivity technology for audio-video and automotive markets, released first quarter results on May 13, 2026 (covering the period ending March 31). Overall, the quarter showed margin resilience and signals of product adoption but highlighted concern over persistent losses and cash usage.

Notable Developments
Revenue was $16.9 million, surpassing the top of management’s guidance range, but stayed essentially flat year over year.
Gross margin remained strong at 65.2% (Non-GAAP), outdoing expectations, with higher-value product mix in the audio-video segment boosting results.
The cash position declined to $86.1 million, reflecting ongoing losses and investment needs, though the firm has no debt.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management projects second-quarter revenue of $17.2–$17.6 million, with stable but slightly lower margins and a smaller adjusted EBITDA loss. The company expects incremental progress, but not a break-even result. Investors should closely monitor whether growth in the automotive segment and renewed momentum in audio-video can reverse the stagnating top line and narrow losses further.

Quarterly Highlights

The company’s Cross-Industry Business, which covers audio-video connectivity products, composed 65% of total revenue but saw sales decline compared to both the year-ago period and the prior quarter. Despite this, management highlighted increased adoption of its VS6320 and VS3000 chipsets, indicating ongoing industry engagement and new product launches.

Automotive segment revenue rose 15.7% over the prior year, driven by growing demand for chipsets that support advanced driver-assistance systems (ADAS) and new ecosystem demonstrations. The segment now makes up 35% of company revenue, but margins remain less attractive than in audio-video.

R&D and overall operating costs fell slightly, while losses remained similar to the prior year as cash outflows continued. The ending cash balance fell more than 23% from the prior-year quarter, underlining the importance of executing on growth and cost control to sustain long-term operations.

What Valens Semiconductor Does

Valens Semiconductor supplies semiconductor chips for high-performance connectivity in audio-video, automotive, industrial, and other sectors, with technology that transmits video, audio, data, and power over long distances using a single cable.

Track the company for signals of renewed top-line growth and sustained margin strength in coming quarters.
