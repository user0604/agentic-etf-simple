Petróleo Brasileiro - Petrobras (NYSE:PBR) delivered a quarter of strong sales growth, driven by booming crude oil exports, but rising costs and debt limited profit gains. The integrated Brazilian energy company benefited from higher export volumes and continued investment in resource development. Notable developments
Sales revenues increased 11.7% year over year, led by a 50% jump in crude oil export revenues and strong growth in renewables and electricity sales.
Costs outpaced revenue gains, with cost of sales up 14.1% and selling expenses and production taxes rising 39.0% and 23.3%, respectively.
Net debt rose to $62.1 billion, while capital expenditures increased 25.6% as the company invested in upstream projects and low-carbon initiatives.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management did not provide a formal forecast but noted that higher oil prices since the Middle East conflict began are expected to boost second-quarter export revenues. Investors should monitor further cost increases, the impact of new export taxes, and continued debt growth, as these factors could affect future profitability even with higher sales.

Quarterly highlights

Export activity was a key driver, with export revenues up 41.6% and crude oil exports rising 50% compared to the prior-year period. Domestic market revenues grew just 0.7%, as higher volumes were offset by slightly lower average prices.

The cost base expanded rapidly, with selling expenses reaching $1.5 billion due to higher logistics costs for shipping crude and fuel oil abroad. Production taxes and depreciation also increased, reflecting new production platforms and higher market prices.

The company expanded capital spending by over 25%, focusing on exploration and production projects in the Santos and Campos Basins. It also recorded a $417 million impairment reversal after restarting a fertilizer plant, signaling renewed focus on value-added and sustainability-aligned assets.
