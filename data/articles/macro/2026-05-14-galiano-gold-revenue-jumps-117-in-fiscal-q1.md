Galiano Gold (NYSEMKT:GAU) staged a sharp rebound in the first quarter with a swing to profit and robust cash generation. The gold miner, focused on the Asanko Gold Mine in Ghana, reported strong revenue growth and considerably improved margins. The results, released on May 13, 2026, for its first quarter of fiscal 2026, reflect broad operational and financial progress, though royalties and working capital demands also rose. Overall, the period brought clear improvement but left important issues for continued attention.

Notable developments
Revenue jumped to $166.5 million, more than doubling from the prior-year period, driven by higher gold production and stronger prices.
Net income turned positive to $36.9 million, a sharp reversal from a $29.4 million loss last year, reflecting improved operational performance and price environment.
Operating cash flow increased by 80%, supporting an $8.5 million year-over-year rise in cash and cash equivalents to $114.9 million.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintained previous full-year guidance, expecting production of 140,000–160,000 ounces and all-in sustaining costs in the $2,000–$2,300 per ounce range. It noted that both production volumes and costs will remain concentrated in the second half of 2026 as mine ramp-up continues. Investors should focus on production delivery in H2 and the impact of Ghana's new sliding-scale royalty regime on total costs.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue more than doubled from the prior year, benefiting from increased gold output, higher gold spot prices, and Galiano Gold's now 90% ownership stake in the Asanko Gold Mine. The mining segment remains the company’s only reportable segment, producing all revenue and profit. Realized and unrealized hedge losses trimmed some upside, but a reduction in hedge exposure is ongoing.

The swing to a $36.9 million net profit was supported by operating cost increases that were minor compared to revenue growth. However, Ghana’s recent shift to a sliding-scale royalty resulted in royalty expenses more than tripling to $14.5 million, compressing margins somewhat.

The company ended the quarter with $114.9 million in cash, reflecting strong operating cash flow of $46.7 million, up 80% year over year. Investment in inventory and mineral property development also grew, and deferred payments to partners are scheduled for later in the year, adding to future cash flow monitoring needs.

What Galiano Gold does

The company explores, develops, and operates gold mining projects, with the Asanko Gold Mine in Ghana as its core asset.
