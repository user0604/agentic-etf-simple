ZoomInfo Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 28, 2026, 3:10 PM

Comments

ZoomInfo Technologies Inc. (NASDAQ:GTM) is a leading provider of business-to-business (B2B) data and intelligence platforms, powering sales, marketing, and recruiting functions for enterprises through proprietary datasets and workflow integrations. The company’s growth strategy centers on shifting from a traditional seat-based Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) model to a hybrid data consumption platform, aiming to become the core data infrastructure for AI-powered go-to-market systems as customers modernize their sales and marketing operations.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Data Consumption Model Accelerates Expansion into Large Enterprise Budgets

The shift to a hybrid, consumption-based pricing model positions the company for larger, more scalable enterprise contracts and stronger future growth. Management is accelerating its transition from a per-seat SaaS approach to one emphasizing pre-paid data credits and platform fees, with the goal of achieving a 50/50 annual contract value (ACV) mix between seat-based and consumption contracts within the next 12–18 months (up from just under 20% currently). Consumption-based offerings, including Operations and Data-as-a-Service, grew over 20% year over year in Q1 2026 and now make up just under 20% of ZoomInfo’s business. This approach aligns tightly with the increasing preference of large, AI-focused companies to embed third-party data in their proprietary or agent-driven workflows. As more upmarket customers—now already contributing nearly 75% of revenue—adopt hybrid buying patterns, the company’s total addressable market expands well beyond the legacy SaaS user base, setting the stage for recovery and re-acceleration after the transition period concludes.

2. Proven Profitability and Margin Expansion Underpin Resilience Through Transition

Robust profitability and disciplined cost management create a strong foundation for weathering near-term headwinds. Despite lowered 2026 revenue guidance, adjusted (non-GAAP) operating income margin reached 35% in Q1 2026, up more than 2 percentage points year over year, with full-year margin guidance revised upward to 37%. Unlevered free cash flow margin was 39%, and cost actions—including a 20% reduction in headcount and operational restructurings—are expected to yield around $60 million in annual run-rate savings by early 2027. Management sees an achievable path to 40% adjusted margin once revenue growth resumes. This margin expansion, fueled by internal AI adoption and automation, enables the company to self-fund platform investments and strategic share repurchases, even in a challenging macro environment.

3. AI-Driven Product Strategy Deepens Competitive Moat and Ecosystem Integration

ZoomInfo’s commitment to AI-enablement and deep integrations with leading platforms greatly increases its strategic relevance and defensibility. In Q1 2026, the company launched connectors for large language model platforms including ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, and Perplexity, and became the first and primary external data provider for Salesforce’s new AI prospecting agent. HubSpot also natively integrated ZoomInfo data for its agent workflows, while Salesforce's customer base includes over 150,000 enterprise and SMB customers with access to ZoomInfo data. Over one quarter of existing clients have trialed the company’s new AI-enriched go-to-market studio. With R&D efforts focused on data infrastructure, APIs, and seamless workflow integration—rather than standalone end-apps—the company is embedding itself into the next era of enterprise software architecture, creating lock-in that is difficult for competitors to replicate.

4. Strong Balance Sheet, Proactive Capital Allocation, and Ample Liquidity Enable Strategic Flexibility

Sound liquidity and active capital management allow ZoomInfo to invest through volatility while supporting shareholder value. At the end of Q1 2026, the company reported $175 million in cash and equivalents against $1.3 billion in gross debt, for a net leverage ratio of 2.4x trailing 12-month adjusted EBITDA—comfortably within its target range and with no significant maturities until 2029. ZoomInfo upsized its revolving credit facility to $276 million and entered into $425 million in interest rate swaps to gain visibility and stability in debt costs. The buyback program remains highly active, with 13.1 million shares repurchased for $90 million in Q1 alone and over $1 billion in authorization still available. This financial discipline and optionality help preserve investor confidence throughout the business model pivot.

5. Visibility Into Recurring Revenue and Customer Base Supports Recovery Thesis

A large base of committed revenue and performance obligations provides downside protection and forward visibility amid the transition. At the close of Q1 2026, remaining performance obligations stood at $1.812 billion, with $861 million expected to be recognized as revenue in the next 12 months. Net revenue retention, while pressured, held steady at 90% for the third straight quarter, and there was 10% year-over-year ACV growth among large customers ($100,000+ cohort). Traditional verticals like finance, manufacturing, telecom, and insurance delivered solid results despite software sector headwinds, indicating diversification. This backlog and retention base give the company multiple quarters of revenue visibility as the consumption model matures and customer expansion gradually resumes.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Prolonged Top-Line Weakness and Downmarket Churn Threaten Overall Growth Trajectory

Sustained revenue stagnation and persistent customer churn raise concerns about the company’s long-term growth prospects. In Q1 2026, revenue grew just 1.5% year over year, and management revised full-year 2026 revenue guidance downward to a range of $1.185 billion to $1.205 billion—a 4% year-over-year decline at the midpoint. Downmarket (small and medium business) annual contract value declined 11% year over year, and upsell activity into enterprise accounts slowed meaningfully. With net revenue retention stuck at 90% for three quarters and further regression anticipated as the consumption shift accelerates, ZoomInfo must overcome significant macro and competitive headwinds to reestablish a sustainable growth engine, especially if pain spreads beyond the already-troubled software customer base.

2. Execution Risks and Revenue Recognition Variability Could Drive Volatility and Investor Uncertainty

The rapid model transition brings meaningful operational risks, short-term instability, and heightened quarterly revenue variability. Shifting from per-seat SaaS billing to prepaid, consumption-driven pricing means timing and predictability of revenue become less certain, particularly as customers test new usage patterns or start at lower initial price points. Management has explicitly warned of near-term variability in revenue and the risk of further net revenue retention degradation as clients migrate. Additionally, the major restructuring—including a 20% workforce reduction and R&D consolidation—introduces execution risk if productivity, product roadmaps, or customer support are adversely impacted. Such instability can undermine investor confidence and prolong the path to normalized growth and margin improvements.

3. Competitive and Regulatory Threats to Data Moat Are Rising in the AI Era

Erosion of the company’s proprietary data advantage by new technologies or changing privacy rules poses significant long-term risk. As AI systems, automation vendors, and even customers themselves increasingly aggregate, enhance, or commoditize B2B data, the willingness to pay premium prices for third-party data sources like ZoomInfo may be challenged. New data privacy regulations, opt-out requirements, and changing norms around profile information create a moving target for compliance—with the specter that a regulatory or competitive shock could erode the core value proposition. While management highlights strong privacy certifications and industry praise, sustaining the competitive moat long-term will require relentless innovation and defense against both new entrants and further AI-driven disintermediation.
