Key Takeaways
Grupo Aval reported a business setback as headline profits declined due to a one-time Colombian equity tax.
Return on average equity fell to 7.4% from 8.4% in the prior-year period.

Grupo Aval Acciones Y Valores (NYSE:AVAL) saw profitability drop in the first quarter after a substantial new Colombian equity tax reduced headline earnings. The leading Colombian and Central American financial group operates across banking, loan services, payments, and infrastructure. The results for the quarter were released on May 18, 2026, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Despite robust growth in loans, deposits, and infrastructure income, the nonrecurring tax hit meant profitability declined year over year and quarter over quarter.

Overall, the quarter highlighted operational resilience in business volumes, but cost and margin pressures, combined with new tax headwinds, presented a challenging financial outcome.

Notable Developments
Customer deposits increased 11.7% year over year, reaching Ps 216.8 trillion, reflecting strong funding momentum and growth in core markets.
Gross loans rose 6.0% to Ps 193.7 trillion, with mortgages up a robust 16.8%, outpacing other lending categories.
A one-time Colombian equity tax reduced net income attributable to owners by Ps 210.1 billion and increased operating expenses by Ps 311.7 billion.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not provide a new forward-looking outlook for 2026 in this release. Last quarter’s guidance called for higher returns and margins, but current results landed well below those targets because of the new tax headwind. Investors should closely monitor how regulatory changes and ongoing cost pressures affect profitability and operational efficiency in coming quarters.

Quarterly Highlights

The core banking business delivered steady performance: loan volumes grew, deposit inflows accelerated, and asset quality metrics improved. Net loans climbed 7.4% year over year, with the loans past due over 90 days ratio falling to 3.1%, a sign that the group is managing credit risk effectively.

The non-banking segment, which includes infrastructure activities, emerged as a strong contributor with gross profit from sales of goods and services up 26.7% year over year. This performance demonstrates the benefit of diversification, helping to offset the banking side’s margin pressures.

Cost efficiency metrics came under strain, with the efficiency ratio rising to 53.9%. Excluding the tax, however, this measure would have improved. Personnel and administrative expenses continued to grow, suggesting future cost discipline will be important to protect margins, especially in an environment of volatile net interest margins (NIM).
