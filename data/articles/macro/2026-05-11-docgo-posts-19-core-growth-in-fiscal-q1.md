Key Takeaways
DocGo delivered robust growth in core service lines while facing wider operating losses.
Revenue excluding migrant-related contracts increased year over year, despite a drop in total revenue.
Management raised 2026 revenue guidance but kept profit outlook unchanged, citing temporary cost headwinds.

DocGo (NASDAQ:DCGO) regained momentum in its core operations but continued to struggle with profitability as costs outpaced gains. The leading provider of mobile health services and medical transportation delivered strong growth in its core business lines in the first quarter (reported May 11, 2026, for the quarter ended March 31). Overall, the period showed solid underlying demand, but financial efficiency lagged behind revenue growth.

Notable developments
Revenue excluding migrant-related work rose 19.3% year over year, signaling accelerating growth in core businesses.
Adjusted EBITDA loss widened to $10.2 million as higher hiring costs and fuel prices hurt margins.
The company raised its full-year 2026 revenue guidance midpoint by $7.5 million to a range of $300 million–$315 million.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management increased its annual revenue outlook, reflecting stronger demand in areas like virtual care and mobile phlebotomy. Profit guidance remains unchanged, as near-term pressure from workforce expansion and higher fuel prices continues. Investors should watch if operational improvements in the second half lead to margin recovery and positive adjusted EBITDA, as leadership anticipates.

Quarterly highlights

Organic growth in mobile health services, driven by the integration of virtual care provider SteadyMD, led to a 131% increase in revenue for core mobile health lines compared with the prior year’s ex-migrant total. Medical transportation services also grew, with segment revenue up 2.2% and trip volumes climbing 17% over the same period last year.

Profitability remained under pressure, with both adjusted and net losses widening. Margin contraction was mainly due to an expanded hiring pace and incentives for the mobile health workforce, alongside higher fuel expenses attributed to global events—factors management views as temporary.

The company closed the quarter with $59.9 million in total cash and investments, down from $68.3 million at year-end. It reported negative operating cash flow as working capital needs increased alongside growth, but collections from legacy contracts made some progress post-quarter.

What DocGo does

DocGo provides technology-enabled mobile health and medical transportation services, delivering healthcare directly to patients at home, workplaces, and in transit.
