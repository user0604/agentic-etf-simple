Earnings Preview: Flexible Solutions International

The Motley Fool Team

May 13, 2026, 9:04 AM

Comments

Flexible Solutions International (NYSEMKT:FSI), a maker of biodegradable polymers and water-saving chemicals for agriculture and industry, is expected to report earnings for the first quarter of fiscal year 2026 on Friday, May 15, 2026, before the market opens.

Q4 FY2025 Recap

FSI reported flat annual sales of $38.51 million for 2025, nearly unchanged from $38.23 million in 2024. Net income for the year fell to $787,000, or $0.06 per share, compared to $3 million, or $0.24 per share, in the prior year. The company cited higher costs tied to scaling up new food-grade product lines and the ongoing transition of legacy production to its new Panama facility. These expenses weighed on both profit and cash flow, which declined year over year. FSI’s results missed analyst expectations for both earnings and sales for the quarter.

Positives
Successful ramp-up of new food-grade contracts: FSI achieved full production on its August 2025 food-grade contract, which secures a minimum of $6.5 million in annual revenue and could scale to over $25 million per year. This demonstrates progress in diversifying revenue streams.
Transition to Panama facility underway: The shift of legacy industrial and agricultural products to Panama is progressing, with benefits expected in shipping times and tariff exposure for international sales.
Debt reduction and improved cash flow outlook: The company paid off major loans in 2025, freeing up over $2 million in annual cash flow and reducing financial risk.
Negatives
Declining profitability due to ramp-up costs: Expenses related to new product launches and the Panama plant transition significantly reduced margins and net income in 2025.
Weakness in agricultural markets: Ongoing low crop prices and high input costs led to reduced demand for FSI’s agricultural products, contributing to flat sales.
Margins pressured by tariffs and inflation: Tariffs on Chinese raw materials and rising shipping and input costs, partly due to geopolitical events, further squeezed profitability.
Cash flow from operations dropped: Operating cash flow fell to $3.78 million from $5.57 million in 2024, reflecting the impact of higher operating expenses.
What to Watch in Q1 FY2026's Report
Food-grade contract ramp-up pace: Investors should watch for updates on the January 2025 food-grade contract, which began low-volume production late in Q4. Management expects significant revenue from this contract to appear in Q2, but any acceleration or delays will be important.
Margin recovery and cost controls: Pay attention to whether operating margins begin to rebound as ramp-up expenses subside and production volumes increase, especially in the food-grade and Panama divisions.
Progress on Panama facility transition: The transition of legacy product manufacturing to Panama is ongoing and expected to be largely complete by the end of 2026. Updates on cost savings, shipping advantages, and customer acquisition tied to this move will be key.
ENP division seasonal trends: The ENP division, which serves greenhouse and turf markets, typically sees weaker results in the first half of the year. Investors should look for any signs of deviation from this pattern or early indicators of growth for the full year.
Tariff and input cost impacts: Management has warned that continued high oil prices and tariffs could lead to customer price increases in the third quarter. Any commentary on raw material costs or pricing actions will be important for understanding future profitability.
