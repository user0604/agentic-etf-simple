Notable Developments
The Calpine merger expanded total generation capacity, increasing revenue by 64% and net income by over $1.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026.
Operating cash flow jumped to $425 million, while capital spending rose to $1.28 billion in the first quarter of 2026, reflecting investment in integration and growth projects.
Nuclear output declined by 2%, with more outage days and a lower capacity factor at 92.3% versus 94.1% last year.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed its full-year 2026 adjusted earnings guidance of $11.00 to $12.00 per share, highlighting confidence in post-merger results and strong visible cash flow. Executives pointed to ongoing integration activities and growth investments as top priorities. Investors should closely monitor progress on Calpine integration and nuclear fleet operating efficiency in upcoming quarters.

Quarterly highlights

The first quarter results were dominated by the Calpine acquisition, which added 23 gigawatts of capacity and drove the sharp rise in revenue and natural gas, oil, and renewables generation. Calpine’s assets contributed over 26,000 GWh of generation to the business, making up the bulk of the increase outside of the nuclear segment.

Nuclear power remains central to operations, but generation declined 2% year over year as planned maintenance outages increased to 99 days. This reduced the nuclear fleet's capacity factor, a measure of energy plant utilization, to 92.3%, which was slightly below historical company averages but still strong by industry standards.

Capital spending and leverage both rose significantly, with long-term debt more than doubling to $17.0 billion. This reflects both the cost of the Calpine merger and the ramp-up in large-scale project spending, including renewable energy developments and data center partnerships in Texas and California.
