1. The Aerospace Trend Has Wings (or Tailwinds)

By Yasser El-Shimy
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

Aerospace isn’t just a sector — it’s the infrastructure of globalization itself. Air travel demand is on a structural growth curve driven by an expanding Asian middle class, while defense budgets are rising worldwide. That’s a multi-decade tailwind.

The beauty is how many layers you can invest across. AerCap (NYSE: AER) owns the planes — a capital-heavy but cash-generative leasing model for airlines. Rolls-Royce (LSE: RR.) sells the engines cheap, then earns decades of high-margin maintenance fees — a razor-and-blade model at altitude. Embraer (NYSE: EMBJ) makes specialized planes for both the civilian and defense sectors. Rocket Lab (NASDAQ: RKLB) democratizes small satellite launches as the economy increasingly depends on orbital infrastructure.

Old guard or disruptor — aerospace rewards patient capital.

2. Cybersecurity Is Going to Be Crucial

By Jason Moser
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

I think two things can be true: AI is going to be a massive disruptor (it already is). And cybersecurity will be a necessary component of that disruption. There’s been a lot of talk about cybersecurity companies being disrupted by AI. But I’m taking the other side of that argument in that I think cybersecurity must only grow stronger. I mentioned before that I recently opened positions in Palo Alto Networks (NASDAQ: PANW) and Zscaler (NASDAQ: ZS) based on that idea. Rather than try to pick one winner, I’m building a basket of companies in cybersecurity as I believe they will only become more important over time. Those, along with Cloudflare (NYSE: NET), make me feel good about getting some exposure to the space.

3. E-Commerce is Still Scratching the Surface

By Loren Horst
TEAM RULE BREAKERS

My answer today is the same one I’d have given a decade ago: e-commerce. Fed data shows e-commerce is still less than one-sixth of total retail sales and only in the third quarter of 2025 surpassed levels from the height of 2020. Favorites like Amazon (NASDAQ: AMZN), MercadoLibre (NASDAQ: MELI), and Shopify (NASDAQ: SHOP) would all make the cut if I simplified my own portfolio down to just 10 positions from 50. And while the company best positioned to be “the next Amazon” is probably Amazon itself, Team Rule Breakers recommends emerging international players like Grab Holdings (NASDAQ: GRAB), Coupang (NYSE: CPNG), and JD.com (NASDAQ: JD) on the Stock Advisor scorecard. One you probably haven’t heard of? I’m watching Kaspi (NASDAQ: KSPI).

4. The AI Revolution in Drug Discovery

By Rachel Warren
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

My focus is on how Big Pharma is moving past the experimental phase and making AI a core part of their drug development engines. We are seeing a major shift from traditional trial and error to predictive pharmacology, where companies can simulate how a drug will work before it even hits a lab.

By using AI to pick the best drug candidates early on, companies from Eli Lilly (NYSE: LLY) to Pfizer (NYSE: PFE) are looking to cut down the massive costs and high failure rates that usually haunt the industry. Some of these workflows are already shrinking discovery timelines from years down to just months. We are already seeing digital twins used to model trial outcomes, which could get life-saving treatments to patients faster.

5. Housing: Repair & Remodel

By Anthony Schiavone

“You can mess up a lot of things in business and still do well as long as you get the big trend right.” — Brad Jacobs

Brad Jacobs is a serial entrepreneur who is currently the CEO of QXO (NYSE: QXO), a company aiming to consolidate the $800 billion building products distribution industry. Rather than me explain the thesis for housing, here’s Brad Jacobs in an interview with ResiClub:

What I like in terms of the long-term for housing is that you have secular growth. There’s a mismatch between [housing] supply and [housing] demand, the average house is over 40 years old and that’s going to spur more construction over time. Depending on which statistic you look at, we’re like 3 million units short of housing in the United States — that’s a big shortfall. And because of the age of the houses, you’re going to see a lot of repair and remodeling.

6. The Energy Renaissance

By Neha Chamaria
TEAM HIDDEN GEMS

I’m betting big on the future of energy. The world doesn’t just need more power — it wants cleaner power. AI data centers, electric vehicles, modern factories, and digital infrastructure are triggering an electricity arms race we haven’t seen in decades. The companies building the next generation of power systems could soon become the backbone of the global economy.

That’s why I’m drawn to clean energy, nuclear, and grid infrastructure plays. Companies such as Constellation Energy (NASDAQ: CEG) (nuclear), GE Vernova (NYSE: GEV) (natural gas turbines), and NextEra Energy (NYSE: NEE) (renewable energy) are positioned at the center of this shift. I believe the global push for energy independence and decarbonization will be the most powerful structural investment themes and create some of the decade’s biggest winners.

I've been thinking about "The Climate Trend."  Climate will be changing through and beyond the next decade, getting warmer (hotter) in most places, wetter or dryer inb many places, and upending systems of all sorts (natural and human) in ways that we have yet to fully understand or appreciate.  More immediately, the world is facing what may be the largest El Niño event in at least 100 years (possibly centuries), beginning later this year and extending into 2027.  At the same time, the AI buildout is placing unprecedented strains on energy and water systems, our electrical grid in the U. S. is getting older and less reliable, there is a persistent demand for housing, and facilities of all types will need to be refurbished, if not replaced.

Some of the trends noted above by the analysts can be related related to climate trends and effects:  green energy and energy realted infrastructure); the repair and remodeling of housing (but also hospitals, schools, power plants, etc.).  More tangentially, AI may help us to combat the emergence and spread of new diseasea; aerospace technologies may help us to monitor natural (and human) systems; and cybersecurity may help to contain cyberthreats to utilities and other essential infrastructure.  And it's not hard to identify additional trends:  along with the growing need for clean power there is a  growing need for clean water, there are impacts and opportunities relating to real estate and to the insurance and reinsurance industries; and smilarlly for transportation and others.

The "Climate Trend" is thus not really an investing trend as such but a changing global circumstance that will increasingly shape the trajectories of many kinds of investments in many different ways.
