Key Takeaways
FitLife Brands delivered strong revenue growth but faced margin pressures and continued declines in legacy businesses.
Revenue rose year over year, while gross margin and profit both declined as the Irwin Naturals acquisition reshaped the business.
Management reiterated caution, declined to provide formal guidance, and highlighted ongoing challenges for the remainder of the year.

Supplement maker FitLife Brands (NASDAQ:FTLF) posted robust sales gains, yet profitability weakened as integration costs and core business headwinds weighed on results. The company operates a multi-brand nutritional supplement portfolio that now includes the recently acquired Irwin Naturals. Reporting results on May 14, 2026, for the first quarter of fiscal 2026, FitLife saw headline revenues surge, but deeper challenges lingered.

Overall, the quarter highlighted a mix of expansion from the Irwin brand, but also declining performance in legacy product lines, margin pressure, and a cautious stance from management.

Notable Developments
Revenue increased 59%, driven largely by the acquisition of Irwin Naturals, even as legacy business revenue fell 22% compared to the same period last year.
Gross margin dropped to 37.6%, down over five percentage points from last year, reflecting Irwin’s lower-margin profile and supply chain challenges.
Net debt decreased from $43.1 million at year-end to $40.6 million, as management continues to prioritize debt repayment with available free cash flow.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management opted not to provide numerical guidance for the coming quarters, citing continued weak consumer demand and uncertain market conditions. The company emphasized ongoing supply chain improvements, plans for Amazon Canada expansion, and a focus on reducing debt. Investors should watch how quickly Irwin supply constraints ease and whether core legacy brands can stabilize their declines.

Quarterly Highlights

Irwin Naturals, a supplement brand acquired in 2025, contributed most of the revenue growth this quarter, with wholesale channel sales surging and Irwin’s Amazon revenue reaching a monthly run rate of approximately $0.9 million by April. Despite this, organic Irwin sales actually slipped 13% year over year due to supply chain stockouts and a lack of new product launches.

Legacy FitLife portfolio brands, including names like NDS Nutrition and PMD Sports, saw continued weakness. Legacy sales fell 22%, with wholesale revenue down 28% and online sales down 18%; management attributed these declines to softer demand at major customers like GNC and Medical Research Council.

Profitability metrics showed pressure despite higher total revenue. Contribution margin, which measures gross profit less advertising and marketing as a share of revenue, eroded alongside net profit. Free cash flow supported debt reduction, but overall liquidity remains somewhat tight with just $1.2 million in cash at quarter-end and higher interest expense from the Irwin acquisition.
