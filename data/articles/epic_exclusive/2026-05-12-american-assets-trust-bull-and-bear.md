American Assets Trust Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 12, 2026, 11:07 AM

Comments

American Assets Trust, Inc. (NYSE:AAT) is a real estate investment trust focused on high-quality, irreplaceable office, retail, multifamily, and mixed-use assets concentrated in supply-constrained, coastal markets, particularly on the West Coast. The company pursues growth through disciplined capital allocation, active leasing strategies, and operational enhancements—especially leveraging its spec suite program to drive leasing momentum and harnessing technology to improve tenant experiences and operational margins. American Assets Trust aims to deliver stable, long-term cash flows via a balanced property portfolio and proactive asset management in dynamic markets.

Potential Bull Cases
1. High Leasing Activity and Pipeline Conversion Drive Near-Term Earnings Growth

Robust leasing momentum in office assets positions the company for incremental earnings growth in 2026 and 2027. During the first quarter of 2026, American Assets Trust executed approximately 237,000 square feet of new office leases and entered the second quarter with approximately 144,000 square feet of signed-but-not-commenced leases, an additional 122,000 square feet in lease documentation, and over 200,000 square feet in the proposal pipeline. Signed but not commenced leases are projected to contribute over $5 million in rent, or roughly $0.07 per share in estimated 2026 Funds From Operations (FFO). About 100,000 square feet of this new space is scheduled to commence in 2027, offering a visible bridge to future Net Operating Income (NOI) growth. This steady conversion provides strong visibility into the company’s earnings trajectory, mitigating near-term occupancy risks and unlocking rental cash flows as the new leases commence.

2. Spec Suite Program Effectively Attracts and Converts High-Quality Tenants

The spec suite initiative is successfully meeting market demand and accelerating lease-up activity. In the first quarter of 2026, 9 out of 14 new office leases to noncomparable tenants utilized the company’s spec suite program, which focuses on delivering move-in-ready, high-finish spaces. This approach is driving speed-to-market and is resonating with tenants seeking flexibility amid shifting workspace trends. By facilitating rapid lease executions and reducing downtime, the program has proven pivotal in capturing incremental demand—especially from new tenants making up 12 of the 14 executed leases. Continued expansion of this initiative enhances leasing velocity and supports occupancy improvement, elevating the portfolio’s resilience and earning power.

3. Diversified Portfolio Delivers Stability Through Strong Retail and Multifamily Performance

Stable income from retail and multifamily segments offsets volatility in more cyclical sectors. At the end of the first quarter of 2026, the company’s retail portfolio was 98% leased, with less than 3% of retail square footage set to expire during the year and prompt backfilling of recent vacancies previously created by tenants such as Party City and Discount Tire. Retail tenant health is characterized as strong with minimal bankruptcy risk, providing a reliable cash flow anchor. In the multifamily segment, year-over-year same-store cash NOI rose 3%, with San Diego units boasting 98% occupancy. These resilient asset classes help counterbalance softness or unpredictability in office and hospitality, contributing to more stable earnings and supporting ongoing dividend coverage.

4. Conservative Balance Sheet Management and Ample Liquidity Reduce Financial Risk

Recent refinancing actions and significant liquidity reserve provide strategic flexibility. In April 2026, American Assets Trust upsized its unsecured revolving credit facility from $400 million to $500 million and extended maturities to April 2030, eliminating near-term debt deadlines and elevating total borrowing capacity to $600 million. At the end of the first quarter, liquidity stood at $518 million, comprising $118 million in cash and $400 million in available revolver capacity. This robust liquidity buffer, alongside a fixed charge coverage ratio of 3.0x, positions the company to weather sector headwinds, seize opportunistic investments, or support capital expenditures necessary to capture leasing growth, thereby enhancing shareholder value while managing leverage down over time.

5. Strategic Focus on High-Quality Coastal Markets and Asset Differentiation Enhances Long-Term Value

The company’s emphasis on irreplaceable, supply-constrained coastal properties underpins pricing power and asset value retention. American Assets Trust targets premier submarkets like UTC in San Diego (with La Jolla Commons III benefiting from limited large-block availability) and invests in modernizing operational infrastructure, including the early adoption of AI-driven management tools. By concentrating on markets with high tenant demand, significant barriers to new supply, and strong economic bases, the company cultivates durable tenant relationships and justifies above-market rents. This strategic positioning, reinforced by value-add programs and disciplined capital allocation, helps insulate asset values and drives consistent, long-term returns throughout real estate cycles.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Elevated Office Vacancy and Key Tenant Move-Outs Create Persistent Cash Flow Risk

Lingering vacancy and significant move-outs may undermine earnings stability if leasing velocity falters. Despite recent leasing wins, the office portfolio remained only 84.5% leased as of Q1 2026, with notable exposure in assets such as La Jolla Commons III (49% leased) and One Beach Street in San Francisco (36% leased). The upcoming departure of Genentech from 67,000 square feet at Lloyd District in Q4—an exit not previously factored into guidance—highlights ongoing tenant churn and submarket uncertainty. Slow replacement of large tenants or missed leasing timelines could delay NOI ramp, placing downward pressure on FFO and challenging the sustainability of current dividend payouts, particularly as macro trends for office demand remain tepid.

2. Elevated Dividend Payout Ratio Amid Profit Compression Heightens Distribution Risk

The high dividend payout ratio, which reached 111% of first quarter 2026 FFO, increases vulnerability to earnings shortfalls. American Assets Trust maintained its quarterly dividend at $0.34 per share, but continued margin compression—evidenced by declining GAAP net income and a net margin of 12.76% in Q1—indicates that underlying cash flow growth is sluggish. While management expects the payout ratio to moderate as new leases commence, sustained margin weakness or slow occupancy improvement could erode capacity to fund dividends internally. Any persistent gap between cash earnings and distributions may force a reevaluation of the dividend policy or the need to access external capital markets to bridge funding shortfalls, especially if leasing risk materializes.

3. Sector and Market Headwinds Expose Portfolio to Downside Risks From Macro Volatility

Concentration in challenged office assets and exposure to volatile tourism markets add uncertainty to growth prospects. Slow recovery in office leasing fundamentals, especially in San Francisco and other urban markets, coupled with the impact of international tourism swings—such as reduced Japanese visitor trends in Waikiki—could weigh on mixed-use and hospitality segments. Weather shocks, like the unprecedented rain events affecting Waikiki in Q1 2026, further highlight operational unpredictability. Even with strong position in retail and multifamily, material underperformance in office or hospitality could offset gains elsewhere and limit American Assets Trust’s margin for error, particularly during downturns or if refinancing conditions tighten in a higher rate environment.
