Key Takeaways
REX American Resources posted record first-quarter profitability, driven by higher margins despite slightly lower revenue.
Net income per share rose 115.4% year over year as gross profit more than doubled.
Management expects continued strength, with new production capacity and carbon capture progress to watch in 2026.

REX American Resources (NYSE:REX) achieved a milestone quarter, notching its highest ever first-quarter net income per share. The ethanol producer, which owns and invests in several production facilities and focuses on low-carbon energy solutions, benefited from a favorable cost environment and important federal tax credits. The company released its results for the first quarter of fiscal 2026 on May 28, 2026, reporting a strong start to the year despite some areas to watch. Overall, the period highlighted strategic progress even as top-line revenue edged down slightly.

Notable developments
Gross profit more than doubled, supported by $7.5 million in Section 45Z production tax credit income and lower corn costs.
Selling, general, and administrative expenses rose 64% year over year, reaching $9.7 million for the quarter.
Progress continued on the One Earth Energy expansion and carbon capture projects, with capital expenditures totaling $176.3 million so far.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management maintains a positive outlook focused on operational expansion and carbon capture initiatives, though it did not give firm guidance for coming quarters. Investors should monitor the ramp-up and commissioning of the expanded One Earth plant and watch for regulatory updates on the permitting of its carbon capture project, which could impact future tax credit income.

Quarterly highlights

The company achieved a steep rise in profitability for the period, as reflected in net income per share more than doubling and gross profit benefiting from federal production tax credits and lower input costs. While ethanol prices fell, strong gross profit and margins were supported by federal production tax credits and lower corn costs.

Operating costs were notably higher, with a significant jump in expenses such as selling, general, and administrative costs. This increase, if it continues, may require further explanation by management in future periods.

Strategic capital spending remained on pace, with projects to expand ethanol production and build out carbon capture and sequestration infrastructure at the One Earth Energy facility progressing within the budget of $220–230 million. Permitting remains a key hurdle to unlocking future value from these projects.

What Rex American Resources does

The company produces and markets ethanol and related products through ownership and investment in multiple U.S. production facilities, focusing on low-carbon energy and strategic growth in renewable fuels.
