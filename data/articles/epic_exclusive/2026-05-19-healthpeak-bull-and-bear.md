Healthpeak Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 19, 2026, 7:09 AM

Comments

Healthpeak Properties, Inc. (NYSE:DOC) is a leading real estate investment trust specializing in healthcare discovery and delivery through ownership, operation, and development of medical office buildings, life science/lab facilities, and a major stake in senior housing via Janus Living REIT. The company’s growth strategy centers on dominating core U.S. submarkets—especially Boston, San Francisco Bay Area, and San Diego—while recycling capital from non-core assets and leveraging demographic and technological demand across its focused portfolio.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Portfolio Concentration in Dominant Healthcare Clusters Enables Superior Pricing Power

Healthpeak’s deliberate geographic concentration in top-tier submarkets fuels competitive advantage and earnings growth – The company maintains a highly targeted presence in five primary healthcare innovation clusters, including Boston, San Francisco, and San Diego, which support deep local market penetration and product diversity. This approach mitigates risk through diversification at the customer and property-type level while enabling local scale to drive leasing momentum and above-average pricing. Recent acquisitions like the South San Francisco Gateway campus, completed in early 2026, are already generating substantial leasing upside above underwriting expectations. By emphasizing local dominance, Healthpeak is positioned to benefit from embedded demand and sustained high occupancy, resulting in stronger rent growth and stable cash flows relative to sector peers that lack scale in marquee locations.

2. Robust Leasing and Operating Metrics Signal Resilient Underlying Demand

Q1 2026 saw exceptional leasing activity and high occupancy across Medical Office and Life Science assets – The company executed 1.1 million square feet of leases in its outpatient medical office segment, achieving a 91% occupancy rate and positive cash re-leasing spreads of 5.4% (5.8% since its most recent merger). Life Science occupancy improved sequentially to 77.7%, with management guiding to further gains of at least 100 basis points by year-end. Forward visibility into move-outs and move-ins, combined with a tenant base anchored by large health systems and biopharma companies, provides stable recurring income. These metrics demonstrate stickiness of demand, ongoing net absorption, and resilience across economic cycles—a valuable defensive attribute for long-term investors.

3. Capital Recycling and Accretive Acquisitions Create a Secular Earnings Growth Platform

Strategic transactions like the Janus Living IPO and Blackstone outpatient recap deliver immediate and long-term financial benefits – Healthpeak’s March 2026 IPO of Janus Living unlocked public-market valuation multiples for its senior housing platform while retaining significant ownership (81.6%) and economic interest. IPO proceeds and other asset sale recapitalizations generated over $1 billion in capital, immediately redeployed into acquisitions in core markets. These deals are projected to be $0.04 per share accretive once stabilized and have already driven an increase in 2026 adjusted Funds From Operations (FFO) guidance to $1.71–$1.75 per share. This disciplined capital rotation strategy aims to preserve and grow portfolio earnings power, positioning the company to capture both near-term and structural, long-term trends in healthcare real estate.

4. High Dividend Yield Supported by Stable Payout Ratios and Cash Flows

A 7.5% annualized dividend yield signals both value and sustainability – The company paid over $200 million in dividends in Q1 2026, with management emphasized that the payout ratio is solid. This elevated yield is underpinned by recurring rents from outpatient tenants, with embedded 3% annual escalators and strong FFO coverage. The dividend is further supported by robust tenant retention (79% renewal in Q1) and proactive capital structure management, including leverage-neutral share buybacks at double-digit implied FFO yields. For income-focused investors, this combination of current return and dividend sustainability could offer attractive risk-adjusted value, particularly in a volatile rate environment.

5. Cycle Discipline and Execution Strength Reduce Downside and Accelerate Recovery

Management’s operational discipline through prior upcycles limits exposure and creates new growth opportunities – Healthpeak’s early pullback from overbuilding during the 2020–2021 boom, along with a proactive pause in non-core development, left it comparatively insulated from post-pandemic vacancy pain faced by sector peers. Leadership reinforced this through in-house leasing platforms—saving $5 million in commissions in Q1 alone—and targeted hiring (such as Denis Sullivan for the lab portfolio), demonstrating execution capacity. The company’s approach to capital allocation, local market focus, and hands-on management positions it to benefit from the recent upturn in biotech M&A and R&D funding, as reflected by the most active biotech equity issuance month since 2021 in April 2026. These factors suggest Healthpeak is structurally advantaged for outsized earnings growth as market and industry cycles recover.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Large Upcoming Debt Refinancing at Higher Rates Could Pressure Earnings

The $650 million debt maturity in June 2026 presents a material interest expense headwind – The company must refinance senior notes originally issued at a 3.5% coupon, a rate significantly lower than current market levels. This refinancing will drive up annual interest costs, with management explicitly describing it as an “additional headwind” that is partly but not fully offset in revised FFO guidance. Given that sector cap rates and spreads have widened post-pandemic, any further rate volatility or market stress could tighten margins, constrain dividend coverage, or dampen FFO growth in the near term. Investors should be aware that this balance sheet event represents a structural drag at least through the 2026–2027 period.

2. Life Science Vacancy and Absorption Risk Remains if Biotech Recovery Stalls

Ongoing elevated vacancy in the lab portfolio exposes the company to operating and leasing risk – While management guides for occupancy improvement in Life Science assets (from 77.7% and potentially up by 100 basis points by year-end 2026), approximately 2.5 million square feet remain to be absorbed. Should the current uptick in biotech funding and capital markets stall or reverse, there is risk that vacant space could linger, suppressing NOI (Net Operating Income) growth and delaying accretion from recent investments. Because lab property values are highly sensitive to funding and tenant expansion, any macro pullback could affect both earnings and portfolio valuations, limiting capital recycling options just as major deployments are planned.

3. Execution Missteps in Capital Deployment or M&A Could Impair Accretion Plans

Any failure to execute planned acquisitions, share buybacks, or further recapitalizations could diminish projected earnings growth – Healthpeak’s forecasted FFO accretion in 2027 and beyond is highly dependent on timely, efficient deployment of proceeds from the Janus IPO and asset sales. If acquisition pipelines stall, transaction costs overrun, or planned capital recycling with partners like Blackstone faces delays, there is risk that accretion to shareholders may underwhelm. Similarly, disciplined portfolio management and local-market-focused expansion must be maintained; a shift to less stringent underwriting or misjudging new market dynamics could erode the performance edge versus peers. Given the complexity and speed of current capital moves, ongoing successful execution is essential to fulfilling the bullish scenario.
