Everpure (NYSE:P) delivered record first-quarter results, capping a strong start to fiscal 2027 by raising its full-year outlook. The data management and storage platform provider reported broad-based growth in both product sales and subscription services.

The earnings release, published May 27, 2026, for the quarter ended May 3, showcased substantial revenue gains and a continued move toward subscription and cloud-based business models. Overall, the period displayed progress in all key focus areas, despite challenges with cash flow and industry-wide component cost pressures.

Notable Developments
Product revenue surged 55% year over year, fueled by strong demand for all-flash storage solutions.
Subscription-based services posted 17% growth, driving recurring revenue and leading to a 41% jump in committed future obligations.
Management lifted full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance to $4.41 billion–$4.51 billion, with the non-GAAP operating income range also increased.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Everpure’s leadership boosted its revenue and profit forecast for fiscal 2027, citing robust demand and successful product launches. While year-over-year growth in the next quarter is expected to remain high, the company cautioned about possible margin pressures due to ongoing supply chain and cost risks. Investors should keep an eye on free cash flow trends and whether investment spending continues to weigh on cash generation.

Quarterly Highlights

Growth was led by the core product segment, where demand for all-flash storage appliances and AI-ready hardware drove a 55% jump in product revenue compared to the prior-year quarter. Management noted that industry-standard benchmarks were set by products like FlashBlade//EXA, highlighting continued gains in performance and efficiency.

The subscription services business, including storage-as-a-service and cloud data management, grew 17%, underpinning future revenue visibility. Remaining performance obligations — which represent backlog from multi-year customer contracts — rose 41% year over year, supporting the company’s recurring revenue model.

However, free cash flow dropped nearly 50%, reflecting ongoing investment in R&D, integration of acquisitions, and higher stock-based compensation, as management balances growth with operational efficiency.
