Kodiak AI (NASDAQ:KDK) expanded its driverless trucking deployments and achieved strong revenue growth, marking operational progress. Notable Developments
Kodiak AI deployed 28 customer-owned driverless trucks by quarter-end, a 40% increase from the previous period.
Paid driverless operations reached 23,500 cumulative hours, more than doubling compared to the end of Q4 2025.
The company ended the quarter with $90.2 million in cash and marketable securities, excluding a freshly announced $100 million PIPE financing.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Leadership said it remains focused on scaling driverless vehicle deployments and executing a targeted commercial launch of long-haul autonomous trucking by late 2026. Management signaled continued investment in technology and partnerships, while warning of persistent negative free cash flow. Investors should watch Kodiak’s progress on full autonomy milestones and its ability to narrow quarterly losses as revenue grows.

Quarterly Highlights

The period saw Kodiak deploy eight additional fully driverless trucks with customers, reaching 28 in service and hitting the high end of its guidance for the quarter. Paid driverless operation hours surged 120% versus the previous quarter, reflecting both product adoption and growing customer base across logistics and defense sectors. Revenue growth was driven by expansion of the Driver-as-a-Service model, as more customer-owned driverless trucks completed commercial loads.

Kodiak’s non-GAAP operating expenses nearly doubled year over year to $33.7 million, a result of expanding research and development and related operating investments. Free cash flow remained sharply negative at $(35.0) million, though this was slightly less negative than the company’s own forecast. The $90.2 million cash position at quarter-end does not include a new $100 million private investment in public equity, which will add further liquidity as Kodiak pursues growth and works toward profitability.

Strategic initiatives included new partnerships such as a collaboration with General Dynamics Land Systems for military autonomous vehicles and planned integration of Nvidia DRIVE Hyperion technology into future trucks. Management also noted slower improvement in its long-haul Autonomy Readiness Measure, which rose just 2 percentage points to 86%, underlying the careful pace toward full-completion of safety milestones before broader commercial launch.

What Kodiak AI Does

Kodiak AI develops hardware and software technology to enable autonomous (driverless) trucking, providing solutions for logistics, industrial, and defense customers.
