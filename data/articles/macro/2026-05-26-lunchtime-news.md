1. Qualcomm Lands ByteDance AI Deal

Qualcomm (NASDAQ: QCOM) shares jumped as much as 8.3% to a fresh intraday record after Bloomberg reported the chipmaker reached a deal with TikTok parent ByteDance to supply AI data center chips. ByteDance is set to procure millions of application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) to power its AI agent software.

Breaking into AI: Qualcomm has long tried to push beyond smartphone processors into AI infrastructure, where customer wins have been scarce. ByteDance is poised to become one of the first major buyers of its ASICs, giving Qualcomm a high-volume client in a market still dominated by Nvidia (NASDAQ: NVDA).
China-sized opportunity: ByteDance recently boosted its AI infrastructure budget 25% to 200 billion yuan ($29.4 billion). Qualcomm's chips, made through partners like Taiwan Semiconductor (NYSE: TSM), should fall within U.S. thresholds for sales to Chinese firms, avoiding existing export restrictions.
2. AI Optimism Lifts Stocks

The S&P 500 (SNPINDEX: ^GSPC) and Nasdaq (NASDAQINDEX: ^IXIC) pushed near record highs as AI enthusiasm outweighed lingering Middle East risks. Semiconductor stocks led the rally, with the Philadelphia SE Semiconductor index climbing 4.6% to an all-time high.

Chips on fire: Micron (NASDAQ: MU) jumped nearly 18% and crossed $1 trillion in market value for the first time after UBS (NYSE: UBS) lifted its price target to $1,625 from $535. Intel (NASDAQ: INTC) rose 2.7% and Marvell Technology (NASDAQ: MRVL) added 4.2% alongside Qualcomm's ByteDance-driven surge.
Earnings tailwind: Q1 earnings growth is now tracking at 29% year over year, up from the 16% estimated a month ago, according to LSEG data. Investors are also turning attention to a pipeline of major private AI company IPOs, including SpaceX.
3. Lilly Bets $4 Billion on Vaccines

Eli Lilly (NYSE: LLY) shares rose 1.4% after the company announced plans to acquire three biotech firms for nearly $4 billion in cash, expanding into infectious disease research. The deals cover Curevo for $1.5 billion, LimmaTech Biologics for $780 million, and Vaccine Company for $1.55 billion.

Prevention play: Chief scientific officer Dan Skovronsky said the acquisitions reflect a strategy to prevent disease rather than treat its consequences. Curevo brings a shingles vaccine, LimmaTech targets drug-resistant bacterial infections like gonorrhea and chlamydia, and Vaccine Company is developing nanoparticle delivery technology to fight viral pathogens.
Diversifying beyond GLP-1s: Lilly held 60.1% of the U.S. obesity and diabetes drug market in Q1 versus Novo Nordisk's (NYSE: NVO) 39.4%. The vaccine push gives Lilly a new growth lane as it continues rolling out its newly approved obesity pill Foundayo alongside Zepbound and Mounjaro.
