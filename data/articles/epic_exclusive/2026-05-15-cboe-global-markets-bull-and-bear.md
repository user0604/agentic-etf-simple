Cboe Global Markets Bull and Bear

The Motley Fool Team

May 15, 2026, 7:09 AM

Comments

Cboe Global Markets (NYSEMKT:CBOE) operates one of the world's leading options and derivatives exchanges, with a business model anchored by proprietary products like S&P 500 and VIX options. The company’s growth strategy centers on maximizing high-margin U.S. options trading, expanding data services, and streamlining its portfolio through divestitures and realignment to focus on higher-return areas such as technology, analytics, and exclusive index-based contracts.

Potential Bull Cases
1. Industry-Leading Proprietary Products Reinforce Structural Revenue and Market Share Growth

Rapid adoption and dominance of exclusive index options power outsized top-line gains. In 2025, Cboe’s S&P 500 (SPX) zero days-to-expiry (zero DTE) options volume surged 66% year over year, with zero DTE contracts comprising 61% of all SPX trading, up from 51% the previous year. These proprietary products—unavailable on other venues—drove a 40% increase in net transaction and clearing fees from index options, helping the company set records for both volume and revenue. This trend highlights growing institutional and international demand for risk-transfer tools only Cboe offers, reinforcing its industry leadership and defending market share even as new competitors emerge.

2. Strategic Realignment Unlocks Margin Expansion and Accelerates Investment Capacity

Major portfolio optimization yields immediate and sustainable cost savings. Following comprehensive restructuring—culminating in a 20% workforce reduction and exits from non-core Canadian and Australian operations in 2026—the company expects $40–50 million in annualized pre-tax cost savings, with $20–25 million realized in 2026 alone. First quarter 2026 results already show improved operating margins, and management guidance points to further margin expansion as divestiture-related costs are removed from the ongoing expense base. By redeploying freed resources toward high-growth U.S. options, data, and digital asset services, Cboe is positioned to outpace peers in both profitability and innovation.

3. Recurring Revenue from Data and Technology Creates Resilient, High-Margin Growth

DataVantage and technology solutions drive reliable, diversified expansion. The DataVantage segment delivered 10% organic net revenue growth in 2025 and accounted for a rising share of new sales—45% of this segment’s quarterly growth came from international customers. With offerings like ultra-low latency access and novel time stamping, the company’s high-value recurring revenue base is expanding, supporting steady mid-to-high single-digit guidance for 2026. This recurring stream cushions against market cyclicality, adds high-margin income, and reinforces differentiation versus competitors tied solely to transaction activity.

4. Capital Return Commitment Strengthens Shareholder Alignment Amid Financial Flexibility

Consistently increasing dividends and opportunistic buybacks underscore a shareholder-friendly approach. In 2025, Cboe returned $350 million to shareholders, combining a 14% dividend increase and ongoing share repurchases. With $2.2 billion in adjusted cash and a leverage ratio under 1x, management has the flexibility to continue rewarding shareholders, invest in bolt-on innovation, and absorb restructuring costs without compromising financial stability. The anticipated proceeds from Canada and Australia divestitures further enhance the company’s ability to fund both capital returns and long-term growth initiatives.

5. Robust Product Pipeline and Regulatory Tailwinds Support Long-Term Differentiation

Innovative launches and improved regulatory clarity enable platform expansion. Cboe is set to introduce new proprietary event prediction contracts in 2026, leveraging the company’s regulatory expertise and ecosystem integration with SPX options. Recent U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission and Commodity Futures Trading Commission commentary provides clear boundaries for offering new products as registered securities, supporting a smoother path to market for these innovations. The ability to combine education, product design, and infrastructure upgrades anchors a defensible long-term moat in the face of competitive and regulatory complexity.

Potential Bear Cases
1. Intensifying Competition and Fee Pressure Threaten Revenue Capture

An overcrowded exchange landscape could erode pricing power and margins. The number of U.S. options exchanges is nearing 20, driving aggressive pricing schemes and the risk of market share battles. While Cboe leads with 22% multi-list options market share and dominant index contracts, management acknowledges ongoing risks from rivals promoting single-name zero DTE products and heightened pressure to adjust rebates and fees. The Options Regulatory Fee and potential industry-wide fee reforms could compress margins or force strategic concessions to maintain volume, weighing on future revenue growth relative to peers.

2. Execution Risk Around Realignment and Product Launches May Delay Benefits

Strategic realignment and divestiture timing present uncertainty in expense reduction and innovation delivery. Realizing the full $40–50 million in projected annual cost savings depends on the timely completion of workforce reductions that are subject to local laws, especially in international markets, potentially pushing savings realization beyond 2026. Meanwhile, the revenue ramp from new products like event prediction contracts is expected to be gradual, with only a modest contribution included in 2026 guidance. Any setbacks in regulatory approvals, partner readiness, or market adoption could prolong the timeline for translating strategic bets into material financial gains.

3. Market Dependence and Regulatory Changes Pose Volatility to Core Earnings

Reliance on U.S. options trading activity and regulatory shifts create earnings unpredictability. Additionally, ongoing global scrutiny of derivatives and digital asset markets could bring unexpected compliance burdens, business limitations, or competitive realignments. Even with proactive government relations hires, adverse regulatory surprises or pro-cyclical volatility swings could introduce sharp quarter-to-quarter profit swings and challenge the stability of current earnings trends.
