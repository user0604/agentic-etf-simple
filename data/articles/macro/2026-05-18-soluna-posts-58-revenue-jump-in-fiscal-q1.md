Soluna (NASDAQ:SLNH)’s newest projects helped boost sales again, but rising operating costs drove a wider loss for the company. The renewable data center developer specializes in converting underused renewable power into computing capacity used for cryptocurrency mining and cloud services. The results, covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and released on May 18, 2026, mark another period of rapid expansion mixed with mounting expenses and growing project complexity. Overall, the quarter showed robust growth but continued profitability challenges as Soluna invests in future capacity.

Notable Developments
Revenue jumped to $9.4 million as Dorothy 2 and Kati 1 sites started generating sales, but the net loss widened to $17.9 million as stock-based compensation costs soared.
Gross margins held steady at high levels for data hosting sites (up to 41% at Dorothy 2), but cryptocurrency mining margins turned negative as hashprice fell 18% quarter over quarter.
The development pipeline expanded to 4.3 gigawatts, with Soluna acquiring the 150 MW Briscoe Wind Farm to anchor an AI-focused data center campus, and cash on hand ended at $68.6 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Soluna’s leadership is prioritizing the expansion of its renewable-powered pipeline and accelerating into AI and high-performance computing (HPC) markets. While management highlighted a strong gross margin in core data hosting and progress on new projects like Kati 2 for AI, it did not provide specific earnings guidance for future periods. The most important development for investors to watch will be the pace at which new projects ramp to revenue, especially as AI and other non-crypto computing workloads begin to appear in reported sales.

Quarterly Highlights

Growth in revenue was fueled mainly by large new deployments, with Dorothy 2 becoming fully operational and Kati 1 contributing for the first time. Data hosting, now the largest business segment, saw both revenue and gross profit rise as expanded capacity drove higher utilization, offsetting lower margins in cryptocurrency mining, where earnings were squeezed by a drop in hashprice (a measure of mining revenue per unit of computing power).

Soluna completed the acquisition of Briscoe Wind Farm for $53 million, establishing the foundation for its upcoming Dorothy 3 project, a planned 300+ megawatt site targeting AI and HPC clients. Soluna’s project pipeline stands at 4.3 gigawatts at the end of 2025, reflecting management’s strategic ambition and the increasing capital needs for growth.

Cash usage was significant, with $6.4 million in net outflows from operations and a sharp increase in stock-based compensation costs to $10.2 million. The share count also rose, as equity funding continues to support Soluna’s development pipeline and integration of recent acquisitions.

What Soluna Does

Soluna develops and operates data centers that convert unused renewable energy into computing power for cryptocurrency mining, AI, and other cloud applications.
