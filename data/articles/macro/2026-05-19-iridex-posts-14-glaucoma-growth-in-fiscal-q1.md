Glaucoma device gains offset margin and retina pressures for Iridex (NASDAQ:IRIX) in the first quarter. The ophthalmic device maker, which develops laser systems for treating eye diseases, reported results for the period ended April 4, 2026, on May 19, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed steady cost control and recurring revenue, but highlighted ongoing international and margin challenges.

Notable Developments
Revenue from the Cyclo G6 glaucoma device line rose 14% year over year, with probe units sold up by approximately 11.5% to 15,500.
Retina product revenue dropped 12%, mainly due to international supply chain delays and regulatory issues, despite stable U.S. retina sales.
Gross margin declined by 3 percentage points, attributed to higher manufacturing costs and tariffs during Iridex’s transition to third-party contract manufacturing.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management reaffirmed full-year 2026 revenue guidance of $51 million to $53 million and maintained its outlook for positive annual cash flow. Operating expenses are expected to remain between $19 million and $19.5 million on an adjusted basis, excluding depreciation, amortization, and stock compensation. The most important factor to watch next is gross margin recovery, as pressure from manufacturing transition and tariffs continues to weigh on profitability.

Quarterly Highlights

Glaucoma device sales provided the main bright spot in the quarter, especially Cyclo G6 probe volumes, which are a key driver of recurring revenue. Growth was centered in the U.S, with probe sales up from 13,900 to 15,500 units compared to the prior year period.

Retina segment weakness countered glaucoma gains, with revenue in this product family falling $0.8 million year over year. Management cited ongoing regulatory and supply hurdles outside the U.S. as the main obstacle and signaled that future growth there depends on successful navigation of these barriers.

Gross margin shrank to 40.2%, falling short of last year’s level, as Iridex absorbed higher product costs and early expenses tied to shifting manufacturing to outside partners. The company plans to complete this transition by late 2026 or early 2027 to help margins recover.

What Iridex Does

Iridex develops, manufactures, and markets laser-based medical systems used to treat glaucoma and retinal diseases in the eye.
