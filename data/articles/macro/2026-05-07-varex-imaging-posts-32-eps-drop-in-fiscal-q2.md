Imaging technology specialist Varex Imaging (NASDAQ:VREX) reported steady customer demand but faced lower profits and margin pressure in the latest quarter. The company, a key supplier of X-ray imaging components for medical and industrial use, released its second quarter results for fiscal 2026 on May 7, 2026. Overall, the period reflected stable revenue, but profits slipped as higher costs weighed on performance.

Notable developments
Revenue climbed to $216 million, driven by strength in both the Medical and Industrial segments. Medical sales rose 1.6% and Industrial increased 1.2% year over year.
Profit margins narrowed. Non-GAAP gross margin declined by 2.4 percentage points to 33.9%, and Non-GAAP EPS dropped 32.3% from the prior year.
Inventories rose by $47.3 million from fiscal 2025 year-end to $346.7 million. Cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities dropped to $88 million from $155 million, reflecting increased working capital and March’s debt refinancing.
Quarterly highlights

The Medical segment supplied about 72% of total revenue, holding steady with year-over-year sales growth to $156.2 million. Gross profit in this segment declined, reflecting higher manufacturing costs and investments in new capacity, particularly in India.

The Industrial segment contributed $59.8 million to revenue, supported by demand for cargo security systems and new technology sales. Segment gross profit improved by 5.8%.

Research and development spending held at around $22.2 million, or roughly 10% of revenue, underscoring the company’s ongoing investment in advanced products such as photon counting detectors for both medical and industrial markets. These technologies, highlighted by management as key growth drivers, have yet to deliver higher overall profitability due to cost pressures and ongoing facility ramp-up expenses.

On the financial side, operating expenses increased to $58.2 million (up from $54.6 million a year ago), driven mainly by higher selling, general, and administrative costs and a significant rise in interest expense due to recent debt refinancing. Cash flow from operations remained negative, and elevated inventories suggest Varex is preparing for higher future shipments, but could face risk if demand does not materialize as expected.

Management outlook and what to watch

Management forecasts modest revenue growth for the second half of fiscal 2026, expecting it to be about 3% higher than the same period last year. However, profit margins are likely to remain under pressure, and guidance assumes no benefit from potential tariff refunds. Investors should closely monitor inventory levels and working capital, as further buildup may affect cash flow and future flexibility.

What Varex Imaging does

The company develops and manufactures advanced X-ray imaging components, supplying key parts for medical and industrial imaging equipment worldwide.
