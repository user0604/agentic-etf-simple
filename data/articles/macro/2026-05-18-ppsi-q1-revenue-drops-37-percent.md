PPSI Q1 Revenue Drops 37 Percent

Key Takeaways
Revenue declined sharply versus last year as the company navigated timing issues in its core mobile EV charging business.
Gross margin improved substantially to 13.6% despite the lower sales figure.
Management foresees growth as new product platforms ramp up and backlog conversion becomes a key focus.

Pioneer Power Solutions (NASDAQ:PPSI) struggled in its core business during the first quarter, as lower sales in mobile EV charging led to a notable revenue drop. The power equipment provider, known for mobile electric vehicle charging solutions and distributed power platforms, reported results for the period ending March 31, 2026 (released May 18, 2026). Overall, the quarter marked progress in margins and backlog, but losses and execution risks remain a concern.

Notable Developments
The revenue decrease was primarily driven by falling sales and rentals in mobile EV charging solutions (e-Boost), dropping by $2.4 million year over year.
Gross margin improved by more than 11 percentage points compared to last year, mainly from cost actions and better product mix in mobile charging solutions.
Backlog increased 11% to $13.9 million, supported by early commercial wins for the new PRYMUS platform and a $6 million contract award.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management expects future growth by converting its larger backlog and launching new distributed energy platforms like PRYMUS and PowerCore. The company did not provide numerical guidance for revenue or profit but stated it will keep focusing on disciplined execution and operating leverage. The most critical area for investors is whether backlog for new products translates into real revenue as the year progresses.

Quarterly Highlights

The core mobile EV charging unit (e-Boost) saw a substantial fall in sales, which management attributed to the timing of deployments. However, order activity remained steady at over $500,000 per month and the first shipment was made to a new partner in the Middle East, signaling steps toward international growth.

Pioneer announced a major commercial win for PRYMUS, its distributed energy system, securing a $6 million contract with a national logistics firm. The product is aimed at meeting rising energy demands from industries like data centers and artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, which require rapid access to megawatt-scale power.

The company implemented cost reduction measures, mainly in the e-Boost platform, which are expected to lower annual operating expenses by $1.5 million. This helped narrow the operating loss and improve gross margin, even as overall revenue dropped.

What Pioneer Power Solutions Does

Pioneer Power Solutions provides equipment and solutions for mobile electric vehicle charging, distributed power systems, and critical power backups for commercial and industrial customers.
