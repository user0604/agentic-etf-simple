Solv Energy (NASDAQ:MWH) delivered strong operational results in the first quarter, as rapid growth in renewable energy services pushed its project backlog and core profit metrics higher. Notable developments
Revenue climbed to $677 million, up 66% from a year ago, as the company expanded its footprint in large utility-scale projects.
Despite strong adjusted profitability, reported net loss reached $27 million, driven by a $521 million non-cash IPO-related compensation charge.
Backlog increased to $8.2 billion, and Solv Energy announced it will acquire Roberson Waite Electric to enhance its service range.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management raised its full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to the $435-$455 million range, reflecting ongoing demand and strong performance in the quarter. They pointed to robust revenue visibility from a deep project pipeline.

Quarterly highlights

The adjusted gross profit margin improved by 3.9 percentage points compared to the prior year. This margin expansion signals efficiency progress as Solv Energy continues to secure larger, higher-value contracts in utility-scale solar and battery installations.

The quarter’s net loss resulted from a $521 million non-cash compensation expense tied to the company’s post-IPO restructuring of equity awards. Excluding this event, underlying metrics such as adjusted EBITDA and gross profit showed significant year-over-year improvement, underscoring sustained demand for Solv Energy’s offerings.

Solv Energy’s announced acquisition of Roberson Waite Electric marks a strategic move to broaden its service offering, particularly in regulated utility infrastructure, and should further support its growth in key segments. The company’s operations and maintenance contracts now span nearly 22 gigawatts of generating capacity, supporting long-term business visibility.

What Solv Energy does

Solv Energy provides engineering, procurement, construction, and ongoing maintenance services for large-scale solar and battery energy storage projects.
