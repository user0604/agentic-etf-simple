In today's Breakfast News, we asked members, "When a stock you own has been moving in the opposite direction of the S&P 500 for over a year – you're down while the market's up – what do you do? Hold, add, or sell? What goes through your mind when making that call?"

C

CMFGouldberg

My default response is to hold underperforming positions. I always buy with a long time horizon in mind. And unless something thesis-busting has happened, I'm happy enough to give stagnant positions time to rebound. The exception to this is when I might have a tiny "tryout" position. If it's clear I'm no longer interested in adding to it, sometimes, I'll jettison those small positions after a long period of underperformance.

I monitor the thesis. If it's still intact, the underperformance is just noise or conditions outside the company's control. Price alone doesn't move me. If conviction is high, I'll even add at a discount. The beauty of being a Fool!

Mikehersch

re: Today's Question -

If it's a stock I've had for longer than a year, I look back across it's history to understand how it has performed up until this current year. Next, I dig dip to examine my feelings about its potential for the future. Depending on my analysis, I might (and have) done any of the three. Of course, if I've only had it for a year, I simply lean on my convictions that led me to buying it and will definitely hold it for, at least, a 3-5 year period.
