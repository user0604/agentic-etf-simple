Aligos Therapeutics (NASDAQ:ALGS) faced a challenging period as increased investment in clinical trial development led to a net quarterly loss. The clinical-stage biotechnology company focuses on developing therapies for chronic hepatitis B and other serious diseases. The latest results, reported on May 7, 2026, cover the first quarter of fiscal 2026 and show some progress in revenue but also highlight continued heavy research spending. Overall, the period underscored steady scientific progress and partnership activity, but also the financial constraints typical of late-stage biotech firms.

Notable Developments
Revenue from customers grew to $2.8 million, which remains small compared to spending.
Research and development expenses increased 61%, driven by expanded clinical trials and development activity.
Aligos secured a partnership with Amoytop, expected to bring in a $25 million upfront payment and strengthen cash reserves.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management states cash, including anticipated licensing payments, should fund operations into the fourth quarter of 2026. The immediate focus is on receiving the $25 million upfront payment from the Amoytop agreement. Investors should closely watch enrollment and data from the ongoing pivotal hepatitis B trials, as well as any updates on licensing transactions and their timing.

Quarterly Highlights

The company saw revenue rise sharply, reflecting an increase from out-licensing activities but not enough to offset ongoing research costs. Its research and development spending jumped to support expanded enrollment and longer-term dosing in major hepatitis B trials, particularly for its lead therapy, pevifoscorvir sodium. Aligos signed an exclusive licensing agreement with Xiamen Amoytop Biotech for Greater China, which includes a substantial upfront payment and possible future milestone payments, if received as scheduled.

Progress in the pevifoscorvir sodium program included the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) granting Fast Track Designation, which recognizes its potential as an improved therapy for chronic hepatitis B. Enrollment advanced in both negative and positive antigen patient cohorts within the ongoing B-SUPREME Phase 2 clinical trial, with no major safety concerns reported so far. Long-term study data and key interim trial analyses are expected in the coming quarters, with topline results targeted for 2027.

Outside hepatitis B, early pipeline programs such as the antisense oligonucleotide therapy ALG-170675 moved into investigational new drug-enabling studies, but there were limited updates on other candidates like ALG-055009 for metabolic diseases. The cash runway depends on a timely completion of the Amoytop transaction, given higher spending and declining cash reserves over the period.

What Aligos Therapeutics Does

Aligos Therapeutics develops and licenses novel therapies for liver and viral diseases, with a primary focus on chronic hepatitis B and related unmet medical needs.
