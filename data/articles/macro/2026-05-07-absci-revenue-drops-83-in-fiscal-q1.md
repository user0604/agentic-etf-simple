Key Takeaways
Revenue dropped sharply as no new partnership deals were signed.
Net loss per share narrowed to $(0.19) from $(0.21) a year ago.
Management maintained guidance for critical clinical milestones and sees partnership deals as a focus for 2026.

Absci (NASDAQ:ABSI) reported a tough start to fiscal 2026 as it did not secure new partnerships, leading to a notable drop in revenue. The clinical-stage biotechnology firm uses artificial intelligence to design antibody-based therapeutics, focusing on areas where there are unmet medical needs. Absci released its first-quarter results on May 7, 2026, for the period ended March 31, 2026. Overall, the quarter showed solid clinical progress but highlighted short-term financial pressure due to rising research spending and minimal revenue.

Notable developments
Revenue decreased (83.0%) from the prior-year period as no new partner program milestones were recognized in the quarter.
Research and development expenses rose 17.7% year over year, reflecting increased investment in Absci’s lead program and pipeline expansion.
Cash and equivalents stood at $125.7 million at quarter-end, with management projecting funding sufficient into the first half of 2028.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management kept its outlook unchanged for key clinical milestones, including initial data from its ongoing trial for ABS-201 in the second quarter of 2026. It continues to prioritize forming at least one new major partnership in 2026. Investors should watch the pace of partnership agreements as these will directly impact Absci’s ability to support future research efforts without raising additional funds.

Quarterly highlights

Revenue for the period was minimal due to the absence of new deals or milestone payments from existing partnerships, underscoring the irregular nature of the company’s current business model.

Spending on research and development climbed as Absci advanced its AI-designed antibody program, ABS-201, including the completion of single ascending dose cohorts and the start of multi-dose testing.

The company held $125.7 million in cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities at the end of March, which it expects will support its operating plans into 2028, provided spending remains consistent and new partnerships are secured as planned.
