CuriosityStream (NASDAQ:CURI) delivered improved gross margins but faced a setback in revenue momentum and profitability this quarter. The global factual streaming company reported lower earnings and decreased operating cash inflows as it shifted focus onto licensing and new international partnerships. The Q1 2026 results, released May 14, 2026, cover the first quarter of fiscal 2026. Overall, the results captured both operational progress and some new challenges as it transitions its business model.

Notable Developments
Gross margin reached 56.1%, up 3 percentage points from the prior year, reflecting benefits from high-margin licensing revenue.
Operating expenses rose sharply, with combined advertising, marketing, and general administration costs up 27% to $10 million.
The quarterly dividend increased to $0.085 per share, resulting in $4.9 million of dividends paid during the period.
Management outlook and what to watch

Management reaffirmed guidance for double-digit revenue and cash flow growth in 2026 and expects licensing revenue to surpass subscriptions for the full year. Executing this outlook requires a sizeable increase in revenue in Q2 and the rest of the year. The key factor for investors will be whether CuriosityStream can grow its high-margin licensing business fast enough to offset recent pressure on profits and cash flow.

Quarterly highlights

Gross margin improved year over year, reaching its highest level in five quarters as the company increased the role of high-margin licensing, including AI content deals, in its revenue mix. While the margin gain is a positive sign, it was outweighed by a steep increase in operating costs such as marketing and administration, which put additional pressure on bottom-line profitability in the quarter. This combination led to a net loss of $1.3 million and a 36% drop in adjusted free cash flow compared to the same quarter last year.

CuriosityStream launched new subscription partnerships in the U.S., Australia, New Zealand, and Germany, expanding its wholesale and international footprint. It also broadened its advertising-supported (FAST and AVOD) offerings, launching Hispanic-focused channels on Amazon, Roku, and LG platforms. Notably, the company highlighted its fourth straight quarter of growth in AI-related content licensing, signaling continued progress in diversifying revenue streams beyond core subscriptions.

The board raised the quarterly dividend to $0.085 per share, paying out $4.9 million even as free cash flow generation fell below this amount for the quarter. This move underlines management’s confidence but also increases pressure for improved cash generation to maintain future payouts, especially given rising costs and sequential revenue decline.

CuriosityStream operates a global streaming platform focused on high-quality documentary and factual programming, generating revenue from subscriptions, licensing, and advertising.
