Key Takeaways
Playtika delivered another revenue increase but saw significant profitability pressure due to up-front spending.
Revenue rose 5.5% compared to the prior year period.
Management raised its full-year outlook for both revenue and adjusted EBITDA.

Mobile gaming company Playtika (NASDAQ:PLTK) continued its focus on long-term growth but faced compressed profitability during the first quarter of fiscal 2026 (results released May 7, 2026). The company’s strategy drove a strong top-line result, with record Direct-to-Consumer revenue and growth in new casual games. Large, planned investments and a one-time accounting charge tied to a recent acquisition weighed on bottom-line results. Overall, the quarter showed rising revenue and strong DTC momentum, but margin pressure was a clear theme.

Notable Developments
Direct-to-Consumer revenue hit a record $291.8 million, now nearly 39% of total company sales, up from 36.8% in the previous quarter.
Profitability dropped as adjusted EBITDA margin fell to 16.8%, with margin pressure tied to front-loaded growth investments and a non-cash charge from the SuperPlay earn-out.
Free cash flow turned positive at $2.6 million after being negative in the prior year period, reflecting improved cash discipline despite ongoing investments.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management raised full-year targets for both revenue and adjusted EBITDA, citing robust DTC channel growth and strong results from new games like Disney Solitaire. Leadership stated that upfront spending for user acquisition and integration is expected to moderate as the year advances. The most important factor for investors to monitor will be whether margins rebound as projected, especially as major investments in SuperPlay and marketing begin to normalize.

Quarterly Highlights

The company's Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) channel delivered standout growth, crossing $291.8 million and nearly reaching the 40% revenue mix threshold the company has targeted. DTC platforms sell directly to players without major platform fees, strengthening economics and player relationships.

Profitability was held back by heavy marketing and operational spending, especially related to scaling the SuperPlay business and launching new titles. The period also included a large non-cash charge for contingent payouts owed from the SuperPlay acquisition, which are required when those acquired businesses outperform initial projections.

Performance among individual games was mixed: Disney Solitaire, a puzzle and card game from SuperPlay, posted sequential revenue growth above 70%, while legacy titles like Bingo Blitz saw sales slip both sequentially and year over year. This highlights the company’s need to balance investing in new game success while revitalizing established hits.

What Playtika Does

Playtika develops, publishes, and operates mobile games globally, focusing on casual and social casino titles, with an emphasis on live operations and player engagement.
