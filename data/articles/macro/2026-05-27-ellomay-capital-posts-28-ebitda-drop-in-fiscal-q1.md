Ellomay Capital faced a tough quarter as steep declines in electricity prices across Europe and currency headwinds led to a sizeable net loss. The integrated renewable energy producer operates solar, biogas, and pumped-storage hydro plants across Spain, Italy, the Netherlands, Israel, and the United States. In results released on May 27, 2026 (covering the first quarter of fiscal 2026), it reported weaker cash generation and earnings, highlighting ongoing pressure from market conditions.

Overall, the quarter proved difficult, with negative swings in profitability and cash flow despite ongoing progress in capacity expansion projects.

Notable Developments
The company posted a net loss of €12.2 million versus net income of €6.8 million in the prior-year quarter, largely due to weaker power prices and adverse exchange rates.
EBITDA fell from €2.9 million to €2.1 million as operating expenses and administrative costs both increased year over year.
Cash and cash equivalents totaled €83.7 million at March 31, 2026, boosted by asset sales, even as net cash used in operating activities turned negative at €1.9 million.
Management Outlook and What to Watch

Management did not issue formal financial guidance for the rest of fiscal 2026, citing ongoing volatility in electricity prices and regulatory uncertainty, especially in the U.S. and Israel. The most important area for investors to watch is whether European electricity prices stabilize, as this directly affects profitability and project execution. Progress in bringing new projects online and success in obtaining compensation for delays in Israel are also key areas to monitor.

Quarterly Highlights

Revenue dipped year over year as much lower electricity prices in Spain and Italy pulled down results. Net profit swung to a loss as the NIS appreciated against the euro and as both operating and administrative costs increased, partly tied to new projects and higher insurance expense.

Cash flow from operations dropped sharply into negative territory, with the company absorbing higher start-up costs from expansions in Italy, Texas, and the Netherlands. Despite this, total cash on hand rose due to proceeds from the sale of the Ellomay Luzon Energy stake, a move designed to reinforce liquidity during this challenging period.

Strategic projects advanced, including a 160 megawatt solar facility in Italy set for completion by year-end and regulatory milestones in Dutch biogas. However, a major storage project in Israel experienced construction stoppages related to regional conflict, and the company is in talks with authorities for compensation.
